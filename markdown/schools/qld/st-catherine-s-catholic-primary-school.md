Welcome to Wishart, QLD

Wishart feels like one of those suburbs that quietly does family life well. With a median age around 39–40, it’s a neighbourhood of owner‑occupied homes, married couples and kids rather than singles, and generally sits in the middle to upper‑middle income range. It’s mostly residential, the kind of place where weekday rhythms are defined by school runs and evening sports, and you’ll spot a lot of working parents juggling careers and family life. If you want the official snapshot, the Australian Bureau of Statistics and local suburb guides have the most recent numbers.

Inside the Classroom at St Catherine’s Catholic Primary School (Wishart)

St Catherine’s is a classic community primary, and if you’re moving here for school reasons the best next move is to confirm the exact grade range directly with the school. What stands out immediately is the strong creative and sporting culture: strings, band and piano lessons delivered by external tutors, a choir and a school musical mean performing arts get regular spotlight time, while a broad sports program—including netball, soccer, touch football, swimming and cross‑country—keeps most kids active year‑round. That mix tends to suit families who want both music and movement in their child’s week.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For busy families, St Catherine’s runs an on‑site Outside School Hours Care (on-site before and after school childcare) program through Catholic Early EdCare. They offer before‑school care from 6:45 to 8:45, after‑school care from 2:45 to 6:15, and vacation care roughly from 7:00 to 18:00. Practically speaking, that’s the kind of cover many dual‑income households find comforting—just double‑check enrolment details, capacity and the exact vacation‑care timetable with the provider before you rely on it.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Civic Fair and Newnham Road are where you’ll sort out groceries and quick dinners. There’s a neighbourhood FoodWorks supermarket for weekly shops, and a local Kabayan Filipino grocery if you’re after specific Asian ingredients. For evenings when you need something fast and family‑friendly, Vinny’s Pizza serves pizza and pasta, and local seafood outlets like Ocean Seafoods cover fish‑and‑chip cravings. Those practical local options make weeknight meals feel manageable even during the busiest weeks.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your child needs a bit of extra academic support, there are local and online tutoring options that serve Wishart—names like Alchemy Tuition and CloudTuition offer programs targeted at primary students. Commuting into the Brisbane CBD realistically means catching a bus from Newnham Road or Civic Fair: typical journey times average around 41 minutes, with the fastest trips close to half an hour depending on route and time of day. There isn’t a train station called Wishart, so if you or a partner are planning daily CBD travel, it pays to trial the bus at school‑run times and consult the local timetables.

Your Next Steps

Step 1: Call or visit St Catherine’s to confirm the exact grade range and to ask about enrolment requirements so you know where your child would sit in the school’s intake.  
Step 2: Ring Catholic Early EdCare to confirm Outside School Hours Care enrolment, capacity and the precise vacation‑care hours before you schedule work commitments around it.  
Step 3: Do a practical run: try the bus from Civic Fair or Newnham Road during school times to test the commute, and swing by FoodWorks, Kabayan and the takeaway spots to map out weeknight dinner options.
