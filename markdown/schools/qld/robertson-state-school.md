Welcome to Robertson (part of the Sunnybank area)

Robertson feels like the kind of neighbourhood where small everyday conveniences add up to a big, comfortable life. The median age sits around 37 and most households earn in the middle-to-upper range, with about 2.8 people per household on average. You’ll notice a healthy mix of young adults and family households, and one of the real comforts for families moving from overseas is Robertson’s strong overseas-born community — particularly people born in China — which makes settling in and finding familiar shops, food and social networks so much easier. Robertson sits inside the wider Sunnybank retail and food precinct, so parks, local shops and a lively Asian shopping and dining scene are all part of the backdrop to family life here.

Inside the Classroom at Robertson State School

Robertson State School runs Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland), and the classroom culture places a pleasing emphasis on creativity and movement as much as literacy and numeracy. If your child loves music, this is a real draw: there’s a strong instrumental program with multiple string ensembles, two choirs, and band starting in Year 4 (strings begin in Year 3). Sport is equally active in the school week, with regular health and physical education lessons, a school cross-country and athletics calendar, Years 5–6 interschool gala days and clear links to representative pathways for kids who want to push further. External providers also come in to offer tennis, basketball, gymnastics, soccer and cricket, so there are always options for children to try something new or get serious about a favourite.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For working parents, the practicalities matter and Robertson State School provides on-site outside school hours care — that is, before- and after-school childcare on the school grounds — operated by Helping Hands. The program is approved for the childcare subsidy system, and families can enrol directly with Helping Hands. Having care right on campus makes the logistics of juggling work, school pick-ups and afternoon activities much simpler, especially if you’re still learning local drop-off routines and transport options.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Living in Robertson means your closest grocery and dining lanes are part of Sunnybank’s rich food precinct. For weekly shopping you can choose between a large supermarket presence at Sunnybank Plaza and smaller specialty grocers such as Moamart on Zamia Street, which is great for stocking up on fresh Asian ingredients. Evenings and quick weeknight dinners are easy to solve: the Zamia Street and Sunnybank strip is peppered with casual, family-friendly takeaway and restaurants — think Korean and other Asian flavours, seafood spots and countless small eateries where hungry kids can find something familiar and adventurous eaters can explore new tastes.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your child needs a little academic boost, there are local tutoring and enrichment options nearby, including Kumon and Kip McGrath centres in Sunnybank that offer assessment-based programs for maths and English. For families commuting into Brisbane’s CBD, Robertson doesn’t have its own rail station; the nearest practical train option is Banoon on the Beenleigh line. Typical train travel from Banoon to Brisbane Central is around 30 to 35 minutes, which can work well for school-run planning or city commutes, but families often like to double-check local parking and drop-off rules on first visits.

Your Next Steps

First, call Helping Hands to confirm hours, enrolment procedures, any waitlist and how the childcare subsidy applies, so your after-school care is sorted before term starts. Second, ask the school about music-program entry rules, whether you need to hire an instrument and any associated lesson costs or waiting lists for strings (Year 3) and band (Year 4). Third, take a weekend to walk the neighbourhood — pop into Sunnybank Plaza, check Moamart and grab dinner on Zamia Street — and while you’re out, check train times from Banoon and where you might park for drop-offs.
