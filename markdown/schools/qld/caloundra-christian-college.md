Welcome to Caloundra

If you’re picturing sunlit sidewalks, family barbecues and weekend swims with toddlers wrapped in hooded towels, you’re close. Caloundra on the southern Sunshine Coast reads like a coastal family suburb rather than an inner‑city singles precinct: the median age sits around 44 and weekly household incomes cluster near $1,546, with a workforce made up of professionals and managers alongside trades, retail and hospitality. Most residents are Australian‑born, with England the largest overseas‑born group—a small single‑digit slice of the population. That mix helps keep Caloundra feeling familiar to newcomers while still offering those gentle coastal rhythms families chase: beaches, school fetes, weekend markets and enough local shops to make weekday life easy.

Inside the Classroom at Caloundra Christian College

Caloundra Christian College serves younger families through Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland) as part of its broader structure. The school leans into a balanced extracurricular life: performing arts are a clear focus with a primary choir, secondary chapel ensembles, private instrumental and vocal tuition and regular performances and musicals. Sport is equally active, with co‑curricular competitive programs in basketball, futsal and Oztag and clear pathways into inter‑school and local club competition. Parents who want a school day that extends beyond math and spelling will likely appreciate how music and sport are woven into the week without feeling like extras you have to fight for.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

If your weekdays end with the classic juggling act—work, pickup, piano, dinner—you’ll be relieved to know the College operates on‑site Outside School Hours Care (on-site before and after school childcare). Before School Care runs from 7:00 to 8:00 am, After School Care from 3:00 to 6:00 pm, and Vacation Care covers 7:00 am to 6:00 pm during school holidays. Bookings and the fee schedule are handled through the school, so you can arrange regular places or occasional cover without sending kids off to an external provider. That on‑site continuity makes evening commutes less frantic and lets children stay within the school community for clubs and catchups.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Caloundra’s food scene is very family-friendly in the practical sense: casual Vietnamese at Pholklore serves steamy pho and fresh rolls you can eat with one hand while corralling a toddler, Amici Restaurant offers straightforward pizza and pasta for family nights, and The Thirsty Beaver is the kind of pub/takeaway that saves a Tuesday. If you like to cook at home, local grocery options are refreshingly varied: there’s an Asian grocer and an Indian/South Asian shop for hard‑to‑find ingredients, a specialty organic grocer for salads and slow‑cooking staples, and a bulk health food store if you buy nuts, grains and cereals by the kilo. Between takeaway ease and good pantry shopping, weeknights can be both simple and interesting.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

For families who like a little academic insurance, local tuition options are available. Kip McGrath offers English and maths support and Alchemy Tuition provides broader K–12 subject support and exam prep, both handy if your child wants steady catch‑up work or a boost in a tricky subject. Commuting into Brisbane from Caloundra is doable, but be realistic: there’s no train station on the Brisbane–Sunshine Coast rail line in Caloundra itself. Most people use the regional bus and coach services—Route 600 is the main link—and a bus into Brisbane will commonly run about two to three hours depending on the service. A popular workaround for regular train access is to drive north to a Sunshine Coast line station such as Landsborough or Beerwah and take the train from there. If you’re planning a job that requires daily CBD access, try the commute at least once before you commit.
