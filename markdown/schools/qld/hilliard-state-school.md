Welcome to Alexandra Hills

Alexandra Hills feels like the kind of suburb that quietly knows how to make family life work. With a median age of 38 and a median weekly household income around $1,837, most streets are full of partnered households and parents juggling work and kids’ activities. The workforce is a healthy mix of white‑collar and skilled blue‑collar jobs, so you’ll find neighbours who commute to offices as well as those who work trades or local businesses. About three quarters of residents were born in Australia, with New Zealand and England the next most common birthplaces — a little comforting if you’re arriving from those countries and want familiar accents and community vibes while you settle in.

Inside the Classroom at Hilliard State School

Hilliard State School teaches students from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland) and has the kind of extra programs that make a primary school feel alive. Kids in Years 4 to 6 can join the instrumental band, there’s a school choir and active interschool sports that get students out competing with nearby schools. Lunchtime clubs, from dance to rotating interest groups, keep the days interesting beyond maths and reading, and the school’s emphasis on arts and movement makes it an easy place for children to find something they love.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For families who need before and after school care, Hilliard has on‑site Outside School Hours Care run by the YMCA that covers before school, after school and vacation care. That on‑site setup is one less logistic to worry about on busy mornings and late afternoons — drop off at the school, pick up from the same place, and know the same caregivers are seeing your child through homework, snacks and play. It’s a practical convenience that makes working days run smoother for parents who can’t be there at 3 PM sharp.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Grocery runs and weeknight dinners around here are pleasantly low‑stress. Alexandra Hills Shopping Centre is anchored by Woolworths and there’s a local IGA for top‑up runs, while the broader Bayside and Capalaba area add specialty and ethnic grocers when you want ingredients for something more adventurous. When you don’t feel like cooking, there’s a friendly mix of casual options close to home: a comfy café/takeaway inside the shopping centre, some reliable fish and chip spots, and Thai and Southeast Asian takeaways for quick midweek flavours. It’s the kind of place where you can swing by for groceries, pick up dinner and still get the kids to their after‑school activity without a second trip.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you’re keen on extra academic support, there are nearby providers for primary literacy and numeracy tutoring — options that many local parents use to keep skills sharp through the primary years. Commuting to Brisbane’s CBD is doable but worth testing: Alexandra Hills doesn’t have a train station, so most public‑transport trips to the city are by direct bus services, with scheduled travel times typically around 80 to 82 minutes depending on traffic. That makes a trial run during peak times a good idea if you’ll be commuting regularly, or if you need to plan childcare and work hours around getting kids to school and yourself to the office.

Your Next Steps

First, check the Hilliard State School enrolment zone and confirm which year your child would start so you’re clear on timing. Second, book a school tour and ask specifically about the instrumental band for Years 4 to 6, the choir, interschool sports and the lunchtime clubs that matter to your child. Third, contact the YMCA Outside School Hours Care via the school page to confirm before and after school and vacation care hours, fees and enrolment steps, and try the bus commute during peak times so you know how long it will really take.
