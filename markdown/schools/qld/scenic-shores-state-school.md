# Welcome to Redland Bay

If you’re picturing a family-friendly coastal suburb where weekend beach strolls and backyard barbecues feel normal, Redland Bay in Queensland is exactly that sort of place. The median age sits around 39, and you’ll find a lot of established families and home‑buyers here — married households are common and the neighbourhood carries that relaxed, middle‑income vibe. Work in the area tends to be a mix of hands‑on trades and clerical or administrative roles, so you get a comfortable blend of white‑ and blue‑collar energy. Most people are Australian‑born, which helps newcomers settle in with a familiar community rhythm while still enjoying easy access to small‑town coastal life.

# Inside the Classroom at Scenic Shores State School

Scenic Shores State School caters to Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland), so your child will be in a single, close‑knit primary environment from their first year through to Year 6. The school feels small but engaged: you’ll find hands‑on sustainability programs like EcoMarines, a kitchen‑garden through the Garden Gurus, and even Chicken Champions for kids who want to learn where food comes from. A Chess Club meets during breaks and after school for quieter, brainy play. All of this adds up to a practical, grounded primary education where outdoor learning and community projects are part of the rhythm as much as reading and maths.

# Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For parents juggling work and school pickups, Scenic Shores has on‑site care run by Jabiru that covers before school, after school and school‑holiday care. They list hours that can stretch early in the morning to around 6:30am and run until about 6:00pm for families who need extended coverage, and they’re registered on national care directories. That on‑site option means you don’t have to coordinate cross‑suburb pickups and gives you a single place to drop off or collect kids while you’re at work.

# The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

When life gets busy and weeknight decisions need to be fast and family‑friendly, Redland Bay makes it easy. The local supermarket scene includes an IGA and Buy‑Rite for staples, while a short drive brings you to ethnic grocery options if you want a wider ingredient range for stir‑fries, curries or more adventurous weeknight meals. If cooking completely isn’t on the cards, the neighbourhood has casual, child‑approved takeaways: wood‑fired pizza, classic fish and chips and several Thai and Chinese options in the shopping village. It’s the sort of place where you can rotate fresh salads with a takeout favourite without sacrificing variety.

# Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you’re thinking about targeted academic support, there are local tutoring options that will come to the house or run online, including primary‑focused tuition and maths specialists. They’re a good call if you want a little extra confidence for homework or to boost a particular subject. Commuting to central Brisbane is doable but worth trialling: there isn’t a local rail station in Redland Bay, so daily trips to the CBD typically rely on bus services and occasional ferry links. Typical door‑to‑door travel times vary with connections and traffic — expect roughly 50 to 90 minutes depending on when you travel and how many transfers you take.

# Your Next Steps

First, ring Jabiru to lock in the exact before‑and‑after school and vacation care hours, booking rules and any waitlist details so you know your drop‑off options. Second, pop into Scenic Shores to meet the principal and the Jabiru team, and ask to see the EcoMarines, Garden Gurus and Chess Club in action so you can picture your child there. Third, trial the commute at the times you’d actually travel — take the bus route you would use and time the full door‑to‑door trip so school runs and workdays aren’t a surprise.
