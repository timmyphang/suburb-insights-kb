Welcome to Randwick

Randwick sings of easy beacheside life without feeling like a tourist town. Households here report a comfortable median weekly income and a civic mix that leans professional and youthful: young adults, families and plenty of working parents make up the rhythm of the suburb. A large share of residents were born in Australia, with a steady stream of people arriving from overseas in recent years — that cultural mix makes it easier for newcomers to find familiar foods, language links and friendly neighbours who’ve navigated the move before.

Inside the Classroom at Our Lady of the Sacred Heart (OLSH) Catholic Primary School, Randwick

OLSH is a warm K–6 Catholic primary where classroom routines are traditional but lively. Students from Kindergarten through Year 6 follow a structured curriculum that places a clear emphasis on music and ensemble work from Year 3. The school runs an Amadeus small-group music tuition and ensemble model for Years 3 to 6, so if your child loves singing, learning an instrument or joining a choir, the program gives them a path to perform and grow. Sport is an everyday part of school life too, with school sports teams and regular opportunities for competition and fitness. The campus culture feels family-friendly and activity-rich — great for kids who prefer a mix of arts, music and physical outlets.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For parents balancing a career and school runs, OLSH offers on-site Outside School Hours Care through the provider Galaxy, sometimes called “GOSH.” That program runs a before‑school session from around 7:00am and after‑school care until about 6:00pm, giving you a formal, campus-based childcare option so you don’t have to rush between drop-off and the office. If you’ll rely on before and after care, it’s one of the most practical conveniences a school can offer — but popular slots do fill, so early confirmation is wise.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Grocery runs in Randwick are pleasantly neighbourhood-sized. The Royal Randwick precinct includes a mainstream supermarket and Miracle Supermarket for everyday shopping, while Randwick Oriental Supermarket on Belmore Road is a go-to for Asian and other ethnic ingredients that make weeknight cooking feel like home. When you’d rather not cook, Avoca Street’s food strip and the Randwick Food Hub offer casual family-friendly options — everything from classic fish and chips or burgers to kebabs — and there are standout independent choices like the Himalaya Restaurant if you’re craving richer South Asian flavours.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your child benefits from extra academic support or structured after-school learning, there are local options close to the school. Kumon on Avoca Street offers maths and English programs, and Kip McGrath runs tailored tutoring for primary-age students. For your commute into the Sydney central business district (commonly called the CBD), Randwick doesn’t have a heavy-rail station; instead the L2 Randwick light-rail line is the most reliable public-transport link to the city. Typical travel to Central area stops is around 15 to 20 minutes on the light rail, with buses available as alternatives though they can be slower in peak traffic. Planning your day around the light-rail timetable usually gives the most consistent trip times.

Your Next Steps

First, call OLSH to check current K–6 enrolment availability and confirm the details and registration process for the on-site Outside School Hours Care (Galaxy/GOSH). Second, if you’ll need before or after school care, lock in spots early because those sessions are popular with working families. Third, ask about the Amadeus music program and sports sign-up so your child can be placed in the right ensemble or team from the start.
