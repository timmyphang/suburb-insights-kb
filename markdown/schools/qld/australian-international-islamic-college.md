Welcome to Durack (Qld)

If you’re picturing a lived‑in, family‑forward pocket of Brisbane, Durack is that kind of neighbourhood. It’s a mixed, broadly middle‑income suburb where families make up a big part of the scene; average household size sits around 2.6 people and median weekly household income was roughly $1,264 in 2021. The local workforce is a comfortable blend of white‑ and blue‑collar jobs, and you’ll notice a strong Vietnamese presence—Vietnam is historically the largest overseas country of birth for residents here. That visible multiculturalism means many new arrivals find real practical comfort: shops, services and community networks that understand life in a new country, plus neighbours who speak multiple languages and can help with the little things that make settling in easier.

Inside the Classroom at Australian International Islamic College — Durack campus

The Durack campus of the Australian International Islamic College (AIIC Durack) runs from Prep to Year 12, with the primary years organised as Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland). Expect a school culture that balances faith-based learning with lively co‑curricular life: performing arts moments such as student nasheed performances sit alongside Quran and Hifz programs. Sports are actively encouraged through events and teams, and there’s a genuine push on STEM—AIIC has a dedicated STEM building featuring 3D printing, robotics and virtual reality—plus outdoor learning in a bushcraft and conservation park. The overall feel is one where religious studies, creative performance, physical education and hands‑on tech all get airtime, so if your child thrives in a mix of structured faith learning and practical, activity‑based programs, this campus tends to suit that profile.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

If you need care before or after the school day, AIIC Durack’s public campus pages don’t advertise a school‑run Outside School Hours Care (on‑site before and after school childcare), so it’s worth asking the campus directly when you call about enrolment. For families who require guaranteed on‑site before‑and‑after‑school care, nearby Durack State School operates a formal Outside School Hours Care program and can be a practical fallback. Whether you end up relying on a school program, local community vacation care or family networks, the neighbourhood’s family focus means there are typically multiple practical options to explore.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Durack keeps mealtimes lively and easy. You’ll find mainstream supermarkets for staples and plenty of specialty and ethnic grocers that cater to Asian, South Asian and halal needs—shops on nearby Biota Street in Inala such as Tan Phat and Vu Hai make cooking familiar home dishes straightforward, while Krishna Island and local halal butchers mean you won’t be hunting far for ingredients. For quick weeknight dinners, the Durack Tavern is reliably family‑friendly with a bistro menu, Domino’s Durack is the easy pizza fallback, and Inala’s takeaways—places like Abra Kebabs and Lanka Hut—offer halal and South Asian flavours when you want something ready to eat after a busy day. For families arriving from overseas, having those groceries and familiar flavours close by is one of the small comforts that helps the transition feel faster.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your child needs extra academic support, local tutoring options are active and accessible. You can find maths tutors who visit Durack through services like Ezy Math, and online tuition options such as Cloud Tuition are set up to serve local students across primary and secondary years. Commuting to Brisbane’s central business district is realistic for daily workers: Durack doesn’t have its own train station, so people rely on buses and nearby rail connections. Typical bus travel times to the CBD are often in the 20 to 30 minute range depending on the route and the time of day, so many families manage school runs and a reasonable commute without long travel days.

Your Next Steps

First, ring or email the Durack campus contact to confirm year‑level availability, primary‑specific programs, current fees and whether the school runs on‑site Outside School Hours Care. Second, if on‑site before‑and‑after‑school childcare is essential for your routine, ask explicitly for their OSHC details and, if AIIC doesn’t provide it, get information on nearby providers such as Durack State School’s program or community vacation care. Third, arrange a campus visit to see the classrooms, STEM and bushcraft spaces and to ask about participation requirements or extra costs for nasheed/Quran‑Hifz, sports and STEM activities—meeting the teachers and touring the facilities will make the fit much clearer.
