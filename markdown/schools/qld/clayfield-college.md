# Welcome to Clayfield (Brisbane)

Clayfield feels like one of those inner-north Brisbane suburbs where professional life and family life find a comfortable balance. You’ll notice young adults and established families side by side, a median age of about 37, and household incomes that skew higher than average — the kind of place where weekend coffee catch-ups, school sports, and a tidy commute into the city all fit together. If you’re arriving from overseas, you’ll also appreciate that Clayfield has a fairly diverse community mix; seeing neighbours who speak other languages and come from different countries can make the transition to life in Australia a lot easier.

# Inside the Classroom at Clayfield College — Junior School (Prep to Year 6, note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland)

Clayfield College’s Junior School covers Prep to Year 6 and leans into a broad co‑curricular program that parents love. Sport is active and organised: swimming, netball, Australian rules football, cricket and athletics feature in interhouse carnivals and representative pathways, so kids who enjoy weekly training and weekend fixtures have plenty of options. Music is also well resourced for little learners; there’s a staffed instrumental and ensemble program that runs beginner band and string ensembles alongside private instrumental and vocal lessons. For families who want a school day that blends classroom consistency with clear pathways into sport and music, the Junior School has the kit and the culture to support that.

# Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

Outside school hours care is available on campus through a provider called Children First, who run before‑school, after‑school and vacation care. That on‑site option is convenient for working parents juggling office time and school pick‑ups — just check the finer details with the school and the provider as Children First does not provide care for children who aren’t yet in Prep (that is, pre‑Prep or pre‑school age). Confirming daily start and finish times, how bookings are handled, and any waitlist or fee structures before enrolment will make the first term so much smoother.

# The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Clayfield has a surprising little food scene for a suburb that’s primarily residential. If you like cooking Asian meals at home, Genki Mart has Japanese pantry staples, and Raj Food Max stocks a solid range of Indian ingredients. For fruit and veg, Harris Farm Markets is the go‑to for quality produce. When you need a night off, Amalfi Pizzeria is a reliable pizza takeaway, Clayfield Seafood Markets serves classic fish and chips and seafood options for a low‑fuss dinner, and Siam Pagoda Thai Garden covers the dine‑in or takeaway Thai craving. The combination of specialty groceries and easy takeaways makes weeknight logistics manageable — handy when rehearsals, sports or music lessons finish late.

# Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you’re considering extra academic support, Clayfield has local providers like NumberWorks’nWords and KUMON nearby, so arranging after‑school tuition or weekend maths practice is convenient. For commute planning, the train into Brisbane’s central business district is quick: typical services from Clayfield station reach Brisbane Central in roughly 12 to 16 minutes depending on the service and time of day. It’s worth trialling the trip at peak times and checking live timetables before finalising your work arrangements, but overall the suburb offers an easy city commute that keeps your evenings free for school runs and family time.

# Your Next Steps

First, call the school and Children First to confirm outside school hours care times, age limits and how bookings work — remember Children First cannot care for pre‑Prep children. Second, book a visit to the Junior campus to see the sport pathways and the instrumental/ensemble music program in action and to confirm when extracurricular activities run. Third, try a morning or evening train commute on a weekday to see how the timings fit your workday, and while you’re out, check the local grocer and takeaway options so you have a practical weeknight plan.
