# Welcome to Toowong

Toowong feels like a city village where young professionals and students rub shoulders with settled families. The median age is about 30 and a large share of residents have never married, so you’ll find lots of singles and couples, alongside parents who’ve chosen inner‑city convenience without sacrificing green spaces and community feel. Household incomes sit around a comfortable middle‑to‑higher inner‑city level, so cafés, local services and family options are all quietly polished—perfect if you want a neighbourhood that’s lively but not frantic.

# Inside the Classroom at Toowong State School

Toowong State School teaches children from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland). The school has a friendly, arts-and-sport leaning culture: there’s an instrumental music pathway and a school band for Years 4 to 6, senior and junior choirs including an Auslan signing choir, and interschool sport for older primary students. These activities give kids a structured way to find their people—great if your child learns socially as much as academically. Enrolments and extra‑curricular sign-ups are straightforward to manage through the school’s systems, and the volunteering and involvement culture is visible at every school performance and weekend fundraiser.

# Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

If you work full days or have early shifts, Toowong State School helps bridge the gap with on‑site Outside School Hours Care, known as TOSHC. The service opens for before‑school care from 6:45am and runs after school until 6:00pm, and they also run vacation care during the holidays. The program is run by the school’s Parents and Citizens association—parents are actively involved in oversight—and enrolments are handled through Xplor, the school’s booking platform. That means you can get kids fed, supervised and on the way to homework clubs without juggling multiple providers.

# The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Groceries and quick dinners are a practical win in Toowong. Coles at Toowong Village covers the week’s basics, while nearby Asian supermarkets such as Hanaromart are handy for specialised ingredients if you cook familiar family staples. When cooking feels like a stretch, the riverside Regatta Hotel is a relaxed, family‑friendly spot with a view, and there are cosy cafés like Pitch & Fork or a clutch of takeaways offering Asian and casual meals for weekday savers. Between the village shops and the riverside options, weeknight dinner is rarely a logistical headache.

# Tuition and the Daily Commute

For extra academic support, Toowong has options close to home. Kumon offers structured Maths and English programmes and there are local private tutors and small centres such as Alchemy Tuition for targeted, one‑to‑one help. If your family relies on public transport for work or city life, the train is a real time-saver: Toowong Station to Brisbane Central or Roma Street typically takes about seven to nine minutes, roughly eight minutes on many services. That fast rail link makes commuting realistic for parents and older kids heading into the city for study or extracurriculars.

# Your Next Steps

First, contact Toowong State School to arrange a tour and confirm enrolment steps for Prep to Year 6 and any extra‑curricular interests like the band, choir or sport. Second, confirm TOSHC enrolment, opening hours and current fees with the school or the Parents and Citizens association, remembering that bookings are managed through Xplor. Third, if you plan to commute, trial the train at your usual travel time and check timetables so you know how that morning and afternoon rhythm will actually work.
