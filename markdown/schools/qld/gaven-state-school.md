# Welcome to Oxenford

Oxenford feels like a quietly confident family suburb: the median age sits around 37 and households typically bring in about $1,832 a week, which matches the working, middle‑income vibe you’ll notice on playgrounds and at weekend footy. There’s a healthy cohort of kids under 15, so you’ll see prams, scooters and schoolbags everywhere. About two‑thirds of residents were born in Australia, and a meaningful portion of families hail from overseas — many from England — which translates into friendly streets where newcomers settle in quickly and multicultural comforts are part of daily life.

# Inside the Classroom at Gaven State School

Gaven State School projects the cheerful, community‑minded energy you want for primary years. Sport is a big part of school life: athletics, cross‑country and swimming carnivals are regular calendar highlights, and district interschool teams take part in soccer, rugby league, netball and touch. Literacy and community connection sit alongside the sports program — the school runs the Premier’s Reading Challenge, celebrates NAIDOC Week and hosts Life Education visits and school discos. Music and languages other than English are supported with help from the parent body, so children get a balanced mix of active, creative and cultural learning.

# Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

If you need on‑site before‑ and after‑school care, Gaven runs a program operated by PCYC called the Gaven Fun Squad. This service is user‑pays but eligible for the Australian government’s Child Care Subsidy, and families can contact the PCYC coordinator or enrol through the PCYC listings or the national StartingBlocks directory. For many parents, having that supervised, on‑site option makes juggling work, sport and homework far easier — and the PCYC team are experienced with the rhythms of school days.

# The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

For quick grocery runs, SPAR Express Oxenford and the shops at Oxenford Village and Oxenford Boulevard cover day‑to‑day essentials without a long drive. When you want to eat out, local favourites include My Friends Asian Restaurant and a handful of casual Indian and Asian takeaways — perfect for those nights when unpacking the school bag takes priority over cooking. The dining scene here is familiar, unfussy and very family‑friendly.

# Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you want extra academic support, Oxenford is served by NumberWorks'nWords for tailored maths and English tuition, and you’ll also find private and online tutors listed through local services like Cloud Tuition and Superprof. Commuting to Brisbane requires planning: Oxenford itself has no train station, so most families catch a local bus to Helensvale or Coomera and transfer to the Gold Coast rail line. A practical door‑to‑door estimate into Brisbane’s CBD is about an hour and ten minutes, depending on connections and morning traffic, so it’s worth trialling the trip before committing to a daily commute.

# Your Next Steps

Ring Gaven State School to confirm grade levels, enrolment paperwork and to book a visit or open‑day spot so you can see the school in action. Check availability, fees and Child Care Subsidy eligibility with PCYC Gaven Fun Squad for their before‑ and after‑school care, and ask the school for any enrolment guidance they offer. While you’re visiting, watch for the extracurriculars that matter to you — sports trials, a reading event or a music class — and if you think your child would benefit from tuition, reach out to NumberWorks'nWords or local tutors to set up an assessment.
