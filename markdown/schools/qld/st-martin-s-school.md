Welcome to Carina (Brisbane, QLD)

Carina feels like the kind of suburb you can picture before you arrive: leafy streets, established family homes and a steady hum of weekday life where professionals and clerical workers head out to the office and kids pedal to school. The median age sits around 37 and the median household income is slightly above the broader Brisbane area, which shows in neat front gardens and well‑kept local shops. Most people you’ll meet were born in Australia, with the next largest groups from New Zealand and England, so there’s a familiar, settled vibe that makes settling in less of a leap for families moving from overseas.

Inside the Classroom at St Martin’s School

St Martin’s caters from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland) and balances the day with a calm academic core and a lively extracurricular life. If your child likes music, instrumental lessons and both junior and senior choirs are part of the offering; sport plays a healthy role too, with school teams, debating, after‑school drama and the usual school camps giving kids plenty of ways to find their people. The culture is one of participation rather than pressure — a good fit if you want your child to try different things and build confidence across arts and sport as well as the classroom basics.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For the many working parents in Carina, the school’s on‑site Outside School Hours Care (on-site before and after school childcare) makes daily logistics doable. Catholic Early EdCare runs the program with before‑school hours typically from about 6:30 to 8:30 am and after school care until around 6 pm, and they also offer full‑day vacation care. That means you can drop kids off early without juggling multiple pickups, and you’ll have a safe, familiar place for them during school holidays — a real relief when your workday doesn’t match the school bell.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

When it’s time to stock the pantry, Carina Megafresh is the local independent supermarket that parents swear by for weeknight basics. For specialty ingredients or a wider Asian/ethnic selection, Westfield Carindale’s Hanaromart is just a short drive away, which is handy if you like to cook a mix of family favourites. Dinner spots range from casual to family friendly: there’s classic fish and chips at Peter’s Seafood and Takeaway for an easy Friday night, hearty family Italian at Elio’s Osteria, and South Asian flavours if you’re in the mood for takeaway from Bollywood Bar. These options make weeknight meal planning flexible—grab supermarket staples on the way home, then treat the kids to a takeaway night when needed.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you’re considering extra academic support, Carina has local tuition choices like KUMON and NumberWorks’nWords, both offering structured programs that align well with primary school learning goals. Commuting to Brisbane’s CBD is straightforward even without a local train station: Carina is served by buses and the nearest suburban rail option is Cannon Hill on the Cleveland line, with the train from Cannon Hill to Brisbane Central taking roughly 20 to 25 minutes depending on the service. For families balancing school runs with city work, mapping your bus options to school and planning occasional use of the Cannon Hill train for CBD days is the practical rhythm most households adopt.

Your Next Steps

Step 1 — Call Catholic Early EdCare to confirm before/after school and vacation care availability for your child and lock in enrolment if the hours match your work week.  
Step 2 — During your school visit, ask about trying out co‑curriculars — sign your child up for a trial music lesson or sport session so you can see how they click with the program.  
Step 3 — Plan your grocery routine: pick a local staples run at Carina Megafresh, keep Hanaromart in mind for specialty Asian ingredients, and map bus routes (and Cannon Hill train options) so the school‑to‑work commute runs smoothly.
