Welcome to Regents Park, QLD

If you’re scouting family-friendly suburbs that feel settled without being sleepy, Regents Park quietly ticks the boxes. The median age sits at 34 and households tend to be a touch larger than metro averages, so you’ll spot plenty of families out and about. It’s a middle-income pocket with the convenience of a local village centre for everyday shopping and the kind of neighbourhood rhythms that make settling in with kids feel straightforward.

Inside the Classroom at St Bernardine’s Catholic Primary School

St Bernardine’s Catholic Primary School welcomes children from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland). The school leans into creative and active learning: older students can join the choir or pick up an instrument through the Creative Kidz program and there’s a school band for those who want a more structured musical path. Sport and wellbeing are equally prominent. Expect a busy calendar of interschool and intraschool sport — Touch Football, Netball, Australian rules football, Rugby Sevens and athletics carnivals — alongside daily wellbeing options such as Run Club and lunchtime yoga. It’s the sort of place where arts and PE aren’t extras but an everyday part of the school culture.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

One of the real practical wins here is the on-site Outside School Hours Care (on-site before and after school childcare) that runs before school, after school and during vacations. The provider linked to the parish and Brisbane Catholic Education usually opens early, with before-school care commonly starting around 6:00am and after-school care running until about 6:00pm. For families juggling work and school runs, that consistency makes life a lot easier — you can drop the kids before the morning rush and pick them up without cutting it fine at the end of the day.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Village Fair is the day-to-day hub for groceries and quick dinners. There’s a full-service supermarket, pharmacy, bakery and butcher, so basic shopping and last-minute ingredients are simple things to slot into your routine. When the week gets away from you, Mae Ploy Thai and the usual takeaway options — pizza, fish and chips, bakery pies and sandwich shops — are all under the same roof, which is a lifesaver for busy weeknights. Think of Village Fair as the place to run a quick errand and walk away with what you need to feed a hungry family without a lot of angst.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your child needs a little extra academic support, local tuition options are easy to access; centres like Kip McGrath and smaller private tutors operate nearby, mostly focused on primary English and maths. Commute-wise, Regents Park doesn’t have a city rail station, so public transport to Brisbane’s CBD is typically by local bus or a bus plus train combination through a nearby interchange. Plan on journeys to the city taking roughly 45 to 60 minutes depending on which route and connections you use; doing a trial run at your intended travel times is a smart move if you’ll be juggling school runs and a city commute.

Your Next Steps

1) Book a school tour and zero in on the music and sport offerings — ask about choir, the Creative Kidz instrumental program and how sport teams and carnivals are managed so you can see if the vibe fits your child.  
2) Confirm Outside School Hours Care details directly with the provider: enrolment, fees, exact operating hours and vacation-care availability so you can lock in reliable before- and after-school care.  
3) Try a Village Fair run (Drakes for groceries, Mae Ploy Thai or a takeaway) and test the bus commute at the times you’d actually travel; that short rehearsal helps you map weeknight dinners and the school-run without surprises.
