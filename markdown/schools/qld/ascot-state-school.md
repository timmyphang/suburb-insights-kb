# Welcome to Ascot

If you’re picturing tree-lined streets, river glimpses and heritage houses, you’re in the right place. Ascot sits in Brisbane’s inner-north and feels like a well-settled, affluent neighbourhood where many established families and white-collar professionals have made roots. Most households were born in Australia — roughly seven in ten — which means you’ll find a comfortable mix of local familiarity and a friendly sprinkling of people who came from overseas, most commonly England. That balance is exactly the kind of community comfort that helps newly arrived families settle in without feeling isolated. Median incomes and the suburb’s profile point to a family-oriented area where partnered adults and school routines shape the rhythm of everyday life.

# Inside the Classroom at Ascot State School

Ascot State School welcomes children from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland). The school is known for a strong extracurricular heartbeat: instrumental music is a highlight, with school-supported strings, woodwind, brass and percussion opportunities through the Queensland instrumental program alongside private tuition options for students who want more. Sport is another focus — a long‑running junior swim club run by the parents and citizens association feeds into a robust aquatics program and interschool swimming, athletics and cross‑country teams. If your child thrives on music, water time or school sport, Ascot State really supports that growth.

# Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For working families, the on-site Jabiru Outside School Hours Care (on-site before and after school childcare) is a practical lifeline. Services typically start around 7:00am and run after school until about 6:00pm, with vacation-care coverage during school holidays. That continuity means you can plan workdays without having to cobble together multiple childcare providers, and the on-site nature keeps kids close to school routines and friends.

# The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Alexandra Road provides the kind of everyday food options that make weeknights easier. IGA Marketplace is the full-service supermarket for weekly shops and pantry staples, while Genki Mart in nearby Clayfield is the friendly specialty stop when you want Japanese ingredients or Asian pantry finds. When you don’t feel like cooking, there are cafés and takeaways along Alexandra Road — a strong local coffee scene for parents on the run and simple Japanese takeaway options for nights when everyone wants something familiar and fast. For exploring more dining options, Ascot and neighbouring Hamilton have plenty of spots to file away for birthdays and weekends.

# Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your family looks for extra academic support, there are national tuition franchises nearby as well as independent private tutors serving Ascot and Clayfield, so you can compare methods and availability to suit your child. For your commute, Ascot has its own train station on the Doomben line; trains into Brisbane’s central station typically take about 15 to 20 minutes with frequent connections through Eagle Junction. For planning exact schedules and service times around school drop-offs or work hours, use TransLink’s journey planner to check timetables for the times you’ll actually travel.

# Your Next Steps

1) Reach out to Jabiru via the school’s OSHC pages to confirm enrolment steps, current opening hours and fees before you rely on on-site childcare as part of your plan.  
2) If music or swimming matters to your child, look into enrolment for the school’s instrumental music program and the P&C junior Swim Club — both are excellent ways to connect your child to classmates and after-school activity.  
3) Try the commute and the local grocery/run-in routine once or twice: do a trial train trip at the time you’d commute, and pick up a grocery list at IGA Marketplace (and a specialty item at Genki Mart) so you know how the weeknight flow will work for your household.
