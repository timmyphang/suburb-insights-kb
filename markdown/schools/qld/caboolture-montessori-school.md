Welcome to Caboolture

Caboolture has that lived-in, working-and-family energy you can feel the moment you arrive: younger than the Queensland average, with lots of kids in the neighbourhood, a strong Indigenous presence and a practical, hands-on workforce where trades and labouring roles are familiar faces at the school gate. Most people here are Australian-born, with England the next most common birthplace, and household budgets tend to sit below the state median — which shows up in the suburb’s mix of affordable shopping, family services and neighbourhood cafes. If you’re settling a family here, you’ll appreciate the down-to-earth pace: playgrounds, community sports, and neighbours who know their way around a weekend BBQ.

Inside the Classroom at Caboolture Montessori School

Caboolture Montessori School teaches Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland). The school brings Montessori principles into a mainstream primary setting while still offering a lively program of extracurriculars: instrumental music lessons, after-school sports, drama classes and visiting workshop programs like drumming. For kids who like to tinker with strategy and hands-on tech, there’s chess, robotics and visiting enrichment programs that rotate through the year. It’s the sort of school that balances calm, child-led learning with lots of opportunities to try new things — helpful if you want your child to find a niche quickly after a move.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For working parents, there’s genuine on-site childcare support. The school runs Outside School Hours Care — on-site before and after school childcare — plus vacation care for children from Prep to Year 6, with session times and options for casual bookings listed on the school site. That program appears on government childcare directories and meets national quality standards, so parents juggling early starts and late shifts have a familiar, trusted place to drop kids that’s right at the school rather than across town.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Caboolture’s food scene is refreshingly practical for busy families. For weekly groceries you’ll find the major supermarkets in the town shopping precinct — Coles, Woolworths and ALDI — and smaller specialty shops like Lily’s Asian Market and CU Mart if you want regional ingredients or specific pantry staples. On weeknights when nobody wants to cook, there are reliable takeaway and family-friendly options: a couple of local sushi venues deliver quick Japanese meals, and several Thai takeaways are geared toward family orders and delivery. It’s easy to assemble a week of meals from supermarket runs and one or two trusted takeout spots without sacrificing variety.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you’re thinking about extra academic support, Caboolture has local tuition options including Kip McGrath for targeted English and maths coaching and private one-to-one tutors like The Bright Tutors. These can be useful if you want a little boost before assessments or a focused program alongside classroom learning. Commuting to Brisbane CBD is a realistic daily option: Caboolture station sits on the regional rail line into the city, with most direct commuter services taking roughly 45 to 51 minutes depending on the service. That makes it possible for parents to work in the city while keeping family life in a more affordable, suburban setting.

Your Next Steps

First, check the school’s Outside School Hours Care page and register or enquire about session times and casual bookings so you don’t miss out on the childcare routine that fits your work hours. Second, book a school tour and ask for the latest Annual Report, current fees and waitlist details — seeing the classrooms and meeting teachers will tell you more than any web page. Third, use the regional rail timetable planner to test commute options to Brisbane Central and, if you want extra academic support, reach out to local tutors to discuss short-term or ongoing coaching.
