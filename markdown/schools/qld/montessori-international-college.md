# Welcome to Forest Glen

Forest Glen feels like one of those tucked-away neighbourhoods you drive into and immediately slow down for. With a median age in the mid‑40s and a strong share of long-term, family-oriented residents, this is a quiet, semi-affluent pocket where people know their neighbours and local routines. Lots of households here are married and many work in white-collar or professional roles, so the day-to-day rhythm leans towards school runs, afternoon sports and weekend coffee at the village centre. It’s a small locality by population, and the feel is residential rather than bustling — exactly the kind of place that makes settling in feel gentle, not rushed.

# Inside the Classroom at Montessori International College (MIC)

Montessori International College brings a distinctive, arts-forward flavour to primary education in Forest Glen. You’ll find visible performing-arts programming — from ensembles and choirs to instrumental lessons — alongside specialist Mandarin and visual arts offerings, and structured sport and physical education on campus. The school stresses creative expression and languages as part of everyday learning, while still providing the core routines families expect. For practical details about exact year levels and enrolment steps, contact the college directly; they publish campus maps and enrolment guidance for prospective families and can clarify which grades run at the Forest Glen campus.

# Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

If you’re balancing paid work with school pickups, MIC lists on-site After-School and Vacation Care, and they are registered as an approved service for the government’s child-care subsidy program. In plain terms, you can access school-linked before and after school programs and holiday care right on campus, which makes juggling meetings, shifts or commutes a lot smoother. The college provides a contact for after-school and holiday care, so it’s worth confirming opening hours, holiday programs and how the booking system works during busier times of year.

# The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Forest Glen Village is compact and handy for family food runs. White’s IGA is the local supermarket for everyday shopping, and for anything more specialised — think international ingredients or a broader grocery selection — families typically make the short drive into Maroochydore’s Sunshine Plaza. When dinner needs to be quick and pleasing to small palates, the village dishes up approachable options: the Forest Glen Artisan Cafe is great for baked goods and easy café meals, and The Forest Kebab covers fast, family-friendly takeaway nights that kids tend to love. It’s the kind of local provisioning that makes weeknight dinners simple rather than stressful.

# Tuition and the Daily Commute

Enrichment options aren’t far away: Cloud Tuition runs specialist maths support around Forest Glen, and Sunshine Coast Tutoring offers maths and English help across the coast, so extra academic support is easy to arrange without long travel. Commuting to Brisbane is doable but requires planning. Forest Glen doesn’t have a train station; the nearest major rail hub is Nambour, and trains from Nambour to Brisbane Central typically take about one hour and forty-five to fifty minutes depending on the service. Factor in the additional car or bus trip needed to reach Nambour station when you assess daily commute times — it can add a predictable leg to your routine, so it’s worth checking timetables and connector options before you commit.

# Your Next Steps

1) Ring or email Montessori International College to confirm which year levels they offer, ask about After‑School and Vacation Care opening times, holiday care hours and fee structures, and to check Child Care Subsidy eligibility for your family.  
2) Verify the school’s registered care details on the national care listing if you want documented confirmation of subsidy registration and service information.  
3) If it helps, ask MIC for an enrolment pack or a one‑page checklist that gathers fees, OSHC contact info, nearest stations and local shops — then check train timetables to plan the connector trip to Nambour and see how that fits your daily commute.
