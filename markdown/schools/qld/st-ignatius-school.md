Welcome to Toowong

Toowong feels like the city’s calm, professional cousin: streets populated largely by people in white‑collar jobs, a median age around 30, and more singles and couples than the classic big family households. That makes it excellent for newer arrivals who want a neighbourhood with energy but not frantic school‑run chaos. Most residents are Australian‑born, but you’ll also see families from China and England among the most common overseas birthplaces—small but noticeable communities that can make settling in easier when you’re adjusting to a new country. Household incomes sit comfortably in the middle range, and the shopping strip around Toowong Village gives you the essentials and the extras without a long drive.

Inside the Classroom at St Ignatius School

St Ignatius School teaches from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland). Inside those classrooms you’ll find a strong emphasis on music and performance: instrumental lessons across piano, percussion, brass and woodwind feed into concert bands, and there’s a middle‑years choir that gives kids real stage experience. The school offers a broad spread of before‑ and after‑school activities—chess and coding with Junior Engineers, dance, and external sports programs in netball, soccer, Australian rules football and Auskick—so curious kids have options whether they’re into STEM, the arts or team sports.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For families juggling work and school hours, St Ignatius runs on‑site Outside School Hours Care (on-site before and after school childcare) managed by Catholic Early EdCare. Care starts as early as 7:00am, runs after school until 6:00pm, and there’s vacation care for school holidays. That reliability is a real time-saver if you’re commuting or keeping long office hours and want a single, trusted place for drop-off, pick-up and holiday supervision.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

If you love to cook or want quick, reliable takeout, Toowong Village has both Woolworths and Coles for weekly shopping, and there are Asian and specialty grocers nearby for harder‑to‑find ingredients. That blend makes it easy to keep your pantry stocked with staples or try new recipes from home. When you don’t feel like cooking, Pitch & Fork offers a casual café vibe, and a cluster of family‑friendly pizza and Asian takeaway options around the village means weeknight dinners are straightforward and kid-approved.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you’re looking for extra academic support, local options include maths tutors from services like Cloud Tuition and broader tutoring listings through providers such as Tutors Australia—many offer trial lessons so you can gauge fit before committing. Getting into Brisbane’s CBD is fast if you want to avoid peak‑hour driving: Toowong station has direct trains to Brisbane Central that typically take about seven to nine minutes on frequent Zone 1 services, which makes daily commuting quick when trains match your schedule.
