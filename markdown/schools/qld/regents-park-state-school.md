Welcome to Regents Park, QLD (City of Logan)

If you’re picturing a family‑friendly pocket just outside Brisbane, Regents Park fits that image neatly. The median age sits in the mid‑30s and the suburb feels like it was designed around family life—more households with kids than lone renters, a comfortable middle‑income mix and that outer‑city blend of white‑ and blue‑collar workers you see across Logan and greater Brisbane. The Village Fair shopping strip acts as the neighbourhood hub: a Drakes supermarket, small markets and a handful of takeaway dinners mean you won’t have to drive far for the basics. There’s also a noticeable multicultural thread running through the area; many households speak more than one language at home, which can be a real comfort if you’re adjusting to life in Australia and want your kids to feel at ease quickly.

Inside the Classroom at Regents Park State School

Regents Park State School covers primary years, including Year 6, and has an upbeat, hands‑on feel. Music isn’t an afterthought — the school runs instrumental programs, ensemble opportunities and even multi‑day workshops for older students. Sport is equally visible, with house competitions, inter‑school teams and a healthy calendar of carnivals that keep kids moving and connected. If your child enjoys performing or being part of a team, the school’s extra‑curricular schedule gives plenty of chances to try things out without having to be elite from day one.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For families juggling work and school runs, the on‑site Outside School Hours Care (on‑site before and after school childcare) provided by the YMCA is a practical lifesaver. Typical hours start early — around 6:00am for before‑school care — and the after‑school program usually runs until about 6:00pm, with extended hours for vacation care. That structure lets you plan a normal workday without inventing your own childcare solutions, and having the service physically at the school reduces door‑to‑door stress for busy mornings and afternoons.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Village Fair keeps weeknight dinners pleasantly low‑drama. If you’re after fast family meals there’s everything from fish and chips to pizza, and a popular Thai takeaway that’s great for quick midweek dinners. The local markets and specialty grocers offer fresh options if you prefer to cook at home, and having a full supermarket on the strip means you can pick up ingredients for a dinner plan without a detour. It’s the kind of neighbourhood where you can swing by after soccer practice and still have a decent meal on the table by 6:30pm.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your child needs a little academic boost or extra practice, there are local tuition options in both maths and English as well as private tutors. These providers are close enough that evening sessions are realistic for families who aren’t commuting long distances. Speaking of commutes: Regents Park doesn’t have a train station, so the suburb is built around buses. A typical public‑transport journey into Brisbane’s central business district usually involves a bus connection to a train and can take roughly an hour to an hour and a half door to door depending on timing. It’s worth testing your likely trip during peak times so you know whether the daily rhythm is sustainable for your household.

Your Next Steps

First, ring the school or the YMCA to confirm the Outside School Hours Care details — hours, booking rules and fees — before you enrol so you’re not surprised by logistics. Second, visit the school and ask specifically about the music and sport programs: instrument hire, ensemble schedules and how house competitions work will tell you whether there’s a place for your child to belong. Third, test the commute at the time you’d actually travel using the Translink trip planner and local timetables; try the bus routes you’d use and see how the door‑to‑door time feels on a real weekday.
