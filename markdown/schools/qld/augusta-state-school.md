# Welcome to Augustine Heights

If you’re picturing a master‑planned pocket where young families set down roots, Augustine Heights fits the bill. The median age here is about 31, households tend to be larger and family‑oriented, and the area sits comfortably in the upper‑middle income bracket — a lot of professionals, clerical and administrative workers, and managers call this place home. Most residents were born in Australia, but there’s also a healthy thread of overseas‑born families, with India the largest single identifiable birthplace outside Australia. For newcomers, that mix makes it easier to find familiar foods, cultural connections and friendly neighbours who understand the challenges of settling into a new country.

# Inside the Classroom at Augusta State School

Augusta State School runs from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland), and it has a lively arts and sports culture that really gives kids space to try things. Music gets a strong showing: strings start from Year 3 and the band program begins in Year 4, alongside junior and senior choir groups and a Performance Team for dance. Sports are a community affair too, with students taking part in interschool Gala Days for soccer, netball and football. The school feels like a place where extracurriculars aren’t an add‑on — they’re part of the day‑to‑day, which is great if you want your child to explore music or team sports without long drives.

# Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For families juggling work and school runs, Augusta State School has on‑site outside school hours care (on-site before and after school childcare) run by Camp Australia. That includes before‑school sessions, after‑school sessions and vacation care, so you can arrange supervised care at the campus on days you can’t be there. It’s worth contacting the program early to check session times and fees and to lock in a spot — before‑and‑after school care services in family suburbs fill up quickly, especially around term start dates.

# The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Augusta Village is the local retail hub and it’s where you’ll do most of your quick shopping and grab‑and‑go dinners. There’s an IGA for everyday groceries and a compact fresh‑food and food‑service cluster that keeps weeknight life simple. For nights when you don’t want to cook, the village does a nice job of offering variety: Pho Real serves Vietnamese pho and banh mi, Sushi On Augusta covers sushi and takeaway, and MamaGhar brings Nepalese and Indian‑style dishes to the neighbourhood. If you want bigger multicultural supermarket options or a wider retail selection, Springfield Central is a short drive away.

# Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your child would benefit from extra academic support, there are nearby options like Kip McGrath in Springfield for maths and English, and a network of private tutors you can find through platforms such as Superprof. Commuting to Brisbane’s CBD requires a little planning: Augustine Heights doesn’t have its own train station, so most families take a bus to Springfield Central then jump on the Springfield rail line. The train from Springfield Central into Brisbane city is roughly 38 to 41 minutes, so when you add the bus transfer and door‑to‑door time you should plan accordingly. Trial the trip at your usual school‑run time to see how the pieces fit together with school pickups and your workday.

# Your Next Steps

First, book a school tour or an enrolment meeting at Augusta State School to confirm zoning, current programs and Instrumental Music or choir sign‑up windows. Second, if you’ll need before or after‑school care, contact Camp Australia early to check availability, session times and fees and get your child enrolled. Third, trial the commute: do a morning or evening run to Springfield Central, check the Springfield line timetables and time the door‑to‑door journey so weekday routines feel manageable from day one.
