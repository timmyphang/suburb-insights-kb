Welcome to Alexandra Hills

If you’re picturing a family-friendly pocket of bayside Brisbane where the pace is purposeful but not hurried, Alexandra Hills is exactly that: a middle-income, family-oriented suburb where the typical household is a couple with kids and neighbours know each other from the school gate or the weekend park. The median age sits around 38 and the workforce is a practical mix of white- and blue-collar jobs, which gives the place an easygoing, lived-in feel. Most residents were born in Australia, with England the next most common birthplace, and household incomes tend to land in the mid‑$1,800s per week, so you’re looking at a stable, community-focused suburb with parks, shopping and everyday convenience within easy reach.

Inside the Classroom at Alexandra Hills State School

Alexandra Hills State School teaches from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland), and it’s the sort of school that structures a day around both classroom learning and extracurricular moments that build confidence. Children can join interschool sports, athletics days and school carnivals, and there’s an instrumental music program with a Years 3–6 school band for kids who want to try an instrument. The Student Council is active, which means student voices get a regular platform. For parents who want a mix of solid academic foundations and chances for kids to discover new interests, the school’s culture leans toward participation, teamwork and practical opportunities to shine.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

Working families will appreciate that the school has on-site Outside School Hours Care (on-site before and after school childcare). A local provider, Mighty Oaks, runs before-school, after-school and vacation care right at the campus, which makes juggling shift starts, commuting and weekend plans easier. Having an established on-site care program means drop-off and pick-up logistics are simpler, and you can often coordinate school activities without adding extra pickups across town.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

For everyday groceries you’ll be relying on Alexandra Hills Shopping Centre, anchored by a Woolworths and supported by IGA options for last-minute needs. If you crave particular ingredients—think specialist Asian vegetables or pantry items—Capalaba is a short drive away and offers larger ethnic supermarkets like Sunlit Asian Supermarket and other specialty grocers. When weeknight energy is low and pizza is the answer, Scott’s Pizza and Amalfi Pizza make quick dinners easy; Thai Emerald is a reliable local spot for takeout or a casual family meal. It’s a neighbourhood where you can run a quick shop and still treat the family to dinner without a long trek.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you’re thinking about extra academic support, there are local tuition options nearby, for example Kip McGrath in Capalaba and a range of private tutors listed for Alexandra Hills, so arranging assessments or targeted maths and English help is straightforward. Commuting into Brisbane’s central business district can be managed a few ways: Route 267 runs directly to Brisbane City and usually takes around 60–70 minutes depending on traffic, or you can drive or catch a local bus into Cleveland station and take the Cleveland line into Central, which typically takes about 50–60 minutes on train. There isn’t a train station in Alexandra Hills itself, so most families balance timing, congestion and convenience when they decide their daily route.

Your Next Steps

First, confirm the school’s Prep to Year 6 range and the on-site Outside School Hours Care details on the school website, and ring Mighty Oaks to check current before/after/vacation care availability. Second, review the school’s extra‑curricular offerings—music, band and sports—and talk to the school about how those programs fit your child’s interests and commitments. Third, trial the commute at peak times: check the Route 267 timetable and time a drive-plus-train via Cleveland station so you know what a typical school-run or work commute will really feel like.
