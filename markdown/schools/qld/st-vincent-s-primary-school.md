# Welcome to Clear Island Waters

If you're picturing quiet tree‑lined streets, tidy owner‑occupied homes and a slower rhythm, Clear Island Waters will feel reassuringly familiar. It's an older, relatively affluent pocket with a median age around 50, so the neighbourhood leans more toward settled families and owners than high‑density, single‑living precincts. Household incomes tend to be above average and the feel is calm rather than frenetic — a place where weekend walks and backyard barbecues are a regular thing. For families arriving from overseas, there’s also a helpful multicultural thread running through the suburb: while a majority were born in Australia, a noticeable share of residents hail from overseas, with the largest single overseas birthplace being China. That diversity makes meeting neighbours, finding cultural food staples, and settling in a new country feel less isolating.

# Inside the Classroom at St Vincent’s Primary School

St Vincent’s Primary School in Clear Island Waters teaches from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland). The school blends a solid academic program with a lively co‑curricular menu: children take part in school sport and physical education, enjoy specialist music and Italian classes, and can access instrumental music. Beyond the core program, a healthy roster of after‑school activities is available through external providers, from basketball and chess to dance, guitar, singing, martial arts and speech and drama. The overall vibe is deliberately broad — a traditional primary school structure with plenty of options for kids to discover what lights them up.

# Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For working families, one of the most practical pieces of the puzzle is on‑site childcare. St Vincent’s runs Outside School Hours Care, which is on school grounds and operated by Catholic Early EdCare at 22 Fairway Drive. That service covers before‑school care from around 7:00am, after‑school care until about 6:00pm, and school‑holiday (vacation) programs. Having OSHC on site makes those hectic mornings and late afternoons measurably easier: you can drop kids at school, head to work, and collect them without detours across town. If you rely on structured after‑school care while you work, confirming hours, fees and vacancies directly with the provider is a high‑priority early step.

# The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Grocery runs and weeknight dinners are refreshingly simple around here. For the regular supermarket shop, Coles at Chevron Renaissance is the nearby mainstream option, and there’s a SPAR convenience store on Chevron for last‑minute pantry saves. If you crave familiar ingredients from home, several Asian and ethnic supermarkets serve the nearby Surfers Paradise and Chevron Island areas, which is a real comfort for families who want to cook familiar meals. When you need a kid‑friendly takeout or a casual dinner out, Espresso Bonsai on Chevron Island is a reliable local cafe with takeaway options, and the surrounding Surfers Paradise and Chevron strips deliver quick pizza, Thai and Asian choices that fit school‑night rhythms.

# Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you’re thinking about extra academic support, the area is served by local tutoring options — from independent services like Stressless Tutoring to tutor listings on platforms such as Tutor2You — so organising subject‑specific help or enrichment isn’t hard. Commuting to Brisbane’s central business district is doable but takes planning: Clear Island Waters doesn’t have a Queensland Rail station, so most families take a local bus or the light rail (tram) to Robina station, then board the Robina to Brisbane train. The direct train leg from Robina to Brisbane Central is typically about one hour and twenty minutes, and you’ll want to allow extra time for the bus or tram connection. Use the Translink journey planner to map exact services and transfer windows so workday commutes don’t become a surprise.

# Your Next Steps

1) Ring St Vincent’s to ask about the enrolment process, zoning, class timetables and to book a school tour — seeing classrooms in action answers more questions than any brochure.  
2) Call Catholic Early EdCare at the school to confirm Outside School Hours Care hours, fees and current vacancies so you can lock in before‑ and after‑school care if you need it.  
3) Trial your commute: use the Translink planner to chart a bus or tram to Robina and the train times to Brisbane, then do a door‑to‑office dry run on a weekday so you know exactly how long mornings and afternoons will feel.
