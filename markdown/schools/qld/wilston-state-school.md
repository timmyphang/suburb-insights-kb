# Welcome to Grange (inner‑north Brisbane)

If you're picturing tree‑lined streets, tidy houses and a neighbourhood made for stroller walks and after‑school footy practice, welcome to Grange. It's an inner‑north pocket of Brisbane that tends to attract a fairly young, professional and family‑focused crowd — think mid‑30s parents, steady white‑collar jobs and household incomes noticeably above the Queensland average. Most residents are Australian‑born, with England and New Zealand the next most common birthplaces, so newcomers usually find the community familiar and easy to settle into. The suburb feels compact and neighbourly: enough local life to make running errands and meeting other families simple, without the bustle of a large commercial strip.

# Inside the Classroom at Wilston State School

Wilston State School teaches Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland), and it has that classic local primary vibe where music and sport are clearly part of the school day, not just extras. The music program is a highlight: students can join instrumental tuition — strings, brass and woodwind, percussion — and there are choirs and band opportunities for kids who want to trial something new or deepen an interest. Sport is active and community‑backed, with a swim club, interschool sports and regular school carnivals supported by the school’s Parents and Citizens' Association. For parents who care about a balanced primary education — academics plus creative and physical outlets — Wilston sends a clear message that both matter.

# Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For families juggling work and school pick‑ups, Wilston runs an on‑site P&C‑operated program called Wilston Kids Care, providing before‑school, after‑school and vacation care for Prep to Year 6 kids. The Parents and Citizens' Association operates this licensed service, so it’s tied into the school community rather than being an external provider. That means familiar faces, direct communication with the school and an easier handover between classroom and care. If you need consistent before‑or‑after school coverage or holiday supervision without crossing the suburb, this setup is convenient and community‑centred.

# The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Grange and neighbouring Wilston serve up an easygoing weeknight food scene that works well for families. There are always neighbourhood cafes for quick dinners or early‑evening takeaways — spots like Huskk and Quincy's Bar & Cafe are reliably family friendly — and deli‑style choices such as Alcove Cafe & Deli when you want a mix‑and‑match meal. For groceries, you won’t have to trek to a big shopping centre if you prefer small, specialist stores: Genki Mart brings a Japanese grocery option to the area and local corner grocers like Choi’s Corner handle the daily essentials. The overall feel is relaxed: grab a rotisserie, pick up some refrigerated dumplings, or settle into a casual café dinner after sport practice.

# Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you plan extra tutoring or academic enrichment, there are established local options nearby, including a Kip McGrath centre in Grange and Kumon locations in the region, plus a network of private tutors. Those services make it straightforward to schedule weekly sessions without long drives. Commuting to Brisbane’s CBD is also easy to plan: the neighbourhood is served by nearby Ferny Grove‑line stations such as Newmarket and Wilston, and a train from Newmarket to Brisbane Central commonly takes around 10 to 15 minutes. That short rail trip makes it realistic for parents to keep city jobs while living in a calm, family‑oriented suburb.

# Your Next Steps

First, check the Wilston State School events calendar to pin down music and sport program details and the exact enrolment steps — that calendar is where the real‑time activities show up. Second, contact Wilston Kids Care through the school or the Parents and Citizens' Association to enrol for before‑ and after‑school care and holiday programs, and ask about session availability and fees. Third, try the commute at the times you would travel and test a couple of local cafes for weeknight dinners so you can picture school‑day rhythms before you move.
