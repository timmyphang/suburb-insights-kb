# Welcome to Mooloolaba

If your family is imagining life by the sea, Mooloolaba gives you that postcard coastline with a comfortably mature neighbourhood feel. The median age here is 44 and the suburb leans toward professional, white‑collar households in a middle‑to‑upper income market, so you’ll find a mix of owner‑occupied homes owned by couples or empty‑nesters alongside families. Most residents were born in Australia, with England the next most common overseas birthplace. That quieter, settled vibe pairs with easy access to cafés, fish markets and beachfront walks, and it’s exactly the kind of place many families choose when they want a coastal lifestyle that still runs on a dependable community rhythm.

# Inside the Classroom at Mooloolaba State School

Mooloolaba State School caters from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland). The school balances classroom basics with a lively extracurricular program: two choirs (the Nightingales for Years 3–5 and Bella Voce for Years 5–6) give kids a place to find their voice, while instrumental offerings and a Year 5 ukulele club make music a visible part of daily life. Sport is strongly supported too — school swimming, cross‑country and athletics carnivals are regular calendar highlights, with pathways for representative sport if your child wants to take things further. Overall it feels like a small coastal school where creative and active kids both get to thrive.

# Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For families juggling work and school runs, the school hosts an on‑site Outside School Hours Care (on‑site before and after school childcare) program that includes Before School Care, After School Care and Vacation/Holiday Care. The school publishes specific hours, fees and program details on its OSHC pages, so it’s worth checking those early and registering if demand is high; having care right on campus really helps when your day runs long and you need a reliable exit strategy.

# The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Weeknights in Mooloolaba are refreshingly simple for busy families. Coles and a Friendly Grocer cover the mainstream supermarket runs, and for a little variety the Suncoast Oriental Food Mart supplies Asian pantry staples. If you’d rather not cook, local favourites like Mooloolaba Fisheries On The Spit serve fresh seafood and fish‑and‑chips for a tray‑table dinner after soccer or choir, while Pizza Pastaria is a dependable pizza takeaway or delivery option. For casual dine‑in or quick Thai takeaway, places such as Thai Lotus and N Thai Sing are easy neighbourhood stops. It’s the kind of mix that makes feeding a hungry family straightforward and sometimes a little deliciously indulgent.

# Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your child needs extra support or you want to boost strengths, local tutoring options include Extended Learning Centres that offer maths and English tutoring on the Sunshine Coast, and Kumon programmes are available in nearby areas. Commuting to Brisbane is doable but needs planning: Mooloolaba doesn’t have its own train station, so typical public transport involves taking the Sunshine Coast train line to Landsborough or Nambour and then a connecting bus into Mooloolaba. Door‑to‑door by public transport can be roughly two hours each way, so if daily Brisbane travel is on the cards, do a trial run to make sure it fits your family rhythm.

# Your Next Steps

1) Book a school tour or contact the office to confirm your enrolment zone and current availability, and ask for the school’s ICSEA and English‑as‑an‑additional‑language figures if you need them.  
2) If you’re working, check the school’s Outside School Hours Care pages for hours, fees and vacation‑care enrolment and register early if spots are limited.  
3) At enrolment or your first meet‑and‑greet, ask about joining choir, instrumental or ukulele groups and the school’s sport pathways so your child can plug into activities from day one.
