Welcome to Clovelly

Clovelly feels like a quietly polished secret: an affluent, inner‑eastern beachside pocket where weekends orbit around surf, sand and a parade of cafés that spill onto the footpath. Household incomes here are among the higher brackets in Sydney, and the neighbourhood attracts a lot of white‑collar professionals, young families and many couples living de‑facto rather than married. It’s a compact suburb where local convenience stores and independent grocers meet the beach lifestyle — you can pick up dinner essentials at the Clovelly supermarket, then walk down to the sand for fish and chips as the sun goes. If you like the idea of a settled, well‑resourced community with easy seaside downtime, Clovelly has that quietly confident vibe.

Inside the Classroom at St Anthony’s Catholic Primary School (K–6), Clovelly

St Anthony’s runs from Kindergarten through Year 6 and presents as a classic community school with a strong focus on both academics and enrichment. The co‑curricular calendar is broad: team and individual sports like soccer, netball, swimming and athletics sit alongside music offerings — choir and instrumental lessons for guitar, keyboard and piano — plus clubs for chess, STEM and coding. That mix means kids who aren’t sport‑obsessed still have pathways into school life through music or STEM activities, and those who thrive on competition have plenty of options. The atmosphere comes across as family‑oriented and active, with opportunities for students to try different things without feeling pigeonholed.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

St Anthony’s has on‑site Outside School Hours Care (on‑site before and after school childcare), run by SCECS at the school site on Arden Street. The program covers before school care, after school care and vacation care, so parents working traditional hours or with shifting schedules can rely on a consistent on‑site option rather than juggling separate pick‑ups. It’s worth checking current capacity and booking arrangements during a tour — these programs are really convenient but do fill up at popular schools, so getting the enrolment details before you commit is wise.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Clovelly’s culinary pulse is low‑key and family friendly. Sea Salt Clovelly is the beachside go‑to for casual, kid‑friendly meals and takeaway fish and chips, and Out Of The Blue does a reliable burgers-and-chips rotation for one‑dish weeknights. Along the bay you’ll find several small cafés and kiosks that keep things simple for families who prefer to walk home rather than cook. For grocery runs, the suburb relies largely on independent supermarkets and local grocers rather than large ethnic megastores, so plan a slightly broader shop if you want specialty ingredients.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your child needs extra academic support, there are local options like Alchemy Tuition and Global Education Academy offering primary‑level tutoring and exam preparation. These small providers can be easier to coordinate around school and family life than city‑based centres. Commuting to the CBD from Clovelly is straightforward but different from a train commute: there’s no local train station, so many families use the Route 339 bus direct to Central Station. Typical travel time to Central is roughly 23 to 30 minutes depending on traffic and the exact stop, which makes Clovelly surprisingly accessible for city commuters while still feeling like a coastal retreat.

Your Next Steps

Ask the school for their latest Annual School Report so you can check recent NAPLAN results, enrolment trends and staffing information. When you tour St Anthony’s, ask directly about fees, how Outside School Hours Care bookings and capacity work, music tuition costs, and typical class sizes. Confirm OSHC enrolment procedures and current availability with SCECS and double‑check your household’s commute plans using the Route 339 timetable so you know how after‑school pickup will fit into your week.
