Welcome to St Marys

If you’re scanning the map for a family-friendly pocket west of Sydney, St Marys often rises to the top. The suburb skews a little younger than the city average, with a median age in the mid‑30s and a strong family focus — more households with kids than you’ll find in many inner suburbs. The local workforce is a practical mix of blue‑ and white‑collar roles, and household incomes sit around the middle for New South Wales. One thing new arrivals tell me is how reassuring the multicultural mix feels: a noticeable share of residents were born overseas, with the Philippines the most common overseas birthplace at around four to five percent. That kind of diversity makes settling in and finding familiar foods, community groups and new friends a lot easier.

Inside the Classroom at Oxley Park Public School

Oxley Park Public School teaches Kindergarten to Year 6 — in Australia, Kindergarten is the first year of primary school — and it leans into the wholesome, classic public‑school experience. The school timetable includes the usual core curriculum, but what parents often name as the highlights are the extracurriculars: school sport (including athletics and inter‑school PSSA competitions), creative and performing arts with concerts and showcases, and a welcoming array of interest clubs like lunchtime coding and gardening. Student leadership is well supported through the student representative council and there are debating and public speaking opportunities that help kids find their voice. It’s the sort of place where classroom learning and hands‑on activities meet, so your child can be coding one lunchtime and rehearsing for a school concert the next.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For families juggling work and school runs, Oxley Park Public School runs on‑site Outside School Hours Care (on-site before and after school childcare) and also offers vacation and holiday program options. That service is managed in partnership with Penrith City Council, which is handy because bookings, contacts and program details are handled through established local channels rather than ad‑hoc arrangements. For many working parents this is a real lifeline: reliable before‑and‑after‑school care plus holiday programs that keep kids safe and engaged during school breaks.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

One of the nicest surprises for newcomers is how easy it is to eat well here without a long drive. St Marys Village is the local shopping hub with a Woolworths anchor for weeknight groceries, and for ethnic ingredients you’ll find Phil Asian Food Mart and Jayam Supermarket stocking Southeast Asian, South Asian and Middle Eastern staples. If you’re short on time, the local café scene is practical and comforting — Cassandra’s does a solid grab‑and‑go breakfast or coffee run, St Marys Fish & Chips covers those Friday‑night traditions, and bakeries and Vietnamese sandwich shops like Just Baked make for quick, delicious dinners that kids actually eat. That mix of mainstream and specialist grocery options plus reliable takeaway spots makes weeknight life much easier when you’re juggling homework, sport and baths.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you like the idea of a little extra academic support, there are familiar options in town: Kumon runs maths and English programs at St Marys and local tuition centres and private tutors cater to primary students who want more practice, enrichment or targeted help. Commuting to the Sydney CBD is doable if you plan for it: St Marys sits on the T1/T5 suburban rail network and a typical train trip to Sydney Central is around 50–55 minutes depending on the service. In peak planning I’d budget roughly an hour. That makes St Marys a realistic option for families who need metro access without paying inner‑city prices.

Your Next Steps

First, pull the school’s current enrolment zone and start‑date details from the NSW Department of Education so you know whether Oxley Park Public School is the right local fit. Next, ring the school’s Outside School Hours Care contact or the Penrith City Council booking line to check the latest fees, vacation‑care hours and whether there’s space on the days you’ll need. Finally, make a short checklist of questions to take on a school tour — ask about enrolment steps, OOSHC hours and costs, the co‑curricular options your child might join, and how the school handles pastoral care and learning support.
