Welcome to Wentworth Point

If you picture a waterfront suburb that feels modern, buzzy and compact, Wentworth Point will sit nicely in that imagination. The neighbourhood skews young — the median age is about 32 — and about 12,700 people call it home. You’ll find a lot of white‑collar, middle‑to‑relatively‑affluent households, many in new apartment developments, with a big cluster of 30–39 year‑olds, millennial singles and young families. One of the most practical comforts for families arriving from overseas is the multicultural makeup: around one in five residents were born in China, while roughly a third were born in Australia. That mix means local shops, playgrounds and parent groups are used to multiple languages and different family rhythms, which makes settling in a lot less daunting for newcomers.

Inside the Classroom at Wentworth Point Public School

Wentworth Point Public School teaches Kindergarten through Year 6 and focuses on giving primary students a broad, active school life. Sport is a clear strength: the school runs regular PSSA teams in soccer, netball, touch football and Australian Rules football, and competes in inter‑school fixtures. If your kid prefers talking and performing, there are debating and public‑speaking programs, plus a Dance Troupe that performs at community events such as the local Gimbawali Festival. Lunchtime interest clubs add another layer of choice, so whether your child wants to kick a ball, rehearse a speech, or learn a routine, there’s something on most weeks. The atmosphere leans community‑oriented and extracurriculars are well integrated into the day-to-day rather than feeling like extras you have to chase down.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For families who juggle work and school runs, the school hosts on‑site Outside School Hours Care (before and after school childcare) and holiday programs, which can be a game changer for routine. The program at the school is run by TeamKids, so you’ll be liaising with them about session times, enrolment and occasional full‑day holiday programs when school is out. Parents often appreciate the continuity of on‑site care: children move from class to a supervised program without extra drop‑offs, which eases pick‑up logistics and helps working families plan around consistent finish times.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Your practical shopping and dining life centers around Marina Square. For weekly groceries, there’s a full Coles supermarket inside the precinct, and a large Asian/ pan‑Asian grocer — Orange Supermarket — that’s great for multicultural ingredients and fresh produce you might want for home cooking. When the family wants to eat out, the Marina Square dining strip is family‑friendly and casual: a communal seafood boil at Kickin’Inn, Japanese katsu and sushi at Brother Katsu and Kyo Sushi, and easy takeaway or burger nights at Burger Point keep weeknight dinners simple and sociable. For families new to the city, the mix of familiar supermarket chains and strong multicultural food options helps recreate home tastes or explore new ones without long drives.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you’re thinking about extra academic support, local tuition options include nearby providers such as Global Education Academy and Cloud Tuition, both of which offer local, home or centre‑based sessions geared to primary students. For commuters, Wentworth Point doesn’t have a heavy‑rail train station inside the suburb, so residents commonly use buses, the Baylink shuttle or ferry services to connect to nearby train stations at Rhodes or Olympic Park, and many door‑to‑door trips to Sydney’s central business district typically fall in the 25–40 minute range depending on transfers and time of day. There is also a planned extension — Parramatta Light Rail Stage 2 — expected to improve direct links in future, so the suburb’s connectivity is already evolving. Practically, families tend to map school morning journeys and afternoon pick‑ups around bus and ferry timetables or combine car drop‑offs with nearby station access.

Your Next Steps

1) Book a school tour or open day and meet the teaching staff to see how the classroom vibe will suit your child.  
2) Call TeamKids to confirm Outside School Hours Care enrolment steps, session times and holiday program availability so you don’t get caught out on a busy week.  
3) Try the school‑start/finish commute at peak times and pop into Marina Square and the local grocers to check shopping and dining fit for your household.
