# Welcome to Point Clare (NSW)

If you’re imagining a laid‑back Central Coast suburb that still feels connected, Point Clare is exactly that kind of place. The median age here sits around the mid‑40s, and the streets are a comfortable mix of homeowners and people still paying off mortgages — families, tradespeople, and professionals who’ve settled where commuting to work or the city is doable but life feels quieter. Most residents were born in Australia, with England the largest overseas birthplace after that, which gives the community a familiar, easygoing tone rather than the bustling multicultural mosaic you might find in larger cities. The local hub along Brisbane Water Drive is where you’ll find cafés, essential services and a friendly, small‑town rhythm that makes new arrivals feel at home fast.

# Inside the Classroom at Point Clare Public School

Point Clare Public School runs Kindergarten to Year 6, and it’s one of those schools that blends a strong community focus with a surprising variety of opportunities. Music lovers will notice the School Band program, which includes both a Training Band and a Concert Band run in partnership with the Central Coast Conservatorium — so kids can learn and perform with proper support. Sport is active and social through PSSA inter‑school competitions, with soccer, netball, basketball and gala days giving students plenty of chances to play and travel with teammates. Add choir, dance, public speaking and debating into the mix, and you’ve got a school that treats extra‑curriculars as part of a child’s growth, not just filler after lessons.

# Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For families juggling work and school pick‑ups, Point Clare Public School has on‑site before and after school childcare run by OSHClub. Their Rise then Shine program looks after early arrivals, while Stay and Play keeps things safe and engaging after the final bell — and OSHClub also runs holiday programs, which is a genuine lifesaver when school breaks collide with work schedules. Because this care is on campus, you don’t have to shuttle kids between locations: drop them at school, head to the train or the office, and pick them up from the same place with activities already underway.

# The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Groceries are simple and sensible here. ALDI on Brisbane Water Drive is the main full‑service supermarket in Point Clare for weekly shop basics and budget‑friendly buys, and for anything more specialised Gosford is a short drive away with expanded options. If you’re after a quick dinner, the local takeaway scene is reliably family friendly — Chinese takeaway, pizza, chicken and fish‑and‑chip shops and a handful of cafés make it easy to call something in on those nights when homework and uniforms win. For a wider selection of ethnic or sit‑down restaurants you’ll drive into Gosford, but for many families the Point Clare outlets are enough to keep evenings moving smoothly.

# Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your child needs a bit of extra help academically, there are options close by. MWNS in Gosford offers maths and English tutoring that families regularly use, and a range of private tutors advertise on local directories such as Superprof, so you can often arrange sessions either in person nearby or online. Commuting to Sydney is feasible for those who work in the city: Point Clare sits on the Central Coast & Newcastle rail line and trains to Sydney’s Central Station typically take around one hour and twenty to one hour and thirty‑five minutes depending on the service. That makes Point Clare a realistic choice for families who want space and a quieter pace at home, without cutting themselves off from city work and cultural opportunities.

# Your Next Steps

First, check OSHClub’s session times and fees so you know what before‑school, after‑school and holiday care looks like for your child’s year. Second, call Point Clare Public School to arrange a tour, ask about enrolment and sign your child up for band, PSSA sport, choir or whatever sparks their interest. Third, map your commute: look at Central Coast & Newcastle line services and trial a few peak and off‑peak runs so you know which trains line up with your workday.
