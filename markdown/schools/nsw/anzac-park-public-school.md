Welcome to Cammeray

If you’re picturing leafy streets, a tight-knit village feel and a short hop into the city, Cammeray delivers. The suburb leans toward upper‑middle, white‑collar households, which shows up in busy mornings, parent-run events and a lively local community calendar. About two thirds of residents were born in Australia, with England, New Zealand, China, South Africa and India among the more common overseas birthplaces — a small but steady international presence that means new families often find others who’ve recently made the move and can share tips. For families who want cafés, fresh produce and a friendly local scene within reach of Sydney’s CBD, Cammeray has a comfortable, grown-up suburban rhythm.

Inside the Classroom at Anzac Park Public School

Anzac Park Public School runs Kindergarten to Year 6 and feels like the sort of school where children quickly learn everyone’s name. Music is a living part of the school day: an instrumental lessons and ensemble program is available for Years 2 to 6 through a partnered provider, so kids who show an early interest in music can join lessons and ensembles without needing to hunt down outside tutors. Sport is another strong thread — school teams and inter‑school carnivals keep kids active and give them chances to shine beyond the classroom. The parent community is involved too; the P&C helps promote and organise extra‑curricular activities so there’s a steady stream of clubs, events and little projects that give students more to explore after class.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For families juggling work and school drop offs, Anzac Park has practical on‑site care run by TeamKids that covers before school, after school and vacation care. Morning sessions typically start around 7:30am and after‑school care usually runs until about 6:00pm, with vacation care available during school holidays — all bookable through TeamKids and coordinated with the school’s own outside school hours care page. That continuity makes it much easier to plan a commute and still get to work on time, and it gives kids a consistent space for homework, play and supervised activities until you pick them up.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Cammeray’s local food scene is refreshingly practical for busy families. Harris Farm Markets in the suburb is the go‑to for fresh fruit and vegetables, and a compact shopping strip around Miller Street and Cammeray Square covers everyday groceries. For quick weeknight dinners, you’ll find a handful of reliable options: Maggio’s Italian bakery and café is family‑friendly and great for last‑minute dinner rolls or a casual meal, Laneway Cafe handles convenient brunches and takeaways, and several Asian takeaways — including Vietnamese and Chinese choices — make an easy fallback for evenings when you need something fast and tasty on the table.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you think your child might benefit from extra academic support, Cammeray has local tutoring options that are easy to access. Small private providers and online one‑to‑one services are active in the area, and many families supplement school learning with tutors found through platforms for private teachers. Your commute choices affect daily rhythms here: Cammeray doesn’t have a heavy‑rail station of its own, so most families choose direct buses to the CBD that typically take about 20–35 minutes depending on traffic, or they travel to nearby North Sydney or St Leonards stations or the Crows Nest Metro for train access. A quick test run at peak times will give you a realistic sense of travel time and whether you’ll be driving, parking or hopping on a bus most days.

Your Next Steps

First, double‑check the current Anzac Park Public School enrolment zone for your address so you know whether you’re in the catchment — I can look this up for you if you like. Second, contact TeamKids and the school’s outside school hours care page to confirm exact session times, booking procedures and vacation care details. Third, book a school tour or open day and ask specifically about the instrumental program, the sports carnivals and how the P&C runs co‑curricular activities so you get a feel for how your child might plug in.
