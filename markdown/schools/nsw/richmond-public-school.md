Welcome to Richmond (NSW)

Richmond feels like one of those suburbs that quietly holds its own: modest and middle‑income, with a median household income around $1,353 a week and a median age of about 43. You’ll find a mix of established families and older residents, a comfortable patchwork of housing types and household setups that gives the place a lived‑in, friendly vibe. The town centre has that small‑city rhythm—markets on a weekend, a handful of reliable shops on Windsor Street, and neighbours who’ve learned each other’s routines.

Inside the Classroom at Richmond Public School

Richmond Public School teaches Kindergarten to Year 6, and the classroom culture is lively without being flashy. Sport is a big piece of the school’s identity—athletics and district or inter‑school competitions are part of the rhythm—while performing arts get equal billing through choir, dance and instrumental programs. Public speaking, debating, interest clubs and student leadership give kids chances to find their thing and build confidence beyond tests and homework. If you care about a school where your child will have options to try sport, music or public speaking, this one lays out a broad co‑curricular menu.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For families juggling work, Richmond Public School lists community‑run Outside School Hours Care, which covers before‑school, after‑school and vacation care. That’s the on‑site before and after school childcare many working parents look for; providers change their fees and vacancies, so it’s worth calling the school or the listed provider to confirm current operating hours and spots. The presence of organised care on school grounds makes afternoons and school‑holiday planning much more manageable for parents who need reliable supervision that isn’t a long drive away.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Groceries and weeknight meals in Richmond strike a practical, slightly eclectic note. Richmond Fruit Market is a specialty greengrocer with a useful Asian foods range, and there are other specialist greengrocers plus the Richmond Good Food Market for weekend produce runs. For everyday supermarket shopping, Windsor Street covers the basics. When you don’t feel like cooking, there are solid local options that work for family life: Park Lane Café is a neighbourly spot with takeaway and kid‑friendly choices, Second Home Café is casual and comfortable, Sea Fish Cafe covers the classic fish‑and‑chip evenings, and East Court Restaurant is a dependable Chinese family dining option for sit‑down or takeaway.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you think your child might benefit from extra help or enrichment, Richmond has local tutoring options. Love2Learn Richmond focuses on primary levels and Alchemy Tuition offers one‑to‑one and small‑group programs from Kindergarten through Year 12, so you can find support that matches your child’s age and learning style. Commuting to the Sydney CBD is a practical reality for some families here: Richmond station sits on the T1 Richmond line, and a direct train to Sydney Central typically takes about 70 to 80 minutes depending on the service and time of day. Driving times vary with traffic, so check a trip planner for the commute windows you’ll actually use.

Your Next Steps

Step 1 — Ring the school to ask about enrolment steps and to confirm the current Outside School Hours Care provider, fees and vacancies; they’ll point you to the right contacts.  
Step 2 — Check the train timetables and peak travel times on the Transport for New South Wales trip planner so you know whether a daily commute is realistic for your family.  
Step 3 — Scan the school newsletter or handbook on the Richmond Public School website for term dates, excursions and co‑curricular calendars, and reach out to local tutors if you want to explore extra academic help.
