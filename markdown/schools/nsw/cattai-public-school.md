Welcome to Cattai

Tucked along the Hawkesbury River fringe, Cattai feels like the suburb you find when you’re craving elbow room but still want easy access to town conveniences. It’s a small semi‑rural pocket with many established families and a median age around 40, where married couples with children are more common than the New South Wales average. Income and rental patterns, along with low levels of social housing, point to a comfortable, middle‑to‑moderately affluent family neighbourhood. You’ll notice the pace here: lawns, weekend projects, and a sense that neighbours look out for one another, with the Cattai National Park and riverside living giving families a ready weekend playground.

Inside the Classroom at Cattai Public School

Cattai Public School teaches Kindergarten through Year 6, and the school calendar hums with regular athletics and inter‑school sports gala days that keep kids active and building team confidence. Creative and performing arts programs, lunchtime interest clubs and public‑speaking and leadership opportunities give children space to discover strengths beyond the classroom. Community and cultural programs, including “connecting on country” style initiatives, round out the student experience so learning looks like both skills and belonging. If you’re imagining your child in a small school where teachers know names and interests, that’s the vibe here.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For working families, the school has on‑site care run by TheirCare. This Outside School Hours Care (on‑site before and after school childcare) offers before‑ and after‑school sessions and is listed on the national StartingBlocks directory where you can check session times, fees and current vacancies. Having care on campus is a practical lifeline: drop‑offs and pick‑ups are simpler, and kids who are part of the program get supervised time for homework, play and organised activities while parents finish their commute or shifts.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Cattai itself doesn’t host a major supermarket, so weekly shops usually mean a short drive into Windsor or Richmond where you’ll find Coles, Woolworths, ALDI and local IGA options, plus Asian and other ethnic grocery stores if you cook internationally. Grocery delivery services also cover the postcode, which makes last‑minute dinner rescues easy. For family dining, there’s the Riverside Oaks Brasserie at the Riverside Oaks Golf Resort when you want a sit‑down meal by the water, and Windsor’s growing café scene—including comfortable neighbourhood spots like Lime & Coconut—handles casual breakfasts, coffees and kid‑friendly lunches. Pizza, Chinese, Indian and Thai takeaways are widely available for delivery when weekday life is hectic.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you want extra academic support, tutoring and enrichment providers are clustered in Windsor and Richmond; options range from local maths tutors to broader tuition services. For commuters, Cattai doesn’t have its own rail station. Most families take a short bus into Windsor Station, then the train from Windsor into Sydney Central. The Windsor-to‑Central train leg is roughly an hour and a bit, so expect door‑to‑door travel to be longer once you factor in the bus or driving time. It’s worth doing a few trial runs to see how the schedule sits with your morning and afternoon routines.
