Welcome to Glebe (NSW)

Glebe feels like a compact, creative heartbeat tucked close to the city — a neighbourhood where well‑educated professionals and students outnumber the big‑family crowd, and where medium‑density living and renting are the norm. The population skews relatively young, with a strong share of people born in Australia alongside the largest overseas‑born groups from China and England. For families arriving from overseas, that mix of local and international households is actually a comfort: you’ll find familiar foods, community connections and neighbours who’ve navigated the move you’re making. Expect shorter terraces, apartments and leafy street scenes rather than large separate houses; Glebe’s lifestyle tends to suit couples without children, young families and anyone who likes being a short tram ride from the city.

Inside the Classroom at Australian Performing Arts Grammar School

If your child is heading into secondary school with a serious passion for the stage, Australian Performing Arts Grammar School (APGS) is a specialist Years 7–12 option that lives and breathes creative and performing arts. Timetabled specialist training is central here: daily dance, instrumental music and ensemble work, musical theatre and drama classes sit alongside visual arts and regular public showcases. The school’s program is designed to move students into real performance pathways, so expect a curriculum that balances artistic intensity with the academic requirements of senior school. APGS is a secondary specialist school — there’s no primary school offering or on‑site outside school hours care listed on their public pages — so families with younger children should plan care arrangements elsewhere.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For parents who need childcare before and after the school day, the obvious point is that APGS does not list on‑site Outside School Hours Care (on‑site before and after school childcare). That means you’ll want to secure care through local primary schools that run outside school hours programs, or via independent providers in the area. Many families in Glebe mix and match: one parent uses a commuting schedule plus external pickup services, or arranges care closer to home if a younger sibling still needs before/after‑school care. If you’re new to the city, the Department of Education guidance on outside school hours care is a useful place to see what options exist and how providers operate.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Glebe’s food scene is relaxed and practical for busy weeknights. For groceries, you’ll find a friendly local IGA and a Friendly Grocer for quick top‑ups, and a cluster of specialty and Asian grocers along Glebe Point Road that make weeknight cooking easy, especially if you’re looking for specific ingredients from home. When evenings are too full for cooking, the neighbourhood’s takeaway options are a lifesaver: a classic diner for comfort food, a couple of dependable Thai spots for speedy stir‑fries and curries, and a popular Indian restaurant if you want something to share. The mix of grocery convenience and diverse takeaway means dinner doesn’t have to be an elaborate production after rehearsals or late rehearsals at school.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you want to top up school learning with a tutor, Glebe has local options ranging from established centres offering English and maths tuition to private tutors listed through regional tutoring services. Those options can be useful for filling gaps when your child’s timetable is heavy with rehearsal and performance commitments. Transport-wise, Glebe doesn’t have a heavy‑rail train station within the suburb; the practical public‑transport link is the L1 Light Rail (the Glebe — Lower Avon Street stop). Depending on stops and timetable, a light‑rail trip to Central Station typically sits in the 10–20 minute range, so it’s a short commute to the city for work or theatre industry connections. If you’ll be juggling rehearsal pick‑ups and city hours, trial the light rail at peak times to see how it fits your real‑world schedule.

Your Next Steps

First, call or email APGS admissions to confirm sibling priority, ask about transition pathways from primary schools, and check whether they have any partner primary schools or outside school hours care arrangements so you know how the pieces will fit. Second, if you need before‑ and after‑school care for younger children, line up outside school hours care through nearby primary schools or reputable external providers now rather than later — spaces can fill. Third, explore local tutoring and enrichment options and take a trial light‑rail trip to Central at a likely travel time; that practical test will quickly tell you whether the commute and daily routine feel workable for your family.
