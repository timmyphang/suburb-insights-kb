Welcome to Leichhardt (Inner West, Sydney)

Leichhardt feels like a village tucked into the city: tree-lined streets, a lively high street full of cafes and trattorias, and a mix of young professionals and families whose median age sits in the late 30s to 40s. It’s a place where white-collar professionals bump into parents pushing prams, and where the most common overseas birthplace is England — a small comfort if you’re moving here from the U.K. and want neighbourhoods with familiar accents and cultural touchpoints as you settle in.

Inside the Classroom at Leichhardt Public School

Leichhardt Public School teaches children from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland). The school is well known for a long-running, serious music culture: think string ensembles, multiple bands and choirs, instrumental tuition and regular performance opportunities that include city venues. If your child loves music — or if you want them to try it — this is a school that will give them the stage. Beyond music, students can join school sport teams that compete in local carnivals for swimming, athletics, cross-country and gymnastics, and there are intellectual outlets too, from chess to debating and public speaking.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

Leichhardt Public runs a parent-managed Outside School Hours Care (on-site before and after school childcare) service called LOOSH that offers before-school, after-school and vacation care for K–6 children. Spaces can be limited, so families with tight work schedules should enrol early or join the waitlist. Having an on-site program means pick-ups and class transitions feel less hectic, and knowing the care is run by fellow school parents often makes it feel more community-oriented and reassuring.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Norton Street is the culinary backbone of the suburb: local grocers and specialty stores make weeknight life easy. There’s a classic Italian deli and greengrocer for fresh produce and antipasti, an Asian supermarket in Norton Plaza for pantry staples and special ingredients, and a Harris Farm for broader fruit, veg and groceries. When you don’t feel like cooking, Norton Street’s family-friendly Italian restaurants — casual pizza and pasta spots and cozy trattorias — are perfect for an easy midweek meal that kids and adults will both enjoy.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your child needs a little extra academic help, there are local options for maths and English tuition, including a branch of NumberWorks'nWords and smaller private tutors who prepare students for selective schools and NAPLAN. For parents commuting to the CBD, Leichhardt doesn’t have a heavy-rail station named for it, but the L1 Inner West Light Rail and a network of buses make the city accessible. The tram from Leichhardt North to Central Station typically takes around 20 to 30 minutes depending on the time of day, with buses offering useful alternatives. It’s worth doing a trial at your usual travel time to see how it fits your routine.

Your Next Steps

First, lock in care early: enrol or join the LOOSH waitlist so before- and after-school spots aren’t an unknown. Second, get to know the school in person: book a tour or attend an open day, ask about signing up for the music program and check the performance calendar. Third, try the commute and local services: do a run at your typical travel time, and explore Norton Street so you know where to shop and who to call for extra tutoring support if needed.
