Welcome to Earlwood

Earlwood feels like that friend who quietly has it all: an established inner‑south Sydney suburb where tree‑lined streets meet bakeries and family homes. With a median age of 44 and a median weekly household income around $2,164, the place leans middle to upper‑middle and attracts a healthy mix of professionals and managers alongside skilled trades and service workers. It’s quietly family‑oriented without putting on airs, which is exactly the kind of neighbourhood that helps a relocating family unpack, settle in and find routines quickly. Homer Street’s Greek and Mediterranean shops and the local greengrocer give the suburb a warm, lived‑in European feel that can be an easy comfort when you’re adjusting to a new country.

Inside the Classroom at Earlwood Public School

Earlwood Public School teaches from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland), and the classroom vibe is very much about giving kids a full primary experience. Music is a clear strength — instrumental tuition, training and concert bands — and the school nurtures public speaking through debating and choir opportunities. For families who want sport, regular PSSA team sports such as soccer, netball, basketball and athletics are part of the weekly rhythm, so children have lots of ways to plug into school life beyond the classroom.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

If your days are long, the school’s on‑site Outside School Hours Care (on-site before and after school childcare) run by Earlwood Caring for Kids Inc. will be a practical relief. Before‑school care starts at 7:00 and after‑school care runs until 6:00, with vacation programs held on the school grounds during school holidays. The service operates as a community not‑for‑profit and can guide families on eligibility for the Australian government’s Child Care Subsidy, so it’s worth checking current hours, fees and enrolment details directly with the provider to see how it fits your work and family timetable.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Homer Street is the place to make weeknight dinner feel like a treat. For pantry runs, the Greek and Mediterranean specialist grocers and the local produce stand stock the olives, cheeses and seasonal greens that turn an ordinary meal into something Mediterranean‑inspired. When cooking isn’t on the agenda, Little Kalymnos Taverna serves authentic Greek taverna fare and souvlaki, La Famiglia fires family wood‑fired pizzas, and Jailhouse Rock Pizza & Pasta keeps things casual and kid‑friendly. Between pantry supplies and casual restaurants, you’ll find plenty of familiar flavours to anchor the kitchen table.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

For academic support, there are trusted local tutoring options that visit homes or run online sessions, as well as one‑on‑one tuition networks with nearby centres, which makes arranging extra help for maths or other subjects straightforward. Commuting into the CBD is a two‑leg routine for most residents: local buses to nearby Bardwell Park or Wolli Creek stations, then a train into Central. The train leg from those stations to Central commonly takes about 15–20 minutes, but you’ll want to allow extra time for the bus connection when planning school runs or work commutes.

Your Next Steps

Call Earlwood Caring for Kids Inc. to confirm Outside School Hours Care availability, current hours, fees, vacation‑care dates and Child Care Subsidy enrolment details so you know exactly how after‑school care will work for your week. Request a school tour or an open‑morning at Earlwood Public School and ask for the extracurricular timetables — band rehearsal times, choir, debating and PSSA schedules will tell you where your child can plug in. Check local bus routes and timetables to Bardwell Park or Wolli Creek and line those up with train times to Central, and while you’re in the neighbourhood sample Homer Street for groceries and quick dinners to see which shops and restaurants will become your go‑tos.
