Welcome to Soldiers Point

If you’re picturing wide water views, a quieter pace and neighbours who know each other by first name, Soldiers Point fits the bill. With a population of about 1,560 and a median age of 56, this pocket of coastal New South Wales reads as an established, middle‑income community with a healthy retiree presence rather than a turbocharged family suburb. Household sizes tend to be small and weekly incomes sit a bit below the state median, so you’ll find life here is relaxed and community‑minded — a comforting backdrop if you’re a family easing into a new town or country.

Inside the Classroom at Soldiers Point Public School

Soldiers Point Public School educates children from Kindergarten to Year 6. The school has a lively mix of sporting, creative and leadership opportunities that make it feel like more than just a place for core lessons. Students take part in athletics and representative team sport through the local Port Stephens Primary Schools’ Sports Association, which means weekend fixtures and a steady stream of inter‑school camaraderie. The school also invests in the arts: recent whole‑school musicals, classroom performing opportunities and public speaking give kids a chance to discover new strengths on stage as well as in the classroom. Student leadership and debating rounds out a program that’s focused on participation as much as results, which suits a small community school where engagement and confidence-building matter.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

Soldiers Point Public School itself doesn’t currently list an on‑site Outside School Hours Care (on-site before and after school childcare) program, so working parents typically connect with nearby providers in the Salamander Bay and Port Stephens area. Community options like Tomaree OOSH and the council-backed Thrive Kids program are the usual paths for before‑ and after‑school care. That means your afternoon routine might include a short school pickup followed by a handover to an outside provider, or a slightly longer commute to a care hub depending on where your family secures a spot. If reliable on-site care is an absolute must for your week, it’s worth confirming with the school directly in case arrangements have changed.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Daily grocery runs and that emergency milk top-up are handled easily with the Salamander Bay shopping area just up the road. Friendly Grocer in Salamander Bay covers the essentials and you’ll find larger supermarkets such as Coles and Woolworths a short drive away for bigger shops. When life demands fast, family-friendly food, Soldiers Point has classic local choices: fish and chips at Salt Mine, casual café fare at The Deck, and nearby bistros like Blue Swimmer and Sea Salt for simple seafood meals. The dining scene suits low‑fuss weeknights: pick up a rotisserie chicken and salad at a supermarket, or grab takeaway on the walk home and call it a night.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you want extra academic support, there are small local options such as Tomaree Tutoring and a handful of learning hubs listed on regional education directories. These providers offer targeted help in the usual subjects, but you shouldn’t expect large tutoring centres or specialist academic academies the way you might find in big cities. On the commute front, Soldiers Point doesn’t have a passenger train station. Commuters typically take a bus or coach to Newcastle Interchange or Salamander Bay, then connect to intercity trains for travel towards Sydney Central. Door‑to‑door travel to Sydney Central can be long — roughly three and a half to four hours depending on connections — so if you’re commuting to the city daily this will be a significant time investment. For local travel and school runs, family cars and regional buses are the practical options.

Your Next Steps

Call the school and ask about enrolment and whether an on‑site before/after school care provider has been added since the website was updated; a quick phone call clears up the practical stuff faster than guesswork. If you’ll need childcare around school hours, ring Thrive Kids and Tomaree OOSH to check waitlists and reserve places early; these community providers fill up fast at the start of the school year. Finally, book a school tour or an open day and use that visit to ask about the performing‑arts and representative sport schedules so you can map your family calendar — seeing the school in action is the best way to know if it’s a fit.
