# Welcome to Como (NSW)

If you’re picturing a settled, riverside suburb where kids ride bikes to school and neighbours nod hello, you’re thinking Como. The median age sits around 40 in a largely family‑oriented, middle‑to‑upper‑income pocket of Sydney, with a strong share of married couples and households raising children. Most residents were born in Australia—about four in five—which gives the area a familiar, comfortable feel for local families. That said, the community is quietly diverse enough to make settling‑in easier for arrivals: a healthy mix of backgrounds means new families rarely feel isolated when they’re finding their feet.

# Inside the Classroom at St Joseph’s Catholic Primary School Como – Oyster Bay

St Joseph’s is a classic community primary school serving Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland). It leans into a broad, creative curriculum: think school band for Years 3 to 6 with ensemble and instrumental tuition, a lunchtime choir, and a solid line‑up of school sports. The school profile puts a friendly emphasis on the creative and performing arts—music, drama and dance are visible parts of school life—alongside everyday learning and achievement. For parents who want a school that balances structured learning with many ways for kids to explore talent and confidence, St Joseph’s reads like a good fit.

# Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

If your day doesn’t end when the bell rings, you’ll appreciate that St Joseph’s hosts on‑site before‑ and after‑school care operated by Shire ChildCare Centres. That service covers getting kids to school in the mornings and picking them up after lessons, and there’s a vacation‑care program listed on the national early childhood care registry for school holiday coverage. Having on‑site supervised care removes a lot of the daily juggle for working parents: fewer pickups across town, simpler routines, and a single point of contact for seasonal holiday plans.

# The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Evenings in Como are low‑fuss and family‑friendly. If you’re out early, Blackfish Cafe & Grill is a reliable spot for casual breakfasts and lunches, and the Como Hotel’s bistro serves the kind of pub meals that please both small kids and adults. For nights when you’d rather stay in, there’s a useful local mix of Italian and Asian takeaways and a nearby Woolworths Metro plus several independent grocers. The weekly Como Markets are a lovely Saturday‑morning ritual for fresh and artisan produce—perfect for assembling a quick, healthy family dinner and getting the kids involved in choosing ingredients.

# Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you’re thinking ahead about academic catch‑ups or enrichment, there are local options ranging from independent tutors to established centres. You’ll find area tutors who do in‑home math support, and nearby structured tuition centres if you prefer a more formal program. For parents commuting to the city, Como Station on the T4 line gets you to Sydney Central in roughly 25 to 40 minutes depending on the service—about half an hour is common—so it’s realistic for regular city work without the long haul. I still recommend trial runs at your intended school‑time hours just to lock in exactly what your morning and afternoon will look like.

# Your Next Steps

Pull the key ABS QuickStats lines on income, occupations and population into a one‑page PDF so you’ve got a snapshot of the suburb for reference.
