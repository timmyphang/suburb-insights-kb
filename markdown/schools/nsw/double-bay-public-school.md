Welcome to Double Bay

Double Bay feels like a postcard you could move into: tree‑lined streets, boutique shopping along Bay and Knox Streets, and a professional, well‑educated community that skews toward higher household incomes than the NSW average. For families arriving from overseas, that mix is quietly reassuring — roughly six in ten residents were born in Australia, and a large share of neighbours come from England, South Africa and New Zealand, so you’ll find plenty of multilingual households and international backgrounds around the school gate. If you’re picturing playgrounds, local cafés and easy weekend farmers’‑market runs, you’re not far off: the suburb balances quiet residential pockets with a lively foreshore dining and shopping strip.

Inside the Classroom at Double Bay Public School

Double Bay Public School teaches from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland), and its approach feels both local and ambitious. The school runs a healthy roster of extracurriculars — school sport with athletics and inter‑school competitions, a choir and band, debating and public speaking, as well as dedicated programs for high‑potential and gifted students. Kids who like strategy and logic get stuck into chess, budding scientists join STEM activities, and there’s dance for the kids who want to move. The curricular and co‑curricular breadth means families who want a rounded primary experience — academics plus arts and sport — should find something to fit most children’s interests.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For working families, the practical question is always what happens after the final bell. Double Bay’s on‑site care is run by Double Bay OOSH — a parent and community‑run Outside School Hours Care (on‑site before and after school childcare) service that provides before‑school, after‑school and vacation care. It’s set up to be compatible with standard government assistance for childcare, because the program is eligible for the Child Care Subsidy. That combination of community governance and subsidy eligibility tends to give parents predictable options for workdays, school holidays and those inevitable schedule crunches.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

When you need groceries or a quick dinner, Double Bay has you covered without the interstate-style haul. A full Woolworths supermarket stocks the staples, and on weekends the Double Bay Market at Guilfoyle Park brings fresh and specialty produce into the neighbourhood — perfect for midweek salads or weekend brunch spreads. The Knox Street and Bay Street precincts are lined with smaller specialty grocers and food shops for a more curated shopping run. For weeknights, pick‑up is as easy as a stroll: Sushi Maru handles the sushi and takeaway cravings while Matteo keeps the wood‑fired pizza faithful happy; the broader Bay Street café scene rounds out options for no‑fuss dinners and casual family meals.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your child wants extra academic support, Double Bay hosts local options such as Lindamood‑Bell for reading and comprehension programs and Study Space for K–12 tutoring, so targeted help is close to home. Commuting to Sydney’s central business district is commonly done by ferry: the F7 from Double Bay Wharf to Circular Quay takes about 15–20 minutes, after which you can connect onto the wider train network. There isn’t a suburban train station in Double Bay itself, so families who commute by public transport often build the ferry into their daily timings; driving times will vary depending on your exact destination and traffic.
