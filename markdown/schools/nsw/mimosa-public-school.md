# Welcome to Frenchs Forest

If you’re picturing a leafy, family‑first suburb north of the city, you’ve found it. Frenchs Forest leans upper‑middle income, with more married households and kids than the New South Wales average, and many locals work in professional and managerial roles. That shows up in the neighbourhood vibe: tidy streets, active playgrounds, and a real focus on schools and weekday routines. Historically the most common overseas birthplace has been England (about seven percent in the 2016 census), but the area remains predominantly Australian‑born, which can be comforting when you’re settling in and want a sense of familiarity alongside friendly local diversity.

# Inside the Classroom at Mimosa Public School

Mimosa Public School teaches Kindergarten through Year 6 and feels like one of those places where every child can find a corner that fits them. The co‑curricular slate is robust: there’s a performing‑arts program with band, strings ensemble and choir, plus debating and public‑speaking teams. If your kid loves digging in the dirt, the Garden Club is active; if they’re into tech, lunchtime coding clubs run alongside school sport and student leadership opportunities. For parents who care about music participation, check how instrument hire, rehearsal schedules and any fees work during your enrolment tour so you can plan involvement without surprises.

# Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For families juggling work and school runs, Mimosa offers on‑site Outside School Hours Care (on‑site before and after school childcare) through MOOSH, branded as “Mimosa Kids.” Their program runs from 7:00 am until 6:00 pm and includes school‑holiday care, which makes those long workdays and school holiday weeks far easier to navigate. If you need consistent before‑and‑after arrangements, register early—spaces fill fast in neighbourhood schools where routines matter.

# The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Forestway is the local go‑to for groceries and quick meals. You’ll find a Woolworths and an ALDI for everyday supermarket runs, plus an IGA for last‑minute things. Weeknight dinners are simple and satisfying here: Chargrill Charlie’s and Guzman Y Gomez cover fast family favourites, while local cafés like Batch 20 offer coffee runs, pastries and light meals. For evenings when you need something quicker, Forest Kitchen, sushi outlets and takeaway options around the precinct keep dinner stress to a minimum.

# Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you think your child might benefit from extra tutoring, there are nearby options such as Frontrunner Learning Centres and Kumon in neighbouring suburbs. They’re good to contact early if you anticipate additional academic support during the school year. Commuting to the CBD is mostly by bus—Frenchs Forest doesn’t have a train station—so families typically use routes like the 271 from Forestway. Travel time into the city varies with traffic; expect roughly 25 to 35 minutes to Wynyard in good conditions and plan for 30 to 50 minutes to Central Station during peak times.

# Your Next Steps

First, confirm MOOSH / Mimosa Kids enrolment details, their 7:00 am to 6:00 pm hours and holiday‑program sign‑up so you’re not caught short on a Monday morning. Next, take an enrolment tour and ask specifically about joining the performing‑arts groups and co‑curricular clubs—instrument hire, rehearsal times, waitlists and any costs are worth clarifying on day one. Finally, walk Forestway to get a feel for the supermarkets and weeknight dining options, and check bus timetables (route 271) against your commute to plan realistic pick‑ups and work hours.
