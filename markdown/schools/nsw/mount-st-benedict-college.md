Welcome to Pennant Hills

Pennant Hills feels like the kind of suburb you picture when you imagine family life in Sydney’s leafy north: median age about 43, generally middle-to-affluent, steady home‑ownership and a lot of married households. It’s comfortable without being sleepy, and you’ll notice a clear family orientation in weekend parks and school drop‑offs. Roughly three in five residents were born in Australia, with people born in China forming the largest overseas‑born group, which often makes settling in easier for families arriving from overseas because of a visible, supportive multicultural community.

Inside the Classroom at Mount St Benedict College

Mount St Benedict College is a Catholic girls’ secondary college serving Years 7 to 12. It’s focused on single‑sex education through the secondary years rather than offering primary schooling, so it’s an obvious pick for families planning ahead for girls’ high school. The college has a busy co‑curricular life: a Sport Academy that supports competitive and development sports, a Music Academy with brass, strings, woodwind and guitar ensembles, plus debating, dance and full performing‑arts productions. If your daughter loves music or sport, you’ll find pathways to develop those interests alongside the usual academic program.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

Because Mount St Benedict is secondary only and does not run on‑site before and after school childcare for primary students, families with younger children usually arrange care through local providers. Pennant Hills has established options for outside school hours care (on-site before and after school childcare) and vacation programs run by community groups and specialist providers. That convenience matters a lot if both parents work full time: you can enrol younger siblings in a nearby program that picks up the school rhythm while your high‑schooler heads straight to college.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Pennant Hills Market Place covers the basics and then some. You’ll find a Harris Farm for colourful produce and an IGA for staples, and if you want Asian ingredients there are specialist suppliers nearby — useful when you’re cooking quick weeknight dinners that bridge different culinary traditions. When you need a night off, family‑friendly takeaways are plentiful: Thai and Chinese options dot the strip, and pizza or Italian‑style places in West Pennant Hills are a short drive away. Between the supermarket run and a takeaway pick‑up, you can stitch together dinner plans that work around after‑school activities.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you’re thinking about extra academic support, Pennant Hills has local tutoring options that are convenient after school. Small‑group and one‑on‑one providers cover maths, English and exam skills, which can be helpful if your child is juggling co‑curricular commitments at Mount St Benedict. For commuters, Pennant Hills Station sits on Sydney Trains’ Northern line and scheduled services to Central typically take around 45 to 52 minutes depending on the service and time of day. It’s worth testing a peak‑time trip before you commit to a daily routine so you know how it fits with school drop‑offs and work.

Your Next Steps

First, confirm that Mount St Benedict is the right fit academically and socially by checking the enrolment years on the college website and booking a school tour. Second, if you have primary‑age children, arrange outside school hours care with local providers so your after‑school logistics are settled before term starts. Third, match your child’s interests to the college’s co‑curricular program — the Sport Academy and Music Academy fill fast, so review those pages and plan your enrolment conversations around what your child wants to try.
