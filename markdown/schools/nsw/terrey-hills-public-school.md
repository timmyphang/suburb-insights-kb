Welcome to Terrey Hills

If your family is scouting for leafy, calm suburbs just a short hop from the city, Terrey Hills reads like an invitation to slow down without missing out. The neighbourhood skews slightly older than Greater Sydney’s median age and households here report healthy weekly incomes, a reflection of the area’s high share of professionals and managers. Most residents were born in Australia, with England the next most common birthplace, and you’ll meet plenty of established families — it’s the kind of place where neighbours know each other’s kids and weekend walks through the nearby national park are part of the rhythm.

Inside the Classroom at Terrey Hills Public School

Terrey Hills Public School is a classic local primary, teaching kindergarten through Year 6. The school leans into creative and sporting pathways: there’s an Ensemble Band program where children can learn trumpet, clarinet, flute, saxophone, trombone and percussion, alongside a Choir that welcomes musical kids and interschool sport teams with clear pathways through swimming, athletics and cross-country gala days. Those programs make it easy for children to find their tribe, whether they’re into music, team sports or simply being part of a group each week. The school’s facilities and extra-curricular focus give families a sense that students get both a steady academic foundation and plenty of chances to try something new.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For working parents, the school’s on-site Outside School Hours Care — on-site before and after school childcare — is run by Camp Australia, and it covers before-school, after-school and vacation care with bookable programs. That continuity matters: you can send your child to the school, know they’re settled into familiar routines after 3 pm, and choose vacation care options when the school holidays roll around. Camp Australia’s presence on campus means pick-up and drop-off logistics are much simpler than juggling separate childcare providers.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Terrey Hills has a small but friendly town centre that makes grocery runs and quick meals easy. For everyday shopping you’ll find a Friendly Grocer/IGA, and for something a little nicer there’s Forestway Fresh, a gourmet providore and deli for produce and specialty items. When you don’t feel like cooking, neighbourhood favourites include McCarrs for Mediterranean flavours and a warm café vibe, Frankie's Food Factory which doubles as a family-friendly café with a play focus, and Expresso Sandwich Cafe for fast sandwiches and burgers. It’s the sort of place where you can do your weekly shop, grab a picnic fix for a park outing, or meet a friend for coffee while the kids play.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your child would benefit from extra academic support, you’ve got options not far away: Better Tuition Academy (serving the area from Belrose) and Frontrunner Learning Centres on the Northern Beaches are both used by Terrey Hills families for tutoring and enrichment. Commuting to the CBD is straightforward if you’re planning that route: there’s no local train station, so many families use the direct 270 bus into the city’s Queen Victoria Building. Timetabled journeys are typically around 45 to 50 minutes; in peak traffic allow a little extra buffer. Driving times are of course traffic-dependent, but many residents choose Terrey Hills because it balances a quieter home life with reasonable city access.

Your Next Steps

First, book the on-site Outside School Hours Care with Camp Australia for before and after school coverage and to secure vacation care spots. Second, contact the school to enrol your child and ask about joining the Ensemble Band, Choir or interschool sport programs so you’ve got activities locked in early. Third, plan your weekly food runs around Forestway Fresh for produce and deli needs and Friendly Grocer for everyday groceries, and try a 270 bus run into the city on a weekday to time your commute during the school pick-up rhythm.
