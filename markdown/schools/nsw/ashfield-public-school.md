Welcome to Ashfield

Ashfield is one of those inner‑west suburbs that feels familiar the moment you step out of the train: a lively mix of older federation homes standing shoulder to shoulder with low‑rise apartments, young professionals, families and singles all moving through the same tree‑lined streets. The median age here is 36, and households tend to be middle‑income with a steady professional bent. What that translates to on the ground is a neighbourhood that’s both practical and welcoming — cafés where you can take a laptop, parks where kids spill into play, and enough cultural variety that weekend shops and dinner choices never get boring. If your family is new to Australia, you’ll find comfort in the local mix of languages and cultures; multilingual neighbours and specialty grocery stores make the everyday adjustments — from ingredients to greetings — feel friendlier and faster.

Inside the Classroom at Ashfield Public School

Ashfield Public School teaches from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland). The school projects a very community‑focused vibe: alongside regular classroom learning you’ll find community language classes, a Dancesport program, public speaking opportunities and events that celebrate First Nations culture. Sport‑minded families will appreciate the school’s competitive zone sport involvement through the primary school sports association, while the broader extra‑curricular mix helps children find their interests, whether that’s rhythm on the dance floor or confidence on the debate stage. The school’s calendar and activities read like a neighbourhood festival stretched across the school year — great for making friends quickly.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

If you need on‑site childcare around school hours, Ashfield Public School has that sorted: Weldon Children’s Services runs an on‑site Outside School Hours Care program for before‑school, after‑school and vacation care right at the school. For working parents juggling drop‑off, meetings and pick‑up, having a trusted provider on campus reduces daily friction and gives kids a structured place to play, do homework and unwind. Check availability and fees with Weldon early, because convenient spots can fill up in popular neighbourhoods.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Ashfield’s grocery scene reflects its cultural mix. Tong Li inside Ashfield Mall and Little Shanghai are go‑to spots for Asian pantry staples, while long‑standing specialty stores serve Filipino, South Asian and South‑East Asian communities — perfect if you miss particular ingredients or want to keep family meals familiar. For low‑effort weeknight dinners, families in the area lean into casual favourites: Brewfield is a relaxed family café for easy meals and coffee, New Shanghai does reliable Chinese takeaways, and for something a bit different, Korean fried‑chicken spots like Daijobu / 888 Chicken are crowd pleasers. Between the supermarkets and the takeaways, you can rotate quick, comforting dinners without ever feeling stuck.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you’re considering extra academic support, Ashfield hosts tutoring options like Kumon and local private tutors who provide selective‑entry and remedial programs. Those are handy if you want targeted help in maths, literacy or test prep. Commuting to the Sydney CBD is straightforward: Ashfield sits on the T2 Inner West and Leppington train line with direct services to Central Station that typically take about 12 to 16 minutes, making a daily CBD commute short and reliable, especially at weekday peak times. For many professionals, that quick rail link is one of the suburb’s biggest lifestyle wins.

Your Next Steps

Step 1 — See it for yourself: pop over to the school’s “School life” page and arrange a visit or an open morning so your family can feel the classrooms, meet teachers and see extracurriculars in action.

Step 2 — Lock down care and timings: contact Weldon Children’s Services about the on‑site Outside School Hours Care to confirm availability, fees and enrolment, and do a test commute to the CBD at your usual peak times so you know door‑to‑door minutes.

Step 3 — Taste the neighbourhood: explore local grocery options and try a few weeknight venues to familiarise your family with shopping and dinner rhythms; if you want extra academic support, reach out to Kumon or a private tutor to discuss programmes.
