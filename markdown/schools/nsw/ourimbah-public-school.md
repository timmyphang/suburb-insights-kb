Welcome to Ourimbah

Ourimbah feels like one of those places that quietly gets everything right for families. The median age sits around 39 and the typical household income is close to $1,940 a week (2021), which together paint a picture of a settled, family-oriented town where couples with kids are common and the workforce mixes white-collar roles with trades and hands-on work. Most people here were born in Australia, and England is the largest overseas birthplace among those who weren’t, so it’s easy to meet neighbours who’ve made similar moves and can help your family settle in.

Inside the Classroom at Ourimbah Public School

Ourimbah Public School teaches from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland). The school offers a warm, activity-rich environment where kids can join the Blue Gum Flats Band for tutorials and after-school practice, try their hand at debating and public speaking, or get their hands muddy in garden club. Choir and school sport—including inter-school competitions—are regular, visible parts of school life, so there’s something for children who are musically inclined, competitive, or just keen to make friends through shared activities.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

If you’re juggling work and school runs, the school’s on-site Outside School Hours Care (on-site before and after school childcare) run by TASK (The After School Klub) is a practical boon. They run before-school care from 6:30am, after-school care until 6:30pm, and vacation/holiday care, which makes it much easier to cover early shifts, late meetings or school holiday weeks without endless last-minute scrambling. The school and TASK both publish enrolment and contact details so you can lock in spots early.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Weeknight dinners in Ourimbah are uncomplicated and family-friendly. If you want takeaway, the kebab and burger bar is a reliable quick fix; for something a little different try Marine Station Thai or the local Chinese restaurant. If you prefer to buy fresh and cook at home, Ourimbah Produce and the regular local markets are lovely for sourcing local fruit, veg and pantry staples, while larger supermarkets are only a short drive away. For a relaxed family meal out with a view of the local community, The Orchards at the Ourimbah Returned and Services League bistro is an easy, friendly option.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

For families who like a bit of academic top-up, local tutoring options include Cloud Tuition and a roster of private tutors listed through local directories. If you’re commuting to the city, Ourimbah sits on the Central Coast and Newcastle intercity rail line—Ourimbah Station connects you to Sydney Central in roughly one hour and thirty-five to one hour and forty minutes depending on the service. That makes regular commutes feasible, though it’s worth checking live timetables for exact departure and arrival times and planning for peak or off-peak variations.

Your Next Steps

First, check the school website for enrolment zones, term dates and uniform details so there are no surprises when you apply. Second, call or visit the school to see the facilities, talk about Prep transition, and watch extracurricular sessions in action. Third, if you’ll need before- or after-school care, contact TASK early to secure a spot and line up vacation care for busy weeks.
