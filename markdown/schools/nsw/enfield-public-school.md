Welcome to Enfield (NSW)

If you’re weighing suburbs inside Sydney’s inner west, Enfield is a quietly steady choice. It’s a small pocket of about 3,000 people where the median age sits around 41 and the neighbourhood feels like a mix of long‑term residents and busy working families. Roughly half the locals were born in Australia, and a healthy share come from overseas — China is the most common birthplace after Australia, with smaller communities from India, South Korea and Italy — which makes Enfield a reassuring place for newly arrived families who want to live among neighbours who understand settling into a new country. Household incomes here are around the middle range for Sydney: not flash, but comfortable and practical.

Inside the Classroom at Enfield Public School

Enfield Public School is your local public primary, following the typical Kindergarten to Year 6 structure. The school hums with arty and musical energy: there’s a strings ensemble, seasonal concerts and chances to perform at local festivals, plus dance groups and visual‑arts shows that get students onstage and proud of their work. Sport is alive too, from soccer and netball in the local zone competitions to cross‑country, swimming and athletics carnivals. If your child is the kind who loves a recital one term and a running race the next, they’ll find a place here.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For families juggling work and school runs, Enfield Public School runs an on‑site Outside School Hours Care (on-site before and after school childcare) program. The service is open before school from 7:00 to 8:30am and after school from 3:00 to 6:00pm, and there’s vacation care when the school holidays roll around. The program is operated in partnership with a nearby community provider and is approved for around 60 places, so spots can fill quickly — many families enrol early to lock in regular care. If you rely on after‑school supervision, plan to check current vacancies and fees as soon as you can.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

When it comes to groceries and quick dinners, Enfield keeps things low‑stress. There’s a neighbourhood IGA for everyday shopping and a small produce and deli that stocks fresh fruit, vegetables and a few international staples — handy when you want to reproduce a family recipe without trekking to the big supermarkets. For nights when the oven stays off, the local café is family‑friendly and there are Thai and pizza options plus popular Asian takeaways that make weeknight life simple. You’ll find enough variety close by to keep midweek dinners practical and tasty.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your child needs extra academic support, there are local tutors who will come to the house or run lessons online, alongside a couple of private tuition providers in the area. Commuting to Sydney’s central business district is doable but requires a little planning: Enfield doesn’t have an active passenger train station, so families typically use nearby stations such as Burwood or Croydon or rely on buses. Once you’re on the train from Burwood it’s only about 10 to 15 minutes to Central Station; a direct bus from Enfield to the city usually takes around 25 to 40 minutes depending on traffic. For school‑time travel, do a couple of trial runs during peak hours so you know exactly how long the trip will take on a weekday.

Your Next Steps

1) If you need before‑ or after‑school care, contact the school’s on‑site Outside School Hours Care as soon as possible — places are limited and families often enrol early.  
2) Run the commute at school‑time: map routes via nearby Burwood or Croydon stations and test the bus options during the morning and afternoon peak so you’re not surprised on day one.  
3) Visit a school event — catch a concert, sports carnival or art show — to feel the culture and see if Enfield Public School’s mix of music, arts and sport fits your child.
