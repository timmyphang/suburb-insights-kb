Welcome to Sefton

Sefton sits quietly on the western edge of Sydney with a surprisingly young, family‑centred feel. The median age is about 35 and households here tend to be middle income; families show up in the numbers with a higher share of children than the state average. Just under half of residents were born in Australia and a large proportion arrive from overseas, especially Vietnam, with noticeable communities from Lebanon and China. For parents arriving from abroad, that multicultural mix is a real comfort: you’ll find familiar foods, languages and community rhythms that make settling in easier while still giving kids the Australian school experience.

Inside the Classroom at Immaculate Heart of Mary Catholic Primary School

Immaculate Heart of Mary in Sefton is a Catholic primary that teaches Kindergarten to Year 6 (note for international parents: Kindergarten is the first year of primary school in Australia). The school runs an active co‑curricular program — think specialist physical education and competitive school sports for kids who love to move, plus a strong creative and performing arts focus with dance, drama and whole‑school events like Wakakirri. Musically inclined students can join the Amadeus instrumental and ensemble programs. The vibe you’ll get visiting the school is one of busy classrooms balanced with plenty of after‑school activity: pastoral care and community are woven into the routine, and the extra programs give children hands‑on ways to find their place.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For working families, the school hosts on‑site Outside School Hours Care (on‑site before and after school childcare, and vacation/holiday care) operated by Camp Australia. That means drop‑off before work and pick‑up after the office commute is covered in the same campus environment where your child learns. Enrolment and service details are managed through Camp Australia and listed on both the school’s community pages and the national childcare register, so you can confirm places, hours and holiday‑program options before committing.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

If you love shopping local, Sefton Plaza Foodland and the Helen Street IGA are your go‑to supermarkets, supported by a network of smaller specialty grocers and halal and ethnic food outlets that reflect the suburb’s diversity. There’s even a local growers market that helps the neighbourhood feel like a small town inside a big city. For quick weeknight meals, families rely on familiar local takeaways: Cresent Star for kebabs, burgers and pizza, Tai Pak for Chinese takeaway, and Akkar Lebanese Bakery for halal bakery goods and ready meals. That mix makes dinner both convenient and culturally resonant — ideal for evenings when you need something fast but comforting.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you’re thinking about extra academic support, several local providers work with Sefton families, including Alchemy Tuition for maths, Markitup Tuition and online options like Cloud Tuition for targeted one‑to‑one help. Commuting into the CBD is straightforward: Sefton railway station sits on the Main Southern line and scheduled trains to Sydney Central typically take about 33 to 40 minutes depending on the service and time of day. I always recommend trialling the commute at the times you actually expect to travel so you know exactly how long school‑run plus work commute will feel in real life.
