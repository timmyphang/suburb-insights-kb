Welcome to Unanderra

If you’re scouting suburbs with a quietly established, family-friendly feel, Unanderra is the kind of place that settles you in. The median age is 42 and the suburb leans lower‑to‑middle income, with a median weekly household income around $1,211—below the New South Wales (NSW) median—so you’ll find more long‑term residents, practical homes, and neighbourhoods where people know each other. About three quarters of locals are Australia‑born, which means it’s familiar and steady for many families, while still offering enough variety in shops and services to make everyday life comfortable. For a family making a move, that mix of roots and reasonable cost of living is often exactly what you want.

Inside the Classroom at St Pius X Catholic Primary School, Unanderra

St Pius X is a K–6 Catholic primary tucked into the heart of the community. Classrooms are focused on the essentials—literacy, numeracy and faith-based values—while co-curricular life gives kids room to breathe. Sport is a clear highlight: students participate in gala days and carnivals across soccer, netball, basketball, cricket and athletics, with pathways for talented kids to progress to diocesan trials. There’s a genuine music culture too; the school runs active bands and a music program that performs regularly. If your child loves being on stage, on the field, or both, this school offers several ways to get involved beyond the classroom.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For working families, one of the practical wins at St Pius X is on-site Outside School Hours Care (on-site before and after school childcare) provided by CatholicCare. That service runs before school, after school and through school holidays, which takes the pressure off juggling shifts, drop-offs and pickups. It’s worth confirming hours, fees and the pickup/drop-off routine with CatholicCare before you commit, but having that kind of structured care inside the school grounds makes the daily rhythm of work and parenting far easier to manage.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Groceries and easy dinners are straightforward in Unanderra. You’ll find mainstream supermarkets like Woolworths and FoodWorks for weekly shopping, and for a touch more variety, there are nearby specialty options—places that stock broader Asian ingredients and health-focused groceries. Weeknight meals can be as simple as a local family-friendly takeaway; Unanderra Fish & Chips is a neighbourhood favourite, and there are local cafés and small restaurants offering Thai, pizza and kebab options for busier evenings. For families who cook at home, the mix of stores covers most needs without long drives.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If you’re thinking about extra academic support, there are local options that travel to the area and centres in nearby Wollongong. Illawarra Tutoring offers in‑home or local tutors, and NumberWorks'nWords provides English and maths tuition and enrichment programs that many parents use to shore up confidence in those subjects. Commuting to the Sydney CBD is feasible if you need to: Unanderra station sits on the South Coast / Illawarra line, with typical journeys to Sydney Central taking around one hour and thirty to forty minutes depending on the service. Driving times shift with traffic, so if a regular commute is part of your plan, trial the route during likely travel times before you settle.

Your Next Steps

First, lock in a chat with CatholicCare about Outside School Hours Care: check enrolment, hours, fees and how school pickups are handled so your workweek has predictability. Second, book a school tour at St Pius X and ask about representative sport pathways, carnival schedules and the bands/music program—seeing rehearsals or a game day gives a true feel for how your child might fit in. Third, trial commutes and local shopping: do a weekday run to the train station at peak times and swing by the supermarkets and a local takeaway so you know how evenings and travel will slot into your family’s routine.
