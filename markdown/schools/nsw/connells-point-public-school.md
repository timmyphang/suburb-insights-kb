# Welcome to South Hurstville

If you’re picturing a neighbourhood that balances quiet village charm with a lively multicultural heartbeat, South Hurstville fits that description. The median age sits around 40 and local households earn roughly $1,867 a week on average, which matches the mixed blue‑ and white‑collar workforce you’ll find here: professionals share the streets with clerical staff and tradespeople, so it never feels one‑dimensional. Most residents were born in Australia, but there’s a strong overseas‑born community too, with people from China making up a noticeable share. For families moving from overseas, that mix of cultures often makes everything—from language support to finding familiar food—easier than you’d expect.

Connells Point Road is the spine of local life: weekend brunches, quick grocery runs and a handful of reliable eateries line the stretch that locals know well. Hurstville’s larger shopping centres and supermarkets are only a short hop away, so you get village convenience without losing access to big‑city amenities.

# Inside the Classroom at Connells Point Public School

Connells Point Public School teaches children from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland). The school has a lively arts and sport culture—think choir and band, inter‑school athletics and after‑school basketball—alongside language offerings that nod to the community: early Chinese language classes in the first years and Greek for older primary students. These programs make it easy for kids to find their tribe, whether they’re into music, sport or exploring another language.

Teachers and parents I’ve met describe the place as warm and pragmatic: there are structured extra‑curricular options, but the school also leans into community events and performances that invite families to participate. If your child loves being on stage or kicking a ball, they’ll slot in quickly.

# Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For families juggling work and school runs, Connells Point has on‑site Outside School Hours Care run by Adventure OSHC. It covers before‑school, after‑school and vacation care during term and school holidays, which is exactly the kind of reliability that keeps a working week from unraveling. Adventure OSHC manages bookings, fees and vacation‑care sign‑ups, so you can lock down childcare without having ten different phone calls. It’s a practical setup: drop the kids early, pick them up later, and know they’ve had structured activities and supervision in between.

# The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

When you want to cook at home, Lloyds IGA in the South Hurstville village is the convenient local for everyday groceries. If you need Asian ingredients or a wider selection, Hurstville’s larger Asian supermarkets—Tong Li and Wellmay—are just a short drive away and stock everything from fresh produce to specialty sauces and snacks. Eating out is equally easy: Roma 1960 is the neighbourhood pizza place for casual family nights, Jazzi’s Indian & Continental brings hearty comfort dishes, and Oregano Bakery is the kind of café you’ll visit for school‑drop coffee and a pastry. Connells Point Road also has kebab and takeaway outlets for those nights when you want something quick and comforting.

For families who speak other languages or are used to different pantry staples, the local grocery mix is a comfort: familiar ingredients are within reach, and the strong multilingual community means your kids are more likely to meet classmates who understand the transition to a new country.

# Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your child needs a bit of academic extra support, there are local options. Kumon in Hurstville offers maths and English practice, while private tutors and centres in the area provide curriculum help and selective‑entry preparation. These services are easy to pair with after‑school care or weekend routines.

Commuting to the Sydney Central business district requires a short domestic hop: South Hurstville doesn’t have a train station, so most families travel to nearby Hurstville station first. From Hurstville to Sydney Central on the Eastern Suburbs & Illawarra Line it’s roughly 17 to 25 minutes depending on the service, so allow a little extra local travel time to reach the station and you’ll have a realistic door‑to‑desk estimate for your school‑day logistics.

# Your Next Steps

First, take a look through the school’s pages to familiarise yourself with student activities and the on‑site Outside School Hours Care hours so you know what fits your week. Second, ring Adventure OSHC to confirm the enrolment process, fees and vacation‑care sign‑up details so your childcare is locked in. Third, book a school tour or open day—seeing the performing arts classes, sports fields and language programs in person makes it much easier to picture your child there.
