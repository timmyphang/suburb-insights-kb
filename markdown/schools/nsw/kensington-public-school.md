Welcome to Kensington

Kensington arrives like a neighbourhood for people who love being close to the city without giving up a strong local vibe. The median age hovers around 32, and household incomes are well above the national average, which shows up in the cafés, services and convenient amenities that have sprung up near UNSW and Anzac Parade. You’ll find a mix of young professionals, students and family households stacked together—so whether you want a lively evening street or a playground for weekend downtime, both are on offer. For families arriving from overseas, that mix of residents often means a warm, familiar-feeling community where many homes speak other languages and neighbors are used to helping new arrivals settle in.

Inside the Classroom at Kensington Public School

Kensington Public School caters to Kindergarten through Year 6 and brings a chirpy, creative energy to its classrooms. The school leans into performing arts, with choir and public-speaking opportunities that let kids find their voice, while a robust instrumental and band program is run in partnership with an external provider—perfect if your child wants to try an instrument without you needing to hunt down lessons across town. Sport rounds out the weekly rhythm, and the school’s extra-curricular offerings are clearly designed to give a balanced mix of teamwork, performance and everyday learning.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

If you need on-site care around the school day, Kensington Public School runs its own Outside School Hours Care (on-site before and after school childcare) program, commonly known as KPS OOSH. They provide before-school, after-school and vacation care so parents who work full days can have continuity and convenience without juggling multiple providers. It’s a relief to know the care is connected to the school community—drop-offs, activities and pick-ups all happen in a familiar environment rather than a distant centre.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Weeknight meals in Kensington are unpretentious and satisfying. If you’re after quick comfort, Ogalo’s Portuguese-style rotisserie chicken is a neighbourhood favourite; Fana’s keeps things classic with family-style pizza and pasta that please even picky eaters; and Hoh Won is the dependable local Chinese takeaway for nights when you want a fuss-free dinner. For groceries, you’ll usually pop into FoodWorks or the IGA near UNSW, and there are small local supermarkets that stock everyday staples and ethnic ingredients—handy for families who like to cook traditional meals at home.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

For extra academic support, Kensington has local tutoring options that specialise in primary-school programs, so getting a session after school or on weekends is straightforward. The area doesn’t have a heavy rail station named “Kensington”; instead, most families rely on the light rail and frequent buses around the UNSW and Anzac Parade corridor. From the Kensington/UNSW light-rail stop, the tram into the Haymarket and Central areas takes roughly 12 to 15 minutes, though you’ll want to add walking time to the stop and the occasional transfer when planning the school-run or your commute.

Your Next Steps

First, ring the school to confirm enrolment steps, grade placement and the programs your child might join—check out the student opportunities page so you can speak from specifics. Second, contact KPS OOSH to ask about hours, fees and waitlists for before‑school, after‑school and vacation care so you can lock in reliable childcare from day one. Third, if music or band sounds important, reach out to the external band provider about schedules, costs and whether they offer instrument hire so your child can start rehearsals without delay.
