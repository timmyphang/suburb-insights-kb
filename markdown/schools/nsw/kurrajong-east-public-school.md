Welcome to East Kurrajong

If you’re picturing wide streets, larger blocks and a village-y pace, you’ve got East Kurrajong exactly right. With just over 2,200 residents at the last census and an average household of 3.4 people, this semi‑rural Hawkesbury pocket feels made for families—median age sits at 38 and household incomes lean toward comfortable, which shows in the local, low‑density vibe. It’s the kind of place where neighbours know each other, kids ride to the park, and the rhythm of the day is a little slower than the city.

Inside the Classroom at Kurrajong East Public School

Kurrajong East Public School is your go‑to neighbourhood primary school, covering Kindergarten to Year 6. The school leans into creative learning: music, a school band, dance groups and regular performances give children plenty of chances to shine on stage, while chess clubs and other activities feed the quieter, strategic mind. The school’s stories and community highlights show a steady stream of student performances and group events, which is perfect for families who want broad, enriching extracurricular options without driving across town.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

If you need before‑ and after‑school care, Kurrajong East has an on‑site Outside School Hours Care service that operates before and after school and accepts the federal Child Care Subsidy for eligible families. Enrolments and bookings are handled through an online family portal, so working parents can book in without a dozen phone calls. The service is also listed on the national child‑care directory with contact and opening‑hours details, which makes juggling shifts, meetings and school pickups a whole lot easier.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Groceries and quick dinners around here skew practical and local. Friendly Grocer in Kurrajong and several independent grocers cover the weeknight essentials, while small cafés, bakeries and takeaway spots provide reliable options for nights when you’d rather not cook. Think Lisa’s Bright Side Café for easy takeaway, local bakeries for fresh loaves and pastries, and a handful of kebab and pizza outlets for the inevitable busy‑week family meal.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

For families thinking about extra academic support, there are local tutoring options that serve the Kurrajong area across K–12 needs—useful if you want a bit of catch‑up help or extension work close to home. Public transport is car‑and‑bus friendly rather than train‑centric: residents typically take Busways services into hubs like Richmond or Windsor, then transfer to the train for the trip into Sydney’s CBD. Because East Kurrajong has no local train station, expect to plan that bus leg and a Richmond‑to‑Central train connection when you’re estimating door‑to‑CBD times.

Your Next Steps

First, enrol your child in the school’s on‑site before‑and‑after school care through the family portal and confirm entitlements under the federal Child Care Subsidy, plus the service’s opening hours. Second, pop along to a school performance or an open event—watching a band rehearsal or a dance show is the easiest way to get a feel for the culture and the chess and arts programs; skim the school’s recent stories before you go so you know what to look for. Third, line up local support and logistics: contact the nearby tutoring services if you want extra academic help, plan a sample weekday commute using Busways routes into Richmond or Windsor and the Richmond→Central train, and note Friendly Grocer and the local cafés/takeaway options for quick meals while you settle in.
