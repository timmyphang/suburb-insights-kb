# Welcome to Yarrabilba

If you’re scanning suburban options with young kids in tow, Yarrabilba feels like a neighborhood built for them. It’s a rapidly growing, master‑planned family suburb where the median age sits in the mid‑20s and house lots are full of families building routines around school runs, sports training and weekend markets. Household incomes sit around the middle range for the region, which shows through in new parks, playgrounds and an emphasis on community facilities rather than luxury retail. The population is predominantly Australian‑born, so you’ll see lots of familiar schoolyard accents and family rhythms as you settle in.

# Inside the Classroom at St Clare’s Primary School

St Clare’s Primary School teaches from Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland) and shares its site with an on‑site Kindergarten, making the early years transition seamless for littlies. The school’s day-to-day culture leans into a broad co‑curricular program — you’ll find organised partnerships with big sporting names like rugby league, netball and Australian rules football, and a Friday elective block for Years 3 to 6 that reads like a mini creative festival: coding, podcasting, drama, choir, STEM, textiles and sport all rotate through those sessions. There’s also a lunchtime dance club, so between classroom learning and playtime your child can be exploring everything from performing arts to early coding skills.

# Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

St Clare’s has an on‑site Kindergarten and an integrated Outside School Hours Care (on-site before and after school childcare) hub that runs before‑school, after‑school and vacation programs. The close link between the Outside School Hours Care hub and the school makes drop‑off and pick‑up practical for working families; holiday care is organised through the same provider, so your child can stay with familiar staff and join structured holiday activities rather than being moved between separate providers. For parents juggling work, this kind of continuity — same site, coordinated programs — is the difference between a frantic logistics puzzle and a manageable daily rhythm.

# The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

Yarrabilba’s local shopping precinct centres around a Coles supermarket for weekly groceries and essentials, with smaller deli and specialty outlets complementing what you can’t find on the big‑box shelves. If you like the idea of picking up fresh produce and meeting neighbours, the community market‑style events are worth pencilling in; these pop up with artisan stalls, local bites and kid‑friendly treats. For quick outings and casual weeknight dinners, local cafés like Café 63 and Yarrabilba Coffee Hub are friendly breakfast and coffee stops, and you’ll find classic takeaway options such as Jimbo’s Seafood & Takeaway for those family fish‑and‑chip nights. Food‑truck markets and night markets add a relaxed, social dimension to dining when you want low‑prep, family‑friendly meals outside the house.

# Tuition and the Daily Commute

If your child needs extra academic support, Yarrabilba already hosts familiar tutoring options such as Kumon for maths and English, plus private and online tutors like Cloud Tuition, so stepping up learning support is only a local phone call away. Commuting into Brisbane CBD from Yarrabilba typically involves a door‑to‑door public‑transport journey of roughly 70 to 90 minutes, because the suburb does not have a rail station. Most families use TransLink bus Route 587 to a rail interchange such as Loganlea, then continue by train to Brisbane Central — the rail leg is roughly 40 minutes depending on the service. For working parents who need reliable arrival times, it’s worth trialling peak‑time trips to see whether the public‑transport connection suits your schedule or whether a driving alternative will save you time.

# Your Next Steps

First, ring the school to confirm enrolment steps, current fees and how the Outside School Hours Care hub manages registrations and holiday‑care places — ask specifically about drop‑off and pick‑up routines so you can plan the day. Second, book a school tour or time a visit during local open‑week events to watch the Friday electives and co‑curricular activities in action; seeing coding, choir or sports sessions gives you a much clearer feel for whether a child will fit in. Third, do a trial commute at peak time by catching the bus 587 and the connecting train to Brisbane Central so you can measure the door‑to‑door time and test alternative driving routes if public transport adds too much travel.
