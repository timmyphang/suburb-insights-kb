Here’s a concise eight‑point briefing for families considering Brighton Primary School (Brighton, VIC). I used ABS QuickStats / community profiles for suburb demographics and primary sources for school and local-service items; each fact includes a direct citation.

1) Suburb socio‑economic profile  
Brighton is an affluent, predominantly white‑collar suburb with a high median age (48) and high household incomes (median weekly household income reported ~$2,718). The area skews toward established families and older adults rather than a large young singles population [citation:2021 Brighton (Vic.), Census All persons QuickStats](https://www.abs.gov.au/census/find-census-data/quickstats/2021/208011169).

2) Most common foreign country of birth  
The most common overseas birthplace after Australia is the United Kingdom / England (England is the top non‑Australian birthplace in Brighton’s 2021 QuickStats) [citation:2021 Brighton (Vic.), Census All persons QuickStats](https://abs.gov.au/census/find-census-data/quickstats/2021/SAL20337).

3) Working‑parent support (OSHC)  
Brighton Primary runs an on‑site OSHC program operated by TeamKids (before‑school, after‑school and vacation care available) — families can book via the TeamKids Brighton Primary venue listing [citation:TeamKids — Brighton Primary OSHC](https://www.teamkids.com.au/venues/brighton-primary-oshc-6/).

4) School culture — extracurriculars  
The school lists music and arts programs and operates strong sporting programs (school sports field/athletics facilities noted); local listings also mention purpose‑built music, art and science facilities and a strings/instrumental offer in the area — expect music, team sport and arts opportunities [citation:Brighton Primary School (official site)](https://www.brighton.vic.edu.au/) [citation:Brighton Primary School — Australian Schools Directory](https://www.australianschoolsdirectory.com.au/7603/brighton-primary-school-brighton).

5) Suburb vibe and groceries (2 sentences)  
Brighton has a family‑friendly bay foreshore, popular playgrounds (North Road foreshore / Dendy Street beach area) and many cafés that make it easy for after‑school outings. Local grocery options include neighbourhood grocers and small specialty stores (e.g., Brighton Grocer) plus a local Asian grocery on Bay Street for ethnic ingredients [citation:North Road Foreshore Playground / Brighton foreshore (MelbournePlaygrounds & Bayside)](https://www.melbourneplaygrounds.com.au/north-road-reserve-playground-north-road-brighton) [citation:Brighton Grocer listing (Yellow Pages)](https://www.yellowpages.com.au/brighton-vic/bpp/brighton-grocer-pty-ltd-581034073).

6) Weeknight dining — family‑friendly takeaway / casual options  
Examples: Le Feu (Vietnamese / Pan‑Asian on Bay St) for pho/Asian takeaway, and White Rabbit (wood‑fire pizza / Mediterranean) for casual family dining and takeaway — both are popular local, family‑friendly choices [citation:Le Feu Brighton (OpenTable)](https://www.opentable.com.au/r/le-feu-brighton) [citation:White Rabbit — wood fire pizza](https://www.whiterabbitbrighton.com.au/).

7) Tuition and enrichment near Brighton  
Notable local options include Bayside Academics (private/personalised tutoring in Brighton) and a Kumon Brighton centre for maths/English practice programs [citation:Bayside Academics — Brighton tutoring](https://baysideacademics.com.au/) [citation:Kumon Brighton listing](https://www.kumonsearch.com.au/Centre/KUMON-BRIGHTON-VIC).

8) Commute — train travel time to Melbourne CBD (Flinders Street)  
Brighton is served by the Sandringham‑line stations (e.g., North/Middle/Brighton Beach); rail travel to Flinders Street is typically about 20–25 minutes from the Brighton stations, depending on which Brighton station you use and service timing [citation:Rome2rio — Brighton to Flinders Street (typical times)](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Brighton-VIC-Australia/Flinders-Street-Station) [citation:Metro Trains timetables (Sandringham line)](https://www.metrotrains.com.au/timetables/).
