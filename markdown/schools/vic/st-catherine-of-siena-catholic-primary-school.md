Welcome to Prestons

If you’re picturing a suburb where young families set down roots without the fuss, Prestons fits that bill. The median age here is 34 and there’s a strong share of partnered and married households, so playgrounds, weekend sport and school-run rhythms feel very normal. Household incomes sit around the middle of the market, supporting a mix of office-based and hands-on tradespeople; that blend gives the suburb a practical, family-first vibe. One of the quiet comforts for newcomers is how multicultural the area is — many households speak languages other than English and a notable share of residents were born overseas, which makes settling in and finding familiar food, faith and community a lot easier.

Inside the Classroom at St Catherine of Siena Catholic Primary School (Prestons)

St Catherine of Siena is a K–6 school that blends everyday structure with chances for kids to try things outside the standard curriculum. If your child likes to move, there’s dance; if they hum in the car, there’s music and choir; and if they prefer quiet strategy, there’s chess. Sport and performing-arts events are regular features — you’ll see bands and performances at school fairs, and those events are a great way for families to meet and for children to build confidence. The co-curricular mix gives kids both the routine of classroom learning and the chance to explore interests that make school feel like more than just reading and arithmetic.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

For parents juggling work and school pickups, the on-site Outside School Hours Care run by Sydney Catholic Early Childhood Services is a practical lifesaver. Before-school care starts at 6:45am and runs until 8:45am, and after-school care runs from the end of the day until 6:00pm. Those sessions usually include meals and supervised homework time, so you get reliable cover and children get structure and support instead of solo screen time. If a workplace schedule is part of your move calculation, that consistent before-and-after arrangement really smooths the transition.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

When it comes to groceries and quick dinners, HomeCo Prestons is the hub — mainstream supermarkets alongside a handful of specialty and ethnic grocers mean you can do a big weekly shop and then pop back for specific ingredients. For example, Tapsi Supermarket in the precinct stocks many Fijian and Asian pantry items that make cooking familiar meals straightforward. On busy nights, you won’t be short of takeout: family-friendly options range from the popular Lebanese charcoal and fried chicken at El Jannah to casual pizza and manoosh at Classic Manoosh & Pizza, plus nearby Indian and Vietnamese takeaways that are great for sharing. That mix of shopping and dinner options keeps the weekday routine manageable and varied.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If extra academic support is part of your plan, there are local options. Markitup and Alchemy offer maths and English tuition and can do in-home or online tutoring, so you can tailor catch-up or extension work around your family’s timetable. Commuting into the Sydney CBD is doable but typically involves a bus-to-train connection: most families take a local bus (for example route 867) to Glenfield or Liverpool, then catch a train to Central. The train leg from Glenfield to Central is commonly around 40–50 minutes; once you add the bus leg and transfer waits, expect door-to-door travel to be roughly 60–75 minutes depending on where you start and how smooth the connections are. It’s a realistic commute for many professionals, but it pays to trial the school-drop timetable once or twice before committing.

Your Next Steps

Step 1: Book a school tour or contact St Catherine of Siena to confirm K–6 enrolment steps, ask for the school handbook and check their enrolment timetable so you know key dates and forms.  
Step 2: If you need before- and after-school care, reach out to Sydney Catholic Early Childhood Services early to secure a spot and confirm the 6:45–8:45am and 2:50–6:00pm sessions.  
Step 3: Do a real-world commute trial at the times you’ll be travelling — house to bus 867 to Glenfield and then train to Central — so you can measure practical transfer windows and decide what morning routine will actually work.
