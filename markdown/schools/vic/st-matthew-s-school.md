Welcome to Cornubia

Cornubia feels like a suburban sigh of relief for families who want space, green breathing room and an easygoing routine. The median age sits around 40 and households here reported a healthy median weekly income in 2021, which translates into a largely white‑collar and mixed‑occupation community where married families are well represented. It’s the kind of place where neighbours nod, kids play under big gum trees, and weekend plans often include a trip to the local shopping precinct or a stroll through Cornubia Forest Park.

Inside the Classroom at St Matthew’s School

St Matthew’s is a warm, community‑minded primary that teaches Prep to Year 6 (note for international parents: Prep is the first year of primary school in Queensland). The school’s co‑curricular life hums with music and movement: students can learn violin, guitar, keyboard and wind or brass instruments, join the junior or senior choir, and be part of the school band. Sport happens regularly too, from athletics and cross‑country to inter‑school team competitions, and there are quieter options like drama and chess for kids who prefer a different pace. That mix makes it easy for children with many interests to find their people and for parents to feel confident there’s more than just classroom learning happening each week.

Life After 3 PM: Working Parent Support

St Matthew’s runs an on‑site Outside School Hours Care program — that’s before‑school, after‑school and vacation care provided by Catholic Early EdCare. Typical hours cover early starts from about 6:30am until the school day, after‑school care from roughly 2:45pm to 6:00pm, and vacation care with long hours around 6:30am to 6:00pm to cover full workdays. For working parents this kind of on‑campus childcare is a real game changer: drop‑off and pick‑up are simpler, the kids remain on familiar grounds, and vacation care cushions those tricky school holiday weeks.

The Kitchen Table and Weeknight Dinners

When the day runs long, Cornubia’s weeknight options are refreshingly simple and family friendly. There’s a Woolworths at the Cornubia/Loganholme shopping precinct for the weekly shop and a couple of IGA choices nearby for top‑up runs. For dinner, local takeaway spots cover the classics: seafood and burgers at George’s Grill, sushi at Wara Sushi, and Mediterranean kebabs at Empire Kebabs. If you’re easing into a busy evening routine, these options make it easy to feed a family without a lot of fuss.

Tuition and the Daily Commute

If extra academic support becomes part of your plan, help is available locally. Kip McGrath in nearby Loganholme offers English and maths tuition, and private tutors can be found through platforms that connect families with one‑to‑one or small‑group tutors. Commutes into the Brisbane CBD require a little planning because Cornubia doesn’t have its own train station. Most families take a local bus — for example the TransLink route that loops Cornubia to Loganholme or Beenleigh — and then catch a train from Beenleigh into Brisbane Central. On direct services the train leg is about 48 minutes, while some stopping patterns and service types can take up to about an hour or a bit more, so it’s worth checking exact timetables when you plan daily travel.

Your Next Steps

First, ring St Matthew’s OSHC provider to confirm current vacancies, how bookings work and exact vacation care hours so you can line up childcare before school terms start. Second, browse the school’s co‑curricular page for details on instrumental lessons, choir and sports so you know what enrolments and costs to expect. Third, take a weekend to walk Cornubia Forest Park and visit the shopping precinct: you’ll quickly get a feel for family amenities, grocery options and which local takeaways will become your go‑to on busy nights.
