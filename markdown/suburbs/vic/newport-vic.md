# Newport (VIC)

Slug: `newport-vic`

You know a suburb’s got a complex when its biggest moment in recent memory was losing a train change. Yes, Newport – the Werribee line’s forgotten middle child. For years, we were the essential pit stop where Altona passengers changed trains, the junction of our little universe. Then Broadsheet announced: “passengers from Westona, Altona and Seaholme Stations won’t need to change trains at Newport.” Just like that, our purpose evaporated. Now we just wave as the expresses fly through.

But we’re not bitter. Back in the day, The Substation hosted the Fringe Furniture Festival – a 2010 exhibition where designers laid a giant landscape of woodchips across the gallery floor. Very Newport: trying to bring the country to a former substation. The architect said “people thrive in environments that have other forms of life around.” We still thrive on quiet irony.

Whirlpool forums once described parts of Newport as “lovely Victorian properties on the bay side of Melbourne Road.” They’re not wrong. We’ve got heritage overlays and terraces that would make a Fitzroy estate agent weep. But also a Hall Street that gained a “brand spanking new cafe” at some point – though nobody can remember its name. It’s probably closed by now.

The Pedestrian TV train line ranking gave Seddon, Yarraville and Newport a collective “fancy.” Fancy? We’ve got a crossbar telephone exchange that was one of the last in Australia to be decommissioned. That’s our legacy: outdated infrastructure and a train station that used to matter.

Who would hate it here? Anyone expecting a nightlife beyond the 7:45pm train home. Or a food scene that isn’t “there’s a new cafe somewhere on Hall Street, I think.”

Who would love it? People who appreciate a good heritage street, don’t mind the occasional freight train rumble, and take pride in being the suburb that taught Melbourne how to transfer trains – even if nobody thanks you for it. Newport: we were the connection. Now we’re just the gateway to Williamstown. And that’s fine.
