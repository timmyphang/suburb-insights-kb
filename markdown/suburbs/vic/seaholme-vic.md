# Seaholme (VIC)

Slug: `seaholme-vic`

Seaholme, VIC — population: quiet, coastal, and wondering why a 2017 train timetable change is still our biggest claim to fame.

Back in the day, we made headlines. Broadsheet, no less. August 2017. “Passengers travelling from Westona, Altona and Seaholme Stations won’t need to change trains at Newport.” We felt seen. A direct line to the city. We even printed the article out and framed it at the station. It’s still there, yellowing, under the pigeon-proof spikes.

These days, our local news is mostly crickets. Urban List tried to rank Melbourne’s best fish and chips — didn’t mention us. Concrete Playground? Doing coastal walks in Sydney. Even the Sitchu suburb profiles just list our postcode (3018) next to Seabrook and Seaford, as if that counts as a profile. “Seaholme: it’s between Seabrook and Seaford. Next.”

But we’ve got what matters: the beach, the bay, and that glorious feeling of not having to switch trains at Newport. You want a sea-change but cannot afford Portsea? Welcome. You love the idea of a coastal town but also need to commute to Footscray? We’ve got you. You’re the kind of person who says “it’s peaceful” when what you really mean is “nothing ever happens”? This is your paradise.

Yes, there’s no cafe listed in the archives. No chippy that made the cut. The highest-charting Seaholme moment is a train timetable adjustment from eight years ago. And we’re not mad. We’re smug. Because while you’re queuing at some influencer brunch spot in Williamstown, we’re on the beach with a servo pie, knowing we never have to change at Newport.

Seaholme: a small town in the city. Come for the train to Flinders Street, stay because you’ve already unpacked.
