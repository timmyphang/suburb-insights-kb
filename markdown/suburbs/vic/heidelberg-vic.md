# Heidelberg (VIC)

Slug: `heidelberg-vic`

We know what you’ve heard about Heidelberg. You’ve heard it’s where your nan goes for a doctors’ appointment and a vanilla slice. And honestly? You’re not wrong. But you’re also missing the bit where a self-taught pitmaster is selling out brisket from a converted van in West Heidelberg, and a pie dynasty heir is making queues on Bell Street again.

Our Saturday morning routine goes like this: grab a coffee at the little Seven Seeds shrine on Burgundy Street (the one with the salad sandwiches that make you question every decision you’ve ever made), then wander up to Pieman’s Son for a beef ragu pie that sold so well it never left the menu. Pat Cremean, the heir to Boscastle Pastries, grew up kicking pastry around the factory and now his dad’s been forced out of retirement to help. That’s the commitment to pastry in this town.

By evening, you’ve either got a table at Little Black Pig & Sons – where chef David Lakhi explains the origins of your oxtail agnolotti like he’s narrating a nature documentary – or you’re queuing at The Bearded Pig on Mologa Road, praying Matt Antonello hasn’t sold out of burnt ends by 6pm. (He will have, because he cooks extra briskels every week just to keep up. It’s never enough.)

But not everyone survives the Heidelberg gauntlet. There’s the intersection camera on Rosanna Road and Darebin Street that’s been collecting souls since the dawn of VicRoads. Local Whirlpool threads dissect the location of the 40km/h speed signs like scripture. And if you’re thinking of buying in Heidelberg West and wondering why the houses are cheaper than surrounding suburbs… let’s just say the forums are honest: “Now it’s beginning to make sense.” Also, a saltwater crocodile was found in Heidelberg Heights in 2017. Yes, that happened. No one talks about it. We just moved on.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who needs a night out past 9pm. Anyone who flinches when they see “West Heidelberg” on a real estate listing. Anyone who expects their suburb to have a single identity instead of being three different realities – pie shop heaven, BBQ chapel, and the place where the Yarra is technically swimmable if you’re brave enough.

Who’d love it? Food pilgrims who understand that the best Italian in Melbourne is not in Carlton. Fans of 500-square-metre cannoli factories. People who want to live somewhere with actual culture – Heide, Montsalvat, the whole Heidelberg School thing – without the attitude. And anyone who has ever said “the burnt ends are worth the wait.”

Welcome to Heidelberg. Yes, we have speed cameras. But we also have brisket. Fair trade.
