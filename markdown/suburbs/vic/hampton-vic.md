# Hampton (VIC)

Slug: `hampton-vic`

Welcome to Hampton, where the Hampton Street shopping strip has more cafes than actual parking spots, and we wouldn’t have it any other way. Our weekend morning routine: grab a bike, roll past Sebastian’s Food and Wine (still going strong since 2018, a true Hampton North survivor), then weave through the crowds outside Southall at 400 Hampton Street for a 48-hour charcoal kaali daal that’s worth the wait. But by 10am, the real action is at Kalye Marinas – 502 Hampton Street – where you’re eating Filipino seafood boil with your hands, ignoring the yoga mums side-eyeing from their oat lattes.

Petty local drama? Oh, it’s simmering. The Block’s Kristy and Brett bought in Hampton East and suddenly everyone’s a real estate mogul. Whirlpool forums are full of people complaining that houses between Hampton and Parkdale keep getting pulled from the market at $1.6 million. Meanwhile, the rest of us are just trying to get a table at Bang Bang Hampton for their $2 oyster happy hour – a pan-Asian feast that proves we’re not just about smashed avo. But don’t get us started on the “foodie glow-up” of 2024 – yes, we got Southall and Bang Bang, but Moon Dog opened in Frankston, not here. Typical.

Who would hate Hampton? Anyone allergic to prams, beach sand, or property speculation. If you can’t handle the smell of sunscreen mixed with entitlement, this is not your strip. The sand gets everywhere, the train line is a waking nightmare, and the price of a 3-bedroom house will make you cry.

Who would love it? Anyone who wants a beachside suburb that’s actually got a pulse – where you can eat Indian family recipes from the 1990s, then stumble into The Hamptons Bakery in Hampton East for a decent sausage roll. We may be basic, but we’re specific. And we’ve got the food scene to prove we’re not just a postcode – we’re a state of mind.
