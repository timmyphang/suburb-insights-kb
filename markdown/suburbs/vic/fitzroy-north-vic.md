# Fitzroy North (VIC)

Slug: `fitzroy-north-vic`

There’s a moment every Fitzroy North local knows: you’re walking down Nicholson Street, past the burgundy facade of the Royal Oak Hotel (still serving since 1871, now with fancy Marquis of Lorne DNA), and you realise you haven’t seen a single person in activewear who isn’t coming back from Fit Inn with a takeaway smoothie. This is the one Melbourne suburb where “village life” isn’t a real estate tagline – it’s a contract we all signed.

Our Saturday routine hasn’t changed since 2022: grab a congee and a burnt cheesecake from Standing Room on St Georges Road (their first proper sit-down cafe, a full graduation from uni hole-in-the-wall), then cross to Edinburgh Gardens to judge the bocce players at the public court. For emergencies, Annoying Brother on Nicholson Street has dukkah eggs and a blackboard wall that’s been scrawled with daily specials since before we moved here.

The real local drama? The gold car on Rae Street. In 2022, taggers hit it at least three times in two days. Pedestrian TV called it “dicking around” and honestly that’s the most publicity the North Fitzroy Arms Hotel has had since its “light-handed makeover” in 1874. We still love it though – wood panelling, fireplaces, no nonsense.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who thinks Fitzroy North is “just Fitzroy’s quieter neighbour”. We are not a spillover suburb for Brunswick Street rejects. We have our own wine bars (Underwood, Neighbourhood Wine – which Time Out calls a “Euro-classic warmth” second-floor affair) and we will absolutely split the bill after listening to your politics, as a Junkee writer once observed. Also: the 16-storey apartment proposal on Queens Parade? We’re still mad about it.

Who would love it? People who want a pub that’s been a pub since the ’70s (Railway Hotel, still serving lasagne with a ragu that costs you change from a twenty). People who like a coffee spot that literally used to be just standing room. People who don’t mind that the Terminus Hotel is 150 years old and still somehow “adding a Josper oven”. We’re not trying to be cool. We’re just, as Time Out put it, “quieter, gentler (but no less awesome)”. And we have the gold car to prove it.
