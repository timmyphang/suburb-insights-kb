# Rowville (VIC)

Slug: `rowville-vic`

We’re in Rowville, where the biggest local drama is a pair of blokes who allegedly got their gear out for a speed camera operator on Australia Day. Yes, that’s right — according to a Pedestrian TV report, the only time our suburb made national news was for some pest allegedly mooning a radar gun. It’s the kind of energy that sums up life here: stuck between “we’re desperate for attention” and “please don’t look at us.”

For decades, Rowville has been the public transport black hole of the southeast. Concrete Playground called it exactly that. Premier Dan Andrews promised us a tram line from Caulfield via Monash Uni in 2018. The government put $3 million toward planning. It’s now 2026. Still waiting. Meanwhile, we just keep driving, because the only way out of Rowville is via a car park disguised as a suburb.

When we do get somewhere, it’s usually to Stamford Park Homestead — a 19th-century estate on Emmeline Row that Broadsheet reckons was “pulled straight from Maggie Beer’s dreams.” Fair go. Chef Nicholas Boyle does European-Australian fare with local Knox produce. Very classy. Very unexpected for a suburb where the other big culture spot is Rush HQ — a trampoline park at 39 South Corporate Avenue that charges $10–21 and closes at 6pm. That’s the weekend choice: heritage high tea or bouncing like a toddler until dark.

On Whirlpool, locals used to argue whether Noble Park North is better than Rowville. One poster summed it up: “Lifestyle seems decent with all the parks and hills, so long as you don’t need to get to the city regularly.” That’s Rowville in a nutshell — lovely spot if your life exists entirely within a 5km radius of a Scorptec warehouse. Speaking of which, good luck getting them on the phone. “Rowville never answers,” another forum user moaned. Even the tech geeks have given up on us.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who wants to walk to a train station. Or catch a tram this century. Or get a bottomless brunch without driving 40 minutes. Urban List’s boozy Melbourne guide doesn’t even know we exist.

Who would love it? People who already live here and have accepted that the tram is a myth, the speed camera story is our legacy, and that’s just fine. We’ve got a homestead, a trampoline park, and a very good view of the hills from our car. Welcome to 3178.
