# Mill Park (VIC)

Slug: `mill-park-vic`

You know you’ve made it when your suburb’s biggest claim to fame is being a tier‑2 COVID exposure site alongside the Pacific Epping shopping centre. That’s us. Mill Park. We’re the part of Melbourne that city guides forget to list, the place where “up and coming” still means “we have a Community House and a petrol price conspiracy.”

Every morning we wake up, check the fuel prices on Whirlpool, and sigh: Mill Park South Morang – $1.59. Other suburbs – $1.39. The independents – $1.34. For a five‑day stretch. We’ve accepted this as our cross to bear. Meanwhile, the only radio show visionary enough to broadcast from here was, as one Pedestrian.TV love letter put it, “Mill Park had never heard a radio show so visionary, so ground‑breaking, or so I told myself.” Probably hosted from someone’s garage.

The local drama is real, but it’s not flashy. It’s about housing. “I do like Mill Park, just that we are not able to afford something decent there. We did almost buy a house in Mill Park but it had structural issues, mould and rot.” That’s a Whirlpool user, not a satire. We’ve got community houses that run $2 cake‑and‑tea sessions on Thursdays, and yoga studios (Sitchu says we have them) – but you won’t find a single Broadsheet‑approved bakery within our postcode. Not one.

Who would hate it here? Anyone searching for a hidden speakeasy behind a trick bookcase. Mill Place Merchants is in the CBD, not here. Our hidden entrances are to the back of the Coles servo. If you crave a “salted‑caramel Negroni,” you’re five suburbs too far south.

But who would love it? People who get that a suburb isn’t defined by its media clippings. We’ve got a community house that actually means community. We’ve got affordable (ish) houses if you can dodge the mould. We’ve got yoga, petrol prices to bond over, and a radio legacy that one writer still types about. We’re not trying to be the next North Melbourne. We’re just Mill Park. And after a pandemic, a fuel crisis, and a structurally dubious house inspection, we’re still here.
