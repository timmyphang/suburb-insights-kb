# Williamstown (VIC)

Slug: `williamstown-vic`

We’re in Williamstown, where the bay views go for $1.6 million and the industrial heritage comes free. Yes, we’re the suburb that nearly became Melbourne’s CBD but got passed over for a swamp. We’ve been holding that grudge since 1837.

Our mornings start at **Carter Smith Devlin & Co** on Nelson Place — that strip everyone calls “tired” but secretly loves because it’s the only place you can get a cold-pressed juice and watch a shipyard worker eat a bacon roll. If you’re feeling fancy, you queue at **The Kiosk d’Asporto** for a gelato slider on the sand, pretending you’re in Positano while a refinery looms behind you. Or you do the **Williamstown Foreshore Walk** past **Blunts Boat Builders** (fifth generation, 160 years — we don’t let go of things) and the **Ann Street Morgue**, because nothing says “seaside charm” like a former autopsy station.

The big local drama? Our pubs. The **Morning Star Hotel** almost got turned into townhouses, but a syndicate of heroes swooped in. Now it’s back with rattan floors and a $8 pint happy hour. Meanwhile, **Sebastian** landed on the beach and suddenly we’re a “bayside dining destination.” The old guard at the **Hellenic Hotel** looks sideways at the Basque-inspired small plates. Change is coming faster than a developer’s knock on a heritage-listed boat shed.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks “working class” is a vibe, not a lived reality. The train line still passes the Newport rail yards. We’ve got refineries, shipyards, and a housing market that’s telling long-term renters to try Altona. If you want sanitised beachside without the smell of salt and diesel, go to Brighton.

Who would love it? The people who know **Off The Pier** does the best fish and chips in a Victorian-era building. The ones who show up to **Willy Lit Fest** and genuinely care about community. The ones who look at the city skyline from **Gem Pier** and think, “Yeah, we almost had it. But this is better.” We’re not trendy. We’re real. And we’ve got the morgue to prove it.
