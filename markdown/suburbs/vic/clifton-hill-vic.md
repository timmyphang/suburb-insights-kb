# Clifton Hill (VIC)

Slug: `clifton-hill-vic`

There’s a moment every Clifton Hill local knows: you’ve just crushed the Main Yarra Trail loop, Apple Watch congratulating you, and you roll into Clifton Hill Station right as the miso egg sandwich at Open House is hitting the pass. Welcome to the gentrified bubble we call home.

Your morning starts at Open House on Roseneath Street, where Nathen Doyle, India Anderson and Diego Leon have created the community cafe we always wanted. “Your local cafe should be an extension of home,” they say, and we agree — especially when that home serves a miso egg sandwich with Japanese pickles and plans to sling burgers and lasagne on weekend nights. Before that, you might grab a canoli from Cavallini on Queens Parade, where the owners know regulars by name and the courtyard is perfect for pretending you’re in Europe. Or you’re at The Bell Jar on Smith Street, staring at trams while sipping Five Senses coffee.

The real drama? Trying to cross the traffic at High Street and Studley Park Road to get to Cookshop Rotisserie for Melbourne’s best charcoal chook. Guy Grossi’s Clifton Hotel looms at that intersection like a beacon for people who want to drink while counting cars. Meanwhile, old stalwarts like Childplay toy store (since 1972) and Ariel Batteries keep the place honest. And if you need a Birkenstock or a vintage Margiela jacket, we’ve got you covered at the Queens Parade strip.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who doesn’t own a pair of Birkenstocks, can’t sit through a social pickleball session at Clifton Hill Tennis Club, or thinks a curated cocktail bar in a former clothing warehouse (hello Grau Projekt on Alexandra Parade) is too much. Also, people who don’t have curly hair — SO Haircutters on Queens Parade is practically a religion for ringlets.

But if you want a neighbourhood where the barista knows your name, the yoga studio (Clifton Hill’s largest Iyengar) sorts your chakras, and you can cycle home from the trail with a straight chai from Raffy’s in hand, welcome. Just don’t expect to park anywhere near the Clifton Hotel on a Friday night. We’re full, thanks.
