# Kew East (VIC)

Slug: `kew-east-vic`

We’re Kew East, the quiet cousin of Kew who doesn’t get invited to the party but still has a woodfired pizza oven and a bookshop that’s been around longer than the Yarra’s had water restrictions. Yes, we live in the shadow of our more famous sibling, but we’ve got Di Palma’s — a 130-seat Italian institution run by Alfia and John since 1988, where the spaghetti marinara is basically a family heirloom and the limoncello martini will make you forget you’re on High Street between a real estate agent and a cosmetic acupuncture clinic.

Our mornings? They start at Gilbert’s, that bright green and pink-terrazzo all-day diner that took over the old Rémy spot in 2022. We go for the $5 mimosas (yes, every day, all day — don’t judge, we have standards) and the spanakopita that tastes like yiayia made it, but the egg-and-bacon roll is for the tradies who still remember when this street was all panel vans and pie carts. As Broadsheet noted, Gilbert’s is “bright and colourful” — which is exactly what you need when you live in a suburb where the most dramatic event last year was someone’s nanny job expiring.

We don’t do fussy drama here. Our closest brush with conflict is whether Tim’s Bookshop in Kew (a 40-minute walk or a 10-minute argument about parking) counts as “our” bookshop. Spoiler: it doesn’t, but we claim it anyway because we need some culture between the Hays Paddock dog walks and the Renaissance Theatre school productions.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks a night out requires a venue that stays open past 3pm. Gilbert’s closes at 3pm, Di Palma’s is fine dining with a cellar room, and the only late-night buzz comes from the Korean BBQ listicles that mention somewhere 12 suburbs away. Also, if you’re allergic to needles, avoid Cosmetic Acupuncture Melbourne — Dr Vivian Tam will stick pins in your face, and we’re here for it.

But who would love it? Families who need 737 nannies within spitting distance. People who describe “leafy” as a personality trait. And anyone who wants a $5 mimosa at 9am without being judged — because in Kew East, we’re too busy minding our own business to mind yours.
