# Beaconsfield (VIC)

Slug: `beaconsfield-vic`

We know what they say about us: “Beaconsfield is known for having a high proportion of bogans,” wrote one brave soul on Whirlpool. And look, they’re not entirely wrong. But what they missed is that we also have a garden-to-plate tasting menu in a 130-year-old post office that’s been called “a destination restaurant” by Broadsheet. We contain multitudes.

Our Saturday routine? You’ll find us weaving between two realities. At 11am, the Pink Hill Hotel on Pink Hill Boulevard is absolute chaos — kids going wild in the next-level play zone while parents sink a pot and pretend they can still hear themselves think. Then by 1pm, the same people are ten minutes down the road at O.My on Woods Street, tucking into a produce-led degustation shaped by what’s grown on the family farm in Cardinia. Yes, we go from a play centre to a fine diner housed in a heritage butcher shop. That’s Beaconsfield for you.

The local drama? It’s mostly about whether O.My is “worth the drive from Melbourne” or whether you should just stay at the Pink Hill and call it a day. Broadsheet reckons the Metro Tunnel now makes lunch at O.My “feel less like an expedition”. We say the real expedition is getting past the servo without buying a scratchie and a V energy.

Who would hate it here? City snobs who think anything beyond the M3 is “regional”. They’ll complain about the train frequency (it’s fine, mate) and the fact that you can’t get a flat white past 4pm without driving to Berwick. They’ll also clutch their pearls at the Whirlpool forum’s “bogan” label, failing to realise that’s our charm.

Who would love it? Families who want a pub with a playground AND a restaurant that uses the entire pumpkin (core, flesh, seeds, skins and all). People who understand that a suburb can have both fine dining in a post office and a bloke washing his ute in the driveway on a Sunday. We’re not trying to be anything we’re not. We’re Beaconsfield: bogan meets biodynamic. And we’re fine with that.
