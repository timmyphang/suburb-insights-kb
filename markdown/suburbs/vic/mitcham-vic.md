# Mitcham (VIC)

Slug: `mitcham-vic`

You know you’re in Mitcham when the most exciting thing on Whitehorse Road used to be the servo and the fish ‘n’ chip shop. Then Dutch Rules Distilling Co. rocked up, straight outta 2026, bringing gin botanicals from South Asia and a Lankan-leaning menu that’s got the city finally saying “Mitcham? Wait, what?”. We’re still catching up, as Broadsheet politely put it, but we’ve got chef Viveik Vinoharan doing tortellini in a curry-leaf beurre blanc and lamb-belly skewers that make the old-school takeaways look like they’re not even trying.

On a Saturday, you’ll find us at The Dark Horse on the Brit—locals call it ‘The Horse’—working through truffled mushrooms on sourdough while someone argues about the best parking spot at Mitcham Station. Yes, we’re the suburb where the car park is 90% full by 8:30am and you’ll still drive around for 10 minutes hoping for a miracle. That’s our gym.

The real drama? None, really. Our biggest controversy is whether the Risky Kids parkour club on Redland Drive is loud or brilliant (it’s both). And Mitcham Community House has been keeping us connected since 1975—so we’re not exactly a hotbed of feuds. We’re too busy learning pottery and doing ninja warrior training.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who needs a drink after 9pm. We have one cocktail bar (Dutch Rules, upstairs, formal) and it’s a distillery that closes before you’ve finished your gin flight. City-slickers who complain about “not enough culture” will be deeply disappointed by our one-Sri-Lankan-gin-joint and the fact that the Edinburgh Hotel is actually in Adelaide. Yes, that article about “Mitcham institution since 1869”? That’s a different Mitcham. We’ve got history too, but it’s mostly in the form of a community house and a parking shortage.

Who would love it? The person who wants a genuinely good feed without the inner-north wait. The person who thinks a distillery with a fully rebuilt open kitchen and a private mezzanine is a perfectly acceptable upgrade for a suburb that “still catching up.” The person who can parallel park with confidence. Come for the gin, stay for the fact that we finally have something new to talk about.
