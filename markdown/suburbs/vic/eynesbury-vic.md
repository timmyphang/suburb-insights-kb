# Eynesbury (VIC)

Slug: `eynesbury-vic`

**Eynesbury, VIC — Where the Tornadoes Are Cuter Than the Aldi**

Let’s be honest. You don’t end up in Eynesbury by accident. You end up here because you wanted a house with a yard and a horizon, and the bank said “best we can do is a 40-minute commute that feels like two hours.”

Yes, we’re the 3,000-person community south of Melton that made global headlines when a *baby tornado* drifted through. As *Broadsheet* put it, the Bureau of Meteorology assured everyone funnel clouds are “harmless.” Classic Eynesbury — we can’t even get a proper natural disaster. Other suburbs get floods and fires. We get a gentle weather tantrum that someone posted photos of on Facebook.

Our social calendar revolves around the Eynesbury Homestead, which *Time Out* tells us hosts a Summer Festival, a Winter Festival with *snow much fun* puns, and the Kingdom Festival for kids where the main attractions are food trucks and pet markets. Thrilling stuff. If you’re under 35 and childless, your weekend options are: drive to Werribee, drive to Melton, or stare at the horizon and wonder if that’s a cloud or the existential dread of your mortgage.

Over on Whirlpool, the local forum is a goldmine of buyers’ remorse. One resident confessed: *“I have serious buyers remorse purely because this is the first house my family and I have bought.”* Welcome to Eynesbury, where the biggest local drama is whether you’ve made a terrible financial decision. The playground at Eynesbury Recreation Reserve gets a mention from 2026 — at least the kids are having fun.

**Who would hate it here?** Anyone who thinks a “night out” involves a tram ride. Anyone who doesn’t own a car. Anyone who’s allergic to the sound of silence punctuated by the occasional baby tornado.

**Who would love it?** People who genuinely love their quarter-acre block more than they hate the commute. People who look at a dirt path and think “that’s where the mud run for the Winter Festival will be.” And anyone who wants to say they lived through a meteorological event that was described as “harmless” and “cute.”

Go home to your inner-city studio, city slicker. We’ve got a funnel cloud with our name on it.
