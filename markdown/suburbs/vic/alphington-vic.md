# Alphington (VIC)

Slug: `alphington-vic`

We’ve been telling people we live in Alphington for years, and they still think it’s a typo for Armadale. Fine by us. Let them keep driving past on Heidelberg Road while we’re queuing at the Alphington Farmers Market with a gold coin and a reusable bag, pretending we’re not about to demolish a Holy Crumpet.

A Saturday morning here goes: dog at Kissaten for a puppycino and a breakfast ramen in their Japanese garden, because nothing says “leafy suburb” like a bowl of noodles before 10am. Then a stroll past the old bank that became The Alphington Social, where the Breen siblings knocked through a concrete vault to house an entire wall of cheese. We still mourn the taxidermy stag head that guards the fireplace. Then you might grab a soft-shell-crab bun from Benjamin’s Kitchen, if you can find it among the cloud-dappled white decor.

But don’t get too comfortable. Our culinary crown jewel? Decca, the much-hyped all-day restaurant from Adam D’Sylva at the Yarrabend precinct. It was “opening soon” in 2024. It’s still “opening soon” in 2026. We’re starting to think it’s a metaphor for the tram line we were promised.

The real drama? La Sirene Taproom. Finally opened its doors to flaunt complex, acid-driven beers. Closed less than three months later. As Broadsheet reported, “forced to close after less than three months in business.” Classic Alphington: we get a brewery, then we don’t. At least the Red Rooster on Heidelberg Road is eternal.

You’d hate it here if you need reliable dining options that stay open. Or if you think “emerging culinary enclave” means more than one cafe per square kilometre. You’d also hate the constant sound of dogs at Kissaten and the smell of single-use-plastic-free markets.

But you’d love it if you want a quiet life with a solid farmers market, a Japanese garden cafe that also does toasties, and the quiet satisfaction of living in a suburb nobody can pronounce correctly. Just don’t blink — you’ll miss the taproom.
