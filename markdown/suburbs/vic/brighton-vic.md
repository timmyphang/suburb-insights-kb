# Brighton (VIC)

Slug: `brighton-vic`

Brighton, VIC — where the bathing boxes are Instagram-famous and the residents have opinions on everything, including the correct way to walk through the Botanic Gardens.

Our morning starts with a run along the beach, followed by a 50-minute hot mat workout at Love Athletica on Church Street — because nothing says "I'm better than you" like infrared pilates with complimentary tea rituals. Then it's over to St Martin's for a flat white, served in a room that brings the colourful bathing boxes indoors. Because why go to the beach when you can sit in a cafe themed like the beach?

But the real Brighton drama? It’s not the $95 three-course set menu at Baix (Ian Curley's new 40-seater on Bay Street, where the mantra is "we charge what we have to, not what we can get away with" — cheeky). It's not even the two-storey 140-seat Henrys on Well Street, with its 1970s LA meets European Riviera vibes and a converted well as a fountain, because nothing says "new Brighton" like paying homage to the old watering hole while charging $28 for crab linguine.

No, the real scandal was when one of our own, Jodi Grollo — daughter of one of Victoria's richest families — decided to go for a walk around the Royal Botanic Gardens during lockdown and became a viral "Karen." The Herald Sun had a field day. We, as a suburb, collectively sighed. Yes, we know what they say about us. They're not wrong. But they also missed the bit where BP and David Jones teamed up to open a bougie servo in East Brighton that serves sushi and hot roast chook. If you're going to fill up, do it with style.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks a $95 set menu is "a bit much for a Thursday night." Anyone who doesn't own activewear that costs more than their car. Anyone who doesn't understand why you'd pay $1,500 for a studio apartment that smells like the sea and old money.

Who would love it? Anyone who wants their sashimi served by a former Scott Pickett chef. Anyone who likes their pilates with a side of social hierarchy. Anyone who wants to live in a suburb where the biggest local controversy is a well-to-do lady going for a walk. We're Brighton. We're not sorry. Come for the pasta at Henrys, stay for the quiet confidence of the dining room at Baix. Just don't jog on the Tan without permission.
