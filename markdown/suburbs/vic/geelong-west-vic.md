# Geelong West (VIC)

Slug: `geelong-west-vic`

We’re not saying Geelong West has an identity crisis, but when Pakington Street goes from the birthplace of the Geelong Football Club at Murphys to a cheese train chugging into Splatters, you’ve got to wonder if the local water’s been spiked with sourdough starter.

Saturday morning kicks off at Box Office, a shipping container on Preston Street that somehow serves pulled-pork eggs Benedict with native pepper. Then a bagel from GWSC (if you’re early enough) before the Lycra crew and prams jostle for Coffee Supreme. For a fancier affair, Tulip on Pakington will sort you with six courses and a wine list that screams “I read Broadsheet.” Owners Matt Dempsey and Graham Jefferies even donate 100 meals a week to the Geelong Food Relief Centre – so you can feel smug about your duck cracker with pate.

Pholkore brought Vietnamese to Pakington in 2021, and Boss Burger’s been doing the honest work since 2024. But the real local hero is Rossco’s on Shannon Avenue – a sandwich so substantial it counts as a life decision. And if you’re after a souvenir, Bottles & Barrels on Pakington sells pinot from Lethbridge Wines and a leather couch to cry on after you see the price.

Who’d hate it here? Anyone who thinks brunch should be a simple affair. If you can’t handle a cafe that turns into a wine bar, a bottle shop with no bar stools, or a restaurant that changes its menu daily – move along. Also avoid if you’re allergic to toddlers: the Geelong West Toy Library just got $445,000 in grants, and the Neighbourhood House runs playgroups three mornings a week.

Who’d love it? Families who want a suburb where the kids can see Play School at the Town Hall, then hit the toy library, while you grab a Vietnamese feed at Pholkore and a Boss Burger for the road. Yes, we’ve got our hipster veneer now – but the heart is still a 22 bus line running through a community that knows your name, your dog’s name, and exactly how many bagels you bought. Welcome to Geelong West. Don’t tell Melbourne.
