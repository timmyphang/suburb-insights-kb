# Mernda (VIC)

Slug: `mernda-vic`

We’ve been waiting for the train since 2018. When the Mernda Rail Extension opened six months ahead of schedule, we cheered like we’d won the lotto. Three shiny new stations – Mernda, Hawkstowe, Middle Gorge – and 982 services a week to the city. Except half of them get replaced by buses every July for level crossing removals. “Buses will replace trains between Thornbury and Mernda stations,” they said. Again. And again. We know the replacement bus timetable better than our own kids’ birthdays.

But now, tap and go is finally here. From June 2026, you can wave your phone at the reader and skip the myki dance. “The final group joins: Mernda,” they announced, as if we were the last to get the memo. We’ll take it. Anything that shaves 30 seconds off the 50-minute commute is a win.

The locals on Whirlpool forums are still arguing about house blocks. “320m2 block almost took up the entire site,” one wrote. Another lost $6,500 on Accrue Real Estate. We don’t talk about that. We talk about the Mernda Villages Community Centre and the playgroup at Mernda Uniting Church – “large, open, bright space, kitchenette, accessible toilets”. That’s the real estate we care about.

And yes, there was that time in 2020 when Yarra Valley Water told us to boil our water because of a “potential water quality issue”. We boiled for a week. Nothing happened. That’s Mernda: lots of preparation, not much drama.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks a 50-minute train ride is too long, or that “country escape” means actually having a cafe that isn’t a servo. The Magnolias on Mernda – that luxury stay in Olinda – is nice if you’ve got $400 and a weekend. But for us, Mernda is home: where the train eventually came, the blocks are small, and the playgroups are bright. Come for the tap and go, stay for the community centre. Just boil your water first.
