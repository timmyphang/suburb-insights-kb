# Langwarrin (VIC)

Slug: `langwarrin-vic`

Langwarrin, VIC — we’re the suburb that makes you pull over on Cranbourne-Frankston Road for a spontaneous U-pick sesh before you even reach the Peninsula. Yes, we’ve got Bramble Farm at 25 Lyppards Road — Mark and Di Benson have been running it since 1985, now with 10,000 vines of brambleberries and 28,000 canes of raspberries. Entry’s a tenner for adults, free for kids under five, and Di’s jams and honey are the real reason you’re “just popping in.”

A Saturday morning here? You’re either loading the kids into the car for Beretta’s Langwarrin Hotel, where the Urban List says the Worx Zone is a “four-level soft play wonderland” and Kids Club members get free meals and ice cream. Or you’re hitting the Langwarrin Flora and Fauna Reserve for a spring walk — Concrete Playground reckons it “comes alive with native wildflowers” and meandering trails. Just bring your own water, because there’s no servo selling oat lattes on the path.

Petty local drama? We don’t really have any — we’re too busy arguing over which berry farm has the best raspberries (Bramble Farm, obviously) or whether the McClelland Gallery and Sculpture Park on McClelland Drive is worth the drive from the city. Broadsheet called it “sizeable, if remote” back in 2010, and we’ve been smug about it ever since. Sally Smart’s Femmage (Shadows and Symptoms) was there — and so was our patience when tourists asked “is this it?”

Who’d hate it here? Anyone who thinks “culture” means a laneway bar with exposed brick and a $24 negroni. Langwarrin’s vibe is more Cruden Farm — romantic gardens, orchards, and the sense that you’ve stumbled onto a legacy (it’s at 60 Cranbourne-Frankston Road, entry via Cranhaven Road, if you’re fancy). If you need a pop-up vinyl store and a kimchi place to feel alive, keep driving down the Peninsula.

Who’d love it? Families who want a pub where the kids can go wild while you eat a parma in peace. Nature lovers who get excited about a “well-maintained nature reserve”. And anyone who believes the best summer activity is standing in a field with a punnet of raspberries, arguing with your toddler about who gets the last one. Welcome to the Warrin — we’ve got berries, sculptures, and zero pretension.
