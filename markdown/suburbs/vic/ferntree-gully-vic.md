# Ferntree Gully (VIC)

Slug: `ferntree-gully-vic`

We know the morning here: you stagger back down from the 1000 Steps at the Ferntree Gully Picnic Ground, lungs full of eucalyptus and dignity slightly misplaced, and ponder whether you should reward yourself with crumpets at Lorna — which, back in the day, drew a crowd from the base of the Dandenongs. We’ve done that walk. We’ve watched weekend hikers turn into full-time locals by lunchtime.

For years the strip was a grab-bag of delights: Wholefood Merchants — one of Melbourne’s largest health-food stores, apparently also the place to find crystals and tarot cards — sat in our patch of the Dandenongs. Cove even ran a solar-powered factory here, making refillable cleaning products that felt very proud of themselves. The Rogue Squire’s two levels used to preside over the famous corner where Fred Gessner first opened a pub in 1891, and the Ferntree Gully Hotel had live entertainment on weekends for the sober and not-so-sober alike. We’ve queued for On It Burgers at 930 Burwood Highway and whispered that Ranong Town brought proper Southern Thai flavours to our postcode.

We’re not above chaos. Remember the night three cows escaped from a trailer and treated our streets like their own personal gauntlet? We do. Animals outrunning suburbia is practically our unofficial tourist attraction. We’ve also had tougher days — the local cricket community once reassembled and supported each other after a tragic accident — and we showed up for that too.

Who would hate it here? People allergic to hills and stairs, flat‑ground-only types who need a tram, and anyone who thinks “weekender” means “don’t talk to actual hikers.” If you need a skyline or a boutique laneway mural on every corner, this isn’t your RomCom suburb.

Who’d love it? Hikers, picnickers who know 22 Stringybark Drive and Ferntree Gully Quarry Reserve, chocolate pilgrims who remember Hahndorf’s, families involved in PlayConnect groups, and anyone nostalgic for pubs with proper history and cafes that used to make crumpets worth the pilgrimage. We’re not trying to be fancy — we’re the place people come to climb a mountain, eat a burger, and leave having earned their afternoon beer. We wouldn’t have it any other way.
