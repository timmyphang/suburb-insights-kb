# Croydon Hills (VIC)

Slug: `croydon-hills-vic`

Ah, Croydon Hills. Where the streets are leafy, the houses are expensive, and the most dramatic thing that’s happened here in recent memory was in 2020, when Yarra Valley Water told us to boil our tap water for a day. “Potential water quality issue,” they said. “Electrical damage from storms.” We still don’t know if we’re supposed to be offended that we made the list of 88 affected suburbs or relieved that we finally got a mention in the same breath as Doncaster.

A typical weekend in Croydon Hills: you pack the kids into the car, drive past the Croydon Hills Baptist Church playgroup (indoor AND outdoor space, very fancy), then swing by the Croydon Community Garden for a FOGO tour. Yes, a FOGO tour. Because nothing says “we’ve made it” like watching your food scraps get turned into compost on a Thursday morning. Later, you might check the Local Food Connect directory for a beeswax wrap demonstration. We are living the dream.

As the Whirlpool forums so eloquently put it: “Croydon Hills is very nice (and very expensive), most of Croydon North the same. As with Ringwood, a lot of central Croydon is pretty scummy and this is where the dodgy reputation comes from.” We own the reputation of being the fancy sibling. You won’t find the “rubbish” here, mate — that’s down near the station. We’ve got auctions, not private sales. We’ve got houses, not problems.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who wants a pub within walking distance, a train station that doesn’t require a 20-minute bus ride, or a conversation that isn’t about property values or the best time to put out the green bin.

Who would love it? Families who want their kids to grow up thinking “community garden” is a normal weekend activity. People who enjoy being “very nice and very expensive” and don’t mind the water boiling every five years. Welcome to Croydon Hills. We’ve got hills, and we’ve got standards. The tap water is optional.
