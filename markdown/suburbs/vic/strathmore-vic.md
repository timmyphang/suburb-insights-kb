# Strathmore (VIC)

Slug: `strathmore-vic`

We’re the suburb that made the news for an illegal nosé party and our biggest local drama is whether the Urth Clay Studio has a parking spot left. Welcome to Strathmore, where the quiet life meets minor infamy.

A typical weekend morning here: you’re not brunching at some minimalist warehouse — you’re walking distance from DFO Essendon, heading to an unassuming frontage that houses Urth Clay Studio. Inside, we’re all hunched over pottery wheels, whispering about the time a “35-year-old Strathmore woman” was fined $21,808 as part of that Nadia Bartel lockdown party saga. Yes, that was us. We didn’t make the news for a rooftop bar or a bike lane — we made it for a bag of white powder and a group of women who should’ve known better. Pedestrian TV had a field day. We’ve never lived it down.

The rest of our local scene is quieter. Strathmore Flowers keeps the foyer of the IGA smelling nice. And if you’re looking for a career change, the local job board features roles at Strathmore Flowers, Rocket Comms, and BSB — basically, you can work with blooms, PR, or something mysterious. We’re not sure what BSB does either.

Who would hate it here? Anyone expecting a late-night tapas bar like Bomba (that’s in the city, mate) or a 250-bottle wine list like Etta (that’s Brunswick East). We don’t have a rooftop with DJs. We have a pottery studio and a florist. If your idea of culture is a natural wine bar with a projection of silent Spanish films, you’ll be disappointed by the lack of any film at all — unless you count the neighbour’s Ring doorbell footage.

But who would love it? People who want to throw a pot, buy a bunch of proteas, and never have their name in the paper again. We’re the suburb that learned its lesson. Come for the clay, stay for the anonymity — and maybe bring a plate for the next locals’ committee meeting.
