# Surrey Hills (VIC)

Slug: `surrey-hills-vic`

Friday night in Surrey Hills, circa 2016. You’re home, staring at a glass of cordial because that’s literally all we had. This suburb was one of Melbourne’s last dry zones – a temperance holdout so strict that getting a glass of wine required a resident vote. We didn’t have a wine bar. We had a water tower built in 1929 that looked medieval and a very long wait.

Then, in 2017, someone finally said “enough”. The Hills opened at 141 Union Road, serving pizzas alongside 200 wines by the bottle. The team behind Toorak Cellars and Milton Wine Shop walked in, hung a Julie Andrews mural on the wall, and suddenly we had a reason to leave the house after 6pm. Broadsheet called it a song the thirsty residents had been waiting to hear. They weren’t wrong.

These days we’ve got a few more spots. Zimt Patisserie for your French pastries. Acorn Nursery, which has been selling plants for 30 years and counting. Famili Ria for Indonesian from South Sumatra. Pembroke Bar & Kitchen, where Urban List described us as a “sleepy, cardigan-wearing kind of neighbourhood” – which is fair. And a cosy little cafe at 1125 Riversdale Road that tempts you in for rich coffee. Plus The Wool Mill at 1012 Riversdale Road if you feel like getting married.

The drama? Nothing gets the blood boiling like a plan for two luxury townhouses on a single block. Whirlpool threads run hot over parking, bin collection, and the audacity of anyone who dares build something new. We care deeply about our leafy streets and we will fight you over a car space.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who wants a 4am kebab, a nightclub, or a bar that doesn’t close by 11pm. You will perish of boredom.

Who would love it? People who remember when the biggest excitement was a dry-zone loophole. Families who want good schools and a nursery that’s been open since the 90s. Wine lovers who finally don’t have to drive to Balwyn Cinema for a glass. We’re cardigan-wearing, sure – but at least now the cardigan comes with a decent shiraz.
