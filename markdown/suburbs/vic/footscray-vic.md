# Footscray (VIC)

Slug: `footscray-vic`

We’re in Footscray, where the morning commute involves dodging a truck that’s just become the latest sacrifice to the Napier Street Bridge (we’re pretty sure it eats one a week for breakfast). Then you swing past Footscray Market, a brutalist temple where $3 gets you a kilo of avocados and a side of existential crisis about whether you’re still affordable enough to live here. Pop into T. Cavallaro & Sons for a ricotta cannoli that’s been perfect since 1956, because some things don’t need gentrifying.

Speaking of change: we’ve got Moon Dog Wild West in the old Franco Cozzo building, complete with a mechanical bull and 100 taps. Bland mega-pubs haven’t got a toehold here yet — we like our owner-operated joints like Back Alley Sally’s, where the parmas are as unpretentious as our politics. But we also remember when 8Bit got its windows smashed twice in one week. Twice. The stains who did it probably got a seltzer from Footscray-brewed ‘Ray — the new hoon juice.

Our suburb’s had some seedy moments: the heroin epidemic, the closure of Forges, and the tragic loss of Olympic Doughnuts (RIP, you sweet institution). But we’re still the place where Hop Nation’s former winemakers make experimental beer, and where Papelón serves Venezuelan arepas next to Ethiopian injera from Mesnoy. We’re layered with migration waves — Vietnamese, East African, Italian — and we talk in at least three languages at the fish shop.

Who’d hate it here? Anyone who needs a smooth, polished Melbourne suburb with no grit. If you flinch at a bit of industrial chic that’s still industrial, or if you think the West Gate Tunnel will fix everything (it won’t), stay east.

But if you want a suburb that owns its rough edges, where the arts centre has been pushing progressive works since 1974, and where you can still buy a house under a million? Welcome west. Just watch out for the bridge.
