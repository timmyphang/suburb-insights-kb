# Point Cook (VIC)

Slug: `point-cook-vic`

We get it — Point Cook cops a lot of flak. “Too far out.” “Too many new estates.” “That Opticomm speed is basically a Scream sequel.” But ask any local: we’re not here for your validation. We’re here for the 42.3°C record at the RAAF base, the ghost of a jilted bachelor at Point Cook Homestead, and the sheer triumph of getting a breakfast roll at Bean Smuggler before the queue hits.

Saturday morning ritual: you roll into Bean Smuggler for that Five Senses coffee and the call breakfast roll, because nothing says “we’ve made it” like a perfectly fried egg on a brioche bun in a suburb that was literally built on reclaimed swamp. Then you hit Point Cook Coastal Park for a walk, trying to spot the flood zones that Broadsheet warned us about in 2022. (Spoiler: if it rains hard, avoid Cheetham Street near the learning centre — the storm pits are still catching up.)

Petty local drama? Oh, you know it. The eternal turf war between Tarneit and Point Cook on the Whirlpool forums. “I built in Tarneit and saved $130k,” says one. “Enjoy your P‑Class slab on 800mm of infill,” we reply. Then there’s the Opticomm saga: downstream at 0.5Mbps while upstream cruises at 32 — classic Point Cook internet roulette. And if you think that’s spicy, try explaining the ghost of Thomas Chirnside to a new neighbour. He swiped his brother’s fiancée, built Werribee Mansion, and still haunts our historic homestead. Romance is alive, people — Rococo Point Cook just made Australia’s top 50 most romantic restaurants (OpenTable 2026). Nothing says amore like a $79 degustation in a shopping centre.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks “vibrant nightlife” means a nightclub open past 10pm. Also people who are scared of ghosts, hate Indian restaurant row, or refuse to drive 40 minutes to the city. And definitely anyone who panics when the SES says “flash flooding likely” and realises their house was built on an old creek bed.

But who would love it? The most multicultural suburb in Australia (2021 census: 86 countries, 70% with overseas‑born parents). Families who want Alamanda K‑9 College and Emmanuel College, playgrounds with shade, and a community centre that actually runs playgroups. First‑home buyers who don’t mind a bit of infill soil and a long commute, because you get a four‑bedroom house with a yard for the price of a studio in Fitzroy. And anyone who wants to grab a hotpot at David’s Hotpot, then catch a movie at Holey Moley (yes, we have mini‑golf and bowling — deal with it).

Point Cook is not trying to be Fitzroy. We’ve got ghosts, floods, Opticomm dropouts, and the best damn breakfast roll in the west. Welcome to the ’hood. 🌊👻🥓
