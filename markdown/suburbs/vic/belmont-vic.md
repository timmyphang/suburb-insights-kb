# Belmont (VIC)

Slug: `belmont-vic`

Ah, Belmont. The Geelong suburb that keeps getting mistaken for Auckland, Lake Macquarie, and a character in a Netflix rom-com. We’re used to it. When you tell people you’re from Belmont, they say “Oh, the one with the wetlands?” or “The one with the barber chairs from America?” No, mate. We’re the one with the bistro that used to have an Igni alum in the kitchen.

Back in the day, we had Bistrot Plume on Mount Pleasant Road — that elegant French-Australian spot where you could pair blue cheese croquettes with a 700g rib eye and pretend you were in Paris, not a 10-minute drive from the Centro shopping centre. Jackson Fort and Ben McLachlan ran the show, and we’d drop in for cured kingfish and cultured cream like it was nothing. Then there was Archive Wine Bar on High Street — Graham and Ingrid Sutherland’s 160-bottle love letter to wine, where you could get a katsu sando with a side of provolone-stuffed olives. Both were *chef’s kiss*. But as Broadsheet noted, Archive’s entry was last updated October 2023. We’re not saying time stands still in Belmont — but our main drag’s peak hour is still “when the postie delivers.”

Yes, we know what they say about us. That we’re the quiet cousin of Geelong’s flashier suburbs. That our claim to fame is a French bistro and a wine bar that feels like a secret handshake. They’re not wrong. But they also missed the bit where we have *exactly* enough to keep ourselves satisfied — and anyone who needs more can just drive up the road.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who wants a new cafe to open every month, or expects their brunch spot to have a queue of Ponsonby types out the door. We don’t do hype. We do “the same three places you’ve been going to since 2018, and they’re still good.”

Who would love it? People who appreciate a well-aged wine list, a bistro that serves duck à l’orange without irony, and a suburb that doesn’t try to be anything it’s not. Welcome to Belmont. We’re still here. Same Mount Pleasant Road. Same High Street. Come for the croquettes, stay for the lack of drama.
