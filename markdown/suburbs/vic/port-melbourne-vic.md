# Port Melbourne (VIC)

Slug: `port-melbourne-vic`

We know what they say about Port Melbourne. That we’re a former industrial port that’s been buffed, polished, and rebranded into a waterfront playground for people who drink pale ale and own dogs they dress in raincoats. And yeah, they’re not wrong. But they missed the bit where we still have the ghosts of the old Sandridge days rattling around in a 150-year-old pub.

Saturday morning in our hood: you’re weaving through prams and fetch-obsessed labradors on Sandridge Beach, then grabbing a cinnamon and cardamom bun from Lilijana Eatery on Bay Street because Noisette’s wholesale pastries are great but we like to pretend we’re Scandinavian for a few hours. Then you’ve got a decision: do you punish yourself with a bouldering session at Up Climbing in Balaclava (it’s the closest, we know), or do you head straight to The Railway Club Hotel for a rib eye that’s practically a religious experience? We choose the steak. Every time.

Petty local drama? Oh, we’ve got layers. Channel 9 bought a $5M Art Deco factory opposite North Port Oval to film The Block, because nothing says “renovation nightmare” like a suburb that used to be built on a toxic waste dump (cheers, Whirlpool forum for that little gem about the old Port Melbourne Tip under the Westgate). Meanwhile, Kickin’ Inn took over the heritage-listed Rose Hotel and now we’re ripping into mud crab with our bare hands like animals – and we love it. Even the old Bertie Beetle Factory on Bertie Street got turned into Colonial Brewing Co., which is basically a metaphor for the whole suburb: sweets replaced by hops.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks “beachside suburb” means quiet mornings and no container ships. Station Pier just reopened after five years, so now we’ve got cruise ship passengers wandering Bay Street looking confused. Also, if you don’t like dogs, good luck – Sandridge Beach is basically one giant off-leash meeting.

Who would love it? People who want a pub that’s older than federation (The Cricketers, 1871), a $3 million Timber Yard warehouse for events, and a mural by Kitt Bennett at Fisherman’s Wharf that’s the size of a football field. We’re rough around the edges, but we’ve got a $35-per-week bouldering gym, Starward Distillery, and a banh mi counter that’s actually worth the tram ride. Port Melbourne: we used to be a port. Now we’re a personality.
