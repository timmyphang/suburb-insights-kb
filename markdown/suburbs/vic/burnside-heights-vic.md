# Burnside Heights (VIC)

Slug: `burnside-heights-vic`

We’re up here in Burnside Heights, the tiny triangle wedged between Taylors Lakes and Caroline Springs that even NBN forgot. That’s right — while every other suburb got the free FTTP upgrade, our little postcode 3023 got a firm “not eligible” from the national broadband gods. Classic. But we’re used to being left off the map. Ask a cabbie and they’ll tell you they’ve never heard of us. Ask the Whirlpool forums and they’ll say avoid at all costs — “saturated with 4x2s”, “bad investment”, “public transport is limited and unreliable”, and our neighbour Caroline Springs is apparently ‘Criminal Springs’. Rude, but not entirely wrong.

Our morning routine? None of that smashed avo nonsense. We’re heading to Enelssie on Tenterfield Drive for a traditional Filipino bagnet and chicken inasal — because Anthony Herrera has been cooking his lolo’s recipes here for seven years, even doing Filipino pizzas when the mood strikes. The CBD media finally discovered him in 2025, but we’ve been deep in the sisig and sinigang since the pre-boom days. You’ll also see us at Tiny Treasures Early Learning Centre dropping the kids, then heading to Mind Blossom or Mind Psychology for “compassionate and professional support” — because living in a growth corridor with zero infrastructure will do that to a person.

The real local drama? The investment property debate. A young couple came to Whirlpool asking about buying near Taylors Road for low $400k, and the locals roasted them: “cab drivers call the next suburb Criminal Springs”, “growth is behind inflation”, “the traffic on Taylors Road is atrocious”, and a “major investing advice group” explicitly said avoid outer-ring suburbs like Burnside Heights. But hey, where else can you score a 3x2 investment for the price of a Melbourne car park? We’re optimists. We’re also the postcode that had 177 active COVID cases in 2020 — we know how to hunker down.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who needs a reliable train line, a decent coffee that isn’t from a servo, or a sense that their property value will ever outpace interest rates. Also NBN upgraders — we see you seething.

Who would love it? People who want genuine Filipino food without the Fitzroy markup, who thrive on “you get what you pay for” housing, and who don’t mind being the smallest triangle in Melbourne’s west. We’re not much, but we’re ours. And we’ve got crispy pork belly.
