# Sunshine West (VIC)

Slug: `sunshine-west-vic`

Sunshine West, VIC — where the charcoal smoke hits you before the postcode confusion does.

We wake up to the smell of rotisserie chicken drifting off Glengala Road, because Sunshine Social turned an old petrol station into our temple of coals and Israeli couscous. The owners wanted a place that “makes anyone feel at home,” which in Sunshine West means you’re just as likely to be a tradie picking up a skewer as a zero-waste warrior BYO-ing jars to the Mount Zero warehouse on Law Court. Yes, we have a quarterly bulk olive oil market. Don’t act surprised.

But let’s not pretend we’re all chic pantry cleanses. We made national news when our IGA got caught racially profiling African customers — a note on the register that we’re still embarrassed about. We were also one of the ten COVID hotspot suburbs that got the Premier’s “testing blitz” treatment, right up there with Keilor Downs and Broadmeadows. The government sent 800 testers to knock on our doors. We didn’t ask for the attention.

And if you’ve ever tried to return a dodgy eBay office chair, you’ll know our postcode is the black hole of Australian logistics. Whirlpool forums are full of people screaming “it’s been stuck in Sunshine West for days!” while their delivery bounces between here and Chirnside Park. We feel your pain. We also feel the pain of everyone whose eParcel tracking suddenly shows “Sunshine West VIC 8438” — yes, that’s the Mail Centre. We’re the sorting hub of your disappointment.

Who would hate it here? Someone expecting the shiny $2 billion Sunshine Station upgrade to have actually happened. Someone who can’t handle the smell of charcoal chicken at 9am and thinks a pocket park in Yarraville is more their vibe. Someone who reads “McGregor Court” in the homicide reports and immediately starts clutching pearls.

Who would love it? People who know that a good BBQ joint in a converted warehouse beats any sterile Fitzroy wine bar. People who bring their own containers to Mount Zero and feel virtuous. People who look at a “Suburban Testing Blitz” and think, “At least we’re getting free tests.” We don’t have a Lululemon. We have charcoal, community, and a postcode that doubles as a warehouse district. And honestly — we wouldn’t have it any other way.
