# Toorak (VIC)

Slug: `toorak-vic`

You know you’re in Toorak when the biggest local controversy isn’t a noisy pub or a car meet, but a woman allegedly driving her mangled SUV through the alley with no front tyres, airbag deployed, bumper scraping like a shopping trolley. That’s right — and it turned out she was the founder of Kookai. Legendary behaviour, honestly. As Junkee reported, she was filmed driving through a neighbouring alley with her tyres in shreds. We didn’t even bat an eyelid. That’s just a Tuesday afternoon on the way back from Cecconi’s.

Morning routine around here is simple. You start at Drugstore Espresso on Toorak Rd if you follow the herd, but the clever locals hook left on Como Avenue and end up at The Stables of Como — a new summer favourite that thinks it’s a secret but we all know. Or you hit Mabels for a killer fried chicken sandwich and a flaky pie from the Kiwi expats who run it. If you’re feeling fancier, Made in Casa just moved into Toorak Village with house-made pasta and a unique shallow-fried steak. The Grange Road Egg Shop is still poaching, scrambling and frying for the community. And if you need a wine to wash it down, 505 Wine Room on Malvern Road has a 15-page wine list and happy hour from 5.05pm. Or try Toorak Cellars in Armadale — though that’s technically not Toorak, but we claim it anyway.

Petty local drama? Besides the Kookai crash, there’s the constant debate about whether the least bogan place in Australia is Toorak or Watsons Bay (as one Whirlpool user confidently claimed). We also quietly judge anyone who thinks Toorak Rd traffic is negotiable during school pickup — Geelong Grammar and St Catherines mums don’t mess around.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who thinks “artisan” is a verb, or who believes a latte should cost less than a tank of 98. If you’re not comfortable with your neighbour’s car being worth more than your house and your other neighbour having a “wine bar” with a 15-page list — stay in Fitzroy.

Who would love it? People who genuinely enjoy fine food, fine wine, and the ability to buy a ready-to-go meal from Lafayette Fine Foods that costs more than a dinner out anywhere else. Also, people who want their kids to board from Year 7 but also walk to primary school at the Glamorgan campus. It’s a vibe. Own it.
