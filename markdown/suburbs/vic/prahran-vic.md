# Prahran (VIC)

Slug: `prahran-vic`

We’re in Prahran, where the market gets a glow-up every time we blink. Four new casual restaurants just landed in the old Mr Hoddle site — Pacho Taqueria doing birria tacos, Matsuyama Yakitori grilling kangaroo skewers over charcoal, Nouns Hamburgers bringing back the deli energy, and Fatbuoy Lobster slinging lobster rolls from seafood royalty. That’s on top of Maker & Monger’s cheese cave and Wasshoi’s sumibi yaki. Honestly, we don’t even buy produce here anymore; we just orbit the food stalls like confused seabirds.

But let’s talk about Prahran Square. One week it’s a 13-metre inflatable rock garden called Iwagumi Air Scape — ancient Japanese art meets bouncy castle. The next, it’s 20,000 bubbles per minute and a performance called “The Boys in Blue” turning the square into a dreamscape. We’re not sure if our local council is beautifying the precinct or testing how much surrealism a postcode can handle. Either way, it’s free and it beats staring at the Chapel Street traffic.

Petty drama? Back in the Greens-on-Grindr era (yes, that happened — geo-targeted ads for a Sam Hibbins event in Prahran, Windsor, South Yarra and St Kilda East), we learned that even your dating app can’t escape local politics. Also, a Nutella pop-up allegedly for charity turned out to be a $5 fee for Nutella on bread, with proceeds going to Prahran Rotary Club’s Homework Club. We paid it. We loved it. We still don’t know who to blame.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks Chapel Street is just a nicer version of Swanston Street. The pedestrians are dodging cyclists, the cyclists are dodging Teslas, and everyone is trying to find parking near Commercial Road. If you don’t like standing in a queue for cheese toasties while debating whether to try the Wagyu ragu pasta at Officina Gastronomica Italiana (formerly Officine Zero — it transformed, we moved on), this might not be your vibe.

Who would love it? Food freaks, art gawkers, and people who enjoy saying “I’m just going to the market” as a full afternoon activity. Also, anyone who wants a suburb that’s confidently weird — we’ve got a Scandi bathhouse opening, a bike shop that doubles as a gallery, and a David Bowie-shaped apartment building from 2012. We’re not perfect. But we’ve got good cheese.
