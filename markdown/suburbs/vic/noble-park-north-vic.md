# Noble Park North (VIC)

Slug: `noble-park-north-vic`

We know what you’re thinking: Noble Park North is just the bit of Noble Park that got bored and wandered off towards Rowville. And yeah, you’re not entirely wrong. But let us tell you about our suburb — a place where the biggest local news in a decade was that Jacksons Road would finally link directly with EastLink. Back in 2018, we were promised this as part of the Monash Freeway’s rise to 10 lanes. They said it’d create 600 jobs. They said it’d open in 2022. We’re still sitting at the lights wondering if we missed the memo.

This is a suburb that lives in the shadow of its southern sibling — and we mean that literally, because Noble Park has the train station. As one wise Whirlpool poster put it, “Noble Park North would be a better option than Noble Park proper. The only disadvantage is distance to the train station.” Translation: you’ll be driving. Everywhere. On that shiny new EastLink ramp that definitely exists now. We promise.

Our weekend social scene is a two-stop tour: first, a trip to Crazy Climb at 6 Elonera Rd, where your kids can scale walls while you sit on a fold-out chair and wonder when you last had a hobby. Then, if you’re really living it up, you cross the border into Noble Park proper for Lollipop’s Playland — because nothing says “date night” like a ball pit and the faint smell of lukewarm party pies.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who relies on public transport, enjoys browsing a boutique, or expects their street to have a footpath that doesn’t end abruptly at a storm drain. Also, people who think “Monash Freeway” and “peaceful” belong in the same sentence.

Who would love it? Families who’ve accepted that a backyard and a garage are worth trading for a 45-minute commute. People who think “close to EastLink” is a legitimate real estate tagline. And anyone who genuinely enjoys watching the same four kids’ birthday parties every weekend at Crazy Climb.

We’re Noble Park North — where the freeway is our main square, the train is a myth, and we’re perfectly fine being the suburb that’s “better than the original.” Come for the driveway parking, stay because you can’t get home without it.
