# Ormond (VIC)

Slug: `ormond-vic`

We know what you’re thinking: Ormond? Isn’t that just the bit of road you drive through on your way to Elwood while fighting the urge to honk at a Prius? Well, yes, but also: we’ve got a proper little scene, and it runs on caffeine and cycling.

Our Saturday morning starts at Velo Espresso, tucked inside the Velo Rapport bike workshop on North Road (entrance on Glen Ormonde, so you feel like you’re in on a secret). Chris Savage and Lisa Rush—yes, they met on a bike ride—serve Duke’s coffee and house-made scones while you wait for your chain to be fixed. There’s a blown-up photo of Chris’s dad crossing the finish line at the Melbourne to Warrnambool race in 1962, and his grandfather’s trophy from 1934. It’s basically a cycling museum you can eat in. And if you’re feeling fancy, grab a Sebby’s scroll—they’re the cinnamon scroll operation that took over from Caulfield South, and they’re worth the bike ride.

But don’t get too attached to the old guard. Common Ground, the father-daughter coffee roastery that used to be on North Road with its Probat roaster and communal table? Permanently closed. We mourn it the way you mourn a bike you sold too cheaply. The space is empty now, and we’re all pretending not to notice.

You wouldn’t last a week here if you don’t own at least a helmet. Or if you think “community” means small talk about the weather. Our community is people who bring their bikes inside cafes and argue about the best route to Point Ormond—which, by the way, is genuinely one of Melbourne’s dreamiest sunset viewpoints, a grassy reserve with a view of Elwood Beach and the skyline. Even Broadsheet said so.

Who would hate it? Someone who thinks a “group ride” is a group of friends in Lycra being smug. Someone who wants a nightlife—we’ve got a wine bar in Elwood called The Milton, but that’s technically not Ormond. We’re okay with that. We’ve got Walafel (a waffle-falafel hybrid on Ulupna Road) and Bunker Spreckels on Ormond Rd, named after a surfer who lived “pure 24/7 crazy shit.” That’s our kind of energy.

Who would love it? Cyclists, coffee snobs who appreciate a good origin story, and anyone who wants a proper local bookshop delivery—Avenue Bookstore will drop off to Ormond, same day if you order by 3pm. We’re a suburb that reads, rides, and roasts. Not a bad combo.
