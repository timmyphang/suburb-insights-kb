# East Geelong (VIC)

Slug: `east-geelong-vic`

*We’ve been called “spacious, green, and oh so liveable”* — which is real estate speak for “we’ve got a milk bar memory and a half-decent bus connection to the Gordon TAFE.”

East Geelong, you beautiful, weatherboard-wrapped paradox. You’re the suburb where a 1959 European artist built a “Parisian enchantment” showpiece (now a highly Instagrammable Airbnb called European Luxe), yet your most famous local institution is a memory of a shop called “Dave’s”. As Broadsheet noted back in 2013, artist Eamon Donnelly drove through here, saw Dave’s closed milk bar with one faded sign, and started a whole photographic archive. We’re still not sure if that’s art or just a very committed nostalgia trip.

Our morning routine? Duck into Goose at 91 Garden St — opened 1996, still selling Mesop and Elk and custom gift wrap like it’s 1999. Or head to The Local Geelong (opened 2019) for fruit toast from the woodfire oven before it turns into Enoteca at night. Yes, we have two identities before dinner. It’s fine.

Petty local drama? Try the eternal debate: do we tell tourists about the Geelong Botanic Gardens (heritage-listed, 1851) or keep them thinking we’re just a gateway to Newcomb ($609k AVM)? Concrete Playground says we’re one of Victoria’s most in-demand coastal suburbs. We’d rather you didn’t mention it — we’re still getting used to people knowing we exist.

Who’d hate it here? Anyone allergic to mid-century weatherboards, split-level layouts, and the constant suggestion to “just take the connecting bus to the Gordon campus.” Urban List called us “nestled” — which means you’ll spend a lot of time nestled in traffic on Garden St.

Who’d love it? Families who want a backyard, a milk bar memory, and the smug satisfaction of saying “we’re in-demand but we got in before the boom.” East Geelong: where your forever home renovation (thanks, Kimmy Hogan) comes with a side of faded signage and zero pretension.
