# Frankston (VIC)

Slug: `frankston-vic`

Frankston, we need to have a chat. For decades, you were the place the Mornington Peninsula pointed at and said “at least we’re not that.” The place where “Chris, please call about the baby” was spray-painted like a public service announcement. Where the main street had more machete incidents than latte art. But then something very suspicious happened: you got a glow-up.

These days, you can’t swing a chia seed without hitting a new venue. Bang Bang on Playne Street is now serving hawker-style plates and late-night DJs to 130 guests, which is 129 more than the old fish-and-chip shops ever catered for. Moon Dog Beach Club just dropped a 2,000-square-metre tropical cabana paradise on Port Phillip Bay, because apparently Frankston decided “servo pies and a windy beach” wasn’t the only personality on offer. And Humdinger? Sliders and ribs in little red plastic baskets – the kind of American food we used to drive to the city for.

But let’s not get ahead of ourselves. We still have the Frankston Sunday Market followed by eggs at Eeny Meeny, where the chalkboard out back has more sass than your auntie at Christmas. Nutrition 6 is still serving protein yoghurt to yoga mums who’ve been doing downward dog since before it was trendy. And the Sand Sculpting Championships turn the waterfront into an open-air gallery – because nothing says “we’re classy now” like a giant sand recreation of the Eiffel Tower.

Of course, the rep dies hard. The Frankston serial killer doco on Stan still gets rewatched every time someone mentions the suburb. The shopping centre machete attack from 2024 still echoes in every “is it safe there?” text from your friend in Fitzroy. And yes, we still have more anti-Muslim vigilante patrols than we’d like to admit, along with a certain politician who made national headlines for all the wrong reasons. We don’t pretend we’re perfect.

But here’s the thing: 100+ murals across the suburb, a growing arts scene at the Frankston Arts Centre designed by Daryl Jackson himself, and a beach boardwalk that’s actually lovely when the wind isn’t trying to steal your soul. We’re the weird middle child between the city and the peninsula, and we’re finally owning it.

So who would hate it here? Anyone who wants a sanitised bayside experience with zero character. Who would love it? People who see potential in a place that’s been roasted for 30 years and decided to roast back. Come for the sand sculptures, stay for the chaotic energy. Just maybe text Chris first.
