# Glenroy (VIC)

Slug: `glenroy-vic`

Glenroy, VIC — where the train gets you to the city in 25 minutes and the property price gets you there in a 30-year mortgage, or as the Whirlpool forums call us "still very sub-par suburb that's far from gentrified". Thanks, mate, we’ll take that as a compliment.

We’ve got the leafy parklands and bike trails that Time Out handpicked for their 2024 list of Melbourne’s most affordable and liveable suburbs — $815,000 median price, baby. That’s well below the citywide average, which means our lawns are just as green as Toorak’s, we just don’t have the matching letterbox. And yes, we know the internet says we’re “mostly Middle Eastern or Indian” — one Whirlpool user did, anyway. We’re also mostly families who know the value of a dollar and a curry, so take that however you like.

Back in the day, there wasn’t much to do except catch the train and hope the servo had fresh pies. These days we have the Glenroy Community Hub — a room where you can attend a playgroup and pretend you’re not still living in your parents’ house while your savings account quietly weeps. There’s also a Glenroy Hub Community Centre, which is basically the same thing but with a different door.

The only drama in Glenroy is deciding which of the two train stations to use (Glenroy or Jacana) and whether the guy selling mangoes from his ute on the corner is charging a fair price. No name-brand cafes, no smashed avo on sourdough — just the sound of the Upfield line rumbling through and the distant hope that one day they’ll extend the tram.

If you can’t handle a suburb where the biggest cultural event is a new recycling bin rollout, don’t come. But if you want a home under $850k, a decent commute, and neighbours who wave even if they’ve never met you — Glenroy will sort you out. Just don’t expect a feature in Broadsheet anytime soon.
