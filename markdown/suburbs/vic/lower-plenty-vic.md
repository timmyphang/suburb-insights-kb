# Lower Plenty (VIC)

Slug: `lower-plenty-vic`

We’re Lower Plenty, and yes, we know what they say about us. “Sleepy.” “Quiet.” “The sort of place you drive through on your way to somewhere with a train station.” And yeah, we lost our Hurstbridge line for six weeks in 2018 while they ripped out those level crossings at Grange and Lower Plenty. Hour-long delays. Buses that smelled like regret. But we survived. We’re tougher than we look.

A Saturday here starts with you realising the only real coffee option is a 15-minute drive to Mister Nguyen in Rosanna (shout-out to the Vietnamese, but technically not *ours*). Then you remember: we’ve got Stix now. Since September 2023, chef Paul Cooper–yes, actual Michelin-star pedigree–has been serving woodfired steaks and handmade pastas at the old Stix and Stones spot on Main Road. He kept the ute out front. He kept the spirit. He even does a parma and a Wagyu cheeseburger for the purists. The man breaks down whole pigs and turns the bones into sauce. That’s romance, baby.

Our local drama? Well, in August 2020, Yarra Valley Water told 88 suburbs to boil their water because storms knocked out power. Lower Plenty made the list. We boiled for days. Also, the Lower Plenty Hotel still has that indoor playground with the giant goofy shark slide. Adults love the gin gardens; kids love climbing through jaws. It’s chaos. It’s beautiful.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who wants a Sunday night train home. Anyone who thinks “hyperlocal cocktails made with Naught Gin from Eltham” is a gimmick. Anyone who can’t handle that our biggest cultural export is a band literally named Lower Plenty (yes, that jangly indie rock act from the Junkee article–they were real).

But who would love it? People who want a Michelin-trained chef cooking nose-to-tail in a “sleepy” suburb. People who understand that a ute on the restaurant’s front lawn is a design choice, not an accident. People who never need to leave Main Road because everything you actually want–parma, playground, woodfired steak–is right here. Welcome to Lower Plenty: we’re not much, but we’ve got a chef who’s cooked at Pied à Terre, and that’s more than your suburb can say.
