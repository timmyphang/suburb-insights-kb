# Balnarring (VIC)

Slug: `balnarring-vic`

We’ve been keeping a secret down here on the Western Port side, and frankly, we’re nervous you’ll find out. Balnarring: where the Mornington Peninsula goes to have a quiet wine and a lie-down.

Our Saturday morning routine? Roll out of the weatherboard beach house (400 metres from Balnarring Beach, thanks Urban List), wander up to the main strip, and queue at Dreamer — the Mornington favourite that opened a second cafe here in July 2026. Owners Ben and Melisa say they wanted to “become part of another community.” Translation: they saw how many of us spend Sunday afternoons stretching into… more Sunday. The menu’s got Turkish eggs with pomegranate labneh, toasties on Penny for Pound sourdough, and coffee from Commonfolk. The design? “Warm materials, terracotta accents” — basically our Airbnb’s Pinterest board come to life.

After that, it’s off to Quealy Winemakers at 62 Bittern-Dromana Road, where Kevin and Kathleen have been making innovative pinot grigio and friulano since 2006. The cellar door only seats six at a time, which suits us fine — we’re not keen on sharing anyway. In summer they do a Pizza Luna residency, because nothing says “Balnarring” like a less-is-more slice after a swim.

The biggest local drama? The Jackalope Hotel opened in Merricks North in 2017 and everyone still pronounces it wrong. (It’s “Jack-uh-lope,” not “Jack-a-lope.” We’re not kidding. We’ll fight you at Rare Hare.)

Who would hate it here? Anyone expecting nightlife, a train station, or a barista who doesn’t know your name by day three. Also people who think “coastal” means surf beaches — we’ve got calm Western Port bay, mate, not Bells Beach.

Who would love it? Weekenders who want to feel like locals without actually moving. Wine adventurers. Anyone who thinks a sculptural stone counter is worth a 90-minute drive. And dreamers — the kind who open cafes and actually mean it.

Come for the Dreamer, stay for the Quealy. Just don’t tell everyone.
