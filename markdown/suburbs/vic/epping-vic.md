# Epping (VIC)

Slug: `epping-vic`

Epping, VIC — where the most famous fruit and veg in the state come from a market you’re not even allowed to enter. That’s right: the Epping Wholesale Fruit and Vegetable market is our culinary crowning glory, and it’s closed to the public. So while inner-north foodies parade around with their $8 kales, we locals just nod knowingly — we know where the good stuff actually lands before it gets marked up 300% for a Brunswick flat white.

A Saturday morning in Epping: you hit Pacific Epping for the Telstra dead zone in the carpark (still no 5G upgrade, as Whirlpool noted back in 2022), then you swing past the Republic Tavern — the “Revolution of the North” — for a schooner that tastes like it was brewed in 2018, which it probably was, because we don’t get a lot of new pub energy out here. The rail line extension to South Morang? Mate, that opened six months ahead of schedule in 2018, and we’re still not over it. We tell the Mernda line commuters, “Back in our day, we had to catch a bus to the end of the line. In the rain.”

Petty drama? Try explaining to your Uber Eats driver that no, we are not the Epping in Sydney. “Just put Epping VIC in the GPS, mate. Yes, it’s the one with the wholesale market you can’t enter. No, I don’t know where the night market is — that’s the other one.”

Who would hate living here? Anyone who uses the phrase “village vibe” unironically. If you need a matcha degustation within walking distance, you’ll be disappointed. High Street runs all the way up from Northcote to Epping, but by the time it reaches us, the best restaurants are back in Reservoir.

Who would love it? Anyone who respects a suburb that doesn’t pretend to be cool. We’re the wholesale market of Melbourne — essential, unpretentious, and you can’t just waltz in. But if you know, you know. And if you don’t, that’s fine — we’ll be over here, waiting for the next rail extension overdue since 2018.
