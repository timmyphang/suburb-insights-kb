# Yarraville (VIC)

Slug: `yarraville-vic`

Yarraville - where the Sun Theatre still draws a queue for Oscar noms, and Rocco's Deli lines are so long you’ll age into a senior before getting your Rocco Roll. We’ve got Cobb Lane’s 36-hour-fermented sourdough (now also in the CBD – we knew them before they were famous), and a tiny restaurant called Navi that’s so exclusive you need a three-month wait list and a second mortgage for the $120 tasting menu. Julian Hills makes his own plates, for crying out loud. We’re not just a suburb; we’re a curated gallery of foodie pilgrimages.

But let’s talk about our little village-y drama. Eleni’s Kitchen & Bar – a family legacy since 1971 – got firebombed. Again. April 2025, a white Toyota SUV rammed into the back, set alight, fled. The Amanatidis sisters just reopened after the first arson in June 2024. They said “resilience is in my blood.” We said “yeah, and maybe install a sprinkler system for the Toyota.” But seriously – the family reopened Jimmy’s Deli next door the next day. That’s Yarraville grit: our Greek eateries bounce back faster than a flat white at Cornershop on a Sunday.

Who would hate it here? Anyone allergic to community – the main strip feels like a permanent farmers’ market where everyone knows your coffee order. If you need a 24-hour servo or a nightclub that isn’t Hotel Mexico’s rooftop DJs till 2am, jog on to the city.

Who would love it? Foodies who want to argue whether Rocco’s cannoli or Gigi’s Gelato is better. Families who want the Art Deco cinema, the village feel, and a naturopath at Heal.Thy Self Co. We’ve got it all: old-school Greek, modern fine dining, and a pub that painted itself red, white and blue for the Bulldogs. Come for the tacos, stay for the weekly newsletter on whose car got burnt. Yarraville: still charming, still dodgy, still ours.
