# Gembrook (VIC)

Slug: `gembrook-vic`

There’s a moment, just after the Puffing Billy whistle fades into the hills, when Gembrook exhales. The day-trippers have either boarded the return train or wandered two doors down to Gembrook Bakery for a lemon tart. We sit on the bench outside, watching the steam clear, and think: yep, this is our whole plot.

A Saturday morning here goes like this: you wake to the sound of a heritage steam train chugging through the Dandenong Ranges, which is charming until you realise it’s also your alarm clock. Head to the bakery — “just up from Gembrook’s Puffing Billy Railway Station, set slightly back from the main street” as Broadsheet calls it — for a no-frills pie. Then you might stroll across to The Station House, our “friendly local Italian eatery and pizza parlour” that recently got a big renno. If you’re feeling fancy, you drive five minutes up the Belgrave-Gembrook Road to Bam Bam Italian in Avonsleigh, where the pizzas use biodynamic flour from Monbulk’s Rock Paper Flour. But let’s be honest: most of us are still recovering from the Paradise Valley Hotel’s parma — the AHA’s Best Parma in Victoria, apparently. We’re not arguing.

The petty local drama? The 55-minute stopover during the Train of Lights. We’ve got 55 minutes to show you our town. That’s enough time for a coffee and a bakery visit, but not enough to explain why the Scouts Australia Gilwell Park has been here since 2012 and we still haven’t been. Also, the great debate: is the Gembrook-Lilydale trail a 72-kilometre hike or a test of marriage? According to Sitchu, it’s “beautiful” and for “all levels of fitness.” We say it’s for people who hate their knees.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who needs a nightlife beyond the Puffing Billy evening service. Anyone who thinks “Yukon gold potatoes grown in Gembrook” (yes, Pedestrian TV, that’s a real thing) is not a flex.

Who would love it? The kind of person who packs a bag for the historic train journey, disembarks at Gembrook Station, and thinks: “This is it. This is the whole weekend.” We’ll be at the bakery. Save us a Stockman’s pie.
