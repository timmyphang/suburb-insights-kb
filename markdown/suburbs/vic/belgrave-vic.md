# Belgrave (VIC)

Slug: `belgrave-vic`

Belgrave, VIC — where the train line ends and the creative eccentricity begins. We’re draped in forested hills, a rum distillery that smells like a science experiment, and a main street that somehow houses 90 local makers and a century-old Irish pub that just got bought by a hospo mogul for $4.7 million. As one local parent put it on a playgroup blog: “We moved here for cheaper rent.” That’s the Belgrave pitch — affordable housing with a side of hogo rum.

A Saturday morning here looks like this: coffee at Earthly Pleasures Cafe on Burwood Highway, then a wander through the Belgrave Emporium where Lucy and Claire saved the artisan market from closure in 2016. You’ll see Farida Iqbal’s artwork, Concrete & Clay ceramics, and vintage clothing that’s “slow fashion” because nobody’s in a rush up here. Then you mosey to Cameo Cinemas for an outdoor film under the stars — or wait, it’s 2026, so maybe you’ve already been on the Twilight Train, a three-and-a-half-hour round trip to Lakeside on a Thursday night. Because nothing says “I live in the Dandenongs” like commuting for a steam train cocktail hour.

The petty drama? It’s the Micawber Tavern, mate. For over a century it was the reliable pitstop before Puffing Billy or the 1000 Steps. Then Julien Moussi’s Only Hospitality Group snapped it up for $4.7 million, promising a seasonal menu and native plants in the beer garden. The locals are torn between “good, the schnitty was getting stale” and “great, another Collingwood-style venue in the hills.” Meanwhile, the Killik Rum brothers are quietly making Australia’s first hogo rum at the start of a Sherbrooke Forest walking track, serving cheeseburgers from 5pm. It’s the kind of place where you can taste a $16 paddle and still make it home before the Puffing Billy whistle.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks “creative eccentricity” is code for “too many fairy lights and not enough parking.” Also people who don’t like steam trains, because Puffing Billy is the town’s heartbeat and you will hear that whistle at 10am, 11am, and again at twilight. And if you’re allergic to gumtrees, mossy fern leaves, and the smell of wet forest — Belgrave is not your vibe.

But if you want affordable rent, a community where the playgroup meets in a cottage among the gumtrees at Lake Park Reserve, and a Thursday night train journey that feels like a Wes Anderson film — welcome. Just don’t tell too many people. We’ve got a reputation to maintain.
