# Wantirna (VIC)

Slug: `wantirna-vic`

Back in the day our Saturday loop in Wantirna began with the most reliable orbit: a pilgrimage to Knox Shopping Centre (you know it as Westfield Knox) at 425 Burwood Hwy, Wantirna South — a place so sprawling it doubles as our town square and indoor weather system. We used to promenade the two-level Knox City and then drift into the Knox O‑Zone pretending we lived in a lifestyle magazine.

If you wanted a low-key gem, we’d point you — nostalgically — to Crest Cafe, once touted as an under-the-radar local favourite. For a more theatrical meal, there was Korona Csárda tucked in a small lodge at the community centre off Boronia Road near the M3, where Zsolt and Eniko Vigh ran an all-you-can-eat buffet of beef goulash and nokedli and even let you BYO wine. Broadsheet used to file paeans about it; we rolled our eyes and saved room for dessert.

For those December evenings when suburbia committed fully to sparkle, Warwick Close staged a nightly light opera — inflatables, music and a walk-through display from about 8.30pm to 11pm for the month. We’d stand on the footpath, pretending the neighbours were philanthropists and not just very competitive decorators.

There was also a time when the big mall doubled as a pickleball hub — Level Up Pickleball and The Jar bringing indoor courts to the Westfield Knox crowd. And yes, Broadsheet loved to remind everyone that PwC’s liveability report once slotted Wantirna high up the list (Wantirna #4, Wantirna South #12), which made us busily pretend we’d always known we were underrated.

Who would hate living here? If you need rooftop bars on tap, endless boutique hair salons or a skyline you can see from the backyard, we’re probably not your vibe. We keep our sky low, our centres big and our Christmas lights louder than necessary.

Who would love it? Families, anyone who appreciates a proper shopping centre that doubles as a social hub, people who still believe in heavy-duty buffets, and anyone chasing a suburban evening where pick-up games and over-the-top Christmas displays feel like community. We’ll be here — quietly proud, slightly cheeky and definitely ready for another loop around the mall.
