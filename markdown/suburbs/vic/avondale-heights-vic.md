# Avondale Heights (VIC)

Slug: `avondale-heights-vic`

You’re driving down Riviera Road on a Friday morning. The houses are mustard brick, the garden hoses are tangled, and honestly? It’s completely unremarkable. Except for one tiny thing: you’re three minutes from a cannoli that your nonno would weep into his espresso over.

Welcome to Avondale Heights — where the street names read like a Mediterranean cruise (Venice Court, Capri Crescent, San Remo Drive) and the local economy is held up by three things: ciabatta, cannoli, and the single most profitable speed camera in Melbourne.

Our morning routine is a pilgrimage. Start at Impasto Forno Antico on Military Road for a prosciutto provolone panini and a loaf of ciabatta that’s been leavened for 36 hours — because we’re not hipsters, we’re not foodies, we’re not yuppies, we’re Italian. Then wander a few shopfronts to Il Porto, where Alessio and Armando Villani will tell you about their 55-hour pizza dough on a marble bench. “It’s the best surface,” they’ll say. We believe them. Then finish at Cannoli Bar, the old milk bar turned Sicilian shrine, where Carlo Mellini pipes ricotta on the spot and the elephant logo’s trunk must always be up — superstition, you know.

The petty drama? Every local knows the intersection of Canning Street and Bellavista Drive. That speed camera at the bottom of the hill has earned more than seven figures. We’ve all been caught creeping a few k’s over. The council calls it road safety. We call it a revenue stream with a view of the Maribyrnong River.

Would you hate it here? If you need a rooftop bar with avocado toast and a DJ, absolutely. If you think “diversity” means a choice between three gelato flavours, you’ll go spare. If you can’t handle a shop that closes at 3pm because the owner is at his mother’s for lunch — move along.

Who would love it? Anyone who understands that a quiet, one-storey street with a tangled hose can hide Melbourne’s best cannoli, a bakery that refuses to be trendy, and a grandparents playgroup that’s been weaving the community together for generations. We don’t need a vibe shift. We’ve got the dough.
