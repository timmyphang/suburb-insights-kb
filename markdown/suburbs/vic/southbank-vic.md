# Southbank (VIC)

Slug: `southbank-vic`

We know what you’re thinking: Southbank is just Crown Casino’s fancy foyer and a river walk where you pay $28 for a glass of prosecco that was poured three tables over. And yeah, you’re not entirely wrong. But as locals, we see the real Southbank—where the “New York end” of Melbourne (as Nathan Toleman half-joked) sits directly under the Kings Way overpass, giving more *The Sopranos* than *Sex and the City*. 

Our weekend routine? Start at DOC Southbank for a mozzarella tasting plate and a pizza that costs more than your weekly rent. Wander past the NGV, where you’ll dodge selfie sticks and kids touching Yayoi Kusama’s *Flower Obsession* (free entry, but good luck getting a timed slot). Then grab a burger at Five Guys—the first Melbourne outpost, because nothing says “cultural precinct” like American fast food. End the night at Rosetta Ristorante, pretending you’re on a Roman holiday while the traffic from the Queens Bridge Street hums outside.

The petty local drama? It’s the constant reinvention. For years we watched Marmont and Kolkata Cricket Club try to “breathe new life” into the area. Now there’s a $150 million Hannah St Hotel opening December 1, with five Mulberry Group venues (Coupette, Bar Hannah, The Carriage Lounge, Propeller Residency) all under one 62-storey tower. The team is in “mad recruitment,” which means your next barista might be a chef who burned out at Molli. And let’s not forget the “Lebo-terranean” arrival of Bar Bouni on Sturt Street, serving $34.90 bottomless Dayze—because nothing says Southbank like a bottomless deal under a freeway.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who values peace, quiet, or a genuine sense of community. The foot traffic is relentless—tourists, theatre-goers, high-rollers. Your morning coffee from The Barre (tucked under the Arts Centre spire) will be accompanied by a busker’s cover of “Hallelujah.” Plus, the construction never stops. If you hate overpriced pasta and the smell of the Yarra, steer clear.

Who would love it? Culture vultures who want the NGV, Malthouse Theatre, and ACCA all within a 10-minute stumble. Pre-theatre diners who need a quick Nobu or a Rockpool steak. And anyone who finds a strange comfort in being surrounded by glittering chandeliers and roaring concrete—welcome to the hood.
