# Middle Park (VIC)

Slug: `middle-park-vic`

Middle Park — the suburb that sits between Albert Park and St Kilda like the family’s middle child who’s just trying to keep the peace. As Urban List put it, we’re the “South’s classic middle child: sandwiched between its Grand Prix park-brother, Albert, and the grunge and glam of St Kilda.” And you know what? We own it.

Here’s your Middle Park morning: start with a coffee and a sheepish wave at the St Ali beans at Neds, the European bakery that used to be the neighbourhood’s pride — fruit loaves, almond croissants, maybe a bag of their famous olive grissini for the picnic by Albert Park Lake. Then pop into Jack the Geezer on Canterbury Road or grab a bite at Mart130, because we’re a suburb that loves a good cafe crawl. Armstrong Street was always the “village” hub, and when Middle Park European opened its doors (walnut handcrafted by a local boatbuilder, head chef with Michelin-star pedigree), we finally got the wine bar we deserved. But let’s be real — most of these places have been here for years. We’re not exactly a hot new thing.

Petty local drama? The Formula 1 Grand Prix. Every March, our peaceful leafy streets turn into a hub of fan chaos. Middle Park Hotel becomes the unofficial pit stop, with Jack Daniel’s buses and rev-heads spilling onto Canterbury Road. We love it and we hate it. The dark wood and carpets of the MPH — which Time Out once compared to the MCC Long Room — suddenly fill with people yelling at screens. The 45 beer taps get a workout. And then it’s over, and we go back to our sleepy existence.

Who would hate it here? Anyone looking for nightlife. Our pubs close early. There’s no late-night kebab run. The biggest excitement is watching someone walk a pug down Armstrong Street. Also, anyone who can’t handle the passive-aggressive kerbside parking wars during school pickup.

But who would love it? Families. Dog owners. People who think “exciting Saturday” means a pastry run and a stroll by the lake. We’re comfortable, not flashy. As Sitchu said, “comfortable rather than flashy, Middle Park is family territory first and foremost.” And we’re perfectly fine being the middle child.
