# Emerald (VIC)

Slug: `emerald-vic`

**Emerald, VIC — We Are the Real Emerald City, No Ruby Slippers Required**

Let’s be honest: our name is basically a dare. We’re Emerald, the literal town in the Dandenongs, and everyone from Broadsheet to Silicon Valley thinks “Emerald City” belongs to a four-seat chef’s table in Healesville (Emerald City restaurant, 207 Maroondah Highway — beautiful, yes, but it’s 15 minutes up the road, folks). Meanwhile, we’ve got the *real* magic: a straw-bale villa at Alkira Eco-Glamping Retreat on Kallista-Emerald Road, where you can meet cows, horses, alpacas and ducks. And gumboots are provided.

The Saturday morning routine here is a Puffing Billy pilgrimage. You’ll get stuck behind a kombi going 40km/h on the winding road to the farmer’s market, someone’s labrador hanging out the window, and you’ll still be in a good mood because the air smells like eucalyptus and tree ferns. The landscape is “part farmland, part vineyards and part eucalypt and tree-fern forest,” Broadsheet noted. They are not wrong.

Our petty local drama? The hot water debate. Whirlpool forums are absolutely feral about whether to go with an Emerald 320L heat pump or the Aquatech X8. One installer told locals “the element kicks in at -3c, so it is not required in Melbourne.” Another forum user said “Emerald E model with heating element has complaints.” This is the kind of beef that keeps the Cardinia Reservoir full.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who needs a proper nightlife, Uber Eats at 1am, or a street with more than one pub. If you think “boho dinner party” means somewhere with a velvet curtain and whisky pairings, you’re better off in Healesville. We’ve got farms, composting toilets, and a very real chance you’ll have to pull over for a wombat.

Who would love it here? You. If your idea of a perfect Friday night is a campfire at Alkira, granola from a fold-out timber breakfast bar, and arguing with strangers on Whirlpool about heat pump brands. There’s no place like home — but only if home comes with gumboots and a straw-bale yurt.
