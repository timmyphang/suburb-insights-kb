# Carlton (VIC)

Slug: `carlton-vic`

There is a specific moment every Carltontonian knows: you’re standing on Lygon Street, 11am on a Saturday, and you’ve already had three arguments about where to eat. Tiamo has been there 30 years and is still slinging parma and pasta. Jimmy Watson’s has been there since 1935 and still has that cute courtyard. But you’re not going to either because your friend read an Urban List article and now you’re pacing outside Heart Attack & Vine waiting for a porchetta sub. Classic Carlton.

Yes, we are the suburb that built an entire identity on Italian food and then decided to mix in a sake spot, a Japanese ramen shop (Hakata Gensuke, welcome to the strip), and a Chinese-inspired menu at Lagoon. We’ve got Market Lane Coffee on Faraday Street, the shyest coffee roastery hidden down a laneway at Vertue (through a petrol station carpark, obviously), and a wine list at Carlton Wine Room that runs five levels deep. Our New Year’s Eve plans? Either a cosmic disco at Johnny’s Green Room or a $300 per head dinner at Bistra. There is no middle ground.

The petty drama here is eternal: whose pasta is actually the best? D.O.C. claims authority, Scopri has Fabio Angelé’s legacy, Brunetti Classico still has white-aproned waiters weaving through the chaos, and 400 Gradi swears it’s Neapolitan. Meanwhile, the King & Godfree precinct keeps expanding — Delmonte just opened as an Italian wine room, and Garfield Pizzeria’s owner says it’s “built for the neighbourhood first.” Sure, mate, when locals can afford it after the rent hike.

Misfits: anyone who thinks Lygon Street is “authentic” or cheap. Tourists who queue for Brunetti and then complain about the noise. People who want a quiet meal and accidentally wander into a pub called The Carlton Club at Lincoln Square South.

But if you love a city that wears its European influence like a badge, that can fit a hidden laneway roastery, a 1935 bar, and a cosmic disco rooftop all in one postcode — welcome. Just bring your appetite and a willingness to argue about where the best pasta is. The answer is always “the one you didn’t pick.”
