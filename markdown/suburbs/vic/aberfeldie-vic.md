# Aberfeldie (VIC)

Slug: `aberfeldie-vic`

We know what you’re thinking. “Aberfeldie? Isn’t that just the bit of Moonee Ponds that forgot to change its name?” And fair call. But let us take you on a stroll down Holmes Road on a Saturday morning, past the Incinerator Gallery, where right now (until October 2025) you can crawl inside the Lozziwurm — a 1972 Swiss worm sculpture designed to foster “social and physical interaction”. In Aberfeldie, our public art is a giant worm that you climb inside. We’re not saying we’re deep. We’re saying we’re playful.

Then you drift to 25 Tilba Street, the Italian espresso bar from Carlo Mellini (he also runs Cannoli Bar, but skip the queue and get your cannoli fix here). Park on a milk crate out front, watch locals heading to the Maribyrnong River, and order a four-cup Bialetti moka pot straight to your table. Nomadi coffee, burnt orange chairs, a vintage telephone table. It’s like your nonna’s lounge room, if your nonna also served Bricklayer rolls with pork-and-fennel sausage.

But let’s talk drama. Back in 2018, a fictional cafe called “Calmer Coffee” appeared in the VCE English exam, with a man-bun waiter, bland coffee, and terrible service. Teen trolls immediately hit the real Calmer Cafe on Google, tanking its rating from five stars to three. Owner Tara Conron threatened legal action against the VCAA. Manager Elise Jenkins shares a surname with the fictional writer. It was a beautiful, stupid mess. The cafe offered free small coffees to Year 12 students who mentioned the exam. We’re still not sure if that was genius or madness.

Who would hate it here? Anyone expecting a happening nightlife. Our big news is that a yoga studio opened at 361A Buckley Street. We get excited about a new Cantonese spot (Benyue Kitchen, run by former Lau’s Family Kitchen chefs, at 365 Buckley Street) and a travelling playground exhibition. If you need a wine bar with a sommelier, go to North Melbourne.

Who would love it? Families who want their kids to socialise via a Swiss worm. Italians nostalgic for burnt orange. VCE students with free coffee vouchers. And anyone who thinks a cafe legal fight with the exam board is peak local character. Welcome to Aberfeldie — we’re small, we’re weird, and we have a worm sculpture that stays.
