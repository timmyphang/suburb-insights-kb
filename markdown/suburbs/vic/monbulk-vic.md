# Monbulk (VIC)

Slug: `monbulk-vic`

If you’ve ever driven through Monbulk and thought, “this is where tulips go to retire, and also where my phone signal goes to die,” welcome — you’re not wrong.

We are the suburb that makes the Dandenong Ranges feel like actual wilderness, even though there’s a bakery and a distillery nearby. We’re the kind of place where the headline news is a flower festival. The Tesselaar Tulip Festival, to be precise — over 900,000 colour explosions at 357 Monbulk Road, Silvan. As Broadsheet once noted, the only evidence spring has sprung may be your annual asthma attack, but head to the Dandenongs and it gets very real. Very Dutch. Very damp.

Speaking of damp: Joost Bakker once rolled 35,000 of our unsellable tulips into Hosier Lane. His brother from Monbulk was about to bin them, so Joost turned the laneway into a free-for-all pluck-a-tulip rave. That’s us: a suburb so generous we give away our second-best flowers to the city. The rest we keep in mist.

If you’re looking for a proper morning routine, you’d head straight for Killik Rum in Belgrave at 64 Monbulk Road — a distillery built by two brothers, Ben and Callan Pratt. It’s one of only two dedicated rum distilleries in Victoria. You can get a tasting paddle of five handcrafted drops for 16 bucks. But that’s technically Belgrave, not Monbulk. We’re just the scenic backdrop.

There’s also the Micawber Tavern at 65/71 Monbulk Road, which Julien Moussi’s Only Hospitality Group recently bought for $4.7 million. A century-old Irish pub now getting a beer garden makeover with native plants. Which means soon we’ll have a pub where the garden matches the bush, and you can drink a Guinness while surrounded by ferns.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who needs a 24-hour servo, reliable Telstra coverage, or the ability to buy a flat white after 3pm. Also: people who think “community” is a neighbourhood watch app, not an actual church craft group on a Monday arvo.

Who would love it? Flower farmers, weekend rattlers on Puffing Billy, people who want to say “I live near the tulip festival” without irony, and anyone who misses the smell of damp soil and the sound of nothing.

Monbulk: it’s not a suburb. It’s a moody mossy state of mind.
