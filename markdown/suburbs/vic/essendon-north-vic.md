# Essendon North (VIC)

Slug: `essendon-north-vic`

**Essendon North — where the tram line gives up and so do you.**

There’s a moment on the 59 tram. You’re rattling past Moonee Ponds, past the ice rink that appears for two weeks a year and makes everyone pretend they’re Torvill and Dean. Then the tracks end. And you think: right, I guess we walk now. Welcome to Essendon North — the suburb that got the short straw in the 3040 postcode lottery.

Our pride and joy is **North and Eight**, a cafe that used to be a fruit shop, then a lolly shop, and now serves a chocolate hazelnut shake called the **Northella** that is topped with a literal Nutella doughnut. It’s run by a bloke who thought he was done with hospitality but couldn’t say no to a massive empty space. As Broadsheet noted, the menu does “Korean fried chicken waffles” alongside “zucchini fritters” — because this suburb can’t decide if it’s a health kick or a heart attack, so it does both.

Back in 2019, we had a **giant ice slide** for the Moonee Valley Winter Fest. For one week in July, kids were sliding down a frozen hill on Keilor Road while parents stood there holding hot chocolates wondering if this was the peak of local entertainment. It was. It still might be.

There was a moment when someone from Sitchu called us “this new Essendon favourite bringing a touch of inner-city cool to the suburbs” at **324 Keilor Road**. We are not inner-city cool. We are “Man, Essendon’s been getting some solid venues lately” energy — which is the Melbourne food writer equivalent of “you’re trying, sweetie.”

**Who would hate it here?**
Anyone who needs a train station. We have none. The tram spits you out like a bad oyster. You live here because you couldn’t afford Essendon proper, or you wanted a big block and don’t care that your nearest rail is a 15-minute bus ride to Glenbervie.

**Who would love it here?**
People who understand that the Northella is not a drink, it’s a personality trait. And anyone who remembers the ice slide and thinks, “yeah, that was a good winter.” We’re not trying to be Fitzroy. We’re just the place where the fruit shop became a lolly shop became your Saturday brunch spot. That’s the story. And we’re sticking to it.
