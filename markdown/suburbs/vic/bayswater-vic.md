# Bayswater (VIC)

Slug: `bayswater-vic`

Bayswater, VIC — where the industrial estate meets a three-day croissant. We’re not a suburb, we’re a mood board of contradictions: a dodgy mechanic on one corner, a Swedish-named bakery on the other, and a pho joint that’s been holding Mountain Highway together since before your moccasins were cool.

Your Saturday morning routine: line up at Drom Bakery at dawn with 1200 other dreamers (literally, that’s how many pastries they churn out daily). You’ll wait behind a ute from the Keith Fagg Tiles carpark — except Keith Fagg is now a patchwork quilting store, because that’s Bayswater: we replace tile shops with craft rooms. You grab a crème brûlée croissant made over three days, then walk past the dodgy automatic transmission mechanic that one Whirlpool forum user called “unprofessional, dishonest”. We know who you are.

Petty local drama: remember when Yarra Valley Water told 88 suburbs to boil their tap water after a storm? Bayswater was on the list. We don’t get our own scandal — we share it with 87 other suburbs like a group project nobody wanted. And our most famous attraction? Thiên Long Restaurant, where the pho is so authentic you’ll forget you’re in a strip of industrial warehouses and not Springvale.

Who would hate it here? Anyone expecting a village green. Our “town centre” is an industrial estate with a cafe that has an Alice in Wonderland theme. The Hatter and the Hare is genuinely charming — a family operation that started in mum’s garage, now serving Knave of Heart’s Treat with pistachio fairy floss in an old warehouse. But if you want a park bench, good luck. We’ve got concrete, smokers, and four 4000-litre woodfired barbeques that Big Don’s once promised would bring “unmatched theatre” to Bayswater’s industrial area. (We’re still waiting for the show, Don.)

Who would love it? The kind of person who sees beauty in a former billiard hall turned wine bar, who appreciates a three-day croissant as a lifestyle choice, and who understands that sometimes the best pho in Melbourne is served on a Mountain Highway next to a hydraulic filter shop. Bayswater isn’t trying to be the next Fitzroy. It’s just trying to get its plumbing sorted. And honestly? That’s kind of our charm.
