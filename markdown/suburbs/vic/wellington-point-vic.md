# Wellington Point (VIC)

Slug: `wellington-point-vic`

Sunday morning in Wellington Point: you’re queuing at Citron for a spritz and a Darvella pastry while pretending the polished concrete floors don’t make every footstep echo like a council meeting. The Mediterranean interior screams “I’ve been to Greece once,” but we forgive it because the Lost Race coffee is legit and the arched doorway practically seduces you off Main Road.

Yes, we’re the bayside suburb Urban List called “more of a reserve with water on both sides than a beach.” But we’ve got King Island — a mangrove-ringed islet you can walk to at low tide if you time your sand-flat dash between the incoming tide and your dignity. It’s our version of a tropical holiday, minus the passport and plus a high chance of getting your thongs stuck in mud.

Our food scene punches above its weight for a Redlands pocket. Citron’s all-day Mediterranean bistro, Manny’s Italian with its wood-fired pizza and duck confit ravioli, Jaipur Palace — dubbed “Brisbane’s oldest Indian institution” by Time Out — and Treats & Co’s dessert slingers who finally parked their pop-up trailer permanently. Even Acai Brothers Superfood Bar set up shop on Old Cleveland Road. As Broadsheet noted, Giorgina Venzin called Wellington Point “the next bayside precinct.” We’re not sure if that’s a compliment or a threat.

Petty local drama? Remember when two blokes from here (and a mate from Sheldon) got caught drinking and vaping mid-flight on a Virgin plane? AFP fined them, and we all quietly nodded — because that’s the most excitement Main Road has seen since the poinciana tree dropped its leaves. We’re a Social Mums Club territory, okay? Wild nights involve a Manny’s takeaway and a King Island sunset.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks “bayside” means Miami. We’re sleepy. The train takes you to Cleveland, not South Bank. If you need late-night kebab shops or a resident DJ at brunch, move along.

Who would love it? Families with prams, dogs named after Greek islands, and people who understand that a 20-bottle wine list “easy on the wallet” is the closest we’ll ever get to a Michelin star. Wellington Point: where the biggest decision is Citron or Jaipur Palace, and the walk to King Island justifies the extra helpings. Come for the still-water views, stay for the smugness of telling city mates you’re “beachside” without specifying the reserve situation.
