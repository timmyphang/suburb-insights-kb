# Mont Albert (VIC)

Slug: `mont-albert-vic`

You know you’re in Mont Albert when the 109 tram rattles past a former mechanic’s workshop that now serves Axil coffee and a brioche-croissant hybrid called a “briont.” Yes, we have a coffee menu that requires a glossary. Yes, our dog gets a dedicated section on the menu at Petite By Matilda — “toasted scraps” and a puppy-chino. The locals nod approvingly as their Staffies sip beside them under a sky-blue facade that screams “I’m cosy, not try-hard.”

But let’s talk about the real drama: the Cosentino siblings. Four of them. One deli. Five hundred metres from mum and dad’s place in Mont Albert. They opened Via Porta in 2019, and it’s so Italian you half expect Nonna to pop out from behind the flagstone floor and smack you with a wooden spoon for ordering a flat white. Jonathan, Simon, Sarah, Ryan — they’ve turned a family pantry into a multi-venue empire, with pastries shipped to Brunswick and a seafood lasagne partnership with The Fishmonger’s Son. Meanwhile, Mister + Miss is hanging on with its emerald-green hubcap tiles and a former hoist replaced by a mountain-ash table, because nothing says “we’re overpriced” like paying homage to a Jaguar.

The property market here is bonkers. Back in 2020, median house prices lifted a whopping $1,815 each day. That’s not a typo. Your morning coffee at Via Porta costs less than the equity gain you just missed because you stopped to admire the egg-blue facade.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who doesn’t own a pair of linen trousers or a rescue greyhound. Anyone who thinks a “paint and sip” class in a 100-year-old former steel factory is just wine with extra steps. And definitely anyone who can’t handle a cafe that serves crab spaghetti and then asks if you want to take home some ricotta and spinach cannelloni because the deli fridge has 50 Australian and Italian cheeses on standby.

Who would love it? You. If you want to live in a postcard of leafiness where the biggest local controversy is whether the “briont” is superior to a cronut. If you want to feel like you’re in Paris but with better trams and worse weather. And if you’re okay with your suburb being so small that the main street is basically Whitehorse Road and the biggest event is a new pastry shipment. Welcome to Mont Albert. Bring your appetite and your willingness to pay $10 for a single-of-six cake. You’ll fit right in.
