# Collingwood (VIC)

Slug: `collingwood-vic`

Collingwood is what happens when a light-industrial suburb hits its thirties, buys a fixer-upper and decides it’s now into natural wine and Pilates. We’re Smith Street’s gritty older sibling — the one who still has the Keith Haring mural on Johnston Street but also has a bloke from the 80s leaning on the Tote’s bar telling you about the time he saw Nirvana there. Yes, we’ve got Proud Mary and Acoffee now, but we’ve also got the Gasometer and the Peel, so don’t get too fancy.

A Saturday here goes like this: you queue at Cibi for Meg Tanaka’s Ora King salmon breakfast, then wander the grocers at Alimentari wondering if you can afford a $40 cheese. You pop into Spares for a Carhartt beanie you don’t need, then get a beer at Stomping Ground Brewing Co. because it’s socially acceptable at 11am. By afternoon you’re at Sense of Self bathhouse trying to sweat out the existential dread of your rent, and by evening you’re debating whether to hit Goldy’s Tavern for the knockout pool comp or Above Board for a $24 cocktail in a room that seats 16 people max.

The drama? Someone stole a chunk of the Keith Haring mural in the 90s — returned it anonymously, thank god. Meatball & Wine Bar on Smith Street is no more, which broke a few hearts. And the Collingwood footy club? Yeah, we know. Even the suburb’s own president once made a joke that aged worse than a flat beer. But we’re still wearing the black and white.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks “industrial charm” is an oxymoron. If you need a footpath wider than a cheese board, you’re out. If you want a suburb where the ratio of vintage furniture stores to hardware stores is even, go somewhere else. The trucks on Rokeby Street will wake you up at 5am, and the “punk-rock attitude” is both a selling point and a warning.

Who would love it? The person who wants a pub that does live doom metal on a Tuesday, a Japanese cafe that’s been doing wabi-sabi since 2008, and a bar called The Moon where the wine list is longer than the menu. This is a suburb that’s somehow holding onto its soul while developers build apartments that block out the sun. It’s rough, it’s loud, and the stickers on the Tote’s toilet door have been there since 1994. And we wouldn’t have it any other way.
