# Hamlyn Heights (VIC)

Slug: `hamlyn-heights-vic`

**📍 Hamlyn Heights, VIC — where the coffee is excellent but the cultural tension is… well, let’s just say the local playgroup could write a Netflix series.**

Look, we love our little pocket of Geelong. But let’s be real — Hamlyn Heights is the suburb that can’t decide if it’s a quiet family haven or a scene of petty local drama worthy of its own doco.

**The morning ritual:** It starts at Eddy and Wills on Church Street. A former butcher shop that now serves Code Black coffee and eggs Benedict with potato rosti that’s “too popular to ditch.” The walls are plastered with old newspapers found in the roof — including a special edition on the Queen’s coronation, donated by co-owner Matthew Colman’s grandma Doreen. Very cute, very heritage, very Hamlyn.

**The petty local drama:** Our data reveals that in September 2017, local parents Sally and Ben Sanderson threw a baby shower that ended with Sally experiencing what she *thought* were Braxton Hicks contractions. The article headline? “History made as Kit comes first.” We don’t know what happened next, but we can only assume the entire street is still talking about whether she actually went into labour or it was just a bad cheese platter.

**Who would HATE it here:** Anyone expecting a bustling nightlife or a train station. Hamlyn Heights is *residential quiet* — the kind of quiet where your most exciting Tuesday is deciding whether to go to Bell Post Hill Playgroup or Bethany Kindergarten. Also: anyone who doesn’t like the smell of freshly renovated butcher shop floors.

**Who would LOVE it:** Families who value a homey cafe, a good playgroup (call 0418 365 903, we dare you), and a community where the biggest news is a baby shower that *might* have been a false alarm. Also: anyone who wants to live in Geelong but with exactly 30% more “we found old newspapers in the ceiling” energy.

**Bottom line:** Hamlyn Heights is for people who want their coffee strong, their history quirky, and their drama mild enough to discuss at playgroup without needing a therapist. We’re not boring — we’re *strategically uneventful*.
