# Albert Park (VIC)

Slug: `albert-park-vic`

We know what you’re thinking: Albert Park is where you go to run a perfect 5km loop around the lake while pretending you’re an F1 driver, then buy a $12 almond croissant from Bread Club on Cardigan Place and feel morally superior to everyone in the inner west.

And yeah, that’s pretty much the gig.

Sunday morning starts with a queue at Bread Club — French bakers Tim and Brice crossed the river from North Melbourne and now we get flaky viennoiseries and “Bonjour” tiled at the entrance, which is aggressively charming. If that’s too busy, you’re ducking into Ned’s for a chicken sandwich with walnuts and toasted coconut, or grabbing a Portuguese tart from that long display cabinet. The real power move? A “Proper Roast” at the Bleakhouse with a view of Port Phillip Bay, because nothing says inner-south prestige like eating a $30 roast while watching a cargo ship.

But let’s talk about the elephant in the park. For six months of the year (December to May, according to the locals on Whirlpool), the Albert Park Grand Prix circuit takes over like an uninvited relative who sleeps on the couch. The notice boards go up, the barriers appear, and suddenly your scenic lake walk is a 5.3km lap with Gatorade stations and 465,000 people. We cop it — the noise, the road closures, the tourists asking where the paddock is while you’re just trying to get to the Albert Park Hotel for a Peking duck in the Happy Valley dining room. The APH got a massive renovation in 2020, and now there’s a four-storey glass atrium, a cocktail lounge with Szechuan bitters, and a fig tree growing inside. It’s a pub that used to be 137 years old and now feels like a Hong Kong racecourse Instagram filter. We love it.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who doesn’t own a pair of Lululemon shorts and isn’t prepared to explain why they weren’t at the F1. Also, people who want a cheap house. A three-bedroom townhouse on the border of Port Melbourne and Albert Park was $1.8m in 2017 — good luck now.

Who would love it? The kind of local who does the 5km lake loop, waves at the Peter Norman statue at Lakeside Stadium, then settles into Wally’s — now evolved from wine bar to restaurant with a produce-driven menu — and watches the skyline from a leafy terrace. We’re prestige with parkland calm, just 15 minutes from the city by tram. We run on pastries and pit stops. And we’re fine with that.
