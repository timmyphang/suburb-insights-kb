# Niddrie (VIC)

Slug: `niddrie-vic`

Niddrie: Where we brunch in a hole in the ground and call it culture.  

You know you’re from Niddrie when your local lake was once a basalt quarry that paved half of Melbourne’s roads. Yes, we’ve turned a hole in the earth into “Valley Lake” – and yes, we’re very proud of it. Development Victoria bought it in 2000, spent years building houses, and finally delivered the cafe they promised back when you signed your first mortgage. Perry’s? Peregrine Social Club? Whatever you call it, it’s our lakeside redemption arc. The owners also run Holmes Hall in Moonee Ponds, so expect the same vibe but with a doggy drink station and miniature boat races they keep threatening to actually hold.  

Our Saturday morning loop: grab a wattleseed iced coffee at Perry’s, then trundle down Hoffmans Road to Mister Hoffman – a pink-and-green wonderland that won a World Interiors News award in 2016. Petra Parker’s apple cake is still the only thing that gets the kids out of bed. If you’re after a sandwich, Charlie’s is slinging some of Melbourne’s best, big and bold. We don’t brag, but we do queue.  

But here’s the thing: for years, Keilor Road was our claim to food fame. Then Aberfeldie – a suburb so small it’s basically a roundabout – opened Benyue Kitchen (chefs from Lau’s Family Kitchen) and immediately stole our thunder. Broadsheet literally said Niddrie’s food strip was “overshadowed.” We’re not bitter, but we’re also not over it.  

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks “nightlife” means anything past 4pm on a weekday. Perry’s locks up at 4pm Monday to Thursday. On weekends it stays open till 9pm – that’s considered a big night. Also, don’t move here expecting a train station. We have a bus. It’s fine.  

Who would love it? Families who want a lake, a cafe, and zero pretension. We’re the suburb that was always the quiet cousin of Moonee Ponds – and we like it that way. Come for the brunch, stay because you can’t find parking on Puckle Street anyway.
