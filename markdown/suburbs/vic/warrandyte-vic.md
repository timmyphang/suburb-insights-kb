# Warrandyte (VIC)

Slug: `warrandyte-vic`

**WARRANDYTE, VIC**

You know that friend who's always saying "I just need to get out of the city for the weekend" but never actually does? We are that friend, except we actually moved here and now we can't stop telling everyone about it.

Saturday morning in Warrandyte: you're at **Field Day** on **2A Webb Street** — the cafe that finally gave this suburb a proper reason to leave the house before 11am. Lewis and Ali, ex-Allpress Coffee legends, are serving warm baguettes with lamb shoulder and confit garlic, while you pretend to read a book but actually just stare at the Yarra through the window. The locals keep telling them "this is exactly what Warrandyte needed" — which is nice, but also a bit of a backhanded compliment when you think about it.

In summer, every Melburnian who owns a li-lo and a sense of entitlement descends on **Pound Bend Reserve** in **Warrandyte State Park**. As Broadsheet noted, it's "the closest swimming spot to the city" — 28 kilometres, 40 minutes, and one near-heart attack trying to park. The Yarra runs in a loop so you can float down and end up back where you started. Clever, unless you account for the fact that the man-made tunnel is "dangerous and should be avoided" — which feels like the most Warrandyte warning possible. "Here's a beautiful swimming hole, but also there's a death tunnel."

Speaking of danger: **Pound Bend** has had "quite a few water rescues and drowning incidents," according to Concrete Playground. So if you see someone floating downstream on a blow-up unicorn looking serene, they're either having the best day of their life or about to become a cautionary tale.

The people who would HATE it here: anyone who needs a proper supermarket that isn't 15 minutes away. Anyone who thinks "artisan pottery shop" is a euphemism for "I'll be broke by lunch." Anyone who looks at bushfire risk maps before signing a lease — which, let's be honest, should be all of us.

But you'd LOVE it here if: you have a car, a kayak, a dog, and a willingness to pretend the 40-minute drive to the CBD is "not that bad." You like swimming holes that come with a side of mild existential risk. You want to live somewhere that feels like a country town but technically still has a Coles delivery option.

As the Urban List put it — Warrandyte is "a suburb most Melburnians couldn't plot on a Melways." And honestly? We're fine with that. Keeps the tourists out. Mostly.
