# Melbourne (VIC)

Slug: `melbourne-vic`

Ah, Melbourne — or as we call it, the city that’s been Google-searching itself since 2015 to find out if our laneway coffee is still “the best in the world.” We’re the people who’ll spend $35 on a Comedy Festival ticket, then complain there’s nowhere to park a fixie. We live for Broadsheet’s Hot List and Urban List’s “Best Oysters for 2026” (Maison Batard? Legend has it they shuck with attitude). We do Open House Melbourne weekend and pretend we’re architecture critics while squeezing through a 2m-wide Fitzroy terrace.  

The drama? Tom Gleeson roasted the absolute crap out of us on *The Weekly* — and fair call. We’re the city that bounced back from COVID restrictions by reopening at 11:59pm on a Tuesday and immediately queued for a flat white at that cult coffee spot in Cremorne that’s gone full sci-fi. We love a list. Concrete Playground has a guide for everything, from Sunday roasts to “after-dark adventures” that somehow still involve dumplings at 2am in Chinatown. We’re so proud of our “world’s most liveable city” status that we’ll fight you, but also admit that the running rankings from Time Out are a bit much.  

Who’d hate it here? Anyone who doesn’t want a second opinion on their brunch order, anyone who thinks a city can have too many rooftop champagne pools (lookin’ at you, FINNS Beach Club — yes we know you’re in Bali, we’re just jealous), and anyone who can’t handle the existential crisis of choosing between ten “best” bookstores.  

Who’d love it? The person who wakes up at 6am to snag an UberAir from the CBD to the airport in five minutes (still waiting on that one, 2020 dreams). The bibliophile who’s made a pilgrimage to every one of those 13 bookstores. The romantic who’s tried all 19 romantic restaurants. And the local who knows that behind every “hidden gem” from Sitchu is a queue of people filming it for Instagram. We’re a walking Broadsheet Dine Out festival — sweary, obsessed, and still pretending we’re cooler than Sydney. Come for the food, stay for the four seasons in one day.
