# Berwick (VIC)

Slug: `berwick-vic`

There’s a moment in Berwick on a Saturday morning when you realise the city has forgotten we exist – and honestly? We’re fine with that. The Pakenham line still rattles past Clyde Road every ten minutes, but who cares? We’re at **Do You Even Yoga** for a $10 Vinyasa class with max eight students, because even our yoga studio knows we hate queuing. After that, it’s a quick stop at **Blanc Bakery** for a sourdough loaf – the same one that ends up inside a fancy sandwich at Fannys Sannys in Prahran, but we get it fresh, thanks.

By lunchtime the family convoy rolls into **Two Fat Indians**, that cheeky homage to brothers Kush and Michael who’ve been serving butter chicken since 2006. You want experimental? Go for the sour lime chicken. You want to watch the kids run feral? That’s what the **Berwick Spring Hotel’s Kids Planet** is for – a warehouse-sized indoor playground Urban List called “Melbourne’s answer to KidZania,” which is code for “you can drink a pint while your child disappears into a foam pit for 90 minutes.”

Yes, we know what they say: Berwick is a sprawling family factory. Forty-five thousand people and growing, with a main street that Time Out describes as having “a number of cafes, restaurants and shops” – the most aggressive compliment they could muster. But we’re the suburb that finally got level crossing removals at Clyde Road and Cardinia Road (after years of boom-gate rage), and we’ve got **Clover Cottage Estate** reopening after a decade of silence, ready to bring old-school garden dining back. Plus, there’s **Wilson Botanic Park** if you need actual greenery between the housing estates.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks “nightlife” means a jazz den down a laneway. Our idea of a big Friday night is arguing about P-class soil in Whirlpool forums or spotting a **lobster walking down the street** after a storm – yes, that happened. We don’t make it up.

Who would love it? Families who want space, schools, the **Akoonah Park markets** with 350 stalls, and a pub where the kids can play until 8pm. We’ve got the self-possession of a suburb that knows exactly what it is: a little bit bogan, a little bit proud, and completely unbothered by your inner-city smugness.
