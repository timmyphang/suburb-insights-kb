# Mount Duneed (VIC)

Slug: `mount-duneed-vic`

Back in the day — and by that we mean the archives, because there aren’t any recent write-ups — Mount Duneed quietly became the place the festivals piled into when the coast got dramatic. We remember the morning after a big one: sunburned tents, glitter in the gutters and the Mt Duneed Estate sign on 65 Pettavel Road looking like it’d had a very loud weekend.

Our weekend routine is modest. We do the Mount Duneed Primary school drop-off, cruise past titled lots on Buffalo Circuit (Lot 117, if you’re window-shopping for a new life), then swing by Armstrong Creek shopping centre for whatever mum-and-dad errands look like in suburbia. For a minute you forget you live next to a venue that’s hosted Falls Festival and A Day on the Green — and has had The Chemical Brothers roll through (they played Mt Duneed Estate on March 2, 2024, according to the listings). Fatboy Slim was even listed for a 2025 stop there. Fancy that: barnyard to rave in a heartbeat.

There isn’t much local drama in the files — just the delightful contradiction that our sleepy slopes can turn into a national concert ground. One minute it’s titled lots and building guidelines, the next it’s a venue for huge acts or RNB Vine Days. People complain about traffic after shows, but the data doesn’t give us a soap opera, so we’ll leave the theatrics to the stagehands.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe surprise crowds, impromptu music festivals and the idea that your “quiet estate” can be rented out as a big outdoors gig, Mount Duneed will test your patience. If you’re allergic to the sight of a repurposed aircraft hangar, we’ll remind you Armstrong Barbecue used to be described as set inside one near Armstrong Creek — very industrial-chic, very local lore.

Who would love it here? Families stacking new builds on Buffalo Circuit, festivalgoers who want to be first in for the next A Day on the Green, and anyone who enjoys living in a place that quietly doubles as Geelong’s event playground. We’re small, a touch sleepy, and occasionally very loud — and honestly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
