# Northcote (VIC)

Slug: `northcote-vic`

You know you’re in Northcote when someone affectionately calls the local pool “Narc” and you don’t flinch. Yes, the Northcote Aquatic & Recreation Centre just copped an 18-month, 6-star Green Star facelift, and now we’ve got a 50-metre heated outdoor pool, a nature-based splash pad, and reformers Pilates overlooking lane swimmers. Nothing says “inner-north lefty” like doing a hamstring curl while watching a 10-year-old do laps in the spa. As Darebin mayor Julie Williams said, it’s for everyone — including the bloke reading James Joyce at Buck Mulligan’s before he pops over for a khachapuri at All Are Welcome, the bakery that used to be a Christian Science reading room. The pews are still there. The God is now a seeded rye sourdough.

We’ve got Community Wine House hiding behind a roller door on Beavers Road, attached to Matt and Leni Purbrick’s Minimum Wines operation where you can order tinned Spanish seafood and a local cheese plate like you’re in a European alley. No kitchen. Just vibes. Meanwhile, Seoul Soul is doing Korean BBQ on High Street, and Shoku Iku is out there with raw vegan organic energy. It’s a suburb where you can start your day with a Michelin-trained pastry chef’s medovnik and end it watching an Irish whiskey-themed bookshop bar pour you a dram named after a character from *Ulysses*. Buck Mulligan? More like Buck Mortgage.

And yes, we remember #Hipstergeddon. Back in 2013, a blackout hit Brunswick West and Northcote for six hours, and the internet lost its collective mind. Pedestrian TV called it “a positively middle age-like lifestyle.” We just called it a chance to finally talk to our neighbours. The High Noon festival tried to bring us all together in 2011 with bicycle valets and olive curing demos, but let’s be real — we’re still recovering from the demise of High Vibes in 2009.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who doesn’t want to discuss the depth of discharge on their GoodWe battery while queuing for a San Francisco sourdough. This is a suburb where the Whirlpool threads get deep. If you’re not ready to be a Greens-voting, warehouse-sale-queuing, water-ballet-watching (yes, The Clams performed at Narc) local, maybe stick to the car-dependent outer rings.

Who would love it? The person who sees a cream brick former reading room and thinks “this would make an excellent bakery.” The person who knows that the coolest street in the world (according to Time Out) is probably in walking distance. The person who’s fine with their suburb being a “cultural and creative hub” that still looks like it could be in a David Wadelton photo — a bit rough, a bit perfect. Northcote: we’re not gentrified. We’re just pre-gentrified, with a wine bar in the winery. And we like it that way.
