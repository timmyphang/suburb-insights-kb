# Thornbury (VIC)

Slug: `thornbury-vic`

**Thornbury: Where a 1920s Petrol Station Became Your Friday Night**

Let’s be honest: Thornbury is what happens when Northcote gets priced out, grows a beard, and starts a podcast about fermentation. We’re a suburb defined by transformation — where a former Morris Minor factory became **Welcome to Thornbury**, the giant beer garden that feeds 700 people per night ramen from a truck while they sip “iced-tea moonshine” called Trailer Park Tea. Classy.

Our cultural hub? A 1920s drive-through petrol station — **Thornbury Picture House**. Gus Berger stripped decades of shoddy paint to reveal original “motor spirit” signs, then filled the place with 1930s cinema seats he tracked down from the old Carlton Movie House. Now we watch Agnes Varda over polenta chips from Umberto. Real north-side energy.

The latest drama? A jazz bar called **Jacket**, designed not from blueprints but from a playlist. Tamer Taşkaya made a moody mix, Studio Heck designed the wood panelling based on “rhythm and tempo”, and now we have flamenco nights with the Senes Flamenco Trio. This is what happens when you give musicians a budget.

**Who would hate it here?** Anyone who still calls High Street “sketchy” and misses when Thornbury was just a cheap rent stop between Preston and Northcote. If you want your cinema seats to match and your beer garden to serve something less chaotic than nugget-donut hybrids, maybe try Camberwell.

**Who would love it?** The person who needs a 57-seat cinema, a food truck festival, a fromagerie at 666 High Street (yes, that’s the address), and a bar called **Nasty’s** that’s actually “nice as pie.” We’re a suburb built on reclaimed materials, rotating trucks, and the profound belief that a former petrol station makes a perfectly fine date night venue. Come for the **Juju’s** life-changing focaccia — stay because you can’t afford Northcote anymore.
