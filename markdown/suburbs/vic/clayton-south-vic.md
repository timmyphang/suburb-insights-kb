# Clayton South (VIC)

Slug: `clayton-south-vic`

Clayton South, VIC — there’s a reason your GPS forgets we exist. Sandwiched between the Monash Freeway roar and the train line that never stops, we’re the suburb where the biggest local celebrity is a Subaru mechanic and the main attraction is a warehouse full of board games you’ll never finish.

Yes, we have Gameology — Australia’s largest gaming warehouse. On a Saturday arvo you’ll find grown adults arguing over whether Catan is valid post-breakfast, while the kids stack Pokémon card booster boxes into towers. This is our equivalent of a nightclub. The only thing that gets serviced more reliably than your Subaru at Suba Bits is the rumour that they might one day expand their opening hours.

Speaking of Christmas: head to 4 Yalta Court if you want festive chaos. As sweet and charming as always, the display goes beyond just a typical glittering facade — there’s reindeer, trees and small hidden gems that make you question whether the owners have a second job in lighting logistics. We don’t have a town square, but we’ve got a cul-de-sac that goes harder than Myer’s window.

The community knows its priorities: Our Place Westall at Westall Primary School is the real beating heart. Co-located with the Kingston City Council hub, it’s where families go for playgroups, not smashed avo. You want a $15 all-you-can-eat Sri Lankan buffet? That’s in Fitzroy North, mate. We get the 7-Eleven on Centre Road and a Subi mechanic who’s been recommended on Whirlpool.

Who would hate it here? Anyone looking for a nightlife scene, a brunch spot with pastries, or a gym that doesn’t double as a storage unit. If your ideal weekend involves art galleries and oat milk, you’ll be on the 631 bus to Chadstone within a week.

Who would love it? People who understand that suburbia is a state of mind. Board game nerds, Subaru enthusiasts, and families who want to drive five minutes to see Christmas lights without queuing. We’re not flashy, but we’ve got a massive warehouse full of Catan expansions and a mechanic who knows his way around a boxer engine. What more do you need?
