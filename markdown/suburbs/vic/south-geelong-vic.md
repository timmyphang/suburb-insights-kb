# South Geelong (VIC)

Slug: `south-geelong-vic`

We used to wake up on a Saturday in South Geelong and feel like we’d accidentally stepped into someone’s very tasteful industrial Pinterest board. Back in the day we’d amble past the repurposed wool mill where Little Creatures made the place feel like a brewery-themed art installation (there was even a cinema, a canteen and a pizzeria, if you were the indecisive sort). A few blocks over, White Rabbit’s Barrel Hall was where the experimental sibling brewed the things that made our uncle pretend he knew about “barrel-aged ales.”

Weekends were for markets — the South Geelong Farmers Market on the first and third Sundays, the Geelong Waterfront Makers & Growers Market lining Steampacket Gardens, and Simon Toohey’s Geelong City Market on Little Malop Street that promised to be the regular food-and-produce destination we told people we ‘discovered’. Rolling Pin Pies appeared in the notes about our pastry prowess (they even had that award-winning surf’n’turf pie in 2021). And if you wanted your Thai smoky and theatrical, Songbird was the place that got a mention for cooking over red gum and charcoal.

Then there was the neon pink oddity: Club Chin Chin — Chris Lucas’s 200-seat pop-up in the GMHBA Stadium grandstand — which opened as a 12-month experiment and made us question whether stadium food could also be very loud and very Instagrammable.

Petty local drama? If “pop-up in a stadium” counts as cultural whiplash, we’ve had enough. Otherwise our rows were mostly about whose sourdough was superior and whether the Bellarine Rail Trail really was a cyclist’s dream.

Who would hate it here: people who covet quiet cul-de-sacs with no fermentation, no markets, and no weird neon in a sports arena. If you can’t tolerate brewery tours, barrel-aged experiments, or occasional smoke from creative kitchens, you’d wither.

Who would love it: first-home buyers looking for Victorian charm and accessibility to Melbourne, food obsessives who liked to tick off bakeries and distilleries (yes, Bellarine Spirits had its moment), cyclists who treated the Bellarine Rail Trail like a secular pilgrimage, and families who relied on local playgroups, Leisure Networks events on Verner St and kids’ classes at the Bellarine Sewing Centre. We may be a little rough around the edges — but we’ve always been deliciously useful.
