# Brunswick West (VIC)

Slug: `brunswick-west-vic`

We know what they say about us. “Brunswick West? Isn’t that just the bit of Brunswick you drive through to get to something better?”

And yeah, we copped a pizzling during COVID. When the concrete playground called us a “hotspot” and the memes started flying, our 3055 postcode became shorthand for “stay the hell away.” But you know what? We also got mobile testing vans from Time Out. We got *attention*. That’s more than Brunswick East ever got.

Back in the day, when Lankan Tucker was still on Albion Street (2016–2024, rest in peace biryani burrito you legend), you’d find us queuing for Sri Lankan food that actually had soul. Over at Lolo & Wren, we pioneered the “early arrival” café scene, bringing 5 Senses coffee to a suburb that previously only knew instant. Postmistress turned a former post office on Melville Road into rustic Italian with wood-fired pizza, and we all felt very cosmopolitan for a Tuesday night.

Weekend routine? A walk to the Brunswick Fish and Chippery where they’ll batter a Mars bar like it’s 1985. Rude Boy Burgers flipping patties in a backstreet diner, because we like our burgers with a side of Rhianna. Then a stop at the old Lankan Tucker for take-home curries and butter chicken pies. But let’s be honest: most of that is nostalgia now. Lankan Tucker moved to Lygon Street, and we’re left with the memory of a staff-meal-turned-menu-staple.

The drama? In 2013, a blackout hit Brunswick West and Northcote for six hours, and the internet called it #Hipstergeddon. We were literally powerless and still got roasted. Then there’s the Whirlpool forum legend: “some of the people can be a bit undesirable.” Mate, we’re not pretending. Our $950-a-month share houses are the price you pay for “tree-lined avenues” and a 41st-most-liveable ranking. We’ve got alcoholics on the porch and art students in the shed.

Who’d hate it here? Anyone who expects Brunswick East’s high street energy. We’re the quiet cousin with the big garden and the medical centre on the corner. We don’t have a flashy strip, we have convenience stores and a fish and chip shop that deep-fries Snickers.

Who’d love it? People who want actual living space, real community, and a suburb that wears its “undesirable” tag like a badge of honour. We’re not trendy. We’re *home*. And if you don’t like it, the 506 bus will take you somewhere more interesting.
