# Doncaster East (VIC)

Slug: `doncaster-east-vic`

We remember when Doncaster East was the place you drove through to get to the city—until you realised that drive takes an hour and a half each way and you’d rather just stay. But we’re not inner-city move-in ready. We’re “north-east pocket of Melbourne’s most family-friendly neighbourhoods” (Sitchu said it, not us). And we wear that label like a badge of honour—or at least like a pair of sensible walking shoes.

Back in the day (pre-2024, because let’s be honest, nothing new has happened here since), the big Friday night move was Three Monkeys Place on Jackson Court. The Tran family brought South East Asian heritage and Melbourne cafe staples together, and we’d sit in the sunroom with a Symmetry coffee and a soft-shell crab burger with yuzu mayo, pretending we weren’t 20 minutes from the nearest decent bar. Over on Doncaster Road, Meno Zero had 24 flavours of gelato in an aqua-and-pink shop—but that was 2017, so we assume the ice-cream has melted by now. And if you wanted a drive that justified the trip, you headed to Fish & Burger Co. on Doncaster Road for a “bad boy” (Urban List’s words, not ours).

But Doncaster East isn’t all smooth sailing and Asian-inspired pasta. In 2020, Yarra Valley Water told 88 suburbs to boil their tap water—and yes, we made the list. Nothing says “family-friendly” like boiling your water before brushing your teeth. And we’ll never forget 2018, when a 12-year-old found a needle in a cucumber from Woolworths at Devon Plaza. The cucumber was from Woolworths. The needle was allegedly in the cucumber. We still buy our cucumbers from the same Woolworths. We just check them harder.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who enjoys spontaneous nightlife, a public transport system that doesn’t involve a 1.5-hour bus each way (as one Whirlpool forum user frankly put it), or a tap water supply you can trust without a rolling boil. Also, anyone building two units with Metricon—because United Energy will hit you with a $30K bill for underground power pits. Yes, that’s a real thing from a real forum.

Who would love it? Families who value playgrounds, Asian cafes with yuzu mayo, and a sense of community so tight-knit that we all remember the needle-cucumber incident like it was yesterday. Doncaster East: we’re not the destination, but we’re a damn good suburb to live in while you wait for the buses to arrive.
