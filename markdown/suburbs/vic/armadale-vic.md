# Armadale (VIC)

Slug: `armadale-vic`

It’s 8am on High Street and you’re already in a 40-minute queue for a Lune croissant. The New York Times called it the best in the world – and we’ve never let anyone forget it. While you wait, you can admire the polished concrete and aged brass interior (designed by Levi’s Ewert Leaf, because of course). The kouign amann is worth the wait, but the real flex is telling your mates you paid $9 for a piece of butter and sugar that took three days to make.

This is Armadale, where the wine shop (Armadale Cellars) is a double-storey Victorian manor that’s been pouring since 1997. Owner Phil Hude will have you blind-tasting Penfolds Grange in the Shiraz Challenge like it’s a pub quiz. And the pub itself? The Orrong Hotel has been standing on the corner of Orrong Road and High Street since 1875 – a full 27 years before Federation. We’ve got history, but we’ve also got Harvie, a rooftop bar that “refuses to take a bad photo” and a European-inspired moody 68-seater called By The Glass that just opened. The locals are in a constant state of deciding which wine bar to Instagram from.

Our petty drama? It’s between the old guard and the new. You can still buy a steak at Pony on Beatty Avenue (weekly visits, apparently) or grab a Danish-style open sandwich at Ned’s (rye bread piled high with pastrami, exclusive to Armadale). But now we’ve got Libertine Parfumerie bringing rare fragrances, A-ESQUE blurring art and fashion, and Tokyo Head Spa doing Japanese rituals on High Street. The basic bitches among us can shop at Bassike; the real ones hit The New Trend for Alexander Wang. It’s a battle of the bougie.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks a croissant should cost less than a tram fare. People who don’t own a linen shirt or a wine fridge. Folk who prefer a servo pie over a kouign amann from a spaceship-like bakery.

Who would love it? The sort of person who plans their Grand Prix weekend around High Street shopping, who blind-tastes champagne at Armadale Cellars, who says “we’ve got a heritage-listed building” as a flex. If you’ve got a spare $14,000 for art at Metro Gallery, or just want to feel like the richest person in the room while queueing for pastry, welcome home. We’ve got a spot for you at Nine Yards, in that heritage building, with a Build a Benedict and a side of smug satisfaction.
