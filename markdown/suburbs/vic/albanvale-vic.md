# Albanvale (VIC)

Slug: `albanvale-vic`

Albanvale, VIC — we’ve been famous exactly once, and it was for being in postcode 3021 when the Premier read out the lockdown suburbs like he was calling bingo at a crisis meeting. “Albanvale? Never heard of it,” said everyone outside the west. Which is fair — we’re basically the middle sibling between St Albans and Kealba, wearing hand-me-down houses and a lot of pride.

Back in the day, if you wanted proper Filipino food, you didn’t head to the CBD — you came to Happy Hut, a neighbourhood restaurant where chef Christopher Chantioco honed his skills before taking silog and ube lattes to Spring Street. Happy Hut was our secret weapon. The garlic fried rice was so good you’d consider moving here. Now it’s a footnote in a Broadsheet article about someone else’s success. Classic Albanvale — we train ‘em, they leave.

We also got name-dropped in Time Out — not for a cafe, but for mobile testing vans. “Live in these Melbourne suburbs? Mobile testing vans are coming to you.” You know your suburb’s made it when the only media attention is a fluorescent van in a carpark. We were also “priority suburbs” for testing, alongside Maidstone and Sunshine West. We didn’t ask to be famous, but we took the nasal swabs like champs.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who thinks a “neighbourhood restaurant” means a place with Edison bulbs and a communal table. We don’t have that. We have Happy Hut (or had), a few takeaway shops, and the lingering smell of sausage rolls from the servo. If you need a $6 iced latte with oat milk, Albanvale is not your postcode.

Who would love it? Quiet people. People who want a house with a backyard and don’t need a fortnightly market. We’re the suburb that says “yes” to a 3021 postcode and “no” to vibes. And that’s fine — we don’t need your brunch guide. We’ve got memories of Happy Hut and a postcode that’s been locked down harder than most prisons. We’re still standing, we’re still here — just don’t ask us where the good coffee is.
