# San Remo (VIC)

Slug: `san-remo-vic`

**San Remo, VIC — the mainland’s official Phillip Island staging post, where the bridge toll is a tax on hope.**

Weekend mornings here start with a pilgrimage across the bridge from Newhaven, not for the penguins, but for the one thing that unites us all: the desperate hunt for a halfway decent coffee before the kids lose their absolute minds. You’ll find the scene at a spot by that bridge — a charming venue with a rustic seaside fit-out, as Concrete Playground noted back in 2023. We won’t name it because, frankly, by the time you get there, the queue is already out the door and someone’s parked their Prado over the footpath. The real San Remo move is grabbing a pie from the servo and pretending you’re tough.

Our local drama is the eternal identity crisis. Are we the gateway to Phillip Island? A quiet fishing village? Or just the place you slow down for a speed camera on Marine Parade? Broadsheet once mentioned a “leaving the jetty at San Remo” boat cruise with a fella named Captain Brett — and we reckon that’s our only proper claim to fame that isn’t a coffee machine named after an Italian town. That’s right, the most famous “San Remo” in Australian media is actually a bloody espresso machine in Brisbane and Adelaide. We’re not even the best San Remo in our own country. As Time Out pointed out, the George Bass Coastal Walk from San Remo to Kilcunda is a cracker — but even that trail is less about us and more about the cliffs.

Who would hate living here? Anyone expecting a vibrant town centre. The data doesn’t give us a single operating cafe name, a pub that’s still open, or a current retail hero. The 2014 Westernport Hotel gig is long gone. The Tiny Away house is cute but basically a glamping shed. If you need nightlife beyond a cold wind off Bass Strait, you’ll hate it.

Who would love it? People who understand that San Remo’s job is to be the quiet, slightly daggy mainland cousin to Phillip Island’s tourist flash. You get the coastal walks, you get the fish’n’chips, and you get to say “I live in San Remo” without anyone asking if you own a pasta factory. We’re the suburb that doesn’t need to perform. And honestly? That’s our finest feature.
