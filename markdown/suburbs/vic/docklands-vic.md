# Docklands (VIC)

Slug: `docklands-vic`

We know what you’re thinking: Docklands. The suburb that Melbourne loves to hate. The one where the wind off the water cuts through you like a papercut from a lease agreement. But we who live here? We’re used to it. We’re the ones who actually use the 28-metre heated infinity pool at the Melbourne Marriott Hotel Docklands, pretending it’s a private yacht and not a desperate attempt by developers to glue life onto this petal-shaped concrete dream.

Our morning routine? Could be grabbing a miso and avo toast at From Here by Mike inside 1 Hotel – yes, the one with the giant map of Victoria on the menu, as if to remind us that we are, in fact, still in the state. Or maybe a coffee from Corsia (the Marriott’s all-day haunt) while staring at the Cow Up a Tree sculpture on Harbour Esplanade and wondering who let John Kelly have so much bronze.

For dinner, we squeeze into Heartling, Suby Liu’s 10-seat diner on Tom Thumb Lane, where the dumplings are coloured like a Wes Anderson palette and served with a side of “slow dining” philosophy. She gave up architecture for this. Brave. Or maybe she just realised that designing a building in Docklands is hard when there’s no one to use the door.

The local drama? There’s always some attempt to “make the Docklands work.” Remember Dirtybuoy back in 2011? A pop-up cafe with water walls and Melbourne’s best coffee slingers, all to prove “one tiny part of Docklands will work” – for one day. Then came Moon Dog’s plan for Doglands, a 1,500-person bar at Marvel Stadium concourse. Still waiting. A floating sauna from Tasmania rolled in this winter. A new waterfront park is allegedly being built. We’ll believe it when we see something other than a crane.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who wants a proper corner shop. Anyone who hates wind tunnels. Anyone who expects a suburb to feel lived-in before 6pm on a Tuesday.

Who would love it? People who want a quiet waterfront apartment with a view of nothing changing. People who enjoy the irony of a five-star hotel with a rooftop bar nobody from Fitzroy will ever visit. And people who just want easy parking – The District Docklands once offered all-day parking for $5. That’s the dream, right? Docklands: it’s not for everyone, but it is for someone. Probably a Medibank call centre worker on a lunch break from Nine’s offices. And we’re fine with that.
