# Glen Huntly (VIC)

Slug: `glen-huntly-vic`

You know it's a special Glen Huntly morning when you’re queueing up at **St Zita’s** at 8am, already three deep for a Zita’s Scallops with pumpkin rosti while trying to work out if the person behind you is part of the playgroup exodus from the **Glen Huntly Maternal and Child Health Centre** or just lost on their way to **Booran Reserve’s** $10.8 million playground. We don’t have a main street identity crisis — we have a whole strip of them, and we’re fine with it.

The data says **Glen Huntly Road** is "one of Melbourne's most distinctive village strips", and sure, if your definition of distinctive is the 1.2km battle between "elevated old-school Chinese restaurant feel" at **Potluck** (829 Glen Huntly Road, BYO, Esther Sun’s mum making wontons in the back) and the constant siren song of **Chargrill Charlies** at 412 Glen Huntly Road. You can start your day with a 48-hour rise pizza from an Oceania-best pizzeria, pivot to an aloe honey highball at **Potluck**, and end it arguing about whether **Saul’s** seventh sandwich empire actually lands on our side of the road.

Of course, we also have our ghosts. **Upsy Daisy** at 1062 Glen Huntly Road is "Permanently Closed" as of Nov 2024 — but the mural of balloons painted by Marcos Diaz is still there, taunting you when you walk past with a toddler who just wants that $10.8 million flying fox. And **Loco Coffee** (436 Glen Huntly Road) is still a trusted veteran serving Axil beans, but it's been through more management changes than Booran Reserve has had splash pad schedules.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who expects a neat little village. We're a 1950s post office turned pink influencer brunch haven (**Major Mitchell** at 122 Grange Road), sandwiched between a maternals health centre playgroup and a charcoal chicken cult. You need to enjoy contradictions: one end of Glen Huntly Road is hawker-style bottomless brunch, the other end is "if you arrive early ring the door bell."

But we love it. Because where else can you live within walking distance of Glen Huntly station, the 67 tram, Monash Uni Caulfield, and a suburb profile that brags about "footsteps to the local café" but never specifies which café, because we all know it's whichever one hasn't closed yet. Welcome to Glen Huntly: we’re a village strip that knows it’s weird, and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
