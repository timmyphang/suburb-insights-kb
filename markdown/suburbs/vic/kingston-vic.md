# Kingston (VIC)

Slug: `kingston-vic`

See, the thing about Kingston, VIC is that it doesn’t actually know what it wants to be. We’ve got a name so popular it’s basically the John Smith of Australian suburbs — every state’s got one, and half of them are more confident than us. When you Google “Kingston” you get Canberra’s fancy foreshore, a go-kart track in Brisbane, a bakery in Margaret River, and a pub in Richmond that has the audacity to call itself “The Kingston” despite being a 30-minute drive away.

Our morning routine? Head to the Kingston City Hall for a community event — unless it’s election season, because apparently that’s where we cheer on federal politicians as they lose by 175 votes (thanks, Pedestrian TV). After that, you might hit the Kingston Heath golf course if you’re fancy enough to afford the green fees. Time Out reckons it’s one of the best in Australia, so at least we’ve got that.

The petty drama? The locals are still arguing about whether the “Kingston” in that Melway reference actually means our suburb or the council area. Meanwhile, every food guide tells you to go to the Canberra Kingston for Molto’s cacio e pepe served from a hollowed-out pecorino wheel. We don’t have that. We have a pub in Richmond that just got a $1.5 million makeover but still calls itself “The Kingston” — classic Richmond, always annexing our brand.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who orders a banh mi expecting the Kingston Bakery masterpiece from Margaret River. Or people who show up looking for the Kingston foreshore promenade and find a quiet residential street with a golf course.

Who would love it? Golfers who want bragging rights without the fuss. Locals who appreciate that our most notable moment in recent years was being the backdrop for a photo of a losing candidate. We’re not Canberra’s Kingston, and we’re okay with that — we’ve got Heath, a hall, and a pub that’s technically in the next suburb. It’s fine. We’re fine.
