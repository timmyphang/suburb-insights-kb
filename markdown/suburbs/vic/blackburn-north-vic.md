# Blackburn North (VIC)

Slug: `blackburn-north-vic`

There was a time, not so long ago, when we Blackburn North folk woke up, looked at our taps, and thought, “Should I boil this or just risk the gastro?” August 2020. Yarra Valley Water hit 88 suburbs with a “potential water quality issue” after storms fried the electrics. We were on the list. Attwood, Blackburn, Boronia — and us. We boiled water for our coffee, for our baby formula, for our teeth. And we did it with dignity.

Back when the water was safe, we’d head to Little Woodpecker on Katrina Street. There wasn’t much happening on that strip until it replaced a decades-old upholsterer in 2016. Tanya Baharian, raised a few doors down, named it after her carpenter dad — he’s the woodpecker, she’s the little one. He built the hardwood tables. She served The Golden Temple (panko-crumbed chickpea fritters) and La Bamba (Mexican green-chilli burger in a milk bun). They roasted their own house blend through UBlend. It was simple, relaxed, community. But that was 2017. We don’t know if Little Woodpecker still pecks. The data doesn’t say. So we’ll just remember it fondly, like the water we used to trust.

Our main claim to fame now? Bounce. More than 50 interconnected trampolines at Blackburn North. Slam-dunk arenas, giant air bags, sweet aerial moves. You’ll find us there, bouncing out the stress of the day, or bouncing the kids until they’re too tired to ask why the tap water tastes faintly of caution.

Who would hate it here? Anyone looking for a nightlife scene. We have the Koonung Creek trail, Old Orchard Primary School, and a median age of 39. The most exciting thing that happened recently was a water boil notice. If you want to party, try the city.

Who would love it? Families who want top-rated schools, a safe neighbourhood, and a trampoline park big enough to launch a child into next week. Just boil your water first. We learnt that the hard way.
