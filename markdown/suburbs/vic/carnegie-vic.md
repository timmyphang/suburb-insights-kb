# Carnegie (VIC)

Slug: `carnegie-vic`

Carnegie: where Koornang Road is basically the United Nations of soup, and we wouldn’t have it any other way.

It’s 10am and you’re stuck between a Korean beef bone broth that’s been bubbling for two days in a 240-litre cauldron shipped from Seoul (Gaon Gamasot Gomtang, if you’re nasty), a tom yum burrata from a Dinner by Heston alum that comes with doughnuts you’re meant to drag through the sauce (Tyga, obviously), and a gelato scoop made with Jersey milk and St David Dairy cream that tastes like a holiday in Tuscany (Nuvoletta Gelateria, you’re welcome). And that’s just one block.

We’ve always been the quiet cousin in the Bayside corridor — the one between Caulfield and Murrumbeena nobody could quite place. But somewhere between the old Glen Huntly post office turning into a Very Pink Instagram cafe (Major Mitchell, we see you) and Saul’s Sandwiches becoming the sanga king of the south-east, Carnegie decided it was done being a footnote.

Now our biggest drama isn’t the Neighbourhood Watch newsletter — it’s whether you can actually get a table at Tyga without booking three weeks in advance, and whether the ex-Nobu chef at Gaon will ever forgive you for asking for MSG (spoiler: he doesn’t use it). Meanwhile, the Whirlpool forums are lit up with locals debating the cheapest way to the airport — Skybus or Uber — like it’s a parliamentary inquiry.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks fusion is a dirty word, or wants a quiet suburban life where the biggest decision is which servo gets your dollar. Also anyone who can’t handle the smell of charcoal grills drifting through their weatherboard at 9pm.

Who would love it? Foodies who actually eat, not just photograph. Families who want their kids to grow up thinking a proper sandwich has smoked pastrami and a good soup takes two days. And anyone who believes Koornang Road is the best food street in Melbourne — and is finally ready to defend that to the death.

Carnegie: we may not have Carnegie Hall, but we’ve got gaumasot cauldrons and a whole lot of gochujang. Come hungry.
