# Rye (VIC)

Slug: `rye-vic`

We know what you’re thinking: Rye. The suburb named after a bread that’s actually from Balaclava. The bakery called Baker in the Rye? Yeah, that’s in St Kilda East. Classic Rye — named after something we don’t even have.

A Saturday morning here goes: you wake up, drive past the Haba pub at 2201 Point Nepean Road, a sprawling beachfront live-music venue from the Laneway Festival bloke. It’s got a beer garden with change rooms for people who come straight from the water — because nothing says class like changing into your pub shorts in a purpose-built shed after a swim. Down Dundas Street, you hit Store 360 for a coffee from Kelter Coffee and a pie from Caspers. The bi-fold window’s open, dogs everywhere, and you can grab a bouquet from Peninsula Wild Flower. Very coastal, very Rye.

The annual Rye Summer Carnival along the foreshore is our big moment. Locals and holidayers cram together for sunshine and fairy floss, and for one week we pretend this isn’t the same strip that’s dead quiet by June. The real drama? None, really — unless you count the eternal argument over whether Number 16 Beach or St Andrews Beach has better parking. Spoiler: neither does.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who expects a bakery actually called “Rye” selling rye bread. Or people who want nightlife beyond a pub that closes when the last band finishes. Or anyone who thinks “coastal chic” means something more than a cafe with a surf shop next door.

Who would love it? Families who appreciate a beachside pub with Carlton Draught on tap. Surfers who don’t mind queuing for a toastie. People who get irrationally excited about a jam from a local maker. We’re not flashy, but we’ve got change rooms at the pub, a summer carnival, and a bakery in another suburb. That’s the Rye way.
