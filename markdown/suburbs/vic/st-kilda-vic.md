# St Kilda (VIC)

Slug: `st-kilda-vic`

St Kilda, you absolute beauty. You’re the sun-faded, slightly unhinged aunt of Melbourne’s bayside suburbs — equal parts Luna Park nostalgia, breakwater penguins, and a Fitzroy Street that can’t decide if it’s a bougie brunch strip or a set from *Underbelly*.

We start our weekend at **Pontoon** on the Esplanade — because nothing says “we’ve arrived” like ordering a $28 wood-fired pizza while a DJ spins house music at 3pm and a golden retriever licks your ankle. Apparently the crowds are *already* back, as if the 2014 Stokehouse fire never happened. Owner Frank Van Haandel reckons St Kilda looked “a bit sad, a bit flat” for a while there — mate, we were fine, we just had to survive the Espy closure and that period when Acland Street was just one giant construction site.

Speaking of Acland: **Good Love** is still turning out vegan “deceitballs” at the corner of Acland and Fawkner, while **DOC** has planted its sixth Italian flag at the Saint Moritz residences, serving Aperol Spritzes and tiramisu with a side of “we’re not Loti anymore” energy. And if you want charcoal chicken with a Thai twist, hit **Yang Thai** on the Hot List — because apparently St Kilda needed another chicken shop like we needed another frittata at the Esplanade Market.

Yes, we know what they say: St Kilda is a tourist trap where the only locals left are either third-generation boomers or Irish GAA players. And they’re not wrong. But they missed the bit where the **Local Taphouse** on Carlisle Street still pours 400 different beers a year, where you can spot fairy penguins at dusk on the pier (which just won a 2026 urban design award — take that, boring infrastructure), and where the **St Kilda Festival** can still pull Jessica Mauboy and Sneaky Sound System without anyone asking “who?”

The drama? Well, the **Midsumma Pride** protest in 2024 saw 100 cops clash with activists, and someone paint-bombed the Captain Cook statue with “no pride”. Plus the **Gatwick Hotel** almost became a *Block* site back in 2017 — which is probably the closest St Kilda ever came to being on telly without someone being arrested. And yes, we live with the legacy of Nicky Winmar lifting his jumper on this very turf, because some stains are more important than others.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who wants a quiet, well-planned suburb with a library that isn’t also a needle exchange. People who think “vibes” are a waste of rent money. Tourists who expect Byron Bay and get a drunk dude arguing with a seagull at 11am.

Who would love it? Families who don’t mind the occasional broken bottle in the sand. Dog owners who treat **Trinity St Kilda** as their second lounge room. Anyone who understands that St Kilda is not a postcard — it’s a slap in the face with a salt‑tinged breeze, and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
