# Kingsville (VIC)

Slug: `kingsville-vic`

Kingsville, VIC — where your morning coffee comes with a side of existential crisis about whether you’re still in Footscray or you’ve finally arrived somewhere with proper Danish pastries.

We wake up at Nabo, because obviously. This is our corner-side slice of Copenhagen where the menu is “mostly vegetarian” and driven by whatever backyard fruit the regulars drop off. Yes, our neighbours are the kind of people who grow rhubarb and then donate it to a cafe. We eat smørrebrød topped with pickled watermelon and nasturtium, and pretend we’re not 15 minutes from the West Gate Bridge. The coffee is Market Lane. The vibes are “IKEA but cosy.” We love it.

But let’s talk about the real Kingsville drama: the 3012 postcode. Back in 2020 we were lumped in with Brooklyn, Maidstone, Tottenham and West Footscray as a COVID “hot zone.” The whole suburb went into stage three lockdown while the rest of Melbourne watched us like we were carrying the plague. We were the epicentre of everything — except actual cafes, which could only do takeaway. Willow Wine Café had to shut its warehouse-chic doors. That beautiful former cricket-bat workshop (RIP Fisher Bats) sat empty. Our only solace was that Sandro from Burn City Test Kitchen was allegedly setting up shop here, but we’re still waiting.

You’d hate living here if you need a train line that’s actually inside the suburb (we have no station — it’s either Footscray or Newport, good luck). If you think “Scandinavian-inspired” is a pretentious way to say expensive toast. If you own a Hilux and need a Bunnings within walking distance (you don’t). The real estate forums will tell you: “I live in Kingsville in a front unit. The rear unit would have more privacy.” Yes, we debate front vs rear units like it’s the only thing that matters. Because it is.

But you’ll love Kingsville if you appreciate a suburb that refuses to be anything other than itself. We’re not trendy Footscray. We’re not fancy Williamstown. We’re the quiet kid who brings a rhubarb tart to the party and then goes home early. We have a cafe that serves jam doughnuts with fig leaf custard and quince jam. We have a wine bar in a former cricket-bat factory. We survived a global pandemic by buying bagels from Newport. Come for the smørrebrød, stay for the back unit with better privacy. Just don’t expect a station.
