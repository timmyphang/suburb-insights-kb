# Hoppers Crossing (VIC)

Slug: `hoppers-crossing-vic`

Welcome to Hoppers Crossing, where the Christmas lights are brighter than our future prospects. We’re the suburb that takes its festive decorating so seriously that the Urban List literally has a whole dedicated guide to our two-storey extravaganzas — tens of thousands of lights, nativity scenes, inflatables, bubble machines, smoke machines, snow machines, and owners who start unpacking in November and don’t stop until the power bill arrives in January. Some of these displays have been running for over 20 years. We’re not sure if it’s Christmas spirit or a cry for help, but either way, bring your sunnies and prepare to be dazzled from 8:30pm to 10pm (extending to 11pm on Fridays and Saturdays, because we’re rebels).

In the morning, you might have headed to Morris and Heath on Old Geelong Road — back in 2017, it was a sleek cafe with dramatic timber panelling that actually won a national design award. “Bringing high-end design to Melbourne’s western suburbs,” the jury said. It had a buttermilk-fried-chicken burger and chilli scrambled eggs. But that was then. Now? Sweet Street Desserts has a permanent pop-up marquee (no physical shop, just a marquee — very on-brand), open Thursday to Sunday from 5pm ‘til late. That’s our nightlife.

Petty local drama? We don’t need to manufacture it — Nick Cody, the comedian from Hoppers Crossing, is literally “on fire” according to Pedestrian TV. And we’re still waiting for Cinnabon to actually open in our suburb; the nearest one is at Watergardens (yes, we know, it’s technically Taylors Lakes — but we’ll claim it). The Melbourne CBD might be Australia’s entrepreneurial capital, but postcode 3029 is where you come to admire someone else’s hard work from the comfort of your car.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who doesn’t own a pair of novelty Christmas socks, or expects a cafe that’s still serving chilli scrambled eggs from a 2017 menu. Also, people who think “ambient lighting” means something other than a flashing inflatable Santa.

Who would love it? Families who measure their year by the number of drive-by light displays. Dessert lovers who appreciate a marquee that’s “permanent.” And anyone who wants to live in a suburb where the biggest headline is a comedy show and the second-biggest is a baking competition with tinsel. We’re not trying to be cool — we’re trying to be lit. Literally. From December 1st. See you on Warwick Close.
