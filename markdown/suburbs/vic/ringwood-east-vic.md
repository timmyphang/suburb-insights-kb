# Ringwood East (VIC)

Slug: `ringwood-east-vic`

Right, so you’re thinking about Ringwood East. Let’s set the scene: an eight-metre gum tree has just taken out the powerlines on Canterbury Road and now it’s 10am and you’re late for your vanilla slice at Bedford Coffee Co. Real relaxed start to the day down here.

Our morning routine is a finely tuned circuit. Grab a coffee and a Portuguese tart from Juliette Coffee & Bread (the one that showed up in 2020 like a salmon-hued saviour for our lockdown sanity), then swing by Hoshino on Railway Avenue for a Japanese feed that looks so good it’s basically paid Instagram content. Finish with a cut at Bossman Barbers, because even in the outer east we appreciate a badass fade.

But don’t be fooled by the polished surface. The Whirlpool forums say it best: “I moved out 10 years ago and it was a bit rougher.” Yes, we know. We’ve got the scars. The 2020 “boil your water” alert that hit 88 suburbs? Ringwood East was right there, filling up billy cans like it was 1850. And that gum tree through the powerlines? That wasn’t a one-off – it’s a lifestyle. We expect at least one tree-related traffic delay per season.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks “the smaller Ringwood East station” is beneath them. Anyone who doesn’t appreciate a strip of shops that looks “unassuming” but hides the best vanilla slice in the state. Anyone who wants to drive 60km/h on Canterbury Road without a fallen branch ruining their vibe.

Who would love it? Families who moved from Pascoe Vale South because “the air is fresher and the locals are friendly.” People who want a Korean-Australian institution like Mr Lee’s Foods, a Holey Moley at Eastland just up the road, and the quiet satisfaction of knowing your suburb is “likely to improve” – but not so much that it loses its slightly rough charm. Welcome to the East. Keep a kettle handy.
