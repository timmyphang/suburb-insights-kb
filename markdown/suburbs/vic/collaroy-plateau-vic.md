# Collaroy Plateau (VIC)

Slug: `collaroy-plateau-vic`

If you’re looking for Collaroy Plateau on a map, you’ll notice we’re the bit that’s not quite beach, not quite bush—just enough elevation to look down on everyone else and mutter about traffic on Pittwater Road. We’re a suburb that smells like sunscreen, woodfired pizza, and the faint whiff of a teenage party gone very wrong.

Our Saturday morning routine? Grab a post-swim coffee at Arikaya Homewares on Hilma Street, where you can browse vintage South East Asian furniture while sipping plant-based milk and pretending you don’t have a mortgage. Then head downhill to The Surf Deck—Merivale’s tiki-inspired bar plonked right on the sand, serving yellowfin tuna tostadas and Pina Coladas that executive chef Jordan Toft calls “a thing of iced beauty.” It opened in 2022 but got a 2024 glow-up, so yes, it’s still the place to watch the waves while paying $28 for calamari fritti.

Petty local drama: In truly Collaroy Plateau fashion, our claim to fame on Pedestrian TV is the story of a teen who *voluntarily set himself on fire* at a house party. As the Northern Beaches Living newsletter so politely puts it, we also host World Food Markets with live entertainment—just don’t bring a lighter.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks a plateau should have flat ground. We don’t do flat. We do stairs. We do hills. We do walking to the shops on Anzac Avenue and arriving exhausted. Also, if you’re allergic to Merivale branding, tough luck—The Collaroy pub was taken over in 2018, upgraded in 2020, and now serves Vinnie’s pizza, $10 anchovy toast, and a side of “community-friendly atmosphere” that somehow still feels like you’re in a Justin Hemmes fever dream.

Who would love it? Families who appreciate a quiet spot with a Coptic Church on Stella Street, a GurTaj Indian takeaway on Telopea, and a Plank Pilates studio that promises to improve your quality of life. We’re close enough to the beach to feel fancy, far enough to avoid the ferry queues. Welcome to the Plateau—we’ve got ocean views, old homewares, and a fire safety incident we’re still processing.
