# Moonee Ponds (VIC)

Slug: `moonee-ponds-vic`

Moonee Ponds, you absolute shape-shifter. Once the spiritual home of Bev tightrope-walking between hot air balloons on national telly (Junkee, bless), now you’re a glossy destination where ex-Attica chefs dry-age duck at TOTE Bar & Dining inside the old Moonee Valley Racecourse building. We used to be “the commission housing suburb” (Whirlpool, 2019). Now we have a 140-seat cafe called Convoy across from Queens Park that took 18 months and three lockdowns to open. That’s growth, baby. Or inflation.

Our morning routine: you either queue at Convoy for a steak-frites roll and a side of bone marrow gravy guilt, or you shuffle into Sunny Boy Coffee in your retro ‘70s trackie dacks pretending you’re too cool for Puckle Street. Meanwhile, Palace Penny Lane Cinema landed on Puckle Street with 11 screens, 4K projection and a rooftop where you can watch arthouse films while silently judging the Ferraris parked outside. But be honest: half of us still use the Palace loyalty program to get free birthday tickets and call it a date.

Petty local drama: a Whirlpool user once witnessed a driver struggling to reverse park on our streets, and a Porsche driver behind them got so impatient they went around illegally. We take parking that seriously here. Also, our ATO recruitment centre is so popular that candidates are told “Moonee Ponds is currently full, Box Hill is your only option”. We are officially at capacity.

You’d hate Moonee Ponds if you can’t handle a suburb that simultaneously has a heritage-listed former supermarket turned 400-seat beer garden (Holmes Hall, 20 taps, pinball machines) and a Coles sustainable store that’s been written up by Pedestrian TV. We are contradictions on bricks.

But you’d love it if you want to live in a place where you can walk from Farro’s spelt pizza to Yoga Flame hot flow class to the Fernwood women’s gym without removing your athleisure. Where the bagels at Ritzy are decent and the bottomless brunch at The George on Collins (okay, Hall Street) runs $40 for cocktails. Where you can still hear the ghost of Shannon Noll murdering the national anthem at the Cox Plate echoing across the racecourse. Moonee Ponds: we’re not the inner north, and we’re okay with that.
