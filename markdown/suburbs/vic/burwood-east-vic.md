# Burwood East (VIC)

Slug: `burwood-east-vic`

We told the kids the rooftop farm was just a metaphor for growth and sustainability. Then we took them to the top of Burwood Brickworks and watched them eat Vadouvan eggs from a garden that’s literally 2500 square metres of eggplant and Jerusalem artichokes, 11,000 leafy greens, and a quail coop—all sitting on a shopping centre roof. Welcome to Burwood East, where the commute to dinner is an escalator ride and the view is Dandenongs, not carpark concrete.

A Saturday starts at Gold Leaf on Burwood Highway, where we fight the aunties over the last basket of pork and prawn siu mai before the roaming yum cha carts roll away. Then it’s a quick dash to Marrybrown for cheese-covered drumsticks that have been perfecting their secret spice mix since the ’80s. If you’re feeling fancy, you head to Rombe—the sun-filled rooftop barn with floor-to-ceiling windows and pastrami-cured salmon on rosti. Yes, it’s real. No, we don’t know how we pulled this off either.

But we’re not perfect. For years, the only drama on Middleborough Road was the VicRoads hazard perception test freezing mid-description of a hazard. “The screens were alright but the application seemed like it had frozen quite a few times,” someone once wrote. We still bring it up at parties. Also, Acre? The legendary farm-to-table experience? Permanently closed as of January 2025. We’re coping by getting takeaway Schnitz from Burwood One and pretending it’s farm-to-mouth.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks a rooftop farm is too “urban” for a suburb that still has a Telstra server named after it. Anyone who wants authenticity without a shopping centre elevator ride.

Who would love it? Families who want PLC and Deakin within cooee, while also being able to roll a yum cha cart into their dining room. People who look at a 120-seat barn on a mall and think, “This is peak eastern suburbs.” We know we’re not the first suburb you think of. But the honeybees on the roof don’t care, and neither do we.
