# Cockatoo (VIC)

Slug: `cockatoo-vic`

Cockatoo, VIC — where you move when Brunswick gets too loud, but you’re not quite ready for a goat farm.

We’re the kind of suburb where the biggest local celebrity isn’t a footy player or a YouTuber — it’s a chef who cooks for six people at a time in her own living room. As Broadsheet noted back in 2023, Jung Eun Chae’s self-titled restaurant has guests waiting *years* for a reservation. Years. We’ve got a six-seat diner, a veggie patch in the backyard, and a fermentation system that makes sourdough starters look amateur. Meanwhile, our neighbours in Emerald are fighting over the best pie.

Let’s be real: the name is a curse. Tell anyone you live in Cockatoo and they either ask if you’ve seen the Netflix show or if you live on Cockatoo Island. No, Sharon, we are not a UNESCO World Heritage convict site in Sydney Harbour. We are a damp patch of the Dandenongs where the mist rolls in at 3pm and doesn’t leave until Tuesday. The only ghost we’ve got is the one at the old servo that closed in 2018.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who needs a reliable NBN connection, doesn’t have a spare two years to wait for dinner, or thinks “nightlife” means something other than watching a cockatoo tear apart an anti-bird spike from the Coles carpark. Urbanites who can’t handle a three-week silent feud with a local lyrebird — you’d crack before the first frost.

But who would love it? Someone who appreciates a six-seat eatery that serves pine nut porridge inspired by a memoir, where the soy sauce is aged longer than most marriages. Someone who wants to trade the FOMO for JOMO — the joy of missing out on queueing for brunch in Fitzroy, and instead waiting *patiently* by the mailbox for a monthly lottery email. As the Broadsheet piece put it, Chae’s menu follows the seasons, drawing as much from literature as Korean tradition. We’re literary, we’re fermented, and we’re damp. Perfect.
