# Moorabbin (VIC)

Slug: `moorabbin-vic`

Moorabbin. You know the spot. The one where you can grab a Niccolo coffee from Minnie Miny Mo opposite the station, watch a baker line up croissants four times a day at Penny for Pound in the old Morris Moor factory, then sink a Laneway Lager at Stomping Ground’s 460-person beer hall in a converted cigarette factory. All before you’ve even decided if you’re still in the industrial zone or accidentally became a hipster. 

“Moorabbin is an area that has changed a lot over the past few years,” they say. Understatement. We used to just have Costco hearing aids, a sex shop warehouse for “informal consultations”, and a police custody officer station. Now we’ve got a Holey Moley mini golf, Risky Kids parkour classes for the little ones, and a brewery where the fermenters were smuggled out of Times Square at 3am via a shady New Jersey trucking company. That’s our history, mate. 

The old guard? They’re still at Toast It! and the Mr Detail car wash in Freighter Rd. The new guard? They’re arguing about whether the “croffle” at Penny for Pound is a breakfast or a dessert. Both are valid. 

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks a suburb should pick a lane. One minute you’re dodging forklifts, the next you’re sipping a barrel-aged stout in a beer hall with a three-storey cubbyhouse for kids. It’s identity crisis chic. Also, if you don’t like the smell of hops and ambition, stay north of the Nepean. 

Who would love it? The craft beer devotee who wants 25 taps and a cheesy pretzel. The parent who needs a three-storey cubbyhouse and Risky Kids. The person who wants their morning latte opposite the train station, their afternoon croissant from a 130-seat cafe, and their evening pint in a former cigarette factory. We’re not trying to be anything else. We’re Moorabbin — industrial, awkward, and finally having our moment.
