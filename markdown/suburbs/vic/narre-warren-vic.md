# Narre Warren (VIC)

Slug: `narre-warren-vic`

**Narre Warren, VIC — where the cultural renaissance and the Corey Worthington legacy go head-to-head and somehow both win.**

We’ve got Bunjil Place — an actual architectural stunner with gallery shows like *The Offbeat Sari* and, back in 2022, a world-first edible fruit-flavoured fog installation (yes, we breathed in apple-scented mist while standing next to giant food sculptures). Real art, real fog, real weird. That’s us.

But also: we’re the suburb that launched the infamous 2008 Corey Worthington party — 500 people, MySpace invite, police shutdown. Still the most famous thing to happen here until we got an IMAX coming to Village Cinemas Fountain Gate in November 2025. That’s the Narre Warren paradox: high culture at Bunjil Place, low culture at the servo, and somehow they’re both at home on Princes Highway.

Empire Club on the highway calls itself “Narre Warren’s new answer to luxury city clubbing.” The bouncers at Empire have seen more sequins than the *Offbeat Sari* exhibition. And the Whirlpool forums? They’ve got opinions: “I’d live in Berwick over Narre Warren any day. There are bogans everywhere but the City of Casey area seems to have more than its fair share.” We don’t argue. We just point to the Maybloom Christmas Trees farm in Narre Warren East — 5 to 12 feet, delivery available — and ask: does Berwick have that?

What about the infrastructure? The red light camera at Princes Highway and Webb Street has “got me confused,” per one driver. The NBN in Narre Warren South is flakey — “very flakey connection (HFC), says we are connected then nothing” — but some lucky souls got fibre to the house. Classic Casey.

We’ve got Beaumount Tiles on Victor Crescent, Risky Kids parkour classes at Unit 5 Vesper Drive, and the Narre Warren Kidz Playgroup running Thursday mornings for the under-5s. And for those doing it tough, the Transit Soup Kitchen and Food Support gets food from Second Bite — feeding families while the rest of us argue about IMAX seats.

So who would hate it here? Anyone expecting inner-city polish. This is a suburb where the cultural precinct shares a postcode with a tile shop and a Christmas tree farm. Where the Whirlpool forum says “bogans everywhere” and we just reply “yeah, and?”

Who would love it? People who want a bit of everything — a $15 million library (okay, that one’s in the city), an IMAX, an Empire club, a playgroup, a parkour gym, and the reassurance that even if you skip dinner, your neighbour will bring you a casserole from the food charity. We’re not fancy. We’re Narre Warren. And we’re fine with that.
