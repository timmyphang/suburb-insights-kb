# Brunswick East (VIC)

Slug: `brunswick-east-vic`

Brunswick East, VIC — where we’ve been told we’re the sixth coolest neighbourhood in the world (Time Out, 2023) and we still haven’t forgiven anyone who took that literally.

A Saturday morning here: you roll out of bed, walk to Hickford Street, and queue at The Coffee Bar’s roller door for a stracciatella sandwich and a pear-and-walnut scroll. You’re sitting on a stool next to a Bonsoy shelf and a houseplant, feeling extremely on-brand. Then you wander to Lygon Street and remember you have three new restaurants to try from the Tipo 00 team — Figlia for bug-topped pizza, Grana for mandarin-peel ricotta — but you’d need a second mortgage to afford a drink at Etta’s marble bar. Etta is “modern-Australian” with “lots of vegetables” and no tablecloths, which is basically every inner-north restaurant from 2019 onward.

The petty drama: we lost Café Romantica after 30 years, and the resulting wailing and gnashing of teeth (Concrete Playground’s words, not ours) was only soothed by the arrival of Bar Romantica with its custom sound system and a DJ called JNETT. Now it’s Daphne on Friday nights. Meanwhile, the old Alchemy Brewing site is about to become Bar Elsie — a European bistro with a U-shaped bar and banquette seating for 60, run by a Sydney couple who moved here after a near-fatal cardiac arrest. We welcome the drama, but we also quietly miss the time when Brunswick East’s biggest controversy was whether the Macca’s on Lygon actually had the McPlant on Uber Eats (it did, apparently).

Who would hate living here? Anyone who doesn’t own a linen shirt. Or anyone who thinks “non-alcoholic bar” is an oxymoron — sorry, Brunswick Aces on Weston Street is a real thing, with zero-proof gin and a full roster of designated drivers. Also, anyone who doesn’t appreciate a good pierogi. Eat Pierogi Make Love wants to make Polish food sexy, and they’ve got vodka tinctures and bison grass to prove it. If you don’t want to sit on a concrete floor under a brutalist poster while eating lazy dumplings, this suburb will exhaust you.

Who would love it here? The person who mourned the end of Bluebonnet’s pop-ups in 2014, then wept when they got a permanent home on Lygon with smoked brisket and a pool room. The person who spends Saturday afternoon at Trak Racer on Lygon trying out racing sims, then heads to Mama Manoush for perfect Lebanese cooking. The person who walks through East Brunswick Village and thinks, “Yes, this is what an urban community hub should feel like — if you ignore the fact that my rent went up $200 a week because we’re ‘cool.’”

Brunswick East: we’re not smug, we’re just well-fed.
