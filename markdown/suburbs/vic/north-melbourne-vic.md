# North Melbourne (VIC)

Slug: `north-melbourne-vic`

We’re the ones who can walk from Royal Park to Queen Victoria Market before your mate from the east coast has had time to Instagram his sourdough. North Melbourne is the kind of place that feels like a tiny, stubborn town sneaked into the inner city — deceptively suburban, wide streets, converted warehouses and Victorian-era homes everywhere you look. We know every tram stop and every gnarly gum tree.

Mornings here are ritual: a lap of Errol Street to see what’s new (it’s been quietly bulking up its dining roster for years), an encounter with Palette if you want Korean‑leaning minimalism and that Swiss roll they rave about, or a wander through the freshly overhauled EziStreat when we feel like neon and a big, messy Tokyo‑inspired feed. If we’re feeling indulgent, someone always says “parma” and we end up at The Leveson on Leveson Street — yes, the one Urban List thinks does a cracking schnitty with too much mozzarella for our own good.

There’s local drama, but it’s the friendly kind that keeps the footy talk spicy. Remember when the Kangaroos got a logo upgrade and fans made a louder fuss than a pub on Grand Final day? Classic North Melb. And let’s not pretend the station rename kerfuffle was boring — the Metro Tunnel naming process once had us all shouting into the void about Arden and what used to be North Melbourne station. We live for that mild chaos.

Who would hate living here? Someone who wants a suburb polished to a corporate sheen. We’re not eight-figure gardens and gated monotony; we’ve got character, grit, and pubs with history — places that got renovated and then told their old stories louder. If you can’t cope with charmingly narrow footpaths, period homes, or a tram that arrives on North Melbourne time, this isn’t for you.

Who’d love it? People who want to be terrifyingly close to the CBD but still have room to breathe; culture vultures who rate Time Out’s 2025 nod, food obsessives who prize Errol Street and EziStreat, and families who find the local Mama Tribe meetups handy. We’re small‑town energy in an inner‑city postcode — and we wouldn’t trade our slightly scrappy charm for the world.
