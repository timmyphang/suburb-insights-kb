# Seaford (VIC)

Slug: `seaford-vic`

Seaford, VIC — you’ve heard of us? No, that’s okay. We’re the bit between Frankston and Carrum that Broadsheet forgot. We’re not a “lockdown success story” with a flagship bakery or a tequila bar with 100 hand-selected margaritas. We’ve got 38 South Bar and Café, described once as “a vibrant fresh of air” — back in the day when Urban List remembered we existed. And Seaford Pizza Lodge, a local legend that made national headlines for slinging a pickle-only pizza. Not because we’re trendy. Because we’ll eat anything.

Our morning routine? Head to 38 South if you’re feeling fancy, or just grab a sausage at Bunnings — though you’ll have to go to South Australia for the one that got reviewed. Here, we’re more about the Seaford train that, according to one writer, “still only runs every hour on Sundays.” We don’t have a Red Gum BBQ or a Frankie’s Tortas. We have Eel Race Road, and the only thing smoked is the internet — as per the Whirlpool forums: “Same issue at 3193… on hold for 65-70 minutes.”

Who would hate it here? Anyone looking for a “vibrant fresh of air” that actually stays fresh. We’re the affordable coastal option Sitchu calls “approachable,” which is real estate code for “you’ll get a villa unit on a 320sqm block and the neighbours will borrow your mower without asking.” If you need a $20 cocktail and a leather banquette, we are not your suburb.

Who would love it? People who understand that “beach-side suburb” means a 10-minute walk to the sand and a lifetime of saying “no, we’re not Seaford SA.” We’ve got Patterson Lakes next door, the freeway nearby, and a pizza place that will put pickles on anything. We’re low-drama, high-humidity, and we know exactly what we are — and we’re fine with it.
