# Springvale (VIC)

Slug: `springvale-vic`

We live for the smell of banh mi at 9am and the chaos of the Springvale station carpark at 5pm. This is the suburb where your grocery run turns into a three-hour food crawl and you leave with a trunk full of Asian greens, a Globewest floor lamp, and zero regrets.

A Saturday morning here is a rite of passage. You queue at Bun Bun Bakery — broodily reviewed by Time Out as “pork skewer roll filled with grilled pork dark-pink from the chargrill which tastes akin to reconstituted meat” — yet the line still snakes out the door. Because it’s *good* reconstituted meat, okay? Over at Minh Duc Bakery, they're one of the few places that still ferments their own sour beef, hidden down the laneway near Gong Cha. Coeliacs and card-carrying foodies both worship here. Then you swing by Country Cob for an award-winning pie — yes, the same bakery from Kyneton now has a Springvale outpost, because we do *everything*.

The petty local drama comes courtesy of Whirlpool, where a punter once bought five rolls from Springvale and spent the next day rethinking life choices. “Everything was just fine.. until Monday afternoon,” they wrote. We’ve all had that moment. It’s a badge of honour.

If you hate the smell of fish sauce, if the thought of 80 tonnes of real snow dumped on Buckingham Avenue for Snow Fest makes you shiver (not from cold, from snobbery), or if you think Springvale Homemaker Centre with its IKEA and 24 other major stores is the height of culture — you might want to stick to your sterile chain cafes. We don’t do bland.

But if you’re the kind of person who takes Khanh Ong’s advice — “go to Springvale! Each restaurant is known for one or two dishes” — and you understand that the best banh mi is debated by 16 top chefs, then welcome home. We’ve got the bread, the drama, and the trains that are finally getting longer platforms. Just watch out for Monday afternoons.
