# Hawthorn (VIC)

Slug: `hawthorn-vic`

We’re in Hawthorn, where the only thing leafier than the elms is the passive-aggressive note about your recycling bin placement. Yes, we’re in Boroondara — officially one of the “most liveable” postcodes in Australia, which means we pay a small mortgage for the privilege of having a barista who remembers your name and your dog’s name and your preferred single-origin origin story.

Saturday morning starts with a shuffle down Glenferrie Road past Yo-Chi in Troubadour Arcade, where the frozen yoghurt is self-serve and the self-esteem is self-doubt. If you’re feeling fancy, you grab a book at Readings Hawthorn — because nothing says “I have time and money” like buying a hardcover biography. Then it’s off to Porgie + Mr Jones, the sustainable cafe from the Snow Pony people, where the real hospitality includes a gentle lecture on your oat milk choice.

But the real heartbeat of Hawthorn is The Beehive Hotel on Barkers Road. Built in 1854, rebuilt in 1882, turned into Serafina, and then rescued in 2024 by local lads Julien Moussi and Tony Pantano, who had their first beers there. Now it’s a moody, woodfired, 300-capacity temple to nostalgia with a hall of fame corridor for local footy clubs. Which is perfect, because we are Hawks territory — even if our AFL club’s history includes a premiership and a secret sex tape scandal. (We don’t talk about Zac Webster. We also don’t talk about Jarryd Roughead’s melanoma. Footy is serious business.)

Meanwhile, tucked down a no-name laneway, Bar Selecta offers a 10-seat Tokyo-style listening bar where the drink specials match the musical genres. It’s the kind of place you take a date you’re trying to impress, assuming you can find it. And if you can’t, there’s always the Geebung Polo Club — a Victorian pub that’s ideal for food, beers, and pretending you know how to ride a horse.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who likes a bit of nightlife. As Araliya’s owner once said, “They’re older. They don’t go out at night.” The streets roll up by 9pm, and the most exciting after-dark activity is a Halloween trick-or-treater darting between grand gates. Also, if you’re not into being educated on your coffee by a two-time Australian barista champion, you might find Axil Cafe a bit intense — but that was back in 2011, when the coffee scene was still a “vast wasteland”.

But for families who want leafy streets, good schools, and a café that takes bookings for breakfast (Hawthorn Common — if you have five people, you’re in business), this is your spot. It’s polished, practical, and thoughtfully curated — just like a Virgo. And yes, we know we’re hugely expensive, but at least we’ve got the Beehive back. Come for the croquettes, stay for the passive-aggressive note.
