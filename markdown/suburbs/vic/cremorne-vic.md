# Cremorne (VIC)

Slug: `cremorne-vic`

We’ve got a new kind of morning ritual in Cremorne, and it involves a coffee, a live DJ, and absolutely zero guilt about it being 9am on a Tuesday. On Air opened on Stephenson Street earlier this year – a cafe built for people who miss nightclubs but have a 7am start. Founder Francesca Poci and Deyon Murphy saw that nightclub popularity was declining and thought: “What if we just moved the party to a coffee shop?” So now you can order an Inglewood roast, sit on upcycled-wood bleachers, and watch someone spin vinyl while you stress about your Slack messages. It’s the most Melbourne thing imaginable, and we love it.

But Cremorne is a suburb of contradictions. For every trendy cafe (looking at you, Roti John’s with its halal rendang sandwich that owner Mahinor Masud perfected after years at Subway), there’s a historic pub like The Cherry Tree Hotel getting a new lease on life. We’ve got Baker Bleu’s biggest bakery yet, and we’ve got a former match factory being turned into a golf venue at Matchworks. Yes, Pitch Golf is opening its first Australian site where Bryant & May Redheads used to make matches. You can literally hit a ball where they once made things that go boom. That’s Cremorne for you.

Now for the petty local drama that’s driving us insane: the eTag on Citylink near Cremorne. Some of our old tags just won’t beep on those gantries. Not once. Not ever. Eight-year-old tags, brand new tags – doesn’t matter. You drive under, silence. Then three weeks later a toll notice arrives like a ghost from the past. We all know the exact spot. It’s become a local rite of passage: “Did you beep?” “Nah, mate.” It’s a conspiracy.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who doesn’t want to hear a DJ while they’re trying to order a panini. Or someone who misses the days when Cremorne was just a quiet strip of workshops and warehouses. The gentrification is real – we’ve got Japanese konbini-inspired Suupaa, Korean fine-dining Sogumm, and a rooftop Amatrice that serves mini lasagne ten floors up. It’s a lot.

But who would love it? Any creative who works in one of the big tech agencies or has a Pedestrian TV internship. Anyone who wants to grab a roti john for lunch, then wander into Baker Bleu for a Reuben toastie. We’re a tiny pocket between Richmond and the river, but we’ve got live music at 8am, rendang sandwiches, and a brand new rooftop with views of the Dandenongs. Come for the chaos, stay for the coffee buns.
