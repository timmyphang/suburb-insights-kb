# Caroline Springs (VIC)

Slug: `caroline-springs-vic`

**Caroline Springs — the suburb built on a promise and a really good ube doughnut.**

We wake up here not to the sound of birds, but to the gentle hum of the Western Highway and the distant hope that Wendy’s is *finally* opening its doors. Whirlpool forums have been tracking that since 2022. We’re still waiting. But do we complain? No. We line up at **Pecks Road** on Caroline Springs Boulevard instead, where the Lawang brothers turned their childhood street name into a cult. The triple-ube doughnuts take three days to make. Our patience? Tested in about three minutes.

Yes, we have a **BP servo that sells David Jones products** — because nothing says “aspirational outer-west” like buying a $30 candle while filling up your Camry. And yes, we have the **Caroline Chisholm Society** for baby goods, and the **Cheeky Monkey Market** at CS Square, where the drama is less “who slept with who” and more “who got the last cinnamon scroll from **Roger Roger Deli**.” Sitchu called their scrolls “Biscoff-laced.” We call them the only thing that gets us through another speed bump on Lake Street.

Then there’s the neighbourhood sessions. The Melbourne International Comedy Festival brought stand-up here in 2024, which is brave — because we already have the best comedy on offer: the custom plates thread on Whirlpool. A local was spotted driving a Honda Civic with the plate “GOIN”. We are not making this up. That is Caroline Springs humour. No punchline needed.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks a “micro-bakery” is pretentious. We have **Bakt** making Basque cheesecake in a home kitchen and **Roger Roger** making sandwiches that make city slickers weep. If you need branded everything and a tram every five minutes, stay in Fitzroy. We’ve got speed cameras, solar installer debates, and the occasional stabbing arrest making Pedestrian TV. We own it.

Who would love it here? Anyone who sees the beauty in a place where three brothers can open a Filipino cafe on a suburban boulevard and make the *entire city* drive out for a doughnut. Where your lola’s living room is the vibe, and the local banh mi has chicharron and Pinoy barbecue sauce, and nobody bats an eye. We are the 3023. We are doughnuts, banh mi, and bad internet speeds. And we’re fine with that.
