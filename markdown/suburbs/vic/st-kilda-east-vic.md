# St Kilda East (VIC)

Slug: `st-kilda-east-vic`

We know what you’re thinking: “St Kilda East? Isn’t that just the bit between St Kilda and the cemetery?” And look, you’re not wrong. But we’re the suburb that makes the rest of the bay look like they’re trying too hard. We’ve got Alma Park – literally two parks because the train tracks bisect it, which means you can choose your adventure: family picnic or watching the 3:15 to Caulfield rattle past your esky. The tennis courts are getting an upgrade. Classy.

Our morning routine is a proper south-side shuffle. You’ll find us at Gattica on Carlisle Street – purple furniture, five-dollar wine all day, and coffee from a blend the owner personally picked in Indonesia. Been there since 2003. Or maybe Neighbours Cafe on the corner of Chapel and Inkerman – former milk bar turned Israeli-breakfast haven with Roastcraft coffee. Then there’s The Local Taphouse, 184 Carlisle Street, a European-style tavern with 19 taps, two fireplaces, a rooftop, and head chef Wayne Seberry who reckons a parma is simple but every component must be perfect. He’s not wrong.

But let’s talk drama. Carlisle Street took a hit when Uncle shut. The tragedy? It was snapped up immediately and turned into a breezy rooftop bar and diner. Now everyone’s split: old-school mourners versus the “summer is sorted” crowd. Meanwhile, The Greens were advertising on Grindr in our postcode. We’re not saying we’re a statistical anomaly, but we’re definitely a targeted demographic.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who needs a beach view from their couch. We’re not St Kilda – we’re St Kilda East. The beach is a 20-minute walk, and you’ll have to cross Dandenong Road. Also, if you complain about the 25-minute tram ride to South Bank for a 7km commute, you’ll get no sympathy. And if you can’t handle a market that takes over Alma Park every Saturday (Hank Marvin, 9am–3pm, fresh produce, dumplings, and those vegan mushroom burgers), this isn’t for you.

Who would love it? People who want a village feel without the Acland Street crowds. Families who appreciate Greenmeadows Gardens for barbecues while kids play. Beer nerds who rate The Local Taphouse as “best beer bar in the land.” Anyone who thinks a park split by a train line is an upgrade. We’re underrated on purpose – but we’ll still take your envy.
