# Hampton East (VIC)

Slug: `hampton-east-vic`

We call it the Hamptons—no, not that one. That’s the other side of the Nepean Highway. Here in Hampton East, we’re the Bayside suburb that’s still deciding if it wants to be fancy or just has a really convincing real estate agent. You know you’re home when you’re driving down Charming Street and wondering if The Block’s Kristy and Brett left any leftover Venetian plaster lying around.

Saturday morning starts with a pilgrimage to The Hamptons Bakery—okay fine, that’s technically in Hampton proper, but we claim it. After a flat white and a pastry, we shuffle over to All For One yoga for an unlimited membership we bought in 2026 and have used exactly three times. Then it’s back past the old Hampton Wine Co., where Pierre, Jarrod and Glenn used to serve smoked salmon profiteroles and talk about their European travels. These days the wine list is just as impressive, but the conversation is mostly about school zones.

Our big local drama? The Block. Oh, the hype. “A relaxed, peaceful atmosphere that’s close to the beach,” they said. Then they slapped a fire pit and pizza oven on a 1950s red-brick home and called it an “entertainer’s paradise.” Meanwhile, the real locals are on Whirlpool debating whether 27 Nepean Avenue is priced well for a townhouse. Spoiler: it’s not. You could buy a whole property in Aspendale for that.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who expects Nantucket vibes from a suburb that still has a few too many servo snacks and a Bayside address that’s basically Beach-borrowing. The Block tourists who drive down Charming Street looking for designer living and find a 1950s brick facade behind a construction fence.

Who would love it? Families who want to say “we live near the beach” without paying Hampton prices. People who enjoy an occasional yoga session followed by a wine bar that used to be run by Jacques Reymond alums. And anyone who unironically loves a fire pit in a suburban backyard.

Welcome to Hampton East—where the charm is offensive, the property prices are negotiable, and the best ramen is still in the city.
