# Pakenham (VIC)

Slug: `pakenham-vic`

It’s Saturday morning in Pakenham and you’ve got options. You could be at **Smiley Brewing Co** on Southeast Boulevard, sinking a Pakenham Draught while a food truck from the weekly schedule fires up. Or you could be at **Deep Creek Eco Play** watching the kids go feral on the nature playground while you question every life choice that led you to the end of the Pakenham line. But first, you need a haircut — so you’re at **Dario & Co** inside Pakenham Central, getting a straight shave and a slick fade from a barber who knows exactly how to deal with the stress of living in a suburb that smells like a composting facility for two decades.

Yes, we said it. The **Lakeside stench** — a “sickening” odour that, according to Whirlpool forums, has been on the nose of residents for more than 20 years. We’re not saying the entire suburb stinks, but when the wind blows a certain way, you can’t tell if it’s the wetlands or the council’s broken promise. And if the smell doesn’t get you, the train will. The **Pakenham line** is the longest, most packed cattle run in Melbourne. During peak hour you’re pressed against a stranger’s backpack while praying the **Metro Tunnel** actually delivers those extra 240 services it promised. Also, don’t even think about hopping on a V/Line service — they’ll kick you off faster than you can say “suburban passenger”.

For years, Pakenham was the punchline of affordable housing lists — “Melton West, Wyndham Vale, Werribee, Sunbury, and Pakenham” — as Pedestrian put it, “extraordinarily large distances away from the CBD”. Yes, we know. But that also means you can actually buy a house here without selling a kidney. And if you’ve got kids, there’s **Deep Creek Eco Play**, **Kristina Court** for Christmas lights, and a playgroup at **Pakenham Baptist Church** on Bentley Court. It’s not all stench and train delays — just mostly.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks “city fringe” means Brunswick. Anyone who whinges about a 90-minute commute. Anyone who can’t handle the occasional whiff of decay.

Who would love it? Families who want a backyard. Tradies who love a brewery with local flavours. People who don’t mind being the last stop on the line — because at least you get a seat back. And anyone who’s ever thought, “You know what? I can handle a bit of stench for a three-bedroom house under $600K.” Welcome to Pakenham. Bring a peg.
