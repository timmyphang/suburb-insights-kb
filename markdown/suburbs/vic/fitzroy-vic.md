# Fitzroy (VIC)

Slug: `fitzroy-vic`

We wake up in Fitzroy, roll out of bed in a converted warehouse with exposed brick and a ceiling leak we call "character," and immediately face the morning's first existential crisis: do we queue at Egglab on Johnson Street for a soft scram on a brioche-adjacent bun, or do we get the creamy breakfast congee opposite Edinburgh Gardens at that sunny corner spot where you can stand and drink Standing Room's house blend? The answer, as always, involves both — because we are Fitzroy, and we treat breakfast like a competitive sport.

Time Out called us the "coolest neighbourhood in Australia" (#27 in the world, thank you), and we wear that like a badge sewn onto a vintage leather jacket from Out of the Closet. Yes, we have a boutique hotel (StandardX on Rose Street), a wine bar from Andrew McConnell (Marion, right next to Cutler on Gertrude Street), and a pub with a Tuesday steak night (Marquis of Lorne, $25 sirloin, chips and salad). But we also have a Spanish Club on Johnston Street that survives only because a developer ponied up $35 million for luxury apartments above a tapas bar. Gentrification? We invented it, then sold the T-shirt at Third Drawer Down behind a graffiti-tagged garage door.

And the drama? Oh, the drama. A queer street artist called Lauren YS painted a bondage mural and the critics lost their minds. A bunch of Gen Z blokes threw a garage party in Fitzroy and it went global on TikTok — turns out it was in AFL legend Brian Taylor's actual garage. Someone strapped a penny farthing to a car. Peak Fitzroy achieved.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who wants a quiet life, a Woolies that doesn't smell like sourdough and kombucha, or a pub where you can watch the footy without overhearing a conversation about the semiotics of the Basque cheesecake. Also, if you're a Liberal man sitting in a Fitzroy North wine bar expecting us to split the bill in the name of equality — we see you.

But who would love it? The person who believes that an old Spanish club, a secondhand hotspot, and a 150-year-old pub with a recent spit polish can somehow coexist. We're Melbourne's oldest suburb (1839), still wearing our history like a slightly banged-up leather jacket. Come for the murals, stay because you can't afford to leave. Fitzroy: we're not trying to be cool — we just are.
