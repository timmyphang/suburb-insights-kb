# Doncaster (VIC)

Slug: `doncaster-vic`

We don't call it a morning in Doncaster—we call it a **circuit**. Start at Sushi Jiro in Westfield, where the world’s longest circular sushi train (30.6 metres, if you’re counting) takes a solid 90 seconds to loop past your seat. That’s 80 diners at once, all silently judging who grabs the last salmon nigiri. Then you wander past the new $30-million rooftop precinct that promised Dohtonbori okonomiyaki and Betty’s Burgers—still half-finished, but hey, the pink Pretzel shop is fully operational, so priorities.

Back in the day, this was farmland. Literally. Nicks Wine Merchants started as Nicks Groceries in 1958 when Doncaster was still feeding Melbourne, not just parking its cars. Now three grandsons run the place, holding 500 single malts and a Grange that costs more than your first car. They’ll order you any obscure spirit you want—just don’t ask for beer. “Dan Murphy’s across the street” is their polite suggestion.

For a while we had a proper cafe culture: Three Monkeys Place (RIP 2017) doing soft-shell crab burgers and yuzu mayo, and Meno Zero gelato serving pizza-flavoured scoops on Doncaster Road. That caprese gelato—tomato, mozzarella, basil—was the most Italian thing in the east until the pasta shortage hit. Now we get our kicks from Urban List guides: Secret Kitchen yum cha (since 2007, still the same trolley drama) and Pho Thin’s new outpost.

Petty local drama? The Australia Post on Doncaster Road has Google reviews so bad that Whirlpool users dedicated a whole thread to it. And if you’ve ever tried to service a BMW at Doncaster Audi’s neighbour… let’s just say a $25K DPF repair is not the flex you think it is.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who thinks “nightlife” means more than the Doncaster Hotel or a late-night Zero95 slice. Who loves it? Families who want safe streets, good schools, and a Westfield that has literally everything—including a Lego Star Wars exhibition that made us the envy of every kid in Melbourne. We’re not trendy. We’re just… very well-stocked.
