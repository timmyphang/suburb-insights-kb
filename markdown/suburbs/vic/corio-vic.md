# Corio (VIC)

Slug: `corio-vic`

We’re Corio. We’re the suburb that made international headlines for being where a stolen food truck from Preston was found dumped. Yes, that was us. Rahaf Al Khatib’s beloved Falastini truck turned up here on a Tuesday night in December 2024. We didn’t steal it — we just… found it. Or someone did. Anyway, welcome to Corio, where even the crime is a bit of a letdown.

Back in the day, our morning routine involved waking up to the smell of the Viva Energy refinery — which, as Pedestrian TV reported in April 2026, had a major fire just after 11pm. Explosions, flames, the works. But we powered through. No flat whites here, just the faint taste of industrial ambition and the knowledge that Richard Marles, our local MP and Deputy PM, is probably somewhere on a plane while we’re trying to figure out if the V/Line will actually stop at Corio station today. (It does, occasionally — the Big Bash league bus proved that.)

For years, the only reason anyone outside Geelong has heard of us is Geelong Grammar’s Corio campus. That’s where the kids learn equestrian and fly planes from Avalon airport. Meanwhile, the rest of us are just trying to get on the free commuter ferry that Concrete Playground promised would run from Corio Bay to Docklands. It ran twice in July 2021. We’re still waiting for the third.

Petty local drama? In 2019, Whirlpool forums were arguing about which Melbourne suburb is “blackest” — and Corio got a mention for having “significant African communities.” We’re fine with that — the forum threads are about as sophisticated as our town planning.

Who would hate it here? Anyone expecting a Brunswick bar crawl. The only bar guide we got was for Brunswick, not Corio. Miss us with your craft Negronis.

Who would love it? People who appreciate a suburb that can host a refinery fire, a stolen food truck, and the Deputy PM’s electorate office all in the same week — and still have time to judge the BBL bus schedule. Come for the chaos, stay because you missed the last V/Line.
