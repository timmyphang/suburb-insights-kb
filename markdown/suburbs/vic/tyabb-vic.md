# Tyabb (VIC)

Slug: `tyabb-vic`

There is an hour in early spring when the Mornington-Tyabb Road smells like winner’s hay and burnt snags, and every local with a sensible hat and a folding chair decamps to the Peninsula Cup. That’s our annual moment in the sun — and truth be told, it’s pretty much our only moment. As Broadsheet noted back in 2015, the William Hill Peninsula Cup Day offered “a laid-back coastal alternative to its city counterparts.” Translation: we don’t have the crowds, the fashion stakes, or the $100k millinery prizes. We have the grandstand reno they unveiled that year, some live music, and the strong suspicion that your cousin’s boyfriend is running the BBQ.

The rest of the year, Tyabb is a postcode that appears in Sitchu’s dropdown menu between Tuerong and Tylden. We are the “oh, you turn off before Hastings” suburb. The only businesses we can point to are the ones you pass on the way to somewhere better — a servo, a bakery, a landscape supply yard. If you live here, you drive everywhere. If you visit, you’re probably lost.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who needs a third wave cafe, a co-working space, or a reason to wear linen on a weekday. The 2015 race day vibe is still the peak social calendar. The rest is paddocks, utes, and the faint sound of the freeway from the next town over.

Who would love it? People who want to live within an hour of the city but never actually see it. People who genuinely enjoy the “drive to the racecourse, park in a paddock, drink warm chardonnay from a plastic cup” experience. People who know that “laid-back coastal alternative” is code for “no one will hassle you about your outfit.” The Peninsula Cup put us on the map — and frankly, we’re still happy with that one dot.
