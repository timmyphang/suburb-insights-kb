# Joondanna (NSW)

Slug: `joondanna-nsw`

We like to think our Saturday starts with a gentle chaos: the kids out the door to the double‑storey Green Leaves Early Learning that opened mid‑2021 (we all pretended the carpark was organised), while the rest of us consult the MumsPages like it’s a neighbourhood bible — classes, sports, family support, the lot. For a place that isn’t trying too hard, Joondanna has always leaned into being useful rather than flashy.

Back in the day the food guides talked us up as if we were Perth’s best kept secret: Padthai Cafe on Wanneroo Road (17B) got a shout for being the little spot where you could feed a famished crew for cheap — Pad Thai and satay, apparently, were the kind of reliable things you bragged about. Marco’s Organic Pizza — the Joondanna outpost of that 1976 favourite — even earned a vegan cameo (pea and mint pizza on sourdough, who knew?) and got name‑checked in Urban List roundups. So yes, we were that kind of suburb: unpretentious carbs and a vegan option for the mate who’s suddenly eating green.

If you were a person who needed treatment plans named after gemstones, Kardia Skin and Body at 47 Tyler St used to be on the facials lists, offering sculpting facials with buccal massage and targeted serums for the glow crowd. Meanwhile, those craving a proper local drink had to take a ten‑to‑fifteen‑minute walk up to Mount Hawthorn, where Little Brother Small Bar at 19A Green Street quietly filled the “where do we go after the kids are asleep?” gap.

There’s not a lot of scandal in the files — mostly directories and helpful parenting pages — which is its own kind of charm. People who hate dogs, children, and being within walking distance of a bar that isn’t technically in the suburb will find it dull. Nightlife chasers who want late‑night neon and incessant options will sulk.

Who’d love it? Young families, the person who judges suburbs by where their kids’ classes are listed, vegans with a soft spot for sourdough innovation, and anyone who appreciates that we’ve always been quietly handy rather than desperately trendy. We’ll keep our pad thai, our pea‑and‑mint pizza stories, and our directories — thanks, Joondanna.
