# Smithfield (NSW)

Slug: `smithfield-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start Saturday with the smell of something glorious and defiant — Delizioso’s Nutella doughnuts on Dublin Street. Broadsheet reminded us it was a family-run Sicilian bakery whose fit-out literally came from Palermo in shipping containers, and yes, $2.50 doughnuts were a very good idea.

Our weekend ritual (when we had one) rolled through a very specific food mafia: Mr Chorizo and Parillada for Uruguayan-style charcoal barbecue, Saigon Hustle doing the Southeast Asian heavy lifting (the one that spawned Hustlers in the CBD, if you read Broadsheet), and the long-running comfort of charred-bird cults — El Jannah and Frango both had drive-throughs in Smithfield, according to the papers, so late-night loyalties could get fierce without anyone needing to leave their car.

We don’t have much scandal documented — no council coups, no headline-grabbing feuds — just a civic personality defined by bread, cheese and excellent flame. Concrete Playground catalogued local events and even a Monte Fresco cheesemaking workshop; Monte Fresco was founded here in 2015 by Sam Montalto and ran classes out of 753 The Horsley Drive, which felt proudly specific and oddly domestic all at once. Urban List pointed out our ricotta reputation; we’ll wear that like a badge.

Who would hate living here? People who need a freshly curated inner-city playlist for every coffee purchase, or anyone who thinks “authentic” must be wrapped in oak and Instagram filters. We’re not trying to be sleek — we’ve got grub that speaks louder than our branding.

Who would love it? Cheese obsessives, people who will defend a doughnut price like it’s personal, anyone chasing an honest char from a mysterious urn of coals, and new parents who found a sensible ally in Smithfield Moms Collective when they needed community, not performative advice.

Look, the papers may have filed these stories some time ago. But the smells, the loyalties and the recipes? Those don’t leave Dublin Street overnight.
