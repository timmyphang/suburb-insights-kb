# Peakhurst (NSW)

Slug: `peakhurst-nsw`

Saturday mornings here used to look like a slow trawl down Lorraine Street — we’d spy whoever was first through the door at 70 Lorraine Street and whisper, “Yep, that’s Sandose,” because Broadsheet once put it on a list of new Sydney cafes. They named the owners — Nick Retsas and Ethan Kayem — and the kind of things that make suburban food writers swoon: banana bread iced lattes, creamy coconut cold brews, a stuffed Thoroughbread schiacciata and, yes, a classic smash burger. We liked that a lot, even if the review felt like a badge for our little corner.

By mid-morning we’d dodge the pram brigade heading for Oatley Park — which, if you’ve read the old guides, you might remember as Peakhurst Park back in the day — and stroll the Georges River river beaches while pretending we’re getting our cardio in. If the mood struck, we’d chase a food-truck sighting: Concrete Playground once noted Caminito can be caught regularly at Peakhurst’s Foodies Organic Market, and we still brag about that like it’s the Sydney festival.

Our local dining folklore includes Cheatday — Urban List flagged it for “totally outrageous burgers” and a three‑cheese Mi Goreng toastie that, frankly, should come with a warning label. And for the evening crew there was the Peakhurst Inn, which Time Out pointed out as the place with two bars and the sort of uncomplicated socialising our suburb does best.

There’s no juice-fuelled petty local drama listed in the clippings we’ve got — just a steady, practical community: Playgroup NSW ran sessions at the Peakhurst‑Lugarno Uniting Church at 909 Forest Road in nearby Lugarno; The Acro Company used to run school‑holiday workshops here; Learning Links had a centre offering multidisciplinary support for kids. CareforKids showed 33 childcare services in Peakhurst with a reported average fee of about $131.57/day and plenty of vacancies back then — yes, we’re basically the suburb of sensible childcare.

Who’d hate it here? Nightlife chasers and anyone pining for constant, shiny inner‑city fuss. Who’d love it? Families, dog‑walkers, weekend food‑truck hunters and anyone who likes their parks with river views and their cafes quietly praised in Broadsheet. We’ll keep our river, our sandwich legends and our two‑bar pub — and quietly enjoy being the suburb that doesn’t try too hard.
