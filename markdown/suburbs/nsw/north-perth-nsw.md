# North Perth (NSW)

Slug: `north-perth-nsw`

Back in the day we’d promise visitors a proper North Perth morning and mean it — three kilometres from the city and somehow a whole village’s worth of personality squeezed between Fitzgerald and Angove. As Broadsheet used to note, KCH was the cosy spot dishing up a rust‑orange Sarawak‑style laksa; Time Out once reminded us Namaste India served eggplant curry in an eggplant‑shaped bowl (because of course it did). 

Our weekend ritual used to look like this: stumble out to 45 Hobart St for a Hobart Deli brekky (Urban List flagged it as a sneaky gem), amble down Angove Street poking through op shops — Red Cross Shop at 502 Charles St got a name‑check for actual designer finds — then let the kids loose at Hyde Park’s water play or Hobart Street Park (Perth Mums Group had the intel). If we were feeling virtuous we’d hit Kyilla Community Farmers’ Market (Sitchu) for something that called itself local and tasted honest. By night the Rosemount Hotel at 459 Fitzgerald Street was where we accepted that live music triumphs over silence — Concrete Playground even called its beer garden one of the best.

There isn’t a juicy council scandal in these snippets; the local press mostly did what we do best — eat, shop, raise kids and sort each other out. Rise & Thrive’s programs for young mums show another side of us: practical, community‑minded and quietly useful, not just latte‑reciting aestheticists (their work with young mothers was documented in the local listings).

Who would hate living here? People who want a beige postcode with zero character, no buskers, no weekend markets, and who prefer their suburbs to come without pizza that claims Sardinian soul (Acqua e Sale got that shout in Time Out) or conti rolls from Di Chiera Brothers that cult‑followers swear by.

Who would love it? Food obsessives, families who need Hyde Park and Playgroup WA’s North Perth rooms, bargain‑hunters at the Red Cross, and anyone who appreciates a village strip with a rock ’n’ roll pub out the front. We’re a bit loud, a bit flavourful, and we’ve always been better for it.
