# Hinton (NSW)

Slug: `hinton-nsw`

It’s 6am, the river’s doing that nervous half-rise, and for a glorious two-minute window we get national recognition — Pedestrian TV once stuck us in a headline about the NSW floods: “communities still cut off.” That’s our sort of red carpet. We call it fame by water.

Back in the day — and yes, everything that mentions our name is older stuff — most of what the internet knows about “Hinton” isn’t about our corner of the state. There’s S.E. Hinton on the bookshelf, Jamal Hinton’s Thanksgiving text story in the culture feed, a Joshua Hinton cooking up family curry in a Brisbane Festival piece, Lynda Hinton credited for Hunter Valley snaps, a Tasha Hinton blogging about being a WAHM, and even a Nick Hinton popping up in a memes roundup. We like that variety; it keeps people guessing which Hinton they’re actually reading about.

Our morning routine is modest and practical: we check the river levels, check the older clippings that mention us, and remember that when things go sideways the media remembers our postcode. Pedestrian TV’s phrasing — “tens of thousands are still isolated, especially in places like Hinton, Taree, Kempsey and Dungog” — is the kind of blunt compliment we wear with a shrug. It’s not glamour, but it is memorable.

Local drama? We don’t invent it. The real petty battle here is with geography: bridges, creeks and the occasional generosity of the weather. We get celebrated more for being cut off than for an artisanal anything. And honestly, we’re fine with that.

Who would hate living here? If you need constant curated content about your suburb, weeknight venues and the latest city festival to flex on your feed, you’ll be exasperated. We’re not on a glossy map; we’re the place that makes the headlines when the river remembers its enthusiasm.

Who would love it? People who like a place that holds its stories — resilient, quietly stubborn and blessedly free of trend-chasing. Also, if you enjoy being part of a name that turns up in novels, memes, festivals and family tales across the internet, welcome: we’re Hinton, and our brand of fame arrives with rain.
