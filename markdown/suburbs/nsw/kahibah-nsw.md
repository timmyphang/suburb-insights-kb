# Kahibah (NSW)

Slug: `kahibah-nsw`

We wake up on Hexham Street like it’s a local ritual: kids in one hand, a cricket bat in the other, and the vague idea that today we might make it past the playground gate. Back in the day Kahibah Park Playground (Hexham Street) was the big day out — a small but comprehensive spread set among the trees where kids could run until you couldn’t remember the last time you had a coffee that wasn’t lukewarm thermos sorrow. As Lake Mac Family Life put it, it’s a proper shady patch for little ones and fish & chips dreams.

Weekends drift into bush mode. For years the Yuelarbah Track and Glenrock walks have been our excuse to disappear for 2–3 hours, starting from the Burwood Road carpark near the Fernleigh Track crossing and bargaining with the kids that Leichhardt’s lookout is “just round the corner.” Bridges, waterfalls and a suspiciously photogenic bit of rainforest pop up halfway through, while the Fernleigh Track’s Kahibah-to-Adamstown section has long been listed as that satisfying 3.6km fix, crossing Flaggy Creek and Little Flaggy Creek like a nature-themed obstacle course. As Lake Mac Family Life and Newy With Kids noted, it’s the sort of thing you brag about on a Sunday but wheelbarrow back from on Monday.

If you’re keeping score, Kahibah Sports Club used to be the place for 18-hole mini golf — fully shaded and annoyingly good for all ages — and Sherwood Coffee Bar was noted as sitting right across the road from the playground. Both get name-checked in the guides, so if nostalgia is your cardio, we get you.

Who would hate living here? If you’re allergic to trees, quiet little victories (like finding a parking spot), or can’t abide family groups having actual, non-espresso-based fun — this isn’t your district.

Who would love it? Families who like parks that actually have shade, walkers who enjoy a rainforest waterfall detour, and anyone who values an 18-hole mini-golf scorecard over rooftop bar bragging rights. We’ll still be here on Hexham Street, pretending the kids are exercising when really they’re perfecting the art of negotiating a second sausage roll.
