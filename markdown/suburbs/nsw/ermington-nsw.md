# Ermington (NSW)

Slug: `ermington-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed, wander down to the Parramatta River and feel like we’d nicked the best seat in Sydney — Halvorsen Park (40 Seamist Avenue) gives you those views and the irony that the “new” suburb still insists on being humble. For years locals treated the first Saturday of the month like a small civic ritual: Market by the River at Halvorsen Park, artisan bread, flowers and whatever else you needed to convince your feed that you were outdoorsy. Time Out wrote it up like it was a destination; we treated it like a reason to leave our pyjamas.

Weekends meant kid-traffic too: River Park (aka Pirate Park) on Arista Way got a shiny reopening in late June 2021 and the playground gossip circuit hasn’t stopped since. The Ermington Branch Library down near the shops on River Road ran the kind of kids’ programs (Redsee’s Bibs ‘n’ Books, Redsee’s Story Time) that make you swap life stories with strangers over second-hand Lego. ParraParents also chronicled a riverside “new cafe” at Halvorsen Park — for a minute we were all becoming people who drank coffee by a slide.

We’re also the kind of place that enters the council-run Free Plant Day hall of fame — Cowells Lane Reserve at the corner of Cowells Lane and Monroe Street once handed out trees and shrubs (bring proof of residency, yes they will check). If you needed a plotline about small-town catastrophe, Pedestrian’s takedown of the iconic putt-putt closing in Ermington provided the lament: fun, apparently, is very much handled locally.

Who’d hate living here? If you loathe mums’ meetups, pram walks and local support groups — Playgroup NSW, Mum Walk, Mama Tribe and Netmums have all made Ermington their patch — you’ll feel like a guest at a party you forgot to RSVP to. If you can’t abide monthly markets and riverside playgrounds, move along.

Who’d love it? Families, plant collectors with Tetris-level boot-packing skills, market stalkers and anyone who likes the idea of green parks — George Kendall Riverside Park and the Ermington Foreshore are the kind of places we pretend we discovered. Yes, we’re quiet. Yes, we have a library that reads kids stories like it’s performance art. And no, we won’t apologise for loving a suburb where the river does most of the showing off.
