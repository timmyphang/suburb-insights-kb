# Sorrento (NSW)

Slug: `sorrento-nsw`

We wake up to the sound of gulls and the distant clink of a ferry, because of course our village has its own runway for the well-heeled commuter — hello, the new ferry terminal and Onda, which Concrete Playground pointed out opened on March 26, 2026, hovering over the bay with that glass‑floor flex. Only in Sorrento could a pier double as a dining room and a humble seaside ego trip.

Our morning is a slow one: a walk along Sorrento Beach and a poke around the rock pools at Ocean Beach (Sitchu’s readers swear by them), then a tactical photo-op from the Hotel Sorrento hill — the place was sent for a glow-up, got luxe suites, a 30‑metre pool and a spa, and now poses like it owns the horizon. The Conti, our grand old dame, has been renovated too — Broadsheet wrote about its reopening after a big restoration — so there’s always somewhere suitably historic to sip something overpriced and pretend it’s provenance.

We’re not big on drama (that’s for festivals), but when the Sorrento Writers Festival rolls around — yes, the 2026 run from April 23–26 packed 155 events and 200 speakers, Sitchu reminded us — even our quiet village turns into a literati speed‑dating event. You’ll see more sentence fragments at the book tents than at the pier.

Who would hate living here? If you’re someone who needs constant novelty, anonymous footpaths and zero chance of bumping into your school friend while brunching, you’ll resent the village hum. If you prefer your coastline rugged and uncurated, our carefully curated Mediterranean energy (we’re looking at you, rooftop inspo and Onda’s thinned‑crust theatrics) will be tiring.

Who would love it here? People who like their weekends scenic and slightly theatrical: spa devotees (Aurora Spa & Bathhouse gets a name‑check), festival goers, cliff‑top walkers, and anyone with a soft spot for seaside hotels pretending to be Riviera villas. As Urban List once noted, even the humble Vanilla Slice Cafe earned cult status — and if that’s not local pride, what is? We’re small, we’re scenic, and yes, we’ll always accept that our biggest flex is making a ferry terminal feel like a terrace in the Med.
