# Cheltenham (NSW)

Slug: `cheltenham-nsw`

We’re the lot who know exactly where the school bell echoes off the sandstone: the Cheltenham school run is a competitive sport and, yes, we cheer for the kids but whisper about the assembly coffee situation.

Back in the day the internet’s attention here was sporadic — when it came, it stuck to the parts of our life that make good copy. As Pedestrian reported, one of those moments involved Cheltenham Girls High School and Principal Suellen Lawrence addressing students over a video after a swimming carnival about dressing “conservatively” around male teachers. If you were here that week the town felt like a comment thread with a PA system — equal parts earnest, baffled and deeply invested in uniforms.

We also had that unforgettable human-interest headline: a Cheltenham woman, Lillian Ip, reportedly found after five days in the bush with wine and lollies. It reads like a suburban folktale now — the kind of story you tell over fences to explain why someone will never run out of emergency snacks again. We mention it because it’s the sort of oddly specific local lore that becomes our collective small-town brag.

Who would hate living here? If you need a 24/7 entertainment precinct, micro-district latte culture or an endless parade of food trucks — you’ll be pacing the carpark by day three. We’re not polished for spoilers; we’re quietly stubborn, fond of our rituals and suspicious of anyone who refers to the school tuckshop as “iconic.”

Who would love it here? Families who appreciate a neighbourhood that still has a sense of shape (and a principal who’s unafraid of a stern communique), people who trade predictable playlists for unpredictable headlines, and those who enjoy communal storytelling — the kind that starts with “remember when…” and ends with everyone knowing precisely what you mean.

We’re not flashy. We don’t court viral fame — but when the internet does turn up, it remembers Principal Suellen Lawrence’s memo and Lillian Ip’s improbable survival, and somehow that feels very Cheltenham: small, resilient and impossible to fully explain to anyone from a bigger postcode.
