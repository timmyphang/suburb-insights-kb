# Butler (NSW)

Slug: `butler-nsw`

We live in Butler, NSW — yes, that Butler — and honestly half our identity comes from other people using our name more stylishly than we ever could.

Back in the day the city got all the glamour: The Butler in Potts Point was the terrace with the skyline view (123 Victoria St, if you must know), that French‑colonial fit‑out with sandstone walls, a sun‑drenched rooftop and even an Amok Island mural. As Concrete Playground and Sitchu remembered, the place was Applejack‑adjacent — Hamish Watts and Ben Carroll’s sort of thing — and Sitchu even listed Amber Doig’s bottomless brunch at $125 a head. Broadsheet called it unbeatable for city views. Meanwhile, in Surry Hills the Balkan Butler was quietly folding burek under the watchful eye of David Nedelkovski and his mum, according to Urban List. And somewhere a film crew once turned the old Magpie Milk Bar into a New York bodega for Gerard Butler’s movie — then Macgregor’s moved in with a three‑burger menu and chips cooked in beef tallow, Broadsheet reported.

We say all this not to feel slighted but to accept our lot: Butler is the name everyone wants to put on a terrace, a burger shop or a Balkan bakery — but not necessarily on our suburb. Our postal code gets none of the glossy write‑ups; our claims to fame are mostly borrowed nouns in other people’s lifestyle pieces. The Mother Butler Guild pops up in a different register entirely, and local parenting blogs remember My Butler Service (formerly The Shirt Butler) as the laundry pick‑up that did the rounds.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who likes their suburb to come with reviews, a mural and a bottomless brunch price. Who’d love it? People who enjoy having a name that confuses journalists, foodies and film crews — and gives us free material at dinner parties.

So we keep our quiet streets, let the Potts Point terrace take the skyline photos, and grin whenever someone says, “You’re from Butler? Like the restaurant?” We’ll take the mix‑ups. It’s cheaper than a mural.
