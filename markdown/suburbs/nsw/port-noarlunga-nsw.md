# Port Noarlunga (NSW)

Slug: `port-noarlunga-nsw`

We stand on the jetty with our knees salty and our standards remarkably low — that's Port Noarlunga for you. Back in the day the guidebooks couldn’t stop raving: Port Noarlunga Beach was shoved onto every “best beaches” list for snorkelling the heritage‑listed reef, and Urban List practically handed out snorkel masks at the tourist information desk.

Our weekend routine used to be reassuringly simple. A stroll along the jetty (Concrete Playground reminded folk you could amble here after work), a wander up Gawler Street to where Noori occupied that charming old 1914 horse‑barn-turned-everything spot at 20 Gawler Street — Broadsheet loved the original well and the South Aussie wine list — and an eye‑roll at whatever the “newest kitchen and bar” was calling itself (Fleet Street Social had its moment, apparently serving cocktails and share plates seven days a week). The Arts Centre at 22 Gawler Street, the Licensed Post Office at 23 Gawler Street and the RSL on the Esplanade gave the place that cosy small‑town paperwork vibe.

We don’t have local scandals splashed across the front pages; our drama is more reef‑vs‑rubber‑duck. The writing about us is almost embarrassingly wholesome — Time Out and Sitchu sent photographers to the new Witton Bluff Base Trail linking us to Christies Beach with that 420‑metre boardwalk, and travel roundups keep pairing us with McLaren Vale and the Onkaparinga River National Park like a slightly smug seaside‑meets‑wine weekend combo.

Who would hate living here? People who need three laneway bars within walking distance, zero sand in their shoes and a subway every 400 metres. Also anyone allergic to playgrounds — Jubilee Park Adventure Playground on Saltfleet Street gets taken very seriously by small people and their chauffeurs.

Who would love it here? Families (the Primary School & Aquatic Centre do the heavy lifting), divers and snorkellers chasing that reef, walkers who now get a fancy coastal trail to brag about, and anyone who thinks a pintxos plate in a converted horse barn is peak culture. We’re compact, salty and annoyingly picturesque. Come for the reef, stay because you forgot your keys in the post office on Gawler Street.
