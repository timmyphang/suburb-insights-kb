# Forestville (NSW)

Slug: `forestville-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start a Forestville Sunday like this: pile the kids into the station wagon (yes, we still have those), head for Davidson Park for a sizzle and a stroll along the Lyrebird Track, and pretend we’d discovered some secret tucked into Garigal National Park. Concrete Playground called it perfect for a long graze — and between the barbecues and the creek views, we can see why.

Our weekend strip was never trying to be the CBD. Urban List remembers Ember Sourdough Pizza tucked into a quiet Forestville run by Brian Bao and Parag Panjwani, turning out 72‑hour fermented pies for anyone who fancied leaving the inner city for a proper slice. If you were after comfort food and no fuss, Old English Fish ’N’ Chips showed up in potato‑scallop roundups as the local no‑nonsense option. Time Out used to point people to Green Tucker for pantry bits and biodynamic biscuits — yes, the suburb had its own artisanal cupboard.

We’re not going to pretend everything here is a glossy Instagram reel. The coverage we’ve got is lovingly old‑school: community arts at The Forest Community Arts Centre, pottery kilns and classrooms where real people make things with their hands. And for the new parents among us, Playgroup NSW, Mama Tribe and Match Up Mums were the rabbit hole you fell into when you needed friends who understood nap routines and snack swaps.

Petty local drama? The archives don’t bother with our petty lives, so neither will we — which suits us fine. We prefer polite queueing at the pizza place to soap‑opera council fights.

Who would hate it here: nightclub hunters and anyone who needs a thousand cocktail bars within walking distance. Forestville’s charm is low‑key — it’s parks, a tidy pizza joint, community arts and pantry shops, not a 24/7 neon district.

Who would love it: families who want a barbie with a view, makers who want a kiln to call their own, and people happy to trade frenetic city nights for proper slices and slow walks. For years the local press has been quietly noting these things — and honestly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
