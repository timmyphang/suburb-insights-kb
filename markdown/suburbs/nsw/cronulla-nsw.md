# Cronulla (NSW)

Slug: `cronulla-nsw`

Back in the day we’d stagger down from the train, sand in our seats and coffee on our breath, and pretend the five‑minute walk from Cronulla station to Cronulla Beach was some kind of pilgrimage. We’ve always loved that you can hit the surf, stroll Cronulla Park and be in a bistro queue before the sand dries.

Our weekend routine used to read like a hospitality roll call: a slow breakfast at one of the long‑running local cafés — Concrete Playground even name‑checked Grind Espresso — then a saunter past the new(ish) precincts that have tried to keep up with us. Parc Pavilion arrived promising Euro‑style dining, Bobby’s set up on the waterfront with Mediterranean seafood vibes, Fred’s Providore doubled as a morning providore and a moody wine bar by night, and Meadow Music Room brought vinyl DJs and live jazz when we wanted to sound cultured. Urban List and Broadsheet both mused that Cronulla had “levelled up” into a dining destination — and we lapped it up.

We won’t pretend there aren’t dramas. The odd night at Shark Park has spilled into headlines — Pedestrian reported on a Roosters vs Sharks finals brawl that had police sniffing around the stands — and our coastline has had its moments too, with severe erosion nibbling into North Cronulla and making us all imagine a life of beachfront living that might be mostly wet sand. There was even a Supreme Court ruling that stopped a proposed Cronulla Riots memorial from going ahead; the newspapers kept the rest of us awkwardly polite on the topic.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe queues at CronullaFest (it drew over 50,000 people in 2023) or can’t stand the idea of family mornings — Play2Thrive and Bonjour Babies run the weekly meetups at Cronulla Central — this place will test your patience. If cliffs and coastal walks don’t do it for you, the $6.9 million Hungry Point Reserve cliff‑top walkway that opened in December 2025 will be extra annoying.

Who would love it? Families, festival‑goers and anyone who likes a seaside town that’s trying very hard to be both laidback and fancy. With Pippis at Calico Marina, 1908 on Surf Road and plans like Volta from the Homer team on Surf Lane featured in the mags, Cronulla keeps flirting with being both our messy, sandy hometown and a place the city can’t stop writing about. We’re proud, slightly grubby and forever on the lookout for the next espresso that thinks it’s a DJ.
