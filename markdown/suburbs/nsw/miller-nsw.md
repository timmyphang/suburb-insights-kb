# Miller (NSW)

Slug: `miller-nsw`

We live in Miller, NSW — yes, that Miller — and if you’re looking for a hot-take on our main drag, prepare to be underwhelmed. The press keeps mistaking us for other Millers, so we’ve learned to lean into it: back in the day the only Millers anyone wrote about were bakeries, Miller Streets and the odd film director.

Our morning routine is mostly imagination. We pretend our local has a loaf worthy of Miller & Baker — Broadsheet called them “one of the few Australian bakeries milling its own flour,” and liked that owner Mark Taylor learned the trade in Denmark — then we realise their Lake Street, Perth, bread is only a page away. Urban List even praised Miller & Baker’s kimchi toastie and ham-and-cheese classics, which makes breakfast feel like a spectator sport from across the country. Over on the Mornington Peninsula, Broadsheet rhapsodised about Miller’s Bread Kitchen in Dromana and its rotating goodies; we scroll through those photos like people who can’t have nice things.

The media’s other favourite Miller is Miller Street. Concrete Playground teased Toki Bistro & Bar coming to Miller Street in North Sydney, and reviewed Ost. Cafe at 73 Miller Street — Sitchu even lists Rengaya at 73 Miller Street for bento. Meanwhile, Time Out keeps sending George Miller to our timelines for Furiosa and Mad Max locations. It’s flattering, in a “we’re the cousin on the family tree who only shows up in the credits” kind of way.

There’s no local scandal to gossip about — the archive is mostly other Millers doing their thing — so we gossip about how tiresome it would be to live somewhere that actually had a queue for a kimchi toastie. People who’d hate Miller: anyone who needs Michelin-starred excitement on their corner or thinks every suburb should have its own Miller & Baker. They’d be miserable.

Who’d love it here? Folks who treasure being underestimated. We’re affordable, low-key and mysteriously absent from every food critic’s map unless they mean someone else. Come for the quiet, stay because the rest of Australia keeps sending us crumbs of fame from other Millers. As Broadsheet once reminded us, being “Miller” is apparently a national pastime — even if it’s usually someone else’s.
