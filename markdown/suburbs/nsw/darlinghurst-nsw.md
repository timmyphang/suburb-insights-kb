# Darlinghurst (NSW)

Slug: `darlinghurst-nsw`

Back in the day we’d wander out of the city and into Darlinghurst like it was an open‑plan cupboard of delights: a warren of streets where Bills on Liverpool Street did breakfast pilgrimages, Dimitri’s Pizzeria and Paulie’s Pizza Pezzo kept carb budgets honest, and the Darlo Village Hotel (234 Palmer Street) pretended to be a sleepy local while quietly redoing itself into a modern pub with attitudes.

We know the rhythm here — daytime gallery browsing and boutique-avoiding, then Stanley Street and Crown Street doing their nightly costume change into loud shoes and louder playlists. Time Out used to tell people to catch a show at Darlinghurst Theatre Company or a gig at Oxford Art Factory; Concrete Playground even celebrated Dead Set’s underground rock vibe at Taylor Square. For years the Darlo Night Market under the twinkle of St Johns Church (120 Darlinghurst Road) gave us a monthly excuse to wear pre-loved things and pretend we weren’t out for the food stalls.

Petty local drama? Darlinghurst had its fair share of rebrands and exits: Pink Pony got a rename after a backlash, ARQ went through a reinvention to AURA, and Swillhouse’s Shady Pines Saloon said its last drinks before a redevelopment. Club 77 even closed for an accessibility redesign, promising sensory rooms and braille signage — proof we bicker, then build better.

Who would hate living here: people who like silence, predictable opening hours, or not being cardinal‑directioned by the glow of Oxford Street at 2am. Also anyone allergic to choice — we used to have bars called Love, Tilly Devine, Bitter Phew and The Taphouse, plus a hundred options for pretending you only came out for one drink.

Who would love us: the hungry, the sleepless, the culturally nosy. Foodies who remember Beppi’s long service to Italianness, diners who loved Fabbrica’s pasta takeover, and night-owls who enjoyed the little‑bar culture Broadsheet catalogued with glee. We’re the suburb that’s always been in flux, famous for its mix of theatre, late nights and the odd market under a church. We’ll roast you, but only because we know you’ll laugh and then meet us for dinner.
