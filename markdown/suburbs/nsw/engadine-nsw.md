# Engadine (NSW)

Slug: `engadine-nsw`

Back in the day our press clippings were all older pieces, so we’ll tell you how Engadine felt when people still wrote things about it: a weekend that’s half bush walk, half sandwich pilgrimage.

We start at the river: we know The Needles like the back of our sandy hands — that historic causeway with the rope swing and the banks where you can pretend you’re in a coming‑of‑age movie. Time Out once flagged it for a tense canoe scene in Apex, so yes, we’ve been weaponised by Netflix and survived to tell the tale. A little further on the Engadine Lagoon sits quieter and greener; Sitchu called it a favourite among those in the know, and we’ll defend its murky charm to anyone who calls it a pond.

Our Saturday ritual used to be half outdoor salvation, half carb-loading. Broadsheet recorded that Loaf opened a second location in Engadine in 2024 at 972–990 Old Princes Highway — the place that did house brews from White Horse Coffee and hawked things like a spiced schnitzel sandwich and slow‑cooked beef. We won’t pretend the coverage in my file is fresh, but we do remember the sandwiches that made the walk from the carpark feel justified.

Community’s a flex here. The Engadine Street Festival — which Sitchu said returns with over 200 stalls — gives us an annual excuse to buy dubious artisan soap and queue for gourmet bites. And if you ever wondered where the small people socialise, Play2Thrive ran an Engadine First Time Parents group at Engadine Community Services (1034–1036 Old Princes Hwy), Wednesdays at 10am — the official version of organised chaos.

Who would hate living here? If you live for late‑night rooftop bars and shortcuts to the CBD, Engadine will feel like a cul‑de‑sac of contentment: too leafy, too local, too likely to have you explaining what The Needles actually are. Who would love it? Families who like a genuine community festival, swimmers who prefer rope swings to lifeguards, and anyone who believes a good sandwich is a civic right.

We’re fond of our small dramas and even fonder of our small victories. Come for the lagoon, stay for whatever’s on the street that weekend — just don’t expect the Herald to be writing about us tomorrow.
