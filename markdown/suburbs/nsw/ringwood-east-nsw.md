# Ringwood East (NSW)

Slug: `ringwood-east-nsw`

We know the exact spot where commuters sigh as the Lilydale train slows into Ringwood East station — that little moment where you decide if you’re heading to Ringwood Lake for a quiet loop or to Eastland for the full suburban spectacle. Back in the day the papers loved telling the story of our shiny, revamped Eastland Shopping Centre; Broadsheet ran an explainer and, yes, we ogled the new retail theatre like it was our town’s debutante ball.

Our Saturday morning loop used to be a very particular ritual: pick up beans from Saazaa Coffee on Olive Grove if you wanted roastery bragging rights, maybe nab a vanilla slice from Bedford Coffee Co. (Urban List once put Ringwood East on the map for that), then wander the Town Square where The Cellar Door by The Public Brewery staged its rooftop hose-down of local produce. 400 Gradi at Eastland — the one on Maroondah Hwy — was the sort of place Broadsheet and Urban List liked to point at when they wanted to prove pizza had matured in the east.

We’re not immune to modern oddities. Broadsheet even wrote about drones — Wing and DoorDash buzzing the Maroondah area and eyeing Eastland — which is both convenience and slightly terrifying if you like your Parmigiana un-delivered. And yes, there was that water warning Broadsheet flagged naming Ringwood East among suburbs told to boil water. Nothing like a community-wide tea-making ban to remind you you live someplace with character.

The local food scene got flattered by Time Out too: Mr Lee’s Foods — the 20-seater with three pork-heavy dishes — was christened a Korean‑Australian institution. Holey Moley x Strike at Eastland gave our midlife executives a place to try karaoke with mini‑golf and pretend they’re still 27.

Who would hate living here? If you crave inner-city anonymity or think “mall” is a dirty word, our Eastland-centric weekends and enthusiastic playgroup listings (shoutout to North Ringwood Playgroup and Ringwood Community Church venues) will make you twitch.

Who’d love it? Families who want a working playgroup, food obsessives hunting a vanilla slice, or anyone who enjoys the comfort of a big suburban centre pretending it’s small-town theatre. We’ll take our quirks, our fountains, and our flying parcels — thanks.
