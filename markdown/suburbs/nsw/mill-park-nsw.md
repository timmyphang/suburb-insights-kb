# Mill Park (NSW)

Slug: `mill-park-nsw`

We know this suburb like the back of our hand — or at least like the noticeboard at Mill Park Community House. Back in the day most of the coverage we could find was a bit thin and often pointed at Melbourne’s Mill Park, so consider this our local love-letter slash roast from the Mill Park that actually uses 68 Mill Park Drive as a meeting point.

Morning routine: we drop the little ones at Collard Family Kindergarten, the boutique long day care that brags about an art room and a baby’s room, then swing by the Community House for a low-cost yoga class. The House — at 68 Mill Park Drive, and yes, they’re a Learn Local provider and an NDIS-registered support agency — runs everything from sewing to cake decorating and patchwork. If you think that sounds twee, you haven’t seen our patchwork quilts of passive-aggressive notes about the mailbox.

Weekends look like this: a playgroup at Huntingdale Hall or the main Community Centre (Playgroup Victoria has the schedules), a stroll to the shelter area off Dunlop Cres for a cunningly supervised chaos picnic, and possibly a Social Mums Club meetup — they even had a Tuesday morning Mill Park meet-up listed. For after-school enrichment, Bricks 4 Kidz still gets mentioned in the local listings and Collard’s curriculum gets the thumbs-up from parents who like routine more than we like quiet.

Petty local drama? The press didn’t give us many scandals — mostly helpful directories and event listings — so we’ll invent only a little: our biggest quarrel is whether ORA Studios’ Pilates is the better sweat or the one at the other end of town. Time Out once put Mill Park on an affordability and liveability list (that was their take), and Westfield Plenty Valley’s redevelopment used to be the shopping gossip — useful if you like choosing which mall lane to fight in.

Who would hate living here: people who crave late-night cocktail speakeasies accessed through trick bookcases — Concrete Playground admired Mill Place Merchants, but that was Melbourne’s scene, not our cul-de-sac.

Who’d love it here: families, crafters, playgroup veterans, and anyone who values a community house that actually runs sewing, yoga and cake decorating rather than pretending to know how to fold a fitted sheet. We’re small, practical and quietly brilliant — come for the kindergarten, stay for the patchwork.
