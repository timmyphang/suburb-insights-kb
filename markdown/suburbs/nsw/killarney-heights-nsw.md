# Killarney Heights (NSW)

Slug: `killarney-heights-nsw`

Back in the day we’d queue up like it was a matinee just to get into Fuji Japanese Bakery — custard doughnuts, melonpan and those yakisoba pans that made school lunches feel exotic. Broadsheet remembers it too, and honestly, so do our grandparents.

Our weekend is embarrassingly simple and, frankly, effective: a bushwalk through Garigal, then a slow trundle down Killarney Dve to Flat Rock Beach to cool off after the exertion — Time Out’s idea of a perfect post-hike dip, and they weren’t wrong. If the tide’s playing nice and the kids haven’t absconded with someone’s lunch, it’s very nearly idyllic.

Once a year we don our berets and let the French take over Killarney Heights Public School’s oval. The Killarney Heights French Market — run by the French‑speaking Association of the North Shore — turns the place into 60 stalls of baguette-adjacent chaos, historical reenactments and family activities from 9am–4pm. It’s the one day we pretend our postcode is in Pyrénées rather than the Upper North Shore.

Real estate flex? Sitchu once listed 79 Darley Street — a dual-level family home with poolside entertaining on a 793 sqm block, cheeky reminder that some of us have space for a trampoline and a pool heater. The listing name‑checks Killarney Heights High and the Primary schools; yep, schools are a thing here.

If you need proof we’re very mum-friendly, MumsPages has whole directories for bathroom renovations, party planners, pregnancy massages and even boxing classes in the area. Translation: we renovate, we celebrate kids’ birthdays and occasionally we punch things for stress relief.

Who would hate living here? Nightlife chasers and anyone who needs a 24/7 espresso-and-incident scene. Who would love it? Families, market stalkers, kayakers who know Middle Harbour runs, and people who like their suburb with a bit of community and a lot of sensible backyard.

We’re not trying to be cool. We’re just quietly good at being exactly what we are.
