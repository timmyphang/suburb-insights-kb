# Kelmscott (NSW)

Slug: `kelmscott-nsw`

You know the sound of Kelmscott on a Saturday morning: we used to duck down River Road, past Kelmscott Hall at 60 River Road where graduations and church bazaars used to stretch into afternoons, and nudge our way into Hidden Coffee — the tiny spot tucked behind Foothills Cycles that used to sling Backyard Coffee blend and Holy Bagel Company bagels to cyclists and families. Back in the day, that was our weekday fuel and weekend excuse to pretend we were off for a ride.

Our weekend loop used to include a lap past Frye Park and the Frye Park Pavilion, a detour to Champs at Play on Brant Road when the ankle-biters needed burning out, and the Riverdale Community Centre where Kelmscott Playgroup met at 57 River Rd. We’ve also waved to the Good Shepherd Catholic Church on Streich Ave and the little Congregational Church on Rundle St — the kinds of places that make a town feel stitched together.

We’ll admit it: Kelmscott has had its public dramas. As Your Local Examiner noted, residents showed up to a City of Armadale town‑hall to vent about vacant shopfronts, homelessness and the rougher edges of the main strip. The Kelmscott Progress Network even had a “Make Kelmscott great again” energy — earnest and a little bitter — because yes, we can see the potential and the peeling paint. There’s also the old station master’s house, boarded up in 2014 and still being courted by would‑be renovators. It’s our local soap opera, except with more heritage bricks and fewer dramatic plot twists.

If you need polished, boutique‑strip vibes, Kelmscott will grate on you. If you measure a suburb by emptiness of vacancies and absence of town meetings, you’ll hate the minutes from ours. But if community matters — if play centres like Chipmunks and Champs, volunteer-run playgroups, the Cambodian Buddhist Society monastery on Scott Road (which even fed people after the Kelmscott/Roleystone bushfires), and a hall that holds 400 people mean something — then you’ll love us.

We’re not pretending to be the trendiest postcode, but we’re stubbornly local, practical and full of people who turn up. Come for the bagels we used to queue for, stay for the neighbourhood that still argues with a lot of heart.
