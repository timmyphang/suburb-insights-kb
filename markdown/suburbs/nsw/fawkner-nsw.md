# Fawkner (NSW)

Slug: `fawkner-nsw`

Back in the day we loved how our name did a double-take around the internet: not because we were trending on a weekend brunch map, but because Fawkner Food Bowls turned up as the quiet, grubby-handed supplier to Broadsheet’s food-truck pilgrimage and, separately, because a chef called Frank Fawkner was getting rave reviews up in Pokolbin. We’re honoured, confused, and slightly jealous of anyone who gets hot air balloons over their neighbourhood like the people at Fawkner Park.

Our Saturday starts the same way it always has — drop the kids at the Playgroup Victoria session (yes, we knew the 10:00–12:00 listing, and yes, Lynne Baker has our thanks), wander over to the community house to check the gate on the fenced playground and make sure the childproof lock still works, then swear we’ll make it down to a Fawkner Food Bowls working bee. That community urban farm used to run a market garden, a volunteer program three days a week and the sort of multicultural events that make other suburbs call us “community” in a good way.

There isn’t a lot of flashy press about us — most articles either mean Fawkner Park in South Yarra or are fawning over EXP in Pokolbin — but that suits us. We’re not trying to be a destination precinct with curated aesthetics; we’re the place that keeps the food trucks stocked, the kids safe, and the soil busy.

Who would hate living here? If you need every weekend to look like a glossy guidebook — curated picnic spots, skyline selfies by Moreton Bay figs, or endless lists of “best new cafes” — you’ll be perpetually disappointed. We don’t do boutique drama; we do locked gates that actually keep toddlers in.

Who would love it? Families who appreciate a fenced playground with indoor toilets and a quiet breastfeeding space at the community house. Volunteers who like getting their hands dirty at Fawkner Food Bowls. People who want a suburb that quietly does the important stuff while the rest of Melbourne argues about where to put a rail loop stop. We’re humble, handy and just loud enough to be noticed — on the page, if not on the skyline.
