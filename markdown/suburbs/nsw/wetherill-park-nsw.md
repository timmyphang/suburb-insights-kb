# Wetherill Park (NSW)

Slug: `wetherill-park-nsw`

Back in the day — and by “back in the day” we mean most of what the internet remembers about us — we’re the patch of western Sydney you send your mates to when they want food wars, not footy wars. We know The Horsley Drive like it's our main street of icons: XS Espresso used to be the place doing those ridiculous stacked milkshakes (Broadsheet and Time Out both wrote up the “freak” shakes and Campos coffee). You could queue for a milkshake the size of a small mortgage and still make it to Hoyts Wetherill Park for a matinee; Time Out even notes the free parking and morning social screenings for the gold-card crowd.

Weekends for us used to mean grazing from Point A to Point B: Pauly’s Deli (Broadsheet) with its cevapi and syrup-soaked galaktoboureko, Frank’s Original Philly Cheesesteak & Dogs on Canley Vale Road (Time Out/Urban List), and the Dirty Bird Food Truck doing its Americasian fried-chicken thing (Broadsheet/Urban List). The Factoria at 1003–1009 Canley Vale Road and Grano were the quieter alternatives if you fancied gnocchi or Nutella cronuts (Urban List remembers). Stockland Wetherill Park even hosted summer workshops in Kinchin Lane — which, yes, made us feel like a shopping-centre festival town for two weeks a year (Concrete Playground).

We’ll admit we’ve had moments of intrigue. Pedestrian reported the AFP once found a suitcase with $1.6m at a Wetherill Park property during a warehouse probe — so our warehouses are definitely doing more than just storage. And historically we were part of BRIDJ’s on-demand micro-bus experiments in Sydney’s west, so we’ve always liked a bit of transit innovation (Broadsheet).

Who would hate living here? If you despise long queues at novelty milkshake places, love artisan CBD-only restaurants, or require your nightlife to run until 3am — you’ll be bored. Who would love it? Families who use Stockland events and Kinchin Lane, climbers who remember Evolution Indoor Rock Climbing, and anyone who rates an honest Philly cheesesteak, a reckless milkshake, and the comforting hum of industrial suburbia.

As Broadsheet once said: we’re community-minded, occasionally noisy, and always ready to feed you something brilliant and slightly excessive.
