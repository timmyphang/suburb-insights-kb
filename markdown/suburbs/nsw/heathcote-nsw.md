# Heathcote (NSW)

Slug: `heathcote-nsw`

Back in the day the travel writers made us sound like a secret everyone should know — and honestly, we don’t mind. We hop off the train at Heathcote Station, cross to the eastern side, and follow Wilson Road down to the Karloo Pools trailhead like a well-drilled flock. The walk rewards us properly: Karloo Pool’s turquoise water and the little waterfall above are exactly the kind of smug, photogenic thing the internet can’t stop reposting (Concrete Playground called it one of Sydney’s best wild swimming spots; Sitchu gives the train-and-trail tip).

Our weekend routine is embarrassingly wholesome. A dip, a steep rocky descent and a five-kilometre return walk later (yes, Sitchu mentions the steep bits), we used to tidy ourselves up enough for chips — Time Out reminded everyone that Heathcote Fish & Chips has been feeding folks since 1960, so we’re not pretending to be fancy post-swim. If the tide of the day leans domestic, Barrack Reserve is where local produce and small-talk intersect: Broadsheet notes the Heathcote Region Farmers’ Market brings stallholders together (9am–1pm was in their listing), while Time Out also flagged a Heathcote Bush Market at the same reserve — apparently we’re market-rich and schedule-flexible.

There’s no scandal here, no council soap opera in these snippets — mostly a collective shrug of “come for the bushwalks, stay because you got lost and found a pool.” Concrete Playground even recommends the area for stargazing and swimming, and Sitchu keeps repeating the same sensible advice about catching the train and starting on Wilson Road, which we’ll never stop reminding visitors because they always ask.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe a five-kilometre bush trundle, have an allergy to natural swimming holes, or think a town’s nightlife should extend past sunset and the fish-and-chips sign, Heathcote will feel like a very long weekend you never signed up for.

Who’ll love it? Families who like manageable bushwalks, swimmers who worship clean pools and waterfalls, plant nerds (Concrete Playground nods to local native nurseries) and anyone who appreciates a town that’s quietly brilliant without trying too hard. We’ll keep the trains, the tracks and the pools warm for you.
