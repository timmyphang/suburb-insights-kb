# Woolooware (NSW)

Slug: `woolooware-nsw`

Back in the day, we’d stroll down Wills Road and press our noses against that duck egg‑blue door at Girl Meraki (45 Wills Road) like it was a seaside treasure chest — Concrete Playground and Urban List both loved that mother–daughter curio-cave of Scandi‑ish homewares. On the same stretch, Grills On Wills was the kind of neighbourhood takeaway that made potato scallops feel like a civic ritual; Urban List even tipped it for locals chasing that nostalgic fried-forever joy, alongside barbecue chooks and other comforts.

Weekends here used to look unglamorous and very satisfying: a scallop from Grills, a loiter around Bay Central for a massage at Essence Day Spa when the parenting manual said “you deserve it” (Mums of the Shire called it a proper escape), and then a curiosity‑fuelled browse of Girl Meraki’s layered cushions and coastal knickknacks. If altitude training was suddenly on the agenda, Combine Air at Woolooware Bay Central (453A Captain Cook Drive) was the local flex — simulated 3,500m, for when we wanted to feel like we’d climbed a mountain without leaving the carpark.

If you were hunting for heated drama, the files are disappointingly wholesome. The clips we’ve got are boutique, wellbeing and munch — acupuncture practitioners like Pure Alchemy, Yolk Acupuncture and The Dao Health at 57 Flinders Road make an appearance in the mums’ directory, and Woolooware Shores even turned up as a venue listing (2/28 Alexander Ave, Taren Point) on the local event map. Not exactly front‑page scandal, more “please pass the eucalyptus oil”.

Who would hate living here? If you require 24/7 rooftop cocktail theatrics, metropolitan outrage, or an endless stream of new openings to Instagram, Woolooware will tire you. It’s not trying to be everywhere’s Next Big Thing.

Who would love it here? Folks who want practicality wrapped in quiet charm: a local gift shop with soul, a no‑nonsense chook-and-scallop takeaway, a spa that actually helps, and a weird little altitude studio for the determined. We’ll take that slow, small‑town coastal life and polish it with a duck egg‑blue door any day.
