# Warrawong (NSW)

Slug: `warrawong-nsw`

Back in the day our local headlines read like a planning department’s wishlist and a festival brochure, which suits us — we like to be underestimated.

We start the morning imagining the future: the Warrawong Parklands Master Plan was finalised to turn our 32‑hectare foreshore on Lake Illawarra into something that sounds suspiciously glamorous on paper. Time Out listed nearly ten kilometres of pedestrian and cycle paths, a 320‑metre wetland boardwalk, an Olympic‑standard skatepark, upgraded sports fields, accessible playgrounds, new water access points and environmental restoration tied to a $5 million seawall upgrade. We like to picture kids on the accessible playgrounds and retirees arguing about the best place to watch the sunset — on the envisaged boardwalk, obviously.

Culture pitch: Concrete Playground noted the 2025 Alliance Française French Film Festival expanded and added Warrawong to its slate — apparently we were cosmopolitan enough for subtitled cinema. We’re still grinning at the idea of niche French films turning up in our postcode like fancy takeout. Meanwhile Sitchu reminded people to learn at our little Pioneer Museum and even mentioned a stay at Warrawong On The Darling to “immerse yourself in the abundant wildlife” — which sounds like a holiday brochure written by someone who grew up Conan‑ing into birdwatching.

For years the family crowd has had their refuge in the listings: Parentopia crops up as the one‑stop for local kids’ services and mothers’ groups, the sort of place where you actually know who shows up to pick up the kids from sport rather than just guessing from the carpark.

Who would hate it here? People who need constant nightlife, five different boutique bakeries within a 400‑metre radius, or who can’t handle the concept of a seaside town that sells plans with more enthusiasm than built projects. Also anyone allergic to wetlands and earnest master plans.

Who would love it? Families looking for actual space, skaters dreaming of an Olympic‑standard ramp that may eventually exist, nature lovers who like Lake Illawarra and Pioneer Museum afternoons, and cinephiles who enjoy the idea that the Alliance Française once knocked on our door. We’ll keep the esky cold and the boardwalk daydreams alive.
