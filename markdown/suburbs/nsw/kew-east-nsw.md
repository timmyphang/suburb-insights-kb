# Kew East (NSW)

Slug: `kew-east-nsw`

We know the ritual: Saturday morning in Kew East used to mean a slow drift down High Street with a toddler on one hip, a book on the other, and the faint smell of chocolate from Xocolatl tempting us between playground runs. Back in the day Broadsheet wrote up Gilbert’s opening on High Street — yes, the one with the $5 mimosas and the tsoureki‑inspired French toast — and Tim’s Bookshop was the sort of neighbour you could rely on for a last‑minute children’s story or history tome.

We used to picnic at Hays Paddock Park because Time Out couldn’t stop fawning about its accessibility, or squeeze into the Renaissance Theatre at Kew High School for whatever school production was running that term. Di Palma’s was our go‑to for nights when you wanted to feel like family without hosting: Alfia and John Di Palma had been running that 130‑seater since 1988, and Broadsheet said regulars kept coming back. Even our local businesses made lists — Gong Gan was singled out for its Jeju Gogi Guksu in a Broadsheet roundup of suburban Korean soups, and Concrete Playground pointed out Xocolatl as a sweet spot in the area.

We’ll admit it: Kew East has always been quietly organised. There’s a playgroup rhythm (shoutout to Kew Neighbourhood House Playgroup and Playgroup Victoria), sleep consultants on Windella Ave for the parents who’ve had enough, and even the permaculture cred — Pedestrian named Alexandria Foggo of Takaynar among Australia’s notable young practitioners.

Who hates it here? If you can’t stand playgroups, community newsletters, or the mild competitiveness of private‑school catchments — yes, those Sitchu listings about the “private school belt” and easy access to MLC, Xavier, Trinity et al. — you’ll find Kew East a bit suffocating. If your vibe is “spontaneous late‑night chaos,” you’ll miss the quiet.

Who’ll love it? Families who want parks and bookshops, people who miss proper Italian tableside service, soup devotees chasing Gong Gan’s bone broth, and anyone who likes their suburb to feel like an organised, leafy hug. As Broadsheet and Time Out once reminded us: it’s small, it’s polite, and it knows how to do brunch and braised pork properly.
