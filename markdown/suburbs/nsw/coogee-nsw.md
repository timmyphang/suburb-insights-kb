# Coogee (NSW)

Slug: `coogee-nsw`

We wake up to the same argument every morning: did we come for the view or the vibe? Spoiler — it’s both. We drag ourselves down Coogee Bay Road, past the rainbow path on the steps (yes, the one Randwick councillors voted to keep), cut through the end of the Bondi-to-Coogee coastal walk and pretend Wylie's Baths is our private plunge. Then it’s a detour to The Little Kitchen or The Lion and Buffalo if we’re feeling human — Broadsheet reckons the café scene is small but strong, and we don’t argue with Broadsheet.

Weekends here are a contact sport. The Pavilion is an institution — ground-floor family bistro, rooftop DJs and the adults-only level if you think you’ve earned it — and yes, the Pimm’s Oyster House activations are a thing, apparently. If we’re trying to impress out-of-towners we point them at the view, the two rock pools (McIver Bath included), and whisper about the InterContinental at 224 Arden Street — Rick Stein was announced for Coogee (slated for November 2025) and, honestly, nothing says “we live by the beach” like a 224-seat seafood courtyard.

Petty local drama? We have a cupboard full. The Coogee Bay Hotel’s infamous gelato poo saga lives in our cultural memory (Junkee and Pedestrian dug up the receipts), and not long ago the shore was closed for mysterious black balls that turned out to be tar — because of course. There’s also the council parking consultation that might mean no more free beach parking for non-residents. We love being popular, but we also love a spare park.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who likes quiet main streets, secret beaches, or parking. If you abhor live music at dusk, family-friendly activations on Wednesday nights (Coogee Nights 2026 runs from Arden to Brook Street with Carnivale, Sideshow, Boogie Nights and a Beach Party), or the idea that strangers might out-swim you at sunrise, steer clear.

Who would love it here? Families (hello, Playgroup at Dolphin Street and the Play Zones), runners and swimmers (Coogee Run Club; the coastal routes are addictive), wine nerds (Coogee Wine Room) and people who want a village that can handle a bit of mystery, a bit of spectacle, and still make you feel like you belong. We wouldn’t have it any other way.
