# The Rocks (NSW)

Slug: `the-rocks-nsw`

Back in the day — or at least according to every guide and mum-blog we could find — we still know how to own these cobblestones. We’re the ones who’ve learned to dodge selfie sticks on Argyle Street and queue with dignity outside Pancakes On The Rocks (4 Hickson Rd) when night owls insist there’s something mystical about midnight maple syrup. 

Our weekend runs look like this: Sundays you’ll find us at The Rocks Picnic (10am–3pm) lugging a basket, pretending we meant to get artisanal anything, while the kids do the rounds at the markets on George Street. In winter we’ve bundled up for Christmas in July — Snow Lane, mulled wine and Firepit Cinema — and in summer we pretend the Homegrown Concert Series is why we’re here, not the harbour views. Forage The Rocks used to be our excuse to do a cocktail trail and pretend we were on a tasteful scavenger hunt.

We’ve got stories, and not all of them are glossy brochure copy. As Junkee noted, the Sirius Building was progressively cleared of residents — and that left a weird, very public conversation about who gets to call this harbour-front home. We nod, we grumble, we take our politics to the pub — which, historically, is sort of the point here (Harts Pub, The Australian Heritage Hotel, Glenmore Hotel — Time Out’s pub crawl wasn’t lying).

And yes, we watched the restaurants shuffle: The Collective swaggered into the Argyle precinct, Saké was listed at 12 Argyle Street, and Grappa moved into the old Rockpool site with all the theatricality of a 300-seat encore. Concrete Playground, Broadsheet and Urban List all gave their two cents — we read them, we rolled our eyes, we ate it anyway.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t stand tourists, markets, analogue cobblestones under your heels or the faint, constant hum of events, we’re probably not for you. Who would love it? Families who want harbour views and easy market brunches, festival-goers who book their calendars around Forage and Vivid, and food-obsessed people who’ll forgive a queue for a good story (and a good plate).

We’re old sandstone and new menus, protest art and mulled wine. We’re The Rocks — precious, performative and impossibly ours.
