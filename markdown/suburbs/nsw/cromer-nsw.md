# Cromer (NSW)

Slug: `cromer-nsw`

We know Cromer like the back of our sunburnt hand — the kind of place where the morning smells of sea breeze and reheated bagels mingle, and someone at the golf club will politely correct your swing while telling you about the best vanilla slice in town.

Back in the day Broadsheet made a fuss when Barrel One opened a cafe in a converted warehouse here — yes, the one famous for cheesy toasties and bagels — and for a while we’d queue like it was a coastal pilgrimage. Urban List used to namecheck Barrel One and, oddly enough, local baker Staple Bread & Necessities turned up in a vanilla-slice roundup. There was also Coya — that tuck-away degustation spot people whispered about on The Urban List, where chef Ashraf Saleh plated things that made the rest of us feel casual and clumsy.

Weekends for us are simple: a kayak or paddleboard on Narrabeen Lagoon if we fancy pretending we’re outdoorsy (Time Out reminds us the lagoon does all the usual water sport stuff), a circuit class at Feels Pilates — yes, there was a Cromer studio on their roster — then a wander past Arikaya’s warehouse where homewares and a coffee nook once lived. If we can’t be bothered, there’s always the Cromer Country Club by Diamond Resorts for a sauna and a muted game of “is that a sunrise or a weather front?”

Petty local drama? We’ve had our share: a bushfire once ran from Meatworks Avenue toward Cromer Heights and the emergency radios had us all glued to our inboxes; Miller Reserve’s skate park turned up in an exposure list during a measles scare; and at one point regulators forecasted power outages that included Cromer. None of it made us less smug about our sea breeze, but it did make us check the battery level on our torches.

Who would hate living here? Folks who want nightclubs on every corner, or who can’t stomach being within earshot of gulls and golf clubs. Who would love it? Families (real estate listings sing about north-facing pockets, schools and walking tracks), food obsessives who remember Barrel One and Coya, and anyone who thinks a paddleboard counts as culture. We’ll keep the beach spot for you — just bring a crust for the seagulls.
