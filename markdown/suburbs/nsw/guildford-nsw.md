# Guildford (NSW)

Slug: `guildford-nsw`

We know the sound of a Saturday in Guildford: the sizzle from a roadside food truck, the smell of something smoky and bright enough to wake a sleepy main street. Lately that’s been Jollof Junction — as Broadsheet raved in February 2026 — serving West African–hybrid jollof rice with twice‑cooked chicken wings and lamb suya out of a neat roadside setup. We queue, we argue over who gets the extra chilli, we leave with sauce on our shirts and no regrets.

Back in the day there was El‑Shaddai African Cuisine at 350 Guildford Road, serving fufu, groundnut stew and jollof rice — Broadsheet’s directory remembered it, but it’s now marked permanently closed. We tell newcomers the story like it’s a local ghost: respectable, fragrant, and missed.

Yes, we’ve had our moments in the headlines. Pedestrian reported violent arrests over masks and later that counter‑terror raids touched properties here alongside Merrylands and Wentworthville. It’s the sort of tabloid fodder that makes suburban life feel like it’s auditioning for a true‑crime podcast. We don’t pretend Sydney‑wide debates haven’t landed on our doorstep — Junkee’s old pieces remind everyone Guildford’s always been part of the wider Western Sydney conversation.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t cope with food trucks on the roadside, a pub that’s “had a glow‑up” in the papers, or being on the map for more than just tidy streets and quiet lawns, this isn’t for you. If you panic at the idea of savoury rice stealing the spotlight from your usual weekday routine, we’ll wave you off with a sauce‑stained napkin.

Who would love it here? People who want flavour (literally and socially) — those who’ll try Jollof Junction’s jollof the moment it’s ready, who remember past corner diners like El‑Shaddai fondly, and who like their suburbs with a little history and a little headline. We’re messy, tasty and stubbornly ours — and if that sounds like your kind of Saturday, come grab a wing.
