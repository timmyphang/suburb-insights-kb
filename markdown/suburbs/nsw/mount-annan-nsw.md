# Mount Annan (NSW)

Slug: `mount-annan-nsw`

Back in the day we’d set the alarm early, pile the kids into the car and pretend we were on holiday — because the Australian Botanic Garden Mount Annan used to feel like someone else’s 416‑hectare private park. We’d wander the Wattle and Banksia & Grevillea gardens, find the Connections Garden paper daisy displays, clamber up to Caley Peak lookout and smugly note that, yes, it really is bigger than Central Park. Entry was free, the trails ran for about 20km and, for reasons we still can’t entirely explain, they put goats on weed control duty — peak horticultural theatre.

Weekends were a neat loop: a slow lap of the lakes and pavilions, a market browse at the Macarthur Centre for Sustainable Living’s Markets on the Lawn (last‑Sunday vibe), then a pit stop at the Wild Dragonfly Café inside Flower Power — the kid‑friendly classic where you booked ahead because everyone else had children too. Growing Friends Nursery used to stock plants propagated from Mount Annan collections, so our neighbourhood hedges always looked impressively vetted.

The local drama was low‑budget but relentless: our Blockbuster used to linger as Sydney’s last bastion of rental nostalgia until it finally shut after 16 years. And who could forget when NSW Police got roasted over a post about a man asking for directions in Mount Annan — we watched Twitter like it was a soap opera, popcorn in hand.

Who would hate living here? If you need the city’s buzz five minutes from your doorstep, you’ll grumble about being roughly 60 kilometres from the CBD and about as urban as a postcard. If you’re allergic to kids, ducks on ponds, and volunteers selling boutique jams at community stalls, well, Mount Annan will try your patience.

Who’d love it? Families, plant nerds and anyone who likes lantern festivals over laneway bars — we used to put on Water Lantern Festival nights and even After Dark Feast events that made an evening at the botanic gardens feel a bit epic. For years the playgroups at Birriwa Reserve (the clubroom on 20 Fitzpatrick Rd) and the Play Strong PlayVan kept us caffeined and socialised. That’s our pitch: a suburb that’s quietly enormous, quietly proud, and quietly perfect for anyone whose idea of culture involves paper daisies and goats.
