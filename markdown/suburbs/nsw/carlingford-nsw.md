# Carlingford (NSW)

Slug: `carlingford-nsw`

For years our favourite soap opera has been the transport saga — and honestly, it’s better than any streaming service. Back in the day the T6 Carlingford Line famously shut its doors on January 5, 2020 (as Concrete Playground pointed out), sending us onto 535 buses and into endless group chats about whether the Parramatta Light Rail would ever arrive. Time Out liked to tease us with testing updates and approvals — trams to Westmead via the Parramatta CBD and Camellia, supposedly every 7.5 minutes at peak — and we clapped politely and went back to our barbecues.

Weekends here have the cadence of a suburban rom-com. We’d drop the kids at Carlingford Gumnut on a Tuesday morning (you know the one — park lands, 10:30am, carlingfordgumnut@gmail.com if nostalgia hits), then wander past the Carlingford Branch Library on Lloyds Ave for storytime — the place joined City of Parramatta Council after 2016 and still brags a kids’ corner that keeps toddlers quiet long enough to drink lukewarm coffee in peace. Later we’d take the fam to John Wearn Reserve — reopened December 4, 2020 — where the safari playground and skate park do a fine job of making parents feel both smug and exhausted.

If you’re a weekend bushwalker who likes a payoff, Balaka Falls on Hunts Creek has been our hidden delight; upgrades between 2018–2022 and a new bridge made it respectable enough to bring non-locals. And for the hobby gardeners among us, Swayne’s Dural’s listing noting a second, smaller Swane’s nursery in Carlingford used to be our excuse to buy more roses nobody asked for.

Who would hate living here? People who crave perpetual nightlife, late trains on demand, or instant CBD swagger. Also anyone allergic to quiet prestige — those wide, tree-lined streets, James Ruse Agricultural School bragging rights and heritage homes give us a “we’re-not-trying-too-hard-but-we-are” vibe.

Who would love it? Families, green thumbs, playground connoisseurs, cheesecake hunters (Urban List once sent us to Jiggle Cheesecake for burnt Basque magic) and anyone who appreciates a suburb whose biggest drama is a delayed tram that eventually becomes a love story. We’ll still be here, holding the BBQ tongs and pretending we weren’t counting the minutes between trams.
