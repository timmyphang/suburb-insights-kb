# Bensville (NSW)

Slug: `bensville-nsw`

We know we’re in Bensville the moment a Volvo XC90 with a dusty roof rack edges into the boat ramp carpark and a surfboard so new it’s never been waxed leans against the bonnet like it’s embarrassed to be seen. We sip an oat‑milk flat white from The Boatshed Café at 8:59am in our Lululemon leggings and watch a Toyota RAV4 negotiate a ten‑point turn on Bensville Road while someone texts the Bensville Community Facebook group about council bins. We call the boat ramp the community centre because it has better opening hours than the real one.

Our Saturday is a ritual. Markets first — reusable tote, bargain sourdough, three olives in a jar — then a 45‑minute kayak, because we can say we did exercise and still make brunch. We pop by The Esplanade to check the neighbours’ new trailer like it’s a baby at a christening. Percy, the Labradoodle, has his own Instagram and a favourite table at the cafe. Sunday is mower, pick up a locally made sourdough, and post three photos of the harbour view with progressively smug captions.

Petty drama is our community sport. A parking‑etiquette post on the Facebook page about “proper trailer etiquette” spirals into 300 furious comments about SUVs, tinny launching etiquette and whether boaters can actually read signage. Someone staples a hand‑typed note to a gumtree — “NO OVERNIGHT TRAILERS” — and within 48 hours it’s laminated, framed and passed between letterboxes like a reliquary. Two passive‑aggressive flyers appear in everyone’s mailbox. The real winner? The bloke who quietly nabbed the last picnic table and made it his throne.

We love sustainability talk between Pilates classes at Coastal Pilates, even as we valet our kombucha into the back of a Toyota. We subscribe to recycled‑paper magazines and vote with our wallets for local makers, but we still fight over parking like it’s a seasonal sport. Our strata battles are about garden mulch and whether the communal hose is for rinsing fish or washing dog paws.

Who would hate living here: the relentless inner‑city cyclist with a Brompton who refuses a BBQ and believes anything with a trailer is “too much car.” They’ll be bewildered by the unofficial ten‑minute boat‑ramp rituals, annoyed by the hobby‑marina hierarchy and scandalised at a neighbourhood mourning a gumtree parking spot like it was a minor deity.

But who would love it: a mid‑40s project manager who swapped city stress for waterfront calm, drives a slightly too big SUV, owns a Labradoodle called Percy and can quote tide times while ordering an oat‑milk flat white. We’ll make them feel at home.
