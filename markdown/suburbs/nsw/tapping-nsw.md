# Tapping (NSW)

Slug: `tapping-nsw`

We live in Tapping, NSW, which is either a charming little postcode or the internet’s favourite homophone — depends how many tabs you’ve got open. We start mornings the usual way: someone asks if we do Emotional Freedom Technique and someone else asks if Opal fares have gone up. We shrug, because the only local scene the media ever covers is the concept of "tapping."

Back in the day Broadsheet wrote about Marameo — yes, Marameo — “tapping into pasta obsessions” (nostalgia for a rooftop courtyard, apparently). Urban List was busy explaining the myki-free travel trial and helpfully reminding us that “tapping on will work with a Mastercard or Visa.” Time Out gave us a stern briefing on Opal fare rises in NSW. Pedestrian, Junkee, Concrete Playground and a clutch of mum-blogs all had their own variations on the theme: tapping your feelings, tapping your bus card, tapping your muscles, tapping your kids into calm. If Google had a dinner party, we’d be the pun on the canapé tray.

There’s no local petty drama in the files — which is either a blessing or proof our suburb is too small to bother with. So instead the drama is existential: are we a wellness retreat for frazzled parents (hello, The Tapping Mama), or are we a public-transport training exercise? As the mum blogs keep promising, tapping can “release stress and restore balance.” We’ve tried both approaches: less stress, more identity confusion.

Who would hate living here? People who detest wordplay, anyone allergic to metaphors, and commuters who want their suburb to have an actual nightlife rather than endless metaphysical tutorials on how to tap your feelings.

Who would love it? Parents who’ve bookmarked EFT guides on nomumisanisland.com and reframeparenting.com, copy editors who enjoy a good homophone, and anyone who prefers being quietly amused by national outlets rather than loudly famous. We’re not on many glossy lists — we’re very good at being the pun your search bar can’t resist. Come for the peace, stay because Google keeps sending you here by accident.
