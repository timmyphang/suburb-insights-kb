# Sassafras (NSW)

Slug: `sassafras-nsw`

Back in the day, when the mist rolled down Mount Dandenong Tourist Road and you could still pretend you’d found a secret English village, we’d sling the car into a parking spot and dawdle like it was our civic duty.

Weekends used to look the same in every guide: Miss Marple’s Tea Room was the institution Broadsheet and Urban List loved to point at — a cottage-style stop that famously didn’t take bookings — while Concrete Playground told us to sidestep the over-touristy Miss Marple’s and wander a few doors to Ripe for a heated deck among the ferns. Proserpina Bakehouse (which opened in 2019, Broadsheet noted) turned up on lists for its loud, vaulted room and sourdough, and the Mt Dandenong Bakery & Patisserie was the reliable local excuse to buy pastry.

Our village square was less a square and more a parade of nostalgia: Time Out’s Geppetto’s Workshop with puppets dangling from the ceiling, Tea Leaves importing and blending teas, and Alfred Nicholas Gardens waiting for anyone who wanted to look like they were in a postcard. Concrete Playground even tucked Santosa Cottage and The Blackwood into its winter escapes list — because yes, we are a mood for cosy A-frames and roaring fires.

We don’t have a fresh, trending exposé this week — all our press clips are older guides and listings — so we’ll be old-fashioned about it. The locals’ bulletin board (and yes, the occasional Mum blog) talks up community things like the Dandenong Ranges RSL meet-and-greet and nearby services like The Hills Medical in Olinda, which reminds you this place isn’t just tea and toys — it’s residents too.

Who would hate it here? If you can’t stand cottages, puppets, heated decks or the idea of a Devonshire tea being a weekend plan, Sassafras will feel like a slow-loading website. Who’d love it? Families hunting for a country retreat (Sassafras Country Retreat made those Airbnb roundups), hikers who want the Warrawilla rainforest boardwalk, and anyone who thinks a bakery, a tearoom and a puppet shop are a very good economy.

We’ll always be the place people stop on the way to somewhere else and then refuse to leave.
