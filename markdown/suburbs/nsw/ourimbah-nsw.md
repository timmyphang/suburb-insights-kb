# Ourimbah (NSW)

Slug: `ourimbah-nsw`

We know the exact rhythm of this place: Wednesday mornings used to mean a small army of parents trudging to Bill Sohier Park Community Hall on Ken Cook Way for the Ourimbah Wednesday Weekly playgroup — Playgroup NSW had it listed at 9:30am for years, so you could set your watch by it. Then, if the littlies were still upright, there was the pull of the Ourimbah Markets at Rustique — Little Locals wrote about full days of family fun, Adventure Zones and meet-and-greets with Bluey and Elsa. Yes, we’re that kind of town. Proud, slightly damp and resolutely child-friendly.

Back in the day we’d brag about the easy options: Concrete Playground name-checked Hidden Valley Horse Riding — 986 Ourimbah Creek Road, Palm Grove — as one of the best places to tack up near Sydney, so if your weekend required horses and a little rural pretence, that was your outpost. And for the people who like their civic projects loud and ambitious, Central Coast Industry Connect celebrated the start of construction on the Food Manufacturing Innovation Hub at Ourimbah — a $17.14M, government-funded thing promising pilot spaces and training for food and beverage businesses.

Not everything here gets a ribbon. Central Coast News reported our Burns Rd flood warning system was labelled an “abject failure” by the Ourimbah Region Residents Association — which, frankly, made us cheer. We like a bit of righteous local outrage; it keeps meetings interesting and gives the Residents Association something to wear on their fundraising flyers. The Central Coast News tag archive on Ourimbah keeps a slow-burning record of service station upgrades, Landcare events and the small-operatic moments of suburb life.

Who would hate living here? People who want neon nightlife, a deli that stays open till midnight, or absolutely zero chance of being roped into a playgroup sausage sizzle. Who would love it? Families who need a steady Wednesday routine, stallholders who used to pack up Rustique for market days, horse riders keen on Hidden Valley, and anyone excited by the idea of a local food hub taking flight.

We’re fond of Ourimbah’s low-key, slightly damp charm — and if you’re thinking of moving here, bring sensible shoes and an appreciation for community noticeboards.
