# Wentworth Point (NSW)

Slug: `wentworth-point-nsw`

We watch the SS Ayrfield get gilded by sunrise, coffee-in-hand (or, fine, whatever hot thing we’re allowed to admit to), while joggers and dog-walkers file past the mangroves at Bicentennial Park. That view — shipwrecks, river light and the Bennelong Bridge stretching off to Rhodes — is the thing we brag about when people ask “So…where exactly is Wentworth Point?”

Back in the day the marina was just a plan; now Marina Square hosts a monthly market and occasional twilight Eat Drink Nights, and the place hums on Sunday mornings with stalls and people who treat artisanal bread like a religious experience. The community finally got its gateway sign at the corner of Hill Road and Bennelong Parkway, which feels oddly ceremonial for a suburb that used to be a shipyard in the ’60s.

We used to have eateries that made headlines — Papa Pasta opened in 2021 as Sydney’s first 100% Halal Italian (yes, beef lasagne, prawn fettuccini and truffle mac ’n’ cheese were on the menu) and Bahsa arrived with terrace views over the Parramatta River. Cuto Kids Cafe turned up in Marina Square in 2020 and families have been making the library and community centre a real hub ever since. Playgroups like Junior Jivers and Play Strong have been doing the hard work of keeping small humans entertained and adults pretending they still know nursery rhymes.

There’s also infrastructure on the to-do list: Urban List flagged plans for a Bennelong Parkway Bridge link and a revamp of Wentworth Park once the greyhound lease expires in 2027 — so yes, we’re expecting wetlands, tracks and a proper green heart. Change is coming, and we’re weirdly into it.

Who would hate living here? People who can’t handle high-rise waterfront living, who dislike weekend markets, or who are allergic to kids’ music and community noticeboards. Who would love it? Pet owners (Time Out put us on a pet-friendly list), families who use Bay Park and the library, sunrise enthusiasts who’ll never shut up about the SS Ayrfield, and anyone who thinks a monthly market is peak civilisation.

We’re small, slightly smug, and quietly proud — and yes, we’ll see you at the Marina Square market this Sunday.
