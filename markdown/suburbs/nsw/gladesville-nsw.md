# Gladesville (NSW)

Slug: `gladesville-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start Saturday with the kind of indecision that only a Gladesville local can muster: do we wrestle a scooter onto the ferry at Gladesville Wharf for a sniff of the Parramatta River, or park the car at Banjo Paterson Park and let the kids tackle the rope pyramid swing until someone cries mercy? For the record, Banjo Paterson Park and Peel Park have saved more weekends than our collective playlist.

We know our patch is greener than our council emails suggest. We’re about 9 kilometres from the CBD — a 20-minute drive or a 30-minute bus if you pack patience — and we wear that leafy, riverside badge with quiet pride. The history nerds love that Banjo Paterson spent time here and that the area’s story includes Tarban Creek; the arty ones will tell you Ramesh Mario Nithiyendran had a studio here, carving his path in clay and headlines.

You couldn’t flick through the local food columns without tripping over our openings. District Eatery landed at 10 Monash Road and reminded everyone that a brother-and-sister team can indeed return home and make the locals proud. Broadsheet made a fuss about Cetto Deli becoming a favourite, and if you remember when Stone Ground Bakery set up on Gladesville Road in Hunters Hill, you’ll also remember the sudden uptick in weekend lining-up etiquette. Village Days Brewing Co. turned up in 2023 with a German-trained brewer, a taproom off Victoria Road and beers like Glades Pale and 2111 Lager — yes, we have our own local liquid vanity project.

If you can’t cope with a terrace that closes early, or if you crave a nightclub at the end of every street, you’ll hate living here. If your weekend plan is "catch a Jewfish after 9pm at Gladesville Wharf," then, honestly, we salute your nocturnal hobbies.

Who loves it here? Families who want playgrounds that actually keep kids occupied (Peel Park), wellness seekers who remember City Cave Gladesville, climbers who found NOMAD, and anyone who thinks a beer from a local taproom or a focaccia from a family deli is peak suburban luxury. We’re leafy, river-side and quietly smug about it — and yes, we’ll share our bench by the water, as long as you bring something decent to eat.
