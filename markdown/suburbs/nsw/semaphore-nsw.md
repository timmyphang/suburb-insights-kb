# Semaphore (NSW)

Slug: `semaphore-nsw`

Back in the day we’d stand on the Esplanade by the Jetty, squinting at the calm water and pretending we weren’t deciding whether to spend our weekend dollars on a $5 record or another plate of gnocchi. The old guides still paint the picture for us: a buzzing foreshore, the Summer Carnival rolling past and the Semaphore Playground’s shaded chairs full of parents who have perfected the art of low-key vigilance.

Weekends used to look like this: a wander down Semaphore Road to Mr V Music (Vic Flierl has been spinning secondhand records since 2001 and even ran the Semaphore Record Fair), a detour past Le Room at 91 Semaphore Rd for whatever hip little thing that shop sold, and a debate about lunch — was it Barry’s Burgers (the ones Broadsheet once called “some of Adelaide’s best burgs”) or small plates at Lickerish Kitchen & Bar, which had been operating since 2012 and liked sharing menus and local art on the walls? Later we might have pretended to be tourists and booked a roast at the Palais Hotel on 86 Esplanade because Urban List once swore by their pork.

Local life, as the listings remembered it, was stubbornly wholesome: Temple Day Spa “officially open,” Miss June the Salon doing beach hair at Shop 2, 59-63 Semaphore Road, Semaphore Flowers serving the town since 1980, caravan parks with beachfront views, and the waterslide complex where the Flying Bee became hero material for small children between October and April. The Semaphore Community Markets sold everything from jewellery to Tupperware — yes, Tupperware — and nobody pretended that was embarrassing.

Who would hate it here? If you crave anonymous laneway cocktail bars, an industrial skyline and no toddlers within earshot, Semaphore will feel unbearably quaint. If your idea of living is never needing to pick a path between prams on the foreshore, move on.

Who loves it? Vinyl hunters, burger devotees, families, pet owners (the pet-friendly stay guides remember us fondly) and anyone who likes a seaside town that still lets you discover your own little haunt. We’ll keep waving from the Esplanade, even if the guides we love are a little nostalgic now.
