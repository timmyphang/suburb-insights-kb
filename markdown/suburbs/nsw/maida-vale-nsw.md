# Maida Vale (NSW)

Slug: `maida-vale-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start the morning with the small victories: drop the littlies off at Portman Early Childhood Centre (they used to have two centres, one in Church Street and the other in Maida Vale), wave at whoever was hauling soft socks into Soft Play Maida Vale, then sigh with pride because our honey—yes, Maida Vale Natural Honey—once turned up in a Kalamunda café menu. As Broadsheet noted, Mason & Bird spotted a handpainted sign and suddenly our bees were city-famous, for a minute at least.

We know every corner because there isn’t that many. 155 Watsonia Road came up in a Sitchu roundup of local plant and pot shops, so yes, the green thumbs among us would quietly smug about potting mix and leaving you to your plastic monstera. Parents would swap notes about schools like they were exclusive recipes: Maida Vale School on Saltram Crescent gets debated endlessly online. Mumsnet was full of it — one thread praised the new facilities, strong art and music, and “outstanding” SEN provision; another warned of high churn (people leaving at 10–15% a year) and behaviour problems. So choose your thread, bring a helmet.

The petty drama? It’s mainly a digital soap opera: earnest parents on Mumsnet arguing about whether the school is blossoming or barely treading water. Nobody is dramatic in person; we’re too busy carting kids to Rascals at Swiss Cottage Leisure Centre or checking which faith school (St Saviour’s, St Joseph’s) has the better admissions tactic. The local chatter tends to be practical, slightly weary, and always a bit smug when a neighbour’s jar of honey makes the rounds.

Who would hate living here: people who need a decades-long academic CV to brag about, or those allergic to a bit of parental chat on forums. Who would love it: the families who want decent community services, music and art-loving kids, gardeners who can find that niche pot at Watsonia Road, and anyone who thrills when a jar of our honey gets a Broadsheet name-drop. We’re not flashy. We’re quietly useful, a little opinionated online, and yes — we put our honey where the cafes can’t ignore it.
