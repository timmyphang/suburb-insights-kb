# St Clair (NSW)

Slug: `st-clair-nsw`

Back in the day we knew exactly when Thursday had arrived: the tiny queue at St Clair Bakery on 4A Endeavour Avenue meant someone had just scored a pork roll worth the trip from across Sydney. That steam, that sliced radish — it used to put the rest of the week on notice.

Our weekday ritual wasn’t glamorous. Thursdays 9:30–11:30am the Autumnleaf Neighbourhood Centre on Timesweep Drive hosted Learn Play & Grow for the under‑fives, and on alternate Wednesdays the Penrith Mobile Playvan used to pop up for a half‑day of chaos and colouring-in (12:30–2:30pm, if you liked a schedule). We had playgroups, parenting workshops and enough sticker charts to wallpaper a small house. There was even a local maker scene — Filled With Love Co. was the kind of bespoke candle and gift business that reminded you St Clair could be artisanal when it tried.

Let’s get one thing straight: we are not Tasmanian Lake St Clair. No Pumphouse Point here, no 250‑metre jetty and definitely no alpine wilderness that formed over two million years. That Lake St Clair gets the glossy travel features and the honeymooners; we get the practical stuff — community centres, bakery lines and a pop‑up playvan. And no, we’re not the seaside St Clair the Urban List keeps raving about in Dunedin with its esplanade pizza and saffron risotto. Different postcode, different vibe.

Who would hate living here? If you need an esplanade view, an Instagram‑famous jetty or a daily saffron risotto, you’ll be restless. If your hobby is complaining about the lack of boutique stays within a 20‑minute drive, you might prefer the Tasmanian version or the New Zealand one.

Who would love it? Families who live by the calendar of playgroups and pop‑ups, people who judge a suburb by the quality of its pork rolls, and anyone starting a small home business who wants a community that buys local candles and answers the newsletter. We’re proud, practical and loud at 9:30am on a Thursday. Come for the bakery rumours, stay for the playgroup gossip — and bring a spare sticker.
