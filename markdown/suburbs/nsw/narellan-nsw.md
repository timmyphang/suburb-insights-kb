# Narellan (NSW)

Slug: `narellan-nsw`

There was a time we could tell the hour by the floral lights flickering over Narellan Town Centre — giant stiletto in bloom, 4,500 annuals pretending this place invented spring. We know every brick of that Restaurant Plaza (and every face that queued for the Spring Zone craft table). Most of what we’re riffing on comes from older stories and event listings — so, yes, a lot of this is fond memory rather than current intel. Treat it like nostalgia with better lighting.

Weekend ritual (back when the centre ran a full festival schedule): we’d do a lap past the floral display, dodge a roving performer, drop the kids at the free Art Play the Alan Baker Art Gallery once partnered for, and pretend the face-painting counted as culture. The Town Centre’s Festival of Spring was peak Narellan: lawn games, collaborative puzzles and a floral spectacle that made our shopping centre feel inexplicably like a pageant.

Food-wise, we’ll tip the hat to the local history — Concrete Playground once wrote about Antico’s being housed in a heritage-listed Narellan building. And for anyone with a taste for boerie rolls or bobotie, Sitchu remembers Savannah Café & Bar at 326 Camden Valley Way. We mention them in the past-tense way you use for exes and pop-up pizzerias: fondly, and with a little wistfulness.

Our community is earnest: the Narellan Child, Family and Community Centre on Queen Street used to be the place you actually called for stuff that mattered, and organisations like Big Yellow Umbrella (Camden Community Connections) made the centre feel like a neighbourhood that shows up. Childcare and activities — World of Learning Narellan on Richardson Rd, Complete Dance Training and Centre Stage Dance Studio hosting Keeping Mum Kangatraining groups — have been the spine of our weekday chaos.

Who would hate living here? If you crave late-night anonymity, a suburb that stages giant floral stilettos and schedules kids’ craft at the same plaza will feel like being stuck in a community musical number. Who would love it? Families, mums, anyone who rates free school-holiday entertainment and a centre that doubles as a horticultural theatre.

We’re Camden-Narellan people, according to Time Out, proud of our suburban multitudes. So yes, we’ll keep planting annuals, hosting spring zones and turning the shopping centre into a festival — because if you’re going to be a small town, do it with florals and conviction.
