# Banksia Grove (NSW)

Slug: `banksia-grove-nsw`

We roll out of bed in Banksia Grove and the first thing we do is check whether the village is still behaving like a village. According to the old listings, Banksia Grove Village used to be the sort of “lively local centre” that came with all the essentials: Coles, Aldi, Woolworths, fast-food outlets, a petrol station and "ample parking" — which is to say you could park five cars and still not find anything to complain about.

Mornings used to mean swinging past the Banksia Grove Shopping Centre — there was a time Property North was hawking immaculate homes at 3/1 Jewel Way with lofty ceilings and “low‑maintenance” promises, and the real estate copy smelled faintly of optimism and engineered timber. For parents, the Child and Parent Centre on 14 Viridian Drive used to be our not-so-secret weapon: playgroups, speech drop-ins and parenting sessions listed in the Term 1 2026 planner, staffed by the apparently omnipresent Cindy Davies (contact details were listed, because of course they were).

Local oddities? We had Grand Syndicates advertising racehorse shares — affordable ownership from about $1,100 for a 5% slice — because where else would you casually diversify your portfolio into horses between school drop-off and the supermarket run?

There isn’t much petty council theatrics in the clippings we’ve got — no dramatic town-hall showdowns, just the steady hum of shops, playgroups and property promo — which is honestly the point. If you despise convenience, quiet suburbia, people who actually use playgroups and those suspiciously serene real-estate photos, Banksia Grove will drive you bananas.

But if you’re the sort of family who likes free community services, sensible shopping options and the comforting sight of a shopping centre that knows what anchoring a suburb means, you’ll adore it. And if you miss the finer things — retro vinyl and velveteen booths — there used to be a Banksia Tavern in Busselton that did those things very well, for those times we wanted to pretend we were on holiday.
