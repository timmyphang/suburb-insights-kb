# Lugarno (NSW)

Slug: `lugarno-nsw`

We’re the ones who know every pothole on Forest Road and can tell you which park bench has the best view of the marina when the river decides to go all glassy. Back in the day the press liked to point at us and say “village” like it was a compliment — and fair enough. Forest Road’s village shops, cafes and eateries, the riverside parks and the marina are exactly what we brag about at street fairs nobody from outside gets invited to.

Weekend ritual used to be simple: kids off to the Thursday playgroup at the Peakhurst-Lugarno Uniting Church at 909 Forest Road (10:00 AM, school term — yes, we checked the listing), toddlers corralled by the promise of stickers, while the rest of us muttered about listings for 17 Lime Kiln Road and admired houses that Sitchu described as “contemporary entertainers” within walking distance of Lugarno Public School. For young families there was even chatter about a purpose-built Giggle and Learn centre at 25 Old Forest Road — sustainable playrooms, a waitlist and all the earnest copy you’d expect.

We’ve also been inexplicably glamorous in print. Broadsheet once imagined a 1950s New York hideaway on our stretch of Forest Rd — Respite, at 1026A Forest Rd — and wrote up coriander-cured salmon ceviche tostadas and a signature vodka cocktail with lime, watermelon and Italian bergamot liqueur as if mid-century Manhattan had relocated to Lugarno for the weekend. Concrete Playground acknowledged our seafood cred too: Lugarno Seafood Restaurant has been serving Albany Rock Oysters and old-school seafood for more than three decades. We wear that maritime lineage like a slightly damp badge of honour.

Petty local drama? The media’s disinterest is our drama: there’s not much recent press about us, so most of what gets written is nostalgic or mildly surprised we exist. We’ll take it — better than being overshared.

Who would hate living here: anyone who needs their nightlife curated by an algorithm or thinks the word “marina” is a sub-genre of nightclub. Who would love it: families, people who actually enjoy a real river view, and anyone who appreciates a place where Respite once tried to be 1950s New York and our oyster joint quietly answered back.
