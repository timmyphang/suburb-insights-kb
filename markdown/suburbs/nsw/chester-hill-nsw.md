# Chester Hill (NSW)

Slug: `chester-hill-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start a Saturday by dodging pram puddles on Proctor Parade to make the 10:00am Chester Hill Music and Playtime session inside the hall at Chester Hill Anglican Church, 125 Proctor Parade — kids singing, craft everywhere, morning tea disappearing faster than you can say “more glue”. As Playgroup NSW recorded, that was our gentle domestic chaos, and yes, we loved it.

After that we'd wander up Waldron Road where Broadsheet once put Jasmin 1 on the map — a family-run Lebanese spot praised for generous mixed plates: tawouk, shanglish, kibbeh and falafels. Nearby, Sitchu had already flagged 82 Waldron Road in a bakery round-up, so if you fancied carbs you knew where to look. All these mentions read like a map of the things that kept us fed and vaguely civilised.

On weekdays, the real drama was transport. Time Out wrote about the Safe Accessible Transport Plan that had Chester Hill station in its sights, and also ran the scary memo about the Bankstown–Sydenham line closure and rail‑replacement bus plans. Translation: we’re proud of our station, and we’ve learned to love contingency timetables like they’re an Aussie pastime.

House-hunters and renovators knew us too. Listings on Sitchu — think 5/27 Campbell Hill Road and a 510 sqm block on 78 Weemala Road — made it clear we were a sensible spot for anyone who liked a bit of elbow grease and a promising frontage. MumsPages and local directories listed ballet and dance options (WSS Dance Studio among them) and the usual local services that make a suburb livable.

Who would hate living here? People who need a perfect, uninterrupted CBD commute or who can’t bear the scent of a working suburb. Who’d love it? Young families, renovators hunting value, and anyone who appreciates a good mixed plate and a playgroup that actually hands you a biscuit.

We’re not pretending to be Bondi — we’re Chester Hill: practical, fed, and quietly getting on with it.
