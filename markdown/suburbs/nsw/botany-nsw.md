# Botany (NSW)

Slug: `botany-nsw`

We wake to the small, stubborn rhythms of Botany: the clatter from a bakery window where Brasserie Bread used to throw fresh loaves (1737 Botany Road, Banksmeadow), the distant orchestration of port logistics at Port Botany, and the faint cheer from kids at the Botany Aquatic Centre — which, for years, was the place everyone’s mum recommended for a wet Saturday (three pools, lawns, the lot).

Our weekend ritual used to be gloriously unglamorous: a slow stroll past the wild little bakery lineage that spawned Malika Bakehouse’s Surry Hills sequel, a detour by 1575B Botany Road where Beer Fontaine kept about 15 taps spinning, then over to the old paper‑warehouse that became One Drop Brewing — Meg Barbic and Clay Grant’s sunlit beer garden with a Botany Bay Lager on rotation. Single O even had a roastery down in Botany, exporting their beans far and wide — so yes, the aroma of industry here is sometimes espresso, sometimes yeast, sometimes shipping.

We’ve had our dramas. As Junkee reported, the Botany View Hotel got itself into hot water over a marriage‑equality mural — hundreds of negative reviews, phones taken off the hook, the whole suburban soap opera. And the port has been a genuine stage for protest: activists once suspended themselves above a nine‑metre pole to block Port Botany access, an episode that even threaded into debates about tougher protest laws. This is not a fantasy; it’s local history with actual scaffolding.

Who would hate living here? If you require tree‑lined boulevards and Instagrammable emptiness, you’ll grumble at the mix of warehouses, craft stores dotted along Botany Road (Sitchu even lists addresses like 11 Salisbury St and 494 Botany Rd) and the constant reminder that this is a working stretch of Sydney. If quiet anonymity is your vibe, Botany will keep surprising you.

Who would love it? Families who remember the Botany Aquatic Centre fondly. Walkers and whale‑watchers who know Cape Solander and Kamay Botany Bay National Park are proper local treasures. Beer geeks and bread devotees who appreciated One Drop, Beer Fontaine and Brasserie Bread will wink knowingly at the way industry and hospitality elbow each other here.

Back in the day, Botany was the sort of place that kept its foot in both worlds — useful, a bit rough around the edges, and stubbornly itself. We wouldn’t have it any other way.
