# Diggers Rest (NSW)

Slug: `diggers-rest-nsw`

We know the ritual: weekend roll-out, bleary-eyed but secretly proud. We’ll amble down Old Calder Highway and end up at Houdini’s Café e Cucina — yes, the one at 52 Old Calder Highway — because somebody in our gang insists a “cocktail‑of‑the‑week” is essential morning fuel. Houdini’s has been posting specials like it’s running a small, very polite Italian embassy and we’re here for it.

If the kids are coming, the itinerary is wholesome chaos. Playgroup Victoria’s Summertime activity booklet still nags us to “become mates with Penny the sheep and Maggie the cow at Animal Land Children’s Farm in Diggers Rest,” and we do. Penny and Maggie are the only locals who’ll let the toddlers climb on them without judging our parenting choices.

Our weekends are a charming blur of local institutions. The Diggers Rest FNC on Plumpton Road — home of the Burras since 1978 — keeps us invested in small‑town tribalism: Round 9, Round 10 updates and argument in the car about who should have taken the mark. The club’s posts are that dependable: footy fixtures, score updates, the social glue we pretend isn’t what it is.

We’ve also been tracking the Cultural & Sports Club’s feed: Gurpurab 2025 made the calendar look like a proper community festival, complete with notes about Guru Nanak Dev Ji’s teachings and shoutouts to the Diggers Rest Women’s Group. We like that we can attend an event that’s both heartfelt and photo‑ready.

The Community & Sports Pavilion has its own history on Playgroup pages — listed as a large room with a kitchen, and quietly noted that it “was previously used,” which is council‑speak for “remember when?” Still, the PlayConnect+ list (April 2025) assures us there’s a network of places for the littlies, so we’re not completely stranded.

Who’d hate living here? People who need a never‑ending stream of boutique everything or who want a rooftop bar every 800 metres. Who’d love it? Families who like honest footy rivalries, a cafe that knows your face at 52 Old Calder Highway, and community clubs that actually show up for Gurpurab and netball finals. We’re small, a bit stubborn, oddly proud — and frankly, Penny and Maggie couldn’t care less.
