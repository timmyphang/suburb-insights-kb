# Wamberal (NSW)

Slug: `wamberal-nsw`

We stand on the sand at Wamberal Beach — the one Time Out used in a supplied photo for the Central Coast round-up — watching the line of surfers at The Bend and the long view back to Terrigal Beach and Forresters. For years the local press has called us Terrigal’s quieter next-door neighbour, and honestly, we’re fine being the sensible sibling who brings the thermos.

Full disclosure: there aren’t any recent pieces in the file we’ve got, so consider this a nostalgic local’s tour through what journalists and guides have already said about us. Sitchu once wrote us up as ‘upscale’ with a lovely lagoon and houses that peer at the ocean; Urban List kept mentioning tiny homes and weekend stays a five-minute drive from the sand. Back in the day we cropped up in cheap-holiday lists and family guides — the kind of place photographed by the council and recommended to people who want surf without the full Terrigal circus.

Now for the community sport: seawalls. If you like small-town meetings that smell faintly of coastal engineering, boy do we have theatre. Central Coast Council lodged a Section 68 authorisation for sand nourishment backed by a sloping rock revetment; the Wamberal Protection Association gave it a thumbs-up, while Save Our Sand volunteer Mark Lamont called it effectively an “81,000-ton rock seawall.” The Hunter and Central Coast Regional Planning Panel has had a go at this too — knocking back or deferring development applications because landowner consent and environmental worries weren’t settled. It’s the kind of debate that turns school pickup chat into expert testimony.

Who would hate it here? If you want a permanent festival atmosphere, or you flinch at conversations about offshore sand nourishment, we’re probably not your vibe. You also won’t love the occasional planning-panel drama.

Who would love it? Families — Wamberal Public School’s bushland setting and its environmental study dam get a mention in the mum-blogs — surfers chasing The Bend, and anyone after a quiet Central Coast holiday (Urban List’s tiny-home roundups knew what they were doing). We’re low-key, weathered, and stubborn about our sand. Come for the beach, stay for the seawall arguments — they’re part of the view.
