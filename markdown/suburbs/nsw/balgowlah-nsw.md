# Balgowlah (NSW)

Slug: `balgowlah-nsw`

We know breakfast has already been decided when the Toyota Prado pulls up at the school gate with a reusable cup in the cupholder and a Lululemon jacket folded over the passenger seat. We are the ones who claim we "surfed Shelly this morning" as we Instagram the sunrise, then drive home to change into Pilates leggings for the 9am class at the studio on Sydney Road. We queue for an oat flat white at the Village Café on Sydney Rd and critique the seating like it's our civic duty. We park our Subaru Outback as "practical" and then launch into a meticulously researched rant about the school's fundraising priorities while organising the next bake sale.

Weekend rhythms here read like a well-curated feed. Saturday: 6:15am walk to Shelly with the labradoodle (brown, ridiculously photogenic), a quick photo for sunrise, oat flat white to go, Pilates at 9, Harris Farm at 10:30 for ethically sourced apples, then a lunch where PTA roles are subtly redistributed between sips. Sunday: kids' soccer at the reserve, a ferry dash to Manly for a long lunch and two boomerangs for the 'gram. The F45 membership gathers dust when our bodies demand "restorative" yoga. Our car seats hold surfboards, soccer boots and a mountain of reusable coffee cups displayed like merit badges.

Everything here escalates into a community referendum. The opening of a third artisan café on Sydney Rd sparked a months-long parking-allocation thread in the Balgowlah Facebook group. Kerb for prams or Prado bumpers? You would think sacred scripture was at stake. Then came the Great Gumtree Debate — one homeowner applied to remove a mature gumtree and suddenly eight petitions, three letterbox leaflets and a quote from a bloke in hiking sandals at the wrong time of year transformed our weekend into a civic opera.

We adore being "very local" and our local sometimes equals loud. We volunteer, we curate, we micro-manage communal life with the fierce tenderness of people who think community means attending everything and Instagramming half of it. We are petty and proud. We are competitive in the friendliest way.

Who would hate living here? A 26-year-old late-night techno DJ with a single-speed bike, thrift-store leather and an allergy to small talk would be made emphatically uncomfortable by our school-gate soap operas and polite-but-territorial parking etiquette. They'd feel like a noisy espresso machine in a yoga studio.

Who would love it? The 38-year-old part-time yoga teacher/marketing freelancer with a Subaru Outback, a labradoodle and three different routes to Manly would move in tomorrow and never look back. Welcome — come for the sunrise, stay for the PTA.
