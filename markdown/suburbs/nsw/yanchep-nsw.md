# Yanchep (NSW)

Slug: `yanchep-nsw`

We stand on Capricorn Esplanade watching a storm roll across the Indian Ocean and admit it: Yanchep has mood swings, and we love them. Back in the day the papers were breathless about the Yanchep Beach Club — a 300-seat, oceanfront idea with an eight‑metre marble bar and “uninterrupted views” — and we’d picture long lunches and the odd Monday Night Quiz while clouds brewed over the lagoon.

Our weekend looks suspiciously simple. First stop: Yanchep National Park — the koala-viewing boardwalk is our tired but beloved postcard, then the 2km Loch McNess loop if we’re pretending to be fit. If someone’s being smug about caves, we’d nod and point to Crystal Cave, the 45-minute stalactite tour that made a few of us feel both small and cultured. The Ghost House Walk Trail still does the spooky-history thing among tuart forests and banksias, and the Dwerta‑Mia and Boomerang Gorge trails get name‑checked in the same breath as “beginner‑friendly.”

Our little local soap has two reliable beats. Junkee once had a dig — in a complimentary way — about our tiny Yanchep LAC turning routine posts into social‑media gold, which is to say our police are conspicuously good at making mundanity entertaining. On the community front, the Yanchep Sports & Social Club on Yanchep Beach Road used to run bistro nights, pickleball sessions and coffee & cake notices; it’s where families and flat‑cap retirees collide amicably.

If you can’t stand kid-friendly lagoons, sheltered beaches or the thought of an 11km kayak in lousy weather (we’re looking at you, Year 11 Outdoor Ed), this isn’t your scene. If you need rooftop bars and a 24/7 tram, remind yourself Metronet was once billed as the thing that would “pull Yanchep onto the map” — that promise is part of the lore.

Who’ll love it? Families, bushwalkers, the kind of people who celebrate a bulk‑billed clinic notice at Lagoon Medical Centre on Ikara Lane and whose kids go to Yanchep Secondary College on Ravensbourne Street. We’re small, silly and stubbornly coastal — and if you can sit through a storm with a view, you’ll fit right in.
