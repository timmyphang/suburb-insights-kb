# North Parramatta (NSW)

Slug: `north-parramatta-nsw`

Look, we’ll admit it — our idea of a weekend is unashamedly low-key: sunburnt shoulders, a lukewarm kiosk coffee and the faint suspicion someone’s left a towel where they weren’t supposed to. Back in the day the reserve was reopened in 2015 and Broadsheet even reminded the city that Lake Parramatta (28 Bourke Street) has a roped-off swimming area, lifeguards in summer, toilets, parking and a kiosk. We know exactly where the BBQs are, where the playground hides from the wind and which track will make your phone die before you reach the waterfall after heavy rain (ParraParents called it a "hidden treasure" — and they weren’t wrong).

Our morning runs turn into accidental history lessons. We used to drag the pram past the Parramatta North Precinct, pause at the sandstone Female Factory and conspire about how a cafe in a building from 1888 felt both ridiculous and completely right. Havenstone — in that very Female Factory site at 5 Fleet Street — was the sort of heritage-brunch setup that made developers nervous (Time Out warned about heritage concerns when apartments started sniffing around). Concrete Playground once floated the idea of turning the precinct into a Carriageworks-style creative hub; we’re still half-for, half-suspicious.

We used to reward ourselves with shipping-container vibes at The Container (51–53 Albert Street, opposite Doyle Oval), which Time Out loved for its Allpress Espresso and açai bowls. And if you fancied a pint by the creek, Riverside Brewing Company — on 2 North Rocks Road — arrived in 2012 and set up a tasting bar beside the brewery floor; Concrete Playground wrote it up like it was ours alone.

Who would hate living here? People who despise dogs (The Container, Urban List noted, was dog-friendly), or anyone allergic to playgrounds and playgroups — we have Sherwin Park’s castle-themed upgrade and Monday and Thursday playgroups listed at Parramatta Life Church (1/106 Grose Street) and All Saints Anglican Church (29 Elizabeth Street). Who’d love it? Families, bushwalkers, weekend rowers (Parramatta Rowboats was in the guides for boat hire), and anyone who likes their coffee with a side of sandstone and civic history.

We’ll roast our postcode, sure — but only because we already know every corner worth defending.
