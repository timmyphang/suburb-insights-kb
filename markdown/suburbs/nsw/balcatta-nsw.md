# Balcatta (NSW)

Slug: `balcatta-nsw`

We know that Saturday morning ritual: balancing a toddler, a tote bag and a suspiciously warm yoghurt pouch while queuing at Jones Paskin Reserve. Back in the day our little circus ran on Balcatta Playgroup sessions on Jones Street — the volunteer committee kept it ticking and Browne’s Dairy even had us collecting yoghurt between 9–11 on Tuesdays. It felt wholesome, and slightly sticky.

For years the local press loved the same cast of characters: Nadia Scaramella coming out of retirement to open Panini Cnr in 2018, serving made‑to‑order rolls with Carmel Fazari; Dough Street’s 100% gluten‑free pastries and hot cross buns — yes, Dough Street at Shop 4/207 Jones St was repeatedly name‑checked for making gluten‑free feel dangerous and delicious; and Kings Euro Foods doing Eastern European deli things that made Saturday shopping feel continental.

If you wanted to swing a small person or big dog into action, Balcatta used to deliver. Axo’s Playhouse at 6 Halley Road got the kids sorted with separate zones for toddlers and big kids, and Kalmpets Dog Day Care — Dr Kate Lindsey’s brainchild — was a “sensory paradise for dogs” (the dogs were apparently very cultured). For anyone with hands that prefer chalk over cutlery, Rockface at 7B Ledgar Rd was billed as Perth’s biggest indoor climbing venue, with walls and routes to make you feel both heroic and mildly petrified.

There’s a small‑town deli vibe too: Balcatta Growers Fresh on Karrinyup Road was the family‑run place for antipasto and paninis, while Kings Euro Foods supplied the cured meats you pretend you only buy for entertaining.

We’ll admit: most of what we’ve told you comes from older write‑ups — lots of nostalgia, little breaking news. So consider this a postcard from a place that likes its playgroups, its gluten‑free croissants, and its deli counters.

Who would hate living here? Someone allergic to small people’s crafts, gourmet buns, and other people’s dogs. Who would love it? Families, climbers, sandwich devotees, and anyone who thinks “continental deli” makes an ordinary Wednesday feel like a holiday.
