# Footscray (NSW)

Slug: `footscray-nsw`

Footscray, NSW — back in the day we’d roll out of bed, rub sleep from our eyes and head straight for the cavernous Footscray Market like it owed us a breakfast. As Concrete Playground observed, the market used to be — and still feels like — the heart of the place: sixty-odd stalls, a busy meat hall, seafood cries and that glorious, chaotic march of cuisines that made us proud to be here.

We remember queuing at Footscray Milking Station or grabbing a sandwich from Ollie’s Deli on Barkly Street, then dawdling past the Footscray Community Arts Centre where the weird and brilliant stuff happens in those heritage-listed buildings beside the river. Broadsheet used to list a million cafés and bars — Cafe Rudimentary, Cafe Elka, West 48 — proof that gentrification came at us with espresso machines and approval ratings. Urban List and Time Out loved to remind everyone of our restaurants; we didn’t mind the attention, just the extra people on our streets.

Speaking of change: who could forget the Franco Cozzo building’s dramatic rebirth? Pedestrian ran with the story — one minute it was furniture folklore, the next it was Moon Dog Wild West with a mechanical bucking bull and three levels of Tex‑Mex chaos. We’ve always been ambivalent about that kind of shiny, loud change, but you can’t fault a suburb for trying a mechanical bull.

If you can’t handle noise and honest markets, Footscray will test you. Folks who hate queues, vinyl walls, or the memory of tougher years — Junkee’s pieces about the area’s rougher moments aren’t fiction — will find it a challenging place to live. Likewise, anyone allergic to multicultural food will starve emotionally.

But if you love food that doesn’t pretend to be something else, festivals that keep going until the street says stop (we’ve had the Sleepless Footscray Festival), craft beer from Hop Nation and small venues like Kindred Bandroom or Back Alley Sally’s — you’ll feel like you belong. We’re messy, loud, brilliant and stubbornly local. We know our market stalls, our arts centre and our history — and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
