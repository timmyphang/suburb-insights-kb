# Thornbury (NSW)

Slug: `thornbury-nsw`

Back in the day, High Street — the stretch between Darebin Road and Dundas Street — felt like one long, slightly tipsy art project. We know every brick of it: the converted cinema with its faded marquee, the late-night diner that thinks neon is still edgy, the bar that started as a playlist and wound up a venue. As Broadsheet noted, Thornbury has been a Greek and Italian stronghold that slowly gentrified from about 2010 — and honestly, we quite like the mash-up.

A weekend for us used to look like this: a matinee at the Thornbury Picture House (that 57-seat boutique cinema carved out of a 1919 garage), a detour to Welcome To Thornbury to see which food truck was winning the Chicken Nugget Festival that year, then a stagger down High Street to check which bar had the best bocce setup. Broadsheet and Time Out have both chronicled the strip; Concrete Playground even reminds us the Thornbury Theatre still overachieves with its art‑deco drama.

We’re not precious about our icons. Little Henri and Rat The Cafe were the sort of places you’d pop into for something that felt like “all-day” dining before brunch became a life choice; Capers slipped between late-night and diner with an unassuming storefront; and Chabela put proper huaraches on the map for those of us who like to pretend we know Mexican food beyond tacos.

The petty drama, such as it is, is mostly cultural: more vinyl shops or more pizza ovens? Broadsheet traced the creeping gentrification, and yes, sometimes we roll our eyes at yet another bar named with ironic seriousness — Northern Git, Carwyn Cellars, Pallino Bar & Bocce, Franklin’s Bar — but it’s our kind of eye-roll.

Who would hate living here? Someone who demands silence, predictability and a suburban lawn without a single quirky venue within walking distance. Who would love it? Folks who want an honest High Street, small cinemas, a theatre that keeps surprising us, food trucks that stage their own festivals, and a neighbourhood that wears its history and its reinvention on its sleeve. We’ll keep the bocce lane warm.
