# Gembrook (NSW)

Slug: `gembrook-nsw`

Back in the day we’d pile into the car for a weekend escape and pretend the city never existed. Our ritual used to be the same: a detour past Puffing Billy’s station, a loaf from Gembrook Bakery (yes, the one "just up from Gembrook’s Puffing Billy Railway Station"), and a nosy at The Station House across the tracks, which Urban List once called our friendly local Italian pizza parlour after a schmick renovation. Pure Dandenong Ranges vibes, minus the Instagram captions.

We’re small but suspiciously well-stocked in attractions for a town that still smells faintly of coal from Kurth Kiln’s charcoal days. Kurth Kiln Regional Park — the WWII kiln turned picnicking and camping spot — is where we take the kids and the visiting in-laws who think “bushwalk” means “bring a picnic rug and a cardigan.” Scouts Australia Gilwell Park gets a shout too; Time Out used to list its gardens as a thing to do here. And yes, Puffing Billy’s Train of Lights has always been the shiny, sell-out ticket — Time Out reminded us how quickly those seats disappear.

We’re practical people. There was a vet on Main St — Gembrook Veterinary Clinic at 87 Main St — and Gembrook & Emerald Mechanical Repairs was the place you rang when your weekend mission met a mechanical mercy dash (they were listed as open Mon–Fri, 8–5). Even the menus nod to our region: Broadsheet once noted potato cakes from "Gembrook, Gippsland" turning up in Andy Lee’s dim sim project. And if you wanted a craftier sip, someone in Sydney bragged about Gembrook Hill on their wine list.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe steam trains, heritage kilns, and people who treat a daytrip like a minor pilgrimage, you’ll find Gembrook unbearably quaint. If you can’t cope with towns that still have proper main streets and actual local businesses (yes, in the listing sense), move along.

Who’ll love it? Anyone chasing family-friendly hikes, a nostalgic puff of steam, honest pizza across from the station, or a quiet weekend that reads like a nature essay. We may be small, and our press clippings are from "back in the day," but we wear that like a well-baked loaf: crusty on the outside, inviting on the inside.
