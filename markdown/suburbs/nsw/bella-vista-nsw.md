# Bella Vista (NSW)

Slug: `bella-vista-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed and treat Bella Vista like a small-town itinerary that somehow learned to like its carbs and its Cabernet. We’d queue—politely—for Good Fella at 5 Celebration Drive for those bagels and the Mont Blanc-style thing Urban List couldn’t stop raving about, then complain about how nice the baristas were. Concrete Playground even called it The Hills’ hidden gem, which made us smug and slightly defensive at the same time.

By lunch we’d argue over where to take visiting parents: the lakeside theatrics of The Meat & Wine Co (Broadsheet said steak was the star, yes including Wagyu, and who are we to disagree?) or Zac’s Great Foods when we wanted to introduce someone to qabli pilaf and the kind of Afghan cooking the listings insist is unmissable. The Bella Vista Hotel used to be the cheeky option for cocktails — Urban List noted its food and that Chef Cemal Erdonmez cut his teeth there before heading off to Quay, which is the culinary equivalent of getting your apprenticeship and then suddenly wearing a silk scarf.

Weekends were babysat by the pocket park by Bella Vista Station — Hills District Mums celebrated its opening — while the kids bounced at Bella Vista Studio on Lexington Drive or ran around with Sydney Hills Gymnastics playgroups. Time Out even booked Bella Vista Farm for a multisensory show called Echoes (that one was slated for 2025), because why not have an immersive light thing after the pavlova and the steak?

Who would hate living here? If you loathe friendly queues, pocket parks, lakeside steaks, or the idea of multisensory light shows in a farm paddock, you’ll probably pack up. If the notion of proposed new Western Sydney airport flight paths floating over suburbs gives you a civic aneurysm (Time Out covered those maps), maybe look elsewhere — we’ll wave you off with a bagel.

Who would love it? Families who like sensible playgroups, people who appreciate a very good bagel, and anyone chasing a night that goes from Afghan comfort food to Wagyu and cheers at the Bella Vista Hotel. We’re quietly proud, mildly culinary, and suspiciously good at hosting.
