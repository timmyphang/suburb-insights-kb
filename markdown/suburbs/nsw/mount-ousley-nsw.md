# Mount Ousley (NSW)

Slug: `mount-ousley-nsw`

We wake up where the mountains meet the sea — literally. As Broadsheet cheekily points out, Wollongong’s coastline is backed by Mount Keira, Brokers Nose, Mount Kembla and yes, good old Mount Ousley. We stand on our little ridge, squint at the water, and pretend we're rugged coastal romantics while actually arguing about whose turn it is to walk the dog.

Our weekend routine is gloriously small‑town. We used to drop the littlies at Mt Ousley Pre‑School on 3 David Crescent (the place that’s been around since 1972 and had Shelley Zipparo as Owner/Director since 1998). The local primary — Mount Ousley Public School on 31 McGrath Street — is the kind of small school that, according to its blurb, “has a big impact.” Which translates to: everyone knows if you forgot to pack snacks and also your birthday cake recipe.

There isn’t much by way of scandal here, unless you count the P&C’s ambitious social calendar. Our Mount Ousley Public School P&C ran a disco fundraiser on Thursday 26 June and even put on a 'Comedy for a Cause' on 21 March 2026 — yes, we fundraise with jazz hands and awkward dance moves. The Mount Ousley Physical Culture Club even advertised for members heading into 2026 — so if your idea of a Friday night is physie rehearsals, we’ll meet you on the community hall floor, clapping in unison.

Who would hate it here? If you thrive on anonymity, sprawling nightlife or a café on every corner, Mount Ousley will feel like a village where the PTA knows your playlist. If you need every weekend to be a food market or an artisanal event, you’ll be disappointed — our main boast is hills, schools, and community notices.

Who would love it? Families, retirees who like a bit of community theatre, physie seniors, and anyone who appreciates being part of a place where the kids’ disco actually doubles as civic duty. We might not be flashy, but between the mountain views and the small-school vibe, we’re quietly insistent: this is home.
