# Somerville (NSW)

Slug: `somerville-nsw`

We live in Somerville, NSW — the town so stealthy even the news gets confused about which Somerville to visit. Every morning we wake up, boil the kettle, open our feeds and watch journalists fall down a rabbit hole of other Somervilles: Broadsheet raves about Dinosaur World in Somerville on the Mornington Peninsula; UrbanList and Sitchu fawn over the Somerville Auditorium and Somerville Outdoor Cinema in Perth; UrbanList also remembers a freshly spruced Somerville Park at 673X Blaxland Road in Eastwood after a $4.7 million makeover. All older stories, all about somewhere else. We’re still here, making do with our own postcode’s anonymity like it’s vintage.

Our weekend routine is delightfully unromantic: we stroll around the suburb, half expecting an arts festival or outdoor screening to pop up and announce itself. Instead we find the kind of placid, unbothered streets that mean you can park where you like and someone will probably borrow your lawnmower without asking. When media do talk “Somerville”, they’re talking Union Square in Boston or LEGOLAND at Assembly Row — lovely places, they just aren’t ours. So if you were hoping for a glowing Time Out spread, you might be disappointed; most of the press we could find is older and about other Somervilles.

Our petty drama is therefore institutional: an identity crisis curated by headline writers. We used to be the answer to someone’s quiz question; now we’re the punctuation mark at the end of other people’s travel pieces. No scandal, no celebrity chef, no buzzy new cafe to stake a claim — just reliable neighbourhoodness.

Who would hate living here? Social media influencers who need their postcode to trend and people allergic to being off-menu. Who would love it? Anyone who likes community without a press release, families who cherish quiet parks (other Somervilles get the makeover headlines — we’ve got the tree-lined afternoons), and people who prefer living somewhere that’s content to be itself, even if the byline keeps going elsewhere.

We’ll keep being Somerville, NSW — a little mysterious, a touch undercaffeinated in the national press, and very much ours.
