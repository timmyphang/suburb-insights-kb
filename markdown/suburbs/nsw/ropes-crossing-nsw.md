# Ropes Crossing (NSW)

Slug: `ropes-crossing-nsw`

We know the sound of 54 Pulley Drive before we see it — a chorus of tiny feet, parachute fabric flapping, and someone humming a kids’ pop song slightly off-key. Back in the day the Ropes Crossing social calendar basically lived at the Ropes Crossing Community Hub (54 Pulley Drive), and if you wanted to know what was on, you checked the Playgroup NSW pages.

We used to do mornings by the hub: Play Strong on Tuesdays at 10am for the littlest ones, PALS Ropes Crossing Supported at 10am on Wednesdays for families who needed a bit more structure, and the Ropes Crossing Hub’s facilitated playgroup Wednesday at midday where parachute, singing and sensory play ruled the roost. If you had questions, you emailed Tina — yes, thitchen@playgroupnsw.org.au — or ssultan@playgroupnsw.org.au and hoped for a reply before nap time.

There wasn’t much in the way of scandal — unless you count the perennial debate over who folded the parachute wrong — but what the archive does show is our insistence on doing community properly. We even hosted an All Abilities Play Day on 2 December, 10am–12pm: face‑painting, balloon twisting, morning tea and info from local disability providers. Inclusive? Absolutely. Loud? Also absolutely.

Who would hate living here? If you’re allergic to children’s singing, karaoke-grade face paint, and the smell of someone’s packed morning tea at 11am, Ropes Crossing might feel like a siege. Nightlife seekers who want curated cocktails at 2am will be underwhelmed. Our busiest intersection is more likely to be a pram queue than a trendy bar line.

Who would love it? Families who want a real support network — daytime programs for 0–3s and 0–6s, inclusive playgroups, and the kind of neighbours who’ll bring an extra biscuit for morning tea. We’re proud of that: small, practical, and loud in all the right ways. So come for the parachute, stay for the community — and bring a spare singalong tune.
