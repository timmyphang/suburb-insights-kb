# Geelong (NSW)

Slug: `geelong-nsw`

Back in the day — and by “day” we mean the clippings we’ve got, which are mostly older — we’d start Saturday at Eastern Beach Reserve pretending the bay hadn’t noticed our sartorial indecision. For a while we’d drift down to the Geelong Waterfront Makers & Growers Market, then wobble through Little Malop Street or Pakington Street pretending we were just “popping in” for one thing. The Dome — the Geelong Library and Heritage Centre — used to be the place you bumped into people who actually read the council reports for fun. Powerhouse Geelong was the other flex: a hulking former power station turned giant street‑art gallery where even our midlife crises look cultured.

We’ll be honest: the press pack we were given is mostly looking back. So this is nostalgic roasting, not current concierge. As Broadsheet and Concrete Playground recorded, Geelong has a history as an industrial powerhouse — recuerdo includes the wool‑scouring mill’s final bale — and a culinary glow‑up (Igni’s discreet room, Sumi’s robata, Songbird in Igni’s old digs, Jack Rabbit’s rabbit-and-pork roulade and Ok Smash’s cheeseburger all pop up in older guides). Pavilion Geelong cropped up as a seaside favourite. Crowne Plaza arriving on the waterfront was read as a sign we were aiming for big‑city ambitions.

The local arc is our favourite bit of drama: Time Out even called us the top Australian tree‑change destination, with a 116% migration spike — so yes, we’re a bit overrun by people who thought “sea change” meant “same problems, different postcode.” There was talk of a huge new waterfront venue, the Nyaal Banyul Geelong Convention and Event Centre, reported as due to open in July — which is code for “prepare for buses.”

Who would hate living here? People who want permanent anonymity. If you despise weekenders, festivals, or the occasional riverside redevelopment, you’ll be filing your relocation paperwork before the next Expression of Interest closes. Who’d love it? Food lovers, culture vultures, market mums — the Roslyn Playgroup in Highton and Keeping Mum sessions used to keep the tiny humans entertained — and anyone who likes a city that can be both industrial and Instagrammable, often in the same street. We know where every corner creaks; we’ll forgive Geelong for its identity crisis because, honestly, it keeps things interesting.
