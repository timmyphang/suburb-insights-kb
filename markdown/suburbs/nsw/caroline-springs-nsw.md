# Caroline Springs (NSW)

Slug: `caroline-springs-nsw`

We stroll down Caroline Springs Boulevard like we own the place — or at least like we’ve got a loyalty card for it. Back in the day the press loved to write about our tiny, very competitive food scene, so forgive us if our weekend ritual reads like a pastry-driven civic duty.

First stop used to be Pecks Road (they opened their first Caroline Springs shop in 2022) — the family-run Filipino cafe that people wrote about for triple-ube doughnuts that apparently take three days to make. We’re not guaranteed that kind of patience, but we will file that away as local legend alongside mentions of Caramilk crullers, ube bombolones and the odd “Filipino banh mi” twist they were dishing up.

If pastries aren’t doing it for you, there was always Roger Roger Deli — the place Sitchu said was making a serious play for Melbourne’s cinnamon-scroll crown and serving next-level focaccias like a chicken cotoletta focaccia. Meanwhile Urban List once noted that Adana Co. was set to plant its kebab flag at Shop 14, Northlake Centre, 114B Gourlay Road, so yes, we’ve been collecting international consolation prizes for some time.

For families who needed to let off steam, Time Out’s write-up of the WestWaters Hotel & Entertainment Complex (10–20 Lake St) called it the gigantic waterfront playground for functions and kids — we can confirm the kids always win. Playgroups? We’ve got Funtastic Playcentre and the Caroline Springs Leisure Centre Tennis Pavilion pencilled into our calendar because someone has to keep the tiny humans entertained.

We’ve also been the kind of suburb that attracts pop-ups and one-hit wonders: Bakt’s Basque cheesecake did an in-house pop-up for a Suburb to City series, St Burgs at 1042 Western Highway earned a nod for banana pudding, and Cocobella (15e 13/15 Lake Street) cropped up on lists about viral drinks.

Who would hate living here? People who crave CBD street cred, or anyone allergic to the smell of toasted dough and mild culinary competitiveness. Who would love it? Families, snack snobs, pop-up chasers and anyone who enjoys a waterfront function followed by a debate over whether Roger Roger’s scroll beats Pecks Road’s ube.

Yes, we know the articles are from earlier write-ups — we’re a place with a quiet pride and a stubborn fondness for dessert-based status symbols. Come for the pastries, stay for the playgroup drama.
