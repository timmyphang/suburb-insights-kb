# Oatlands (NSW)

Slug: `oatlands-nsw`

We know Oatlands like the back pocket of our weekend shorts. Walk down Phillip Street, past Prindle and Alanas Ave, and you’ll end up at George Gollan Reserve where kids and slightly-too-competitive parents share a surprisingly bushy patch: picnic seats, a basketball hoop and enough climbing gear (and a three-swing set) to stage a mini Olympics. As ParraParents reminds us, this is the sort of place where you’ll time your day around swing rotations, not peak-hour trains.

Our little patch of suburbia is delightfully compact. Fred Robertson Reserve is the one that tricks you into thinking you’ve escaped the suburbs — a tiny playground, scooter and bike track, a street library, and bushland that slips into the Vineyard Creek bushwalk. There’s a family-friendly cafe somewhere nearby if you’ve forgotten snacks (ParraParents confirms the cafe is a thing; they don’t name the barista who judges your snack choices).

Then there’s Walter Brown Park — officially listed at 2A Gibbons St but cheekily filed as Telopea on council maps. It’s the fenced, under-sixers’ kingdom: a boat-themed play gym, swings and synthetic grass so soft your toddler will forgive you for not bringing a hat. The cafe across the road is our unofficial tribunal for scraped knees and triumphant cookie purchases.

We’ll own it: we’re wedged between Kissing Point Road, the Oatlands Golf Club, Vineyard Creek Reserve and the Dundas to Telopea train line. That sounds very “map pin” and honestly, we like it that way. Don’t come here if you’re after rooftop bars, artisanal everything, or a metro-size drama playlist — you’ll hate the calm, the trees, and the fact that your phone battery lasts longer without constant notifications.

Who will love Oatlands? Families who court muddy shoes as a lifestyle choice, dog owners who measure distance in bushwalks, and anyone who prefers small reserves with well-labelled swings over the chaos of a shopping centre. We’re not trying to be anything other than what we are: parks, pocket-sized community bits, and that priceless council map quirk that has us filed under a different suburb just to keep things interesting. Come for the playgrounds, stay for the street library, leave with a smug sense of suburban superiority.
