# Carnegie (NSW)

Slug: `carnegie-nsw`

Back in the day we’d wobble down Koornang Road like it was our own little late‑night cruise — not because we were trendy, but because you could get everything from a fierce tom yum burrata to a bagel with more personality than your ex. Broadsheet and Time Out used to agree: the strip was a proper foodie hub.

A typical weekend for us was a hops-and-stagger between spots that used to make this place hum. We’d queue (politely) for Huff Bagelry’s service window on Koornang, ogle Nuvoletta Gelateria’s Jersey‑milk scoops, and whisper reverent things about Left Field — which Time Out pointed out was run by the team behind Tall Timber and Marquis of Lorne. If we were feeling adventurous, Concrete Playground reminded us Tyga brought retro Bangkok energy and nonstop vinyls; Urban List swore by the curry puff at T6 Patisserie, baked by Tommy Er.

We aren’t pretending this strip invented cuisine — it just does a very committed job of borrowing it. Gaon Gamasot Gomtang even had an ex‑Nobu chef couple, Hyunjin Kim and Kyung Lee, making long‑braised broths feel like a local habit. Shyun Sumibiyaki and Good Old Mukka used to stake out their little territories too, so you could build an entire afternoon around broths, sumibiyaki and biryani without getting in the car.

Petty local drama? The papers loved hailing the “new” openings — Tyga on Koornang made the rounds in every food column — but mostly our quarrels were civil: who stole whose parking spot outside Panda Hot Pot at 1060 Dandenong Road, and which bakery really had the best laminated curry puff. No council coups, just competitive snacking.

Who would loathe living here? People who want their suburb to be exclusively one thing — a quiet cul‑de‑sac or a monoculture of beige chain stores. This place used to be deliciously indecisive.

Who would adore it? Anyone who likes eating like they’re on a micro‑holiday down the block: bagels, gelato, katsu, hot pot, and a little neo‑Southeast Asian glamour. For years the local listings were our love letters — nostalgic, slightly greasy, and absolutely ours.
