# Pretty Beach (NSW)

Slug: `pretty-beach-nsw`

We wake up here with sand in our shoes and an identity crisis — is Pretty Beach part of Bouddi or Murramarang this week? Depends which travel writer you ask. Concrete Playground told Sydneysiders to “sashay up” to Pretty Beach House at 83 High View Road, while Urban List cheekily listed Pretty Beach under Murramarang. As locals, we just shrug and pick a park: Bouddi for the cliff-top drama, Murramarang when we want to sound properly rugged.

Back in the day the glossy mags made us a boutique destination. Concrete Playground and Sitchu waxed lyrical about Pretty Beach House — adults-only pavilions, panoramic views — and Urban List called the area one of the Central Coast’s coveted bushland lodges. We’re not above enjoying that sort of thing; we just enjoyed it with muddy boots and a cheeky sense of superiority.

If you like official praise, we’ve got childcare credentials to flex. The Pretty Beach Community Preschool was listed at 150 Heath Rd and, as of 2018, was rated Exceeding the National Quality Standard. The After School Klub used to be listed at 4 Pretty Beach Rd, offering before- and after-school care plus vacation programs. Practical, quiet, reliable — the sort of things travel pieces forget between the sunset photos.

Our local drama? It’s petty and editorial: Sydney magazines arguing which national park gets our postcode. We’ve seen worse feuds (not much worse), but we’d be lying if we said we didn’t enjoy being mentioned in the same breath as John Singleton’s hospitality projects — Concrete Playground even linked our name to his Bon Pavilion and Bells at Killcare mentions. It makes us feel posh for five minutes.

Who would hate living here? People who need certainty on maps, lovers of neon-lit nightlife, and anyone who expects a 24/7 latte shrine on every corner. Who would love it? Walkers lining up the Murramarang South Coast Walk from Pretty Beach to Maloneys Beach (Time Out noted the route), lovers of dramatic coastline, and anyone who likes being the answer to a magazine’s caption.

We’re quiet, occasionally confused by our own press clippings, and impossibly pretty for being so modest about it. Come for the views, stay because Google can’t agree where we belong.
