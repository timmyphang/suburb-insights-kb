# Mount Druitt (NSW)

Slug: `mount-druitt-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed, grab sunscreen and head down to the Mount Druitt Swimming Centre — opened in 1975 and still the place where kids learn to canonball with institutional pride. After that, the ritual used to be a Fried Brothers pit stop (yes, the food truck everyone names when you brag about where you actually ate last weekend) and a cinema session on bean bags at Hoyts Mount Druitt, where watching a rom-com is somehow more comfortable when you can recline like a lazy king.

We know we’ve been a favourite testing ground. Enrico Donatello famously tried his mie bakso in Mount Druitt before taking Bakso Barokah to Surry Hills, and Slim’s Quality Burgers once cut its teeth here too. Teta’s Deli in Potts Point still brags about dough from Mount Druitt — Dina El‑Kaddoumi’s uncle’s bakery and even a nod to local staple Maggie’s Pizza — so don’t tell us we’re invisible, we just prefer our crumbs to be honest.

Yes, the suburb’s had its tabloid moments — SBS’s Struggle Street sparked criticism and even saw an ad pulled amid accusations of “poverty porn,” which we’ll raise a skeptical eyebrow at. But if your only takeaway from western Sydney is a single TV doc, you’ve missed the bit where Mount Druitt produced ONEFOUR, gave Esky the Emcee a stage at the Sydney Sacred Music Festival rooftop, and where Vv Pete cut his teeth at Street University. Diverse? Absolutely. Predictable? Not on your life.

Would someone who wants a one-note suburb hate living here? Definitely. If you can’t cope with debutante balls sitting alongside drill rap, if airport-buzz plans like Route 772 to the new Western Sydney International Airport (yep, planners talked buses to Penrith, Liverpool and Campbelltown) make you nervous, this isn’t for you.

Who’d love it here? Young families using Familylinks and Children First playgroups, music heads who follow ONEFOUR and Esky, bargain burger hunters nostalgic for Slim’s, swimmers who swear by that grassy poolside — and anyone who likes a place that refuses to be defined by someone else’s headline. We know our postcode (2770), we own our contradictions, and we’d have it no other way.
