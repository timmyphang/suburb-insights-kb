# North Adelaide (NSW)

Slug: `north-adelaide-nsw`

Back in the day we’d saunter down O’Connell Street like we owned the footpath—mostly because Broadsheet had already mapped every corner of it. Our weekend ritual used to be a suspiciously theatrical one: queue at Shadow Baking for the brioche stuffed with gelato (yes, that was a thing), nip into the Eighty Eight O’Connell mercantile circus where Mercato set up an Italian pantry, seafood butcher and a wine store — and, famously, pasta bowls and $2 espressos. We felt cultured and slightly over-caffeinated.

If you wanted proper carbs, Bakery On O’Connell and Perryman’s at 54 Tynte were the reliable glue holding our diets together. For pizza emergencies there was Falcone on O’Connell (the Barone siblings’ full-time move from Side Hustle days), and if we were feeling Mediterranean we'd remember Icardis with its woodfired oven and smoky prawns. The Flying Fig on Jeffcott Street fed our bagel cravings; Mini Lokanta put 214 Gover St on a map we sometimes pretended to understand.

Local life came with theatrics. The Adelaide Zoo provided headline material beyond the koala hugs — from a red panda who made an escape and an unfortunate ATM heist that forced a temporary closure, news that had us all scandalised and oddly proud of our zoo’s soap-opera energy. Sports fans and casual window-watchers knew the Oval precinct: you could book a stay at the Majestic Minima or the Oval Hotel on King William Road, and even climb the Roofclimb Adelaide Oval off War Memorial Drive if you wanted your skyline with a side of vertigo.

Who would hate it here? People who can’t stand heritage facades, game-day crowds or the smug satisfaction of a neighbourhood that’s equal parts old pub (British Hotel since 1838, The Welli since 1851) and boutique bakery culture. Also anyone allergic to tiny, perfectly curated menus.

Who’d love it? Families — because Glover North “Helicopter Park” on Lefevre Terrace, Bundey’s Paddock / Tidlangga and Bush Magic Playground mean kids actually remember being kids — and the mums and dads who still use Playgroup SA and Barker Kindergarten on 197 Tynte Street to make friends. Food obsessives, nostalgia seekers and anyone who appreciates a suburb that’s quietly earned its little contradictions: genteel, grubby, and deliciously ours.
