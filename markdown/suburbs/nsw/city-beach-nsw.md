# City Beach (NSW)

Slug: `city-beach-nsw`

Back in the day we were the kind of beach that magazine writers got sentimental about — linen, ocean light and the inevitable coastal martini. Most of what anyone’s written about us is from a few feature pieces and local guides, so think nostalgia-heavy, not breaking-news. We’re the suburb they write about when they want sunsets and scallops.

Mornings here used to mean sand between your toes and a coffee-run that doubled as people-watching: Bert’s City Beach (187 Challenger Parade) cropped up in those stories as the new nostalgic all‑day hang — coastal martinis, oysters, brekkie buns, sourdough pizzas and beers on tap — a place that winked at the original City Beach Tea Rooms. If you went further along the strip you’d read about Odyssea’s wrap‑around deck and its big seafood focus, the sort of 300‑seat venue built for ocean views. Clancy’s was always the sunset-brew spot, Hamptons got a shout for ocean-facing short ribs, and The Groper & His Wife has the proper local backstory — it rebuilt and reopened after a fire and kept its commitment to local, sustainable seafood.

Our weekend ritual, per the guides, involved a walk on the Bold Park Zamia Trail for city-and-ocean views, a playground detour and then a seaside lunch. The coastal playgroup used to meet on Fred Burton Way — 230 metres from the beach — so we’re as kid-friendly as the brochures brag.

Local petty drama? The Groper’s rebuild after the fire made the papers — that was the kind of small-town crisis that turns into a mural and a comeback story. Other than that, we’re more about redevelopments and restaurant roundups than council coups.

Who would hate living here: anyone who can’t stand seafood, or who loathes queues for ocean views — the place leans hard into fish-and-chips and sundowner culture. Also, if you despise nostalgia, steer clear of Bert’s “old-school soul” vibes.

Who’d love it: families (the playgroups and playgrounds get mentioned everywhere), walkers and anyone who judges suburbs by sunsets and seafood. For years the local press framed us as the coast’s polite, linen-clad answer to a good afternoon — and honestly, we’ll take that.
