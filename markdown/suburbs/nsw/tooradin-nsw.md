# Tooradin (NSW)

Slug: `tooradin-nsw`

We like to think our perfect Saturday starts with a tactical deployment of picnic rugs at the Tooradin Foreshore — back in the day the Playgroup Victoria listing told us it was "nestled along the beautiful Tooradin Foreshore," with "ample open space, shady spots, convenient parking" and kids allowed to "search for shells, build sandcastles and splash in the shallows." Truth: half our parenting credentials come from knowing which patch of shade keeps the sand out of sippy cups.

We are the people who can do the morning loop: a stroll along the foreshore until someone notices another mussel shell and the negotiated return to the car — because yes, parking matters here, as the venue blurb politely harrumphs. We relish the kind of coastal calm that looks accidental to outsiders but is very much curated by a small army of community noticeboards.

Speaking of noticeboards, our township has its own fan club online. The Tooradin Community Notice Board Hub on Find It Locally and the Casey City Community Info Hub make us feel busy without leaving the postcode — if it isn’t on the noticeboard, it simply didn’t happen. Nearby towns get name-checked like stage directions: Blind Bight, Cannons Creek, Koo Wee Rup, Lang Lang, Warneet, Dalmore — we keep a mental Rolodex of which aunties live in which locality.

Petty drama? We’ll admit the most dramatic thing around here is deciding which community group gets the hall on a Wednesday night. Otherwise we live where the loudest voice is usually a child announcing they’ve found another shell.

Who would hate living here: people who need neon-lit nightlife or endless choice of boutiques. If your idea of excitement is a new espresso bar on every corner, you’ll be disappointed — but at least you’ll have plenty of noticeboards to complain on.

Who would love it: families who like a peaceful, natural setting for tots to build sandcastles; people who appreciate proximity to local services — the Casey City hub lists Council, SES and Lifeline — and anyone who wants a postcode that says "coast, community and a bit of lovely quiet." We’re proud of our little foreshore and even prouder of the fact we can still find a shady spot.
