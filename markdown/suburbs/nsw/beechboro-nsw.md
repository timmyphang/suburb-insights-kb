# Beechboro (NSW)

Slug: `beechboro-nsw`

We live in Beechboro, NSW, and yes, we know the internet thinks our best bits are a hop, skip and a very confused postcode away. Back in the day, if you fancied fresh pasta or cake you’d be pointed — helpfully, if inaccurately — at Charlie’s Fresh Food Market at the corner of Beechboro and Walter Road in Morley, as Urban List once noted. Urban List also called 3.8 Baysie at 19 Beechboro Rd S, Bayswater “adorable,” which somehow reads as a compliment and a city-reviewer shrug.

Our Saturday morning ritual used to be very aspirational: we’d dream about queues at Charlie’s for something artisanal, while the idea of “adorable” breakfasts at 3.8 Baysie felt like bragging rights. That’s what the internet gave us — potential brunch envy, from suburbs not technically in our suburb.

Locally, the things that actually keep us together are less glossy and more useful. FindGlocal used to list the Beechboro Senior Football Club (complete with the Bombers club song) and the Northern Saints Basketball Club at 332 Benara Road — both reminders that our weekend noise is more whistle-and-three-points than curated playlists. FindGlocal also showed Oz Counselling & Psychological Services at 29 Meagher Way, and a Roe Estate development at 463 Beechboro Road North promising a boutique enclave and “about 30 minutes” to the Perth CBD — the sort of reassurance developers hand out like flyers in a windstorm.

Petty local drama? The data doesn’t give us juicy fights, so we’ll spare you manufactured scandals. The real tension here is deciding whether to laugh at being misfiled on lifestyle sites or to take it as free advertising.

Who would hate it here: people who need nightly rooftop DJs and an endless parade of new concept stores. Who’d love it: families, club-goers, and anyone who values a community centre-of-gravity that isn’t trying to be a postcard.

We’re proud of our slightly-misplaced reputation. For years the guides pointed elsewhere — and that’s fine. We know where the footy club sings, where the basketballs thump, and where, in another postcode, someone will probably call a place “adorable” on our behalf.
