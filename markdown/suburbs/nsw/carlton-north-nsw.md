# Carlton North (NSW)

Slug: `carlton-north-nsw`

We wake up in Carlton North to two things: the smell of buttered pastry and someone arguing on a local forum about whether queues are a public nuisance. Back in the day the guides loved us — Broadsheet kept a tidy Carlton North directory, Time Out wrote loving area guides and Concrete Playground ran pub reviews — so yeah, people have been talking about us for a while (most of the clippings here are from before 2024, so we’ll say “used to” a lot).

A typical weekend used to look like this: we’d amble between Rathdowne and Nicholson, pretend we weren’t judging anyone, and buy croissants from Monforte Viennoiserie at 585a Canning Street or Back Alley Bakes on 645 Nicholson Street. If we were feeling bohemian we’d linger where Gerald’s Bar once hosted The Parlour on Lygon Street; if we wanted decent pub chaos we’d recall The Great Northern Hotel — 1883 vintage, huge beer garden, rotating taps — and the Kent Hotel which reopened with a dry‑aged steak emphasis and a very respectable fish ’n’ chips and roast-chicken resume. Skinner & Hackett at 400 Rathdowne Street used to feed our carnivorous weeknight fantasies. Brico’s ivy courtyard made the Sunday lunch column inches in Time Out.

The local drama? Classic inner‑north: neighbours whining about queues. Pedestrian flagged the NIMBYs who were furious that Florian’s customers were lining up outside — nothing like a line for coffee to summon civic rage. We’re proud of our small, loud debates; they’re basically our version of council theatre.

Who would hate living here: people who crave anonymity, armies of silence, or anyone allergic to foot traffic, boutique pastry politics or well‑meaning wine bars. Misfits include queue‑shunners and anyone who despises heritage terraces and tree‑lined streets.

Who would love it here: lovers of cheap hyper‑local opinion, croissants that make plane tickets to Paris look excessive, pub folk who worship a rotating tap list, and neighbours who actually use Railway House (the old North Carlton station at 20 Solly Avenue) for gardening or scrabble. We’re charming, a bit fussy, sometimes contentious — and yes, we have an Elvis shrine in a small garden, because if you’re going to be niche, be spectacular about it.
