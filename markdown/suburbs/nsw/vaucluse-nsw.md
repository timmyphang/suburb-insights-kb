# Vaucluse (NSW)

Slug: `vaucluse-nsw`

We’re the lot who take our morning cliff‑side airs seriously: a slow walk along the Hermitage Foreshore Track, pretending the city is a prop and the Macquarie Lighthouse is ours to pose beside. Back in the day — and yes, our reporting kit here is mostly older pieces — Urban List and Time Out have been telling the same story: Vaucluse is all harbour views, heritage and tiny, smug beaches.

Our weekend ritual used to look like this: a flâneur’s amble past Vaucluse House (the country’s oldest house museum, as Time Out reminds us), down to Milk Beach or Parsley Bay for a quieter skyline selfie, then a detour via Nielsen Park and Shark Beach to check the water. Broadsheet once rounded up cafés along Old South Head Road and New South Head Road — remember The Trail, Il Dolce Dell’Amore and The Grumpy Baker? We do. Sol Spa was written up beneath The Botanica; Concrete Playground and Sitchu gushed about The Botanica’s millennial‑pink chairs and farm‑to‑table pretensions (all in the archives, but delicious for nostalgia).

Petty local drama? We’ve had our share of headlines — Pedestrian noted shark incidents near Nielsen Park’s Shark Beach, and Broadsheet flagged the century‑old ocean outfalls still near Vaucluse when the state moved to shut them down. Not exactly afternoon tea gossip, but it keeps conversations interesting at Kimberley Reserve playgroup and the Mark Moran intergenerational sessions.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe heritage estates, quiet harbour coves like Kutti Beach, or families queuing for picnic lawns at Christison Park, this is not your postcode. If secrecy, small beaches and the occasional high‑society spa vibe make you itch, move along.

Who would love it? Families who’ve relied on the Kimberley Reserve playgroups and the Vaucluse & Diamond Bay Neighbourhood Fair at Diamond Bay Bowling Club; walkers and boaties who value the Hermitage tracks, the Vaucluse Amateur 12‑ft Sailing Club and secret sand refuges; history nuts who adore Vaucluse House and the Macquarie Lightstation. We’ll keep bragging about our patch in the fond, slightly smug way the old guides did — because when your skyline doubles as a postcard, someone’s got to enjoy it.
