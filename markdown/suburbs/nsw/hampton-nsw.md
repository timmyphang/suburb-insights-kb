# Hampton (NSW)

Slug: `hampton-nsw`

If you stand on Hampton Street at the crack of “we’ll get there before brunch” you can taste the suburb’s personality: part bayside calm, part unapologetic café crawl. We used to wobble down for The Hamptons bakery pastries — not the dawn‑raid kind, mind you, but the ones that magically reappeared in the afternoon as if the ovens had their own second shift (as Broadsheet once observed). 

Weekends were a circuit: a slow browse through the Vinnies on 505 Hampton Street for mismatched crockery and glorious mid‑century chairs (Urban List tipped it as a must), then a courtyard brunch near the station where Sebastian's used to shine in summer (Concrete Playground gave that courtyard a nod). If we were feeling fancy, we’d remember the walls at Hampton Wine Co., where Broadsheet said you were encouraged to peruse the shelves like you owned a sommelier’s day off.

Our strip has flirted with fancier ambitions. Broadsheet and Concrete Playground catalogued places like Kalye Marinas — the Filipino seafood boil spot where you ate with your hands — and Southall, the family‑run Indian that brought 48‑hour tandoor kaali daal to town. Bang Bang even showed up from Mordialloc with bao buns and happy‑hour promises. These stories read like the suburb’s wardrobe changes: beach towel to dinner jacket, sometimes in the same afternoon.

Petty drama? We had it in bite‑sized, market‑research form: Dan Murphy’s once rolled into Hampton with a temporary booze‑free bar pouring more than 30 non‑alcoholic drinks and selling over 200 zero‑alcohol options — Concrete Playground covered the pop‑up like it was a social experiment in our local shopping centre. It felt exactly like Hampton: earnest, a touch experimental, and a little bit pleased with itself.

Who would hate living here? People who loathe secondhand shops, courtyard brunches and wine racks that encourage existential browsing. Also anyone allergic to beaches, because Sitchu has forever described us as that very bayside, family‑friendly kind of place.

Who’d love it? Families, bargain hunters, and anyone who enjoys a town that can be both sleepy and suddenly very into smoked‑salmon profiteroles. Back in the day, that mix made Hampton charmingly indecisive — and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
