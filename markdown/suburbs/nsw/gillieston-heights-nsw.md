# Gillieston Heights (NSW)

Slug: `gillieston-heights-nsw`

We know the queue for the shaded picnic tables at Roy Jordan Oval like it’s a local ritual — except it isn’t a ritual, it’s just what happens when everyone remembers there’s a water play area and a playground with actual shade. Back in the day Newywithkids wrote up the Oval’s upgrade — shaded seating, toilets, and a “million‑dollar” skate park (they reckon it opened in January 2025) — so we’ve got the kind of weekend chaos that makes other suburbs call their lawyers.

Midweek we used to do the Wednesday shuffle: drop the smalls at the Gillieston Heights playgroup at the Community Hub (Redwood Drive & Pines Street), as Playgroup NSW listed — 10:00 AM, every Wednesday, contact was playgroupgilliestonheights@gmail.com if you were checking the details. As Playgroup NSW and the Newcastle guides noted, the Community Hub used to be the place for toddlers, craft disasters and someone’s earnest attempt at a fruit platter.

Victor Warby Reserve gets a shout-out whenever someone wants a quieter fix: an interesting climbing structure, a roundabout, a group swing, and even a half basketball/netball court. Newywithkids helpfully reminds us there’s a water fountain and off‑street parking — but no public toilets, so you learn to time your coffee runs like it’s a military operation.

There’s no juicy council feud in our clippings, no scandalous café feud to feed the tabloids — just parks, playgroups and an allegedly very good skateboard bowl. If you were hoping for a suburb with late-night sushi and existential neon, you’d hate Gillieston Heights. If you need nightlife, think again; if you crave anonymous CBD convenience, move along.

But who would love it here? Families who treat playground upgrades as a cultural event, people who measure suburbs in shaded swing sets rather than rooftop bars, and anyone with a kid who can name every feature of a mogul and a keyhole transition (Newywithkids will tell you what those are). We’ll happily keep trading city pretence for neatly maintained reserves, a community hub that once hosted a reliably cheerful Wednesday playgroup, and a skate park that got everyone very excited in the write-ups.
