# Malabar (NSW)

Slug: `malabar-nsw`

We know Malabar like the back of our sun-burnt hands — that sleepy stretch where the headland does all the drama and we do the rowing, walking and playground duty. Picture this: we’re up at dawn, lugging prams or kayaks, sneaking past the timber staircase Broadsheet once called the access to Little Bay — that almost-secret cove tucked between Malabar and the La Perouse headland — because even our seclusion has a queue.

Weekends here used to be predictably wholesome. Time Out, Sitchu and Concrete Playground all fell in love with the Malabar Headland walks — the Western Escarpment and Boora Point loops give us cliffside views and seasonal whale sightings if we’re lucky. Concrete Playground even reminded everyone that our modest Malabar Ocean Pool has history: born in the 1890s, mothballed by the 1970s, then cleaned up and revamped in 1997 — the sort of comeback story we enjoy bragging about at BBQs.

Yes, we’ve had annoyances. Beachwatch cautions and a “Poor” rating for faecal bacteria made Pedestrian TV’s headlines once, and Broadsheet and others noted the time beaches were closed after that fatal shark attack — we won’t make light of that. Boora Point gets closed on some weekends and parts of the headland aren’t pram- or wheelchair-friendly, as Eastern Suburbs Mums helpfully warned, so pack your sense of adventure (and a spare set of knees).

Local governance had its moments too: council endorsed Cromwell Park North as the preferred site for Randwick’s first bike park back in April 2024 — so expect mountain bike trails and an asphalt pump track to complicate our calm if they actually arrive.

Who would hate living here? People who need 24/7 nightlife, or those who want perfectly pram-friendly cliff-top promenades, or anyone allergic to community groups — Malabar Mums on the Move and Kooloora Playgroup (at 167 Bilga Crescent) run the place like it’s their living room.

Who would love it? Families who prize parks and secret coves, hikers who know the headland’s circuits, kayakers who relish the narrow paddle-friendly channel Urban List praised, and anyone who prefers a suburb that whispers “coastal” rather than screams “trend.” We’re small, a bit stubborn, and quietly brilliant — and we like it that way.
