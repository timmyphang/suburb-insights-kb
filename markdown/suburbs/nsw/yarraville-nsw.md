# Yarraville (NSW)

Slug: `yarraville-nsw`

We know the lunchtime shuffle here — stroller, tote bag, and the tactical decision: Rocco’s Delicatessen or the farmers market? Back in the day we’d wander through Yarraville Village, park a rug in Yarraville Gardens and time our coffee break for a Sun Theatre matinee. Time Out once crowed we were the fifth coolest neighbourhood in the world (2020) and, well, that ego still pays the council rates.

Weekend routine: the fourth Saturday meant Yarraville Farmers Market, a scope around Anderson Street to The Village Store for something only vaguely paleo, then ducking into the Sun Theatre. If we were feeling fancy we’d remember Tzaki on Ballarat Street — the tiny Athenian wine bar — and, later, the chatter about Kokoras being the Tzaki team’s chicken shop (it was listed as opening in August 2025 in Broadsheet). Hotel Mexico’s rooftop at 238 Whitehall Street used to promise margarita-fuelled chaos for groups; Concrete Playground wrote that one up with the sort of enthusiasm that makes us defensive and smug in equal measure.

Petty local drama? Oh, we have layers. There was the famous parking-meter blow-up where councillors were allegedly "king-hit" at a meeting about meters (Junkee), Woolworths trialled a cashless policy at the local store (yep, that was a thing), and then there was the Pam The Bird graffiti saga — a Yarraville name that made national pages for all the wrong reasons. We’ll loudly discuss all of it over Rocco’s monthly meatball day — yes, the deli that’s been feeding us since 1977 and famously sells out its meatball special.

Who would hate living here? If you despise heritage theatres, a slow village pace, delis that start queues, or people who judge you for missing farmers market Saturdays, this isn’t your postcode. If the idea of being in a suburb celebrated in guides — Broadsheet, Time Out, Urban List, Sitchu — gives you hives, move along.

Who’d love it? Young families, people who treasure craftsman backstreets, anyone who enjoys independent shops like Cornershop, Coe & Coe or a kid-friendly stop like Pampered Mummies, and anyone who secretly enjoys arguing about whether we’re still "cool." We’ll save you a meatball.
