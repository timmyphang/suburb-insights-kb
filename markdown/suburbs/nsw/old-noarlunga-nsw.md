# Old Noarlunga (NSW)

Slug: `old-noarlunga-nsw`

We start the morning standing outside 23 Malpas St pretending we’re late for school pickup but really just scouting the footpath for gossip. Back in the day the Old Noarlunga Primary School (the listing’s still there on SAcommunity) ran the usual before- and after-school circus — you can almost hear the OSHC roll call in the breeze that blows off the Onkaparinga.

We shuffle from the school to the hall the Old Noarlunga Community Residents Association hires out when someone’s finally brave enough to throw a fundraiser. ONCRA, as the listing calls it, is the sort of community outfit that keeps this place tidy, nags the council and organises the kind of events you tell your city friends about and then quietly admit were surprisingly wholesome.

If you’re the sort of person who collects local history like it’s a hobby, the Uniting Church entry on SAcommunity teases a ladies craft group and a historic cemetery down Seaford Rd — an excellent place to practice your “I’m from the country” voice at weekend barbecues. We say “teases” because most of our coverage is nostalgic: Broadsheet and Time Out have us in their travel pages and guides, but those pieces read like postcards from a slightly more glamorous era.

As Broadsheet fondly remembered, Cherry Burger (the throwback American diner with thick shakes) was a thing once — located over at Noarlunga Centre but now permanently closed — and nearby Port Noarlunga still gets name-checked for the converted horse-barn-turned-wine-bar (Noori) with its pintxos and oysters. Time Out even wrote up the Witton Bluff Base Trail linking Christies Beach and Port Noarlunga, which is handy for the "we like walks + views" crowd.

Who would hate living here? People who need a packed calendar of newfangled cafés and headline-grabbing openings every month. We don’t have that churn — we have a hall, a school at 23 Malpas St, a church on Seaford Rd and a river nearby that insists you slow down.

Who’d love it here? Families who like reliable community groups, hikers making a weekend of the Onkaparinga and McLaren Vale, and anyone who prefers character over clout. We’ll keep the hall booked and the craft group humming — you bring the marmalade sandwiches.
