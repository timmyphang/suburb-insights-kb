# Potts Point (NSW)

Slug: `potts-point-nsw`

Back in the day — and in every guide we keep bookmarking when we need reminding we live somewhere slightly ridiculous — Potts Point has always been the pint-sized precinct that thinks it’s Paris but keeps a spare pair of thongs under the sofa “just in case.”

We know Macleay Street like the back of our hand: on a Saturday it used to be Sydney Streets, complete with pop-up bars, live music and kids running between boutiques and stalls. Concrete Playground used to send people here for a weekend full of boutique hotels like Hotel Indigo Sydney Potts Point, a coffee at Piña or Room Ten, and a slow meander past Potts Point Bookshop and Macleay on Manning. Broadsheet and Time Out have both chronicled our meals and manners — Fratelli Paradiso, Cho Cho San, Yellow, Franca — names you can’t say without sounding faintly cultured on purpose.

There’s been proper civic theatre too. Junkee archived an Omnia strata letter that blamed lockout laws for changing our character, and Pedestrian TV noted the local heritage types lobbying to keep neon signage on the old Cross-era buildings — yes, we can clutch our pearls and our neon at the same time. Even our gelato made headlines: Good Times Artisan Ice Cream had signage that made people blink and laugh. The Wayside Chapel op shop still gets a nod in thrift roundups, if you like treasure-hunting with a conscience.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who needs a big backyard, absolute silence, or a supermarket that isn’t artisanal will find Potts Point trying their patience. Night owls who want the old, unmitigated Kings Cross may grumble — Junkee’s reports on lockouts have that covered. If your idea of a weekend is “mysterious open-plan quiet,” this is not your postcard.

Who would love it? Lovers of compact, culturally rich streets; people who enjoy eating their way down a list that Broadsheet, Time Out and Urban List keep updating; book browsers, boutique-hotel staycationers, and anyone who appreciates that we can be European in mood and unapologetically Sydney in delivery. We’re small, loud in the best ways, and stubbornly stylish — and we wouldn't have it any other way.
