# Prahran (NSW)

Slug: `prahran-nsw`

We know the choreography here: a Saturday that begins under the rafters of Prahran Market and somehow ends on Chapel Street pretending it isn’t exactly what it is. Back in the day — and in the pages we've been handed — Prahran was always the place with both a proper market and an attitude.

Our weekend routine used to be simple. We’d duck into Prahran Market for the bargains (Time Out and Concrete Playground reminded us it’s Australia’s longest continuously running market, and yes, Swords Select Wines gets a shout), wander Greville Street for the craftier finds (Greville Records, Blackhearts & Sparrows, the Chapel Street Bazaar — Concrete Playground and Time Out have the receipts), then collapse onto the lawns of Prahran Square, which opened on December 1 as a $60 million flex of terraces, fountains and public art on Cato Street (and yes, trams 78 or 72 do the dutiful thing).

Local change is part of our personality. The Smith on High Street packed up for a glow-up and a rebrand as the High St Hotel at 213 High Street, Urban List told us, while Broadsheet has been chronicling new arrivals — Aftermath (a diner from the Whiplash team and three AFL players) and, later on, Prahran Market’s reinvention with four casual restaurants including Pacho Taqueria and a permanent yakitori operator. It’s the kind of turnover that gives locals something to tut about and tourists something to Instagram.

Who would hate it here? Someone who wants suburbia to stay exactly frozen like a vintage poster. If you can’t handle pop-ups, renovations, and people who take boutique shopping seriously, you’ll feel squeezed between the past and a freshly painted facade.

Who’d love it? Market lovers, shoppers who enjoy the Chapel/Greville hop, and anyone who sees public squares and thinks “that’s ours” — Prahran has the little contradictions: French bistro vibes at L’Hotel Gitan on Commercial Road, handmade pasta ambitions at Osteria Renata on High Street, and the comforting predictability of the Prahran Hotel at 82 High St. We’ll keep watching the shopfronts change, pretending we aren’t thrilled.
