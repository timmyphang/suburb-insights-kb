# Seaview Downs (NSW)

Slug: `seaview-downs-nsw`

We live here, which means we can tell you that Seaview Downs is where suburbia and a succulent obsession hold hands and go for a gentle stroll. Mornings for us used to mean a drive past 26–44 Ross St to drop off a kid at Seaview Downs Primary — yes, the school that offered OSHC services for before/after school care and vacation care — and a mental list of who still owes permission slips. The kindergarten at 5a Hume St ran on a reliable routine (Mon–Thu 8:30–3:00, Fri 8:30–12:30), so your toddler was on time for circle songs whether you were or not.

Back in the day, the neighbourhood’s true claim to fame was Stubbytubby — as Broadsheet cheekily noted, Angela McGoran’s backyard pop-up of hundreds of potted cacti and succulents. It was primarily an online specialist but traded for a few hours on weekends: you’d “ring the bell” and peer at a tiny desert army. If you thought that sounded niche, fair — but it was charmingly specific, and Broadsheet updated the listing on 28 July 2020, so it’s part of our folklore now.

We also had Eden Academy Seaview Downs, the architecturally designed nursery that promoted social development and even offered incursions like sport and foreign language lessons — the sort of place that made playgroup feel like a soft-shelled MBA. And for community anchoring, All Nations Baptist Church at 168–170 Seacombe Rd used to host Sunday worship at 10am and run an op shop on Thursdays, because someone has to keep the vintage crockery economy alive.

Petty local drama? The data’s light on feuds, so we’ll keep it honest: our drama is the quiet kind — mismatched gardening tastes, PTA spreadsheet wars, and whether the succulents have really taken over the suburb. No fights on Facebook, just tasteful pruning.

Who would hate living here? If you crave a buzzy café strip or late-night excitement, this isn’t your postcode. Who would love it? Families who want reliable schools, people who think a backyard cactus emporium is peak culture, and anyone who prefers a neat, low-key suburban rhythm with a Thursday op shop to spice a weekend. We’ll keep watering the succulents and the kindy’s routine — and you can ring the bell if you’re curious.
