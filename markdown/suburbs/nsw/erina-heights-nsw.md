# Erina Heights (NSW)

Slug: `erina-heights-nsw`

We remember Erina Heights like a weekend mixtape on repeat: dew on the valley, a stubborn fog that refuses to leave, and a main drag made up of The Entrance Road and a handful of businesses that insist on being both charming and slightly mysterious. Back in the day, Kew Dining was our paddock‑to‑plate fantasy — as Concrete Playground put it, high tea ran from 11am Tuesday–Saturday and you could look over the green valley and pretend you were in a postcard. We used to treat that like a civic ritual.

Our Saturday loop used to be gloriously mundane: swing past Ritchies on 375 The Entrance Road for whatever counted as a supermarket win that week, detour to Burbank House & Garden on 443 The Entrance Road to sigh over faux copper pots and sign up for Plant & Pot Bingo, and then wander the findglocal directory entries that made us feel like we lived somewhere with a scene — Carne & Cucina (1/488 The Entrance Road) with its gourmet butchery and handmade pasta, Burbank Leafy Green Cafe, BamVino, Sara’s Cake Studio and even Photo Biz Xposed. It read like a menu of hobbies.

We had our gin pilgrimage, too. Distillery Botanica — the garden distillery Concrete Playground loved — was the place where garden‑grown gin and tasting experiences made us feel worldly, and the write‑ups about outdoor tents and free gin tastings gave our modest suburb an air of festival‑adjacent glamour. The Woodport Hotel, with its multi‑level setup overlooking Erina Creek and a recent renovation (again, Concrete Playground), used to promise live music and a night out that reliably ended with someone suggesting karaoke.

Who would hate living here? If you need non‑stop excitement, five cafes by midday, or the illusion that every week must bring a major cultural event, Erina Heights will feel like slow TV. But who would love it? People who want a green valley, a few reliably local haunts, and weekend rituals that involve plants, pasta and gin tastings. For us, that low‑key pride never went out of style — even when the listings on findglocal felt like the best kind of humble brag.
