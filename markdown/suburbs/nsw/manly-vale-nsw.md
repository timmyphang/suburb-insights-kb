# Manly Vale (NSW)

Slug: `manly-vale-nsw`

Saturday mornings here used to look like a low-key mash-up of industrial chic and daycare drop-off: we’d park near Condamine Street, wrestle a scooter onto the footpath and head for whatever community thing was on—playgroup at the Manly Vale Community Centre on Innes Road or the King Street Cafe playtime at Christ Church Northern Beaches on King Street. For years the local write-ups have been a bit stubbornly nostalgic, so we’ll lean into that: back in the day the guides loved to point out our odd little contrasts.

As Broadsheet cheekily noted, Belaroma Coffee Centre sits in the semi-industrial (and, yes, cafe-barren) end of town — ideal if you like your roastery with a side of forklifts. Quakers Hat Brewing, which opened a rustic taproom in 2021 in an industrial park, was the sort of place you bragged about once you’d found it. Seven Miles Coffee Roasters and Mike Pawley Sports have been name-checked in local listings, and the kindergarten on Condamine (272 Condamine St) has been quietly doing the parenting heavy lifting for decades.

We are, objectively, very good at organised community things: World Food Markets at Millers Reserve, night walks at Manly Dam (meet at the end of King Street — and don’t try to park inside the gates after dark), and an underdog football club at David Thomas Reserve that asks nothing except your energy and maybe a new pair of boots. Manly Dam itself — the Manly Warringah War Memorial State Park on King Street — is where we picnic, mountain-bike, water-ski and let the kids convince us we are outdoorsy. Anglers would tell you it used to be great for Aussie Bass, Silver Perch, Carp and Redfin.

Who would hate living here? If you want nonstop boutique shopping and a boulevard of waterfront restaurants handed to you on a platter, this isn’t your postcard — we’re more reserve-and-roastery than neon-and-aperol. But who would love it? Families who actually like knowing their neighbours, people who value lanes that lead to reserves not just another latte, weekend anglers and anyone who treasures a community market on a chilly Northern Beaches evening.

Full disclosure: most of the local press on us is a few years old, so come see for yourself — or at least come for the night walk and stay for the dubious coffee in the industrial precinct.
