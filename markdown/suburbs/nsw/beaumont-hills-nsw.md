# Beaumont Hills (NSW)

Slug: `beaumont-hills-nsw`

We know the scene: a stroller convoy at 10:00 AM, a scattering of helmets by the pump track, and someone explaining for the hundredth time where the path down to Windsor Road goes. Back in the day the suburb’s headlines read like a parents’ group newsletter — and honestly, that’s us and we’re fine with it.

Our morning ritual used to be drop-offs at the Beaumont Hills Community Centre — the Tadpoles on Fridays at 10:00 AM and the Bears on Tuesdays at 10:00 AM, if you wanted to plan your life around snack time, as Playgroup NSW once listed. Then we’d head to Beaumont Hills Park: colourful, built for action with a pump track, courts and a skating area — the official opening was a proper thing on Friday 9 February 2024 (Parliamentary Secretary Stephen Bali MP did the honours), which Hills District Mums covered like it was the Olympics of small wheels.

We’re proud of our green bits — Guardian Avenue Reserve (aka Smalls Creek Park) got a makeover with natural landscaping and animal sculptures, and Ironbark Ridge Reserve (directions: Lusitano Street, in case you’re navigating toddlers) still reads like a review written by someone who takes playgrounds very, very seriously.

Our culinary bragging rights? For a flash we had a Burger Head pop-up at 7/70 The Parkway back in 2017 — yes, the buttermilk fried chicken burger and the cheeky Twisties-collab “Mike Tyson” veg option were real, Broadsheet reported. Time Out even name-checked a Vasco da Gama-style rotisserie with a Beaumont Hills location in a roundup once. So we flirt with fame, then go home to our pump track.

Petty grievances? We don’t have late-night bars to be offended by. We do have tolls (and yes, Pedestrian TV covered that Service NSW toll rebate relief scheme — the sort of thing you cheer for at the school gate).

Who would hate living here: anyone who needs nightclub-level excitement or believes a suburb is only real if it has a million food articles. Who would love it: families who treat a 10:00 AM playgroup like Sunday mass, kids who find heroism in a bird’s-nest swing, and anyone who prefers painted seating over pavement pretension. We’re Beaumont Hills — small wheels, big heart, proud of our mundane.
