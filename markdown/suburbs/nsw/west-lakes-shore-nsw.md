# West Lakes Shore (NSW)

Slug: `west-lakes-shore-nsw`

We’re the people who time our walks by the lake like it’s the Series Final — two hours before high tide, because yes, we read the tide table. Back in the day the tide charts (you’ll find the West Lakes Shore tide times on sites like tideschart) were our low-key flex: know the solunar and you own the morning.

Mornings here used to begin with a lakeside circuit and a pretend café choice. As Broadsheet noted, Oar Lakeside — the younger sibling to 303 by the Sea in Henley Beach — was the spot at 100 Military Road with the view, outdoor area and the kind of kid‑friendly options that let the parents nod and relax. Honestly, it felt like a resort for people who’d filed their passports away.

We’re the suburb that wears its 1970s planning like a medallion: Wikipedia reminds you we were developed in the 1970s as a public–private partnership and named for the manmade tidal lake that gives the whole place its reason to exist. That lake is our living room; Sitchu’s profile really nails it — waterways, golf courses and a shopping precinct make up our domestic skyline. Holidaymakers and property lookers know this too: Vrbo and realestate.com.au have long listed whole-home rentals and sales in the postcode.

The local logistics read like a well-organised community noticeboard. The West Lakes Shore School on Edwin St, with its OSHC in “The Old Building,” and the kindergarten (the session times were even listed — Mon–Thurs 8:25–3:10, Fri 8:30–11:30) used to be the things families traded PTA tips about. Our post was officially a West Lakes Shore Licensed Post Office at Shop 2A/114-130 Bartley Tce, if you liked queuing with purpose.

Petty drama? The archives are disappointingly wholesome — no famous feuds, just good planning and decent playgrounds. If you need late-night neon, a 24/7 food truck scene or a skyline you can’t fog for breath, you’ll hate it here. But if you like a manmade tidal lake that flatters every sunrise, sensible schools, and the smug calm of people who can time a perfect catch because they checked the tide table — welcome home. We’ll wave from the seawall.
