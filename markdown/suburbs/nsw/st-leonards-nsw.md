# St Leonards (NSW)

Slug: `st-leonards-nsw`

We start our Saturday like everyone else here — pretending the commute is a scenic detour. Back in the day (or at least in the pieces we’ve got), St Leonards was the suburb that dressed like a north-shore lunch break and tried very hard to be effortless.

Morning ritual used to mean a croissant pilgrimage. Moon Phase — formerly Layers — relaunched with what Broadsheet gushed were “elite croissants” that take five days to make, and an in‑house coffee program meant to anchor the street. Cavalier (Cavalier 1.0 on Oxley Street, if you were keeping score) quietly earned its specialty‑coffee cult, with Marvel Street beans and that annoyingly exacting jersey‑milk combo. If you wanted ramen energy or an izakaya night, Sekka Dining from the Ichibandori team was the sort of place we boasted about over badly timed photos.

Then there was the hospitality drama we all pretended not to care about. Petermen — Josh and Julie Niland’s 60‑seat seafood project — opened in February 2023 and, as the notices said, served its last sitting on December 15, 2024. Broadsheet and Concrete Playground both framed it as the sort of closure that makes you sigh for “challenging conditions” and wonder if the next big name will stick around longer than the lunchtime queues.

Who would hate it here? If you need absolute permanence — a one-note main street with zero turnover — you’ll twitch at our renaissance‑meets‑trial‑and‑error vibe. If caviar‑and‑truffle brunches (The Mayflower’s pink, art‑filled theatrics) feel like a personality disorder, fair call.

Who would love it? Food obsessives who collect onigiri at Miso Mart, grab a Destination Roll banh mi between meetings, or trace coastal nostalgia out to St Leonards Park and the Sydney Harbour lookout. Property listings (yes, the Sitchu ones above the train station and in Clarke Lane) sell the dream of being literally in the middle of it all — the playgrounds, the parks, the breakfasts that once took five days.

We’re a patchwork: ambitious pastries, quietly serious coffee, a few headline closures and a stubborn appetite for reinvention. Come for the croissants, stay for the gossip — and don’t be surprised if next season brings another menu, another opening, and the same dramatic sigh.
