# Magill (NSW)

Slug: `magill-nsw`

Back in the day, we’d stagger out of the foothills like very polite, slightly smug tourists — smelling of vineyard dust and overachieving degustation — and declare Magill our little slice of cultivated chaos. We’re the lot who learnt the difference between a cellar door and a life choice at Penfolds’ Magill Estate, where Scott Huggins used to wrangle fire and seasonal produce into things we’d whisper about for weeks. Broadsheet called it a proving ground for top kitchen talent; we called it our excuse to dress up on a Tuesday.

Mornings used to start with the pastoral: walk past the vines (you could spy apprentice chefs harvesting from the garden patch at Magill Estate Kitchen), then wobble down Magill Road to Studio Spring for pastel Pilates (yes, it’s sunlit and very, very Instagrammable). If we were feeling less virtuous, we’d detour to Prove Patisserie on Magill Road in Stepney for something flaky and morally ambiguous — The Urban List had it pencilled in for our sin list, too. On particularly decadent evenings we’d remember Ed Sheeran once popped by — Sitchu mentioned that — and nod like that explained everything.

There’s no petty local feud to report because frankly Magill’s drama has always been culinary: tiny scallop hot dogs that haunt you (Broadsheet was obsessed), potato ice-cream that makes you reassess your life choices, and Magill Estate Restaurant booking advisories that read like mission statements. The Seniors Citizens Centre on St Bernards Road quietly provides another kind of performance art — knitting circles with better social calendars than us — and Magill Primary on Adelaide Street keeps the place loud, proud and full of urgent permission notes.

Who would hate it here? If you can’t stand a view that doubles as a postcard, or you think degustations are just a conspiracy to spend rent money, you’ll hate us. Also, if you prefer anywhere without vineyards, that’s fair.

Who would love it? Food obsessives, wine pilgrims, pastel-pilates converts, families who like a proper primary school and seniors who run the real nightlife — we were, and still are, very proud of that. Back in the day, Magill felt like a secret everyone worth knowing already knew.
