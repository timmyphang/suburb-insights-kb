# Myrtle Bank (NSW)

Slug: `myrtle-bank-nsw`

Back in the day we’d amble down Fullarton Road and pretend we weren’t checking the name on every letterbox for signs of drama. Myrtle Bank, as the listings used to tell us, was less about late-night cocktails and more about well-timed cuppa etiquette and precise bin-day choreography.

Mornings here were simple: we’d walk past 55 Ferguson Ave and nod at Retirement Living Myrtle Bank, wave to whoever was moving into Heritage Apartments on 18 Cross Rd, and whisper rumours about who’d swapped units for a better view. SAcommunity used to list Carmelite Residential Care at 7 Spence Ave and Ocean Grove Supportive Care (formerly St Michael’s Rest Home) at 494 Fullarton Rd — the suburb always felt tuned to a slower, kinder frequency. Ocean Grove was explicitly described as a supported residential facility serving people with mental illness, intellectual disability and brain injury, which kept our civic conscience pleasantly busy.

Parenting, meanwhile, was its own sport. CareforKids counted eight childcare services in the 5064 postcode — 4 out of 8 had vacancies, and the average cost came in at $119/day. Bertram Hawker Kindergarten at 7 Wooltana Avenue had no vacancies and a sparkling 5.0 rating, so yes, we queued and traded tips like it was hot festival merch. Glen Osmond Primary School OSHC on 5 Fisher Street was listed as meeting NQS standards and actually had spots — a fact we treated like lottery numbers.

There isn’t much petty town drama in the records; instead the real tension is practical: will you find a kinder place, or will the retirement village beat you to the good park bench? If you’re the kind of person who needs nightlife, trendy cafés, or late-night ramen runs, this data would have gently suggested you’d be bored. We wouldn’t sugarcoat it.

Who hates it: people chasing “vibes” would scowl. Who loves it: retirees, carers, and anyone who values neighbourly predictability — the sort who appreciate the tidy reassurance of a retirement village at 55 Ferguson Ave and a well-documented support service at 494 Fullarton Rd. For years the local listings have said the same thing: Myrtle Bank is steady, neighbourly, and quietly committed to looking after each other — and honestly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
