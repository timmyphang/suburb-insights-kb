# Dee Why (NSW)

Slug: `dee-why-nsw`

Back in the day we’d shuffle down The Strand before the tourists arrived and pretend we lived there purely for the soundtrack: gulls, distant surf and the faint thump of a DJ who probably came with a pizza order. Broadsheet even said the cafés were “so close to the water you can virtually swim to it” — and we took that as a mission statement.

Our morning routine used to be reliably indecisive: Girdlers for buckwheat pancakes when we were trying to be mindful, Brew & I when we were not. Ullo Pizza & Wine used to lure us with ocean views and late-night DJs — an ex‑Rockpool chef, snappy menu and neon-lit energy that made us feel slightly sophisticated and mainly hungry. Bar Corretto used to promise a Mid‑Morning Negroni and outdoor seats that faced the horizon, like a seaside office for people with better cocktails than emails.

If you wanted a pub with ambition, the Dee Why Hotel reopened after a $3‑million makeover and we watched in mild awe as the old third‑generation boozer turned retro‑glam — ten‑metre TV wall, Mr B’s sports bar, Jade’s bistro — the kind of place that can host both a Trufflenator burger and a serious game of footy-watching ritual. The RSL even pulled off the Wiggles adults‑only fundraiser one year, which only Dee Why could make simultaneously wholesome and wildly fun for a packed crowd raising cash for Soldier On Australia.

Petty local drama? Our most scandalous night was which venue had the best wings — Battery House boasted spicy wings and Espresso Martinis on tap (historic tram‑charging station chic), Wings And Tins flexed as the dive with great tunes and too many tinnies, and we’d argue until the tide took our resolve.

Who would hate living here: anyone who needs absolute silence, zero sand in their shoes or a postcode without a pub crisis at 9pm. Who would love it: families who used the Dee Why Library’s kids’ programs, market people who’d flock to James Meehan Reserve for the Northern Beaches Market, surfers and anyone who thinks a sea breeze is an acceptable substitute for therapy.

We’ve always been a little brash, a little beachfront and very Dee Why — proud of the strip, nostalgic for the nights and always ready to defend which pizza place used to have the best view.
