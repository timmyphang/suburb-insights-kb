# Surrey Hills (NSW)

Slug: `surrey-hills-nsw`

We know the moment: the sun’s up and Crown Street smells like someone just opened a kitchen for the whole city. We amble past the places Broadsheet once listed in big, proud type — Tera Bar, Ama, Toei — and remember how Surry Hills used to feel like a tiny village wedged between Central and Paddington, only louder and with better napkins.

Our weekend routine used to start at Reuben Hills or Artificer Specialty Coffee for something to steady the hands, then drift toward the Surry Hills Markets because Time Out has been reminding everyone since 1981 that you can score something weird and useful there. If the light rail was running, we’d use the new route as an excuse to wander — Broadsheet even made a guide out of it — and end up wherever the night decided we’d deserve to be: a Belvoir St Theatre show (that tomato‑sauce‑factory stage has been doing its holy work since 1985), then maybe a last stand at The Dolphin Hotel.

We admit we lap up lists. Urban List and Time Out keep filing new restaurant hit lists — Pellegrino 2000 even gets a curious shoutout (yes, Urban List mentions Taylor Swift’s table of choice) — and 40Res earns its place as the kind of wine bar that makes neighbours feel smug. Concrete Playground did its thing too, pointing out Surry Hills Village on Cleveland Street like it was the next inevitable lifestyle crucifixion: a hotel, retail, the usual optimism.

Who would hate living here? Honestly, anyone who likes peace, predictable opening hours and one decent dinner option within walking distance. If you can’t tolerate being within shouting distance of a dozen “best of” lists, curated cocktail dens and pubs that Urban List insists are “timeless,” this place will test you.

Who would love it? Food obsessives, theatre devotees, market scavengers and people who think a square kilometre of drinking destinations is a public service. We’re a messy, brilliant little patch of Sydney — famous for our restaurants, proud of our theatre and always ready to argue about which corner café is “classic.” Back in the day it was a village; these days it’s a village with better press coverage. We like it that way.
