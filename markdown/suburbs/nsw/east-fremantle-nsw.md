# East Fremantle (NSW)

Slug: `east-fremantle-nsw`

Back in the day we’d swear East Fremantle was where riverside charm and civic planning had a polite disagreement and decided to meet for drinks. Picture us on George Street — coffee (or whatever we were pretending was a coffee) in hand — watching tourists gape at the pretty terraces while we pretend they’ve always looked like that. As Broadsheet cheerfully put it, La Lune was our answer to Paris and Manhattan; we remember the terrines and tartines, even if that feels very much like last chapter material now.

Weekend routine: a slow amble down to 15 Riverside Road for the Left Bank’s sweeping Swan views (Concrete Playground never lied about that beer garden), maybe a detour past 133 George Street where Eugene’s Pizzeria used to be touted as a local classic (Time Out keeps the receipts). Families would drop off the toddlers at Sumpton Green Community Centre for East Freo Playgroup — yes, the one run with the earnest efficiency of a not-for-profit — while the rest of us debated whether rooftop yum cha at Sweetwater was decadent or just good value (Urban List said $60pp and who are we to argue with Urban List?).

Local drama? Mild, tasteful, and very Fremantle. Miles Hull’s waterfront small bar was planned for East Street Jetty — Broadsheet reported it was due to open in May — and for a while that was the major speculative conversation at every local bakery. Meanwhile, Broadsheet’s entry quietly tells us Young George at 48 George Street is listed as "Permanently Closed." Cook & Mason get the same fate in the archives. Change comes slowly here, and then it’s a polite email notice.

Who would hate living here? If you like anonymous suburbs with chain stores and zero riverside personality, you’ll find our George Street quirks unbearable. If you need everything to be brand-new and permanently open, the tasteful closures might trigger discomfort.

Who would love it? People who adore weekend river views, long lunches, and a neighbourhood that still remembers its pubs (Left Bank), Italian pizza (Eugene’s), and the tiny triumph of a refurbished Royal George turned wellness precinct with saunas and ice pools (Alchemy Saunas did a thing there). We’re small, a bit theatrical about our history, and quietly, defiantly charming.
