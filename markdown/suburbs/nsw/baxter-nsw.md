# Baxter (NSW)

Slug: `baxter-nsw`

We live in Baxter, NSW — the Baxter that answers to neither The Baxter Inn nor Captain Baxter, and honestly, we’re fine with that. While Broadsheet once told Sydney about a basement called The Baxter Inn with more than 800 whiskies and swinging ’50s soundtracks, and Concrete Playground had Captain Baxter lording over St Kilda Sea Baths with bay views, we’re our own low-key thing. As Broadsheet noted, that Inn was the original speakeasy — not ours. Carry on.

Our Monday morning routine is mostly us checking which other Baxter got the headlines this week. Pete Baxter’s work with Hope St Radio that turned into a luxe wine bar crops up in conversations because, well, name recognition is a free upgrade. Sitchu reminds us Baxter also exists on a bottle: Baxter Vodka picked up international awards, so if anyone asks where we keep our class, we point to the pantry (metaphorically).

Back in the day, Baxter’s name stirred other, murkier waves: Philip Baxter College at UNSW was the subject of protests after a sexist video went viral — a Junkee story that still makes us mutter “too much, not enough” when alumni reunions are mentioned. We don’t relish the scandal, but we don’t shy from it either; it’s part of the messy patchwork that made the name so ubiquitous.

Who would hate living here? If you need rooftop margaritas with Port Phillip views or a custom Hot Wax sound system for your rigatoni nights, head to the other Baxters — Broadsheet and Sitchu have you covered elsewhere. If your life’s mission is collecting rare whiskies in a subterranean lair, likewise.

Who would love it here? People who appreciate being the Baxter that doesn’t try too hard. We’re the proud middle sibling of a name that has been slapped on wine bars, vodka labels, seaside restaurants and student colleges. Quiet. Slightly bemused. Resilient. If you like your suburb with a wink of fame elsewhere and none of the queue for the speakeasy, come for a visit — but don’t expect 800 whiskies. We wouldn’t know what to do with them.
