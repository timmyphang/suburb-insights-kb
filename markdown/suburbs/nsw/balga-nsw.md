# Balga (NSW)

Slug: `balga-nsw`

We live in Balga and yes, we know exactly where the playground drama migrates on a Sunday — from the amphitheatre (where the Balga Community Band used to do Christmas carols) to the doors of the Teen Family Centre on the school grounds. Back in the day the stories we have are more about nappies and community programs than headline-grabbing scandals, so indulge our nostalgia: our files mostly date from before anyone thought to write a hot take on our postcode.

Morning routine: drop-offs used to mean a quick loop past Balga Senior High School at 2 Markham Way — that’s where the Teen Family Centre ran a Young Parent Program so young parents could do school while their children were cared for. The handbook even spelled out program days (Thursdays, if you wanted the play-by-play) and the centre offered a proper hand-holding approach so folks could finish their WACE. Later we’d swing by Balga Salvo Early Years Services on Lavant Way for the Sensory Kids Playgroup — the sort of thing that leaves sticky hands and calmer parents, and apparently happened on 24 May 2024 in the notices.

We’re unglamorous and proud of it. Our cultural brag? Concrete Playground once flagged Christopher Pease’s Balga resin (2008) — yes, the one made from the balga grasstree resin that changes in the light — and listed a piece called “Bali Bali Balga” at Carriageworks. Fancy galleries might riff about texture and ceremony; we just nod and say, “Good on ’em.”

Petty local drama? Not much in the files. If you’re after noisy council feuds or boutique-restaurant brinkmanship, we’re going to disappoint — our paper trail prefers playgroups, a community band, and programs that keep people learning and parenting.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to kids, community services on school grounds, or the occasional brass band in the park. Who would love it: young parents getting a second shot at school, volunteers who like a choir of trumpets in December, and people who measure suburb cred by how well it looks after its own.

Yes, we roast ourselves — but only because we know our suburb actually does the hard, quiet work.
