# Ashcroft (NSW)

Slug: `ashcroft-nsw`

We know Ashcroft by the way a Toyota Prado sits on the nature strip like a suburban peacock, stickers of U10s footy and a dent from that one "camping trip" that never happened. We queue at the Woolworths in-store café pretending to be woke about single-origin; we actually want a skinny flat white, single shot, extra-hot because the Lorna Jane mums from Ashcroft Avenue need fuel before drop-off. We roll up to Bunnings in a Prado that never sees a campsite and another bloke in a Mazda CX-5 who swears he's "popping in for mulch" and comes out with a hose, two plants and an ego. We wear Athleta or Lorna Jane because elastic is a lifestyle. We walk Bruce, our blue Kelpie-cross, past the bakery at Ashcroft Plaza where the shady spot becomes a weekly battleground between soccer parents and tradies. We know every bonnet dent in the carpark. We call it community.

Saturday morning is a choreography of competence: 7am parkrun or footy drop-off, the U10s warm-up where we clap like theatre critics, then the Bunnings sausage sizzle where someone always doubles up on onions. We buy "something for the backyard" and it becomes a whole backyard. We post triumphant photos of school bake stalls and schedule everything around pickup times. Sunday is mow-meditate-mend: mow the lawn, fix the fence, scroll the Ashcroft Facebook group for lost dogs and opinions on the new bin schedule. Someone uploads a spreadsheet. Someone replies with a meme. No one reads the council email and everyone is genuinely incensed.

The local soap opera is a parking war on Main Street that lasts two hours and ends in morning-group-chat diplomacy. There was a council-bin-schedule debate that uncovered more political passion than a footy final — bin day now divides the street like a half-time split. Strata fights over a stray wheelie bin or a suspiciously overfilled green-lid are our telenovela. We will defend our shady bakery spot with the ferocity of a Kelpie defending a shoe.

Who would hate living here? The boutique-minimalist urbanite with a Brompton who makes pour-overs at home and sneers at the phrase "pub roast" would wilt. They will be affronted by driveway BBQ culture, alarmed by the sausage-sizzle economy, and never find anywhere to lock their bike. Who would love it? Time-poor, utterly committed locals who drive a Mazda CX-5, coach U10s, keep Tim Tams in the pantry, and find comfort in the familiar chaos — welcome home.
