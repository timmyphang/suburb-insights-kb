# West Pymble (NSW)

Slug: `west-pymble-nsw`

Back in the day we’d call a Saturday "market day" and mean two things: a leisurely lap at Ku‑ring‑gai Fitness and Aquatic Centre on Prince of Wales Drive, then a queue for something ridiculous and delicious. As Broadsheet noted, Du Plessy Pralin & Otello once packed the footpath for its Japonaise cake — people were reportedly flying in from China to buy one. Yes, that was our tiny mall moment of global glamour.

Mornings here used to look like this: someone swims a few laps at the Ku‑ring‑gai pools, families drop off the kids at the Pymble Playgroup on Merrivale Rd (Hornsby Shire Mums has the deets), and the rest of us congregate at Brick Lane Espresso in the quiet West Pymble pedestrian mall — Urban List called it a hidden gem that served classics like smashed avo and French toast. For wine emergencies there was Tom’s Cellars, the boutique bottle shop that’s called our postcode home since 2008, and for dinner the Sawmill used to be billed as "the big Italian heart of West Pymble" with wood‑fired pizza and rum baba theatrics (Broadsheet and Urban List, take a bow).

Our version of civic theatre, meanwhile, was less Roman holiday and more legal drama. Concrete Playground ran the saga of Norman Griffiths Oval — a soccer pitch redevelopment that ballooned from a $3.3m plan into a potential $20m headache after asbestos, flooding, contract fallout and lawyering. We cheer on grassroots sport and court filings in equal measure.

Who would hate living here? If you crave 24/7 nightlife, rooftop techno, or a CBD that never clocks off, West Pymble will politely show you the way out. We’re leafy, buttoned‑up, and proud of it — Sitchu nailed the vibe: gorgeous homes, gardens, and prestige schools. Pedestrian.TV even put our median house price at about $1.9 million, which is peak "North Shore: still expensive, but not aggressively grand."

Who would love it? Families, swimmers, cake pilgrims, neighbourhood‑restaurant devotees and anyone who prefers thyme in their garden to fluorescent club lights. We won’t be for everyone — and frankly, that’s the point.
