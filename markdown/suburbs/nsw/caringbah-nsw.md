# Caringbah (NSW)

Slug: `caringbah-nsw`

We know Caringbah like the back of our sunscreened hands: the honest chaos of a Sunday at President Lane — the Caringbah Markets that used to pack 200 stalls behind McDonalds & Coles from 7am — is basically our civic ritual. Back in the day we’d stagger past the stalls clutching pastry crumbs from Flour in Caringbah South (Flour even swept Sitchu’s 2025 awards), or queue at Whimsy & Co, Kathryn Bray’s bright-blue coffee mobile stationed in the industrial car park. As Broadsheet pointed out, we’ve never pretended to be inner‑city — we’re the practical, slightly stylish suburb that quietly gets on with it.

Weekends were split between zen and noise: a contemplative wander through the EG Waterhouse National Camellia Gardens on President Ave, then a full-tilt family wrestling match at Flip Out Caringbah when the kids needed to expel their entire week’s energy. If we wanted dinner with options, we remembered the multi‑venue noise of Highfield (32 Banksia Rd) — a pizza joint, a rooftop bar called The Botanical with city views, a pub, and the sneaky sports bar Huxley’s hidden behind Ugly Pizza. UrbanList and Concrete Playground loved telling people how charmingly multi‑layered it all was.

Petty local drama? Oh, we had one: the time Shannon Noll was arrested after a sniffer dog search at the Caringbah Hotel (343 Port Hacking Rd). It made the headlines, reminded everyone that our pubs are still very much part of the community, and then we went back to our trivia nights and schnitzels.

Who’d hate living here? Anyone who thinks “quiet nightlife” means silence after 9pm, or someone who’s allergic to kids, weekend markets, or rooftop sunsets. Also, if your idea of a good night is a single‑room speakeasy sans crusty New York‑style pizza, you’ll be confused by Huxley’s being tucked behind Ugly Pizza — we like our secrets served with extra cheese.

Who’d love us? Plant people (Bonsai South Nursery has been a local shrine), bargain hunters, families who rate YMCA programs and Flip Out birthday bashes, and anyone who thinks a Portuguese chicken shop at 344 Kingsway or a sushi spot opposite the station (Aomori Sushi) is the height of civilisation. We’re unapologetically The Shire: a little sprawling, a little theatrical, and exactly where you want to be when you’re over pretending to be anywhere else.
