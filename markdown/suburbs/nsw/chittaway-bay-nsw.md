# Chittaway Bay (NSW)

Slug: `chittaway-bay-nsw`

Back in the day we’d do the morning shuffle like a ritual: coffee for us (not naming names, because the internet is older than our patience), kids booked into something — or not. The local guide pages tell the tale: MumsPages and Sydney Mums on the Central Coast paint us as a sleepy, bushland suburb that rather likes kids. Chittaway Bay Public School sits in that quiet bushland setting they rave about — park‑like grounds with a football field, tennis court, cricket nets, basketball and netball courts, a pile of fixed play equipment and a covered outdoor learning area. Classrooms had air‑con, wireless and a computer lab, so if your child ever wanted to be an academic while doing cartwheels, we were set.

For years the mums’ directories were our rolodex. MumsPages had lists for everything: sports for kids, party and event venues (because every sixth birthday requires a venue search that feels like an Olympic trial), and a whole page of local businesses and service providers. Little Dynamos' indoor play centre and gymnastics school was the long‑standing answer to “please, anything that’ll entertain them for two hours.” We used to trust it like a second grandparent.

The childcare listings tell the real story if you want the gossip: CareforKids showed 33 childcare services around us, 12 preschools, three family day cares — and an average cost that made us choke on our lukewarm caffeine: $127.65 a day. The OSHClub listing at 101 Chittaway Road was the passive‑aggressive bit of proof that demand outpaced supply — last we saw it, it was showing “No Vacancies” and an NQS rating of Meeting.

Who would hate living here? People who loathe school fetes, toddler gymnastics, and the eternal hunt for child‑care spaces. Also anyone imagining a nightlife of neon and rooftop DJs will be disappointed; we are very much in the “bushland + community” camp.

Who would love it here? Families who want park‑like school grounds, a town that math-themed childcare directories take very seriously, and anyone who thinks “quiet bushland setting” sounds like heaven. We’re proud locals — a little chaotic, highly booked and defiantly family‑friendly.
