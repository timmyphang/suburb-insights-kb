# Ryde (NSW)

Slug: `ryde-nsw`

Full disclosure: most of this is a nostalgia lap — Broadsheet, Time Out and the local mums’ pages did our homework for us, so we’re riffing off their older write-ups rather than breaking any hot new openings.

Picture a Saturday: we’d wheel past Ryde Park’s perfectly manicured gardens, the scooter track with its tiny traffic lights (kids actually know their give-way from our morning coffee ritual), and the little Sotto cafe that cropped up by the playground. From there it was a short trundle to Top Ryde City, where Ryde Library used to be a magnet for the tiny ones — six weekly kids’ reading sessions, apparently — and Top Ryde Baker’s House famously pumped out the crispy pork‑belly banh mi that made queues feel like a local sport.

Food is our favourite flex. West Ryde had Soonhi Banchan, the Korean grocer Broadsheet said Sydney chefs loved for its kimchi and kimbap. If you fancied sitting down, Urban List name‑checked Gyeong Bok Gung on Victoria Road, and Choco Cannelle brought that French patisserie vibe courtesy of Jean‑François Perron. KOI Dessert Kitchen even had its Blaxland Street outpost doing the sweet-thing in Ryde — yes, the Poernomo brothers made an appearance. And if retail therapy was your thing, Time Out noted the Macquarie Centre relaunch and the staple NeNe Chicken at Macquarie/North Ryde.

We had a market life too — Ryde Wharf Market at Anderson Park in Meadowbank was the riverside hit: stalls, produce, people-watching on second (and sometimes fourth) Sundays. The council era even flirted with a new Civic Centre proposal opposite Top Ryde City; the mums’ blog urged locals to “have your say,” because nothing says community like arguing about multipurpose spaces.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe playgrounds with bucket swings, libraries that stage storytime, and the delightful inevitability of banh mi queues, Ryde is not for you. Also, anyone allergic to family-friendly buses, playgroups at Ryde Presbyterian or Cutler Parade, or a market snoop on a Sunday should steer clear.

Who’d love it? Families, bakers, banchan obsessives, and anyone who thinks a suburban Saturday should include scooter circuits, riverside markets and enough dessert options to feel slightly smug. We’ll see you by the scooter track — queue number in hand.
