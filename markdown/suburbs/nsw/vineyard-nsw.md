# Vineyard (NSW)

Slug: `vineyard-nsw`

Vineyard, NSW — we like to think of ourselves as the unloved cousin at the vineyard family reunion. Back in the day (and for the record, there aren’t any recent articles about us), the only time our postcode turned up online it was because someone else had “vineyard” in their name. Broadsheet gushed about Levantine Hill, Oakridge and Chrismont. Time Out and Concrete Playground were off sipping chardonnay in the Yarra, Hunter and Mornington Peninsula. Pedestrian TV was somewhere near Martha’s Vineyard. Meanwhile, our inbox remained gloriously, defiantly local.

Our weekend ritual is modest: we scroll those glossy winery roundups the rest of Australia treats like gospel — Yarra Yering, Chandon, Tahbilk, T’Gallant, Goona Warra, Borrodell — and then we close the laptop and cook. Leslie Dabney of The Vineyard Mom used to be our unofficial domestic ambassador; her Braised Short Ribs in a Red Wine Sauce and Prime Rib Roast are bookmarked like heirlooms, and yes, we have tried that Sparkling Red, White and Blue Cocktail at family barbecues. It’s comforting that the internet can rank 32 best wineries and still not notice how quietly decent our suburb is.

There’s no petty council sagas or barista feud to gossip about in the snippets we found — the web prefers lists and cellar-door envy — so our drama is smaller, more stubborn: we’re the place people confuse with a tasting trail and then feel foolish for expecting vineyard views. People who want vineyard vanity and cellar-door Instagrammability will hate living here. They’ll be perpetually disappointed when the “Vineyard” in the address doesn’t come with sweeping vines and tasting paddles.

Who would love it? Anyone tired of curated wine itineraries and destination guide FOMO. If you like to cook the kind of hearty meals The Vineyard Mom recommends, to host mates who don’t need vineyard views to be happy, and to take pride in being quietly overlooked by Broadsheet and Time Out alike — you’ll fit right in. We don’t need a cellar-door to prove our worth. We’ve already got the casserole.
