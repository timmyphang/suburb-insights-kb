# Bidwill (NSW)

Slug: `bidwill-nsw`

The image we always start the day with: a silver Ford Ranger, Thule bike rack, stubby holder on the antenna, idling outside the Bidwill bakery while someone sips an “extra-hot flat white” they swear came from the BP servo and that instant coffee is only for emergencies. We wear Lorna Jane leggings like a uniform and only change into trackie pants if we’re hitting the pub. We own Prados, older Holdens and the occasional Commodore with a ute personality that precedes us down Bidwill Road.

Saturday morning is a ritual. Tyre check, servo flat white, Staffy on the lead, swing past Coles or the IGA for milk and Rosella ketchup, then a quick circuit at Anytime Fitness or a backyard HIIT where neighbours peer over fences to count burpees. By arvo the portable Weber is out of the Prado boot and someone has already brought lamingtons to the park. Kids are in footy kits by three; someone’s got the stippy blower for the half-time siren.

Petty local drama is our sport. There’s a weekly standoff in the Bidwill shopping centre carpark where someone always insists “I was just behind you” for the last spot in front of the bakery. The Facebook page once ran a two-month campaign about repainting the park BBQ shelter — half the street wanted “navy blue forever,” the other half said “leave it, it’s got character.” Then there was the council kerfuffle about whether the new bench at Bidwill Park should face the footy oval or the toilets. We solved that the way suburbs do: everyone had an opinion and the bench stayed exactly where the contractors left it.

We swap paint recommendations at the post office, keep a stubby holder in the glovebox, and know every tradie’s apprentice by name. Saturday arvo is footy at the park; Sunday is a pool drop-off and a scavenger hunt for the best kebab in the strip. We post photos of kids in uniforms, of snag-filled snags, of the Prado with a bike rack and a beer coaster on the dash.

Who would hate living here? The fixed-gear, single-origin pour-over hipster with an ultra-compact electric car and a closet full of thrifted linen shirts. They’ll protest our servo coffee, scoff at ute culture, and spend weekends searching for a boutique café that doesn’t charge for soy — and we’ll politely tell them Bidwill’s ethos is “bring your own snag and join in,” which is not a vibe they want.

Who would love it? The friendly tradie-turned-weekend-chef with a Prado in the driveway, a team cap, a Weber in the boot and a heart the size of the oval. Come for the flat whites, stay for the lamingtons. Welcome to Bidwill.
