# Dromana (NSW)

Slug: `dromana-nsw`

We’ll be honest: we’re biased. We’ve watched the Dromana Pier get preened, queued for that bakery out of a converted mechanic’s garage, and argued about whether a drive‑in is still peak nostalgia or just peak noise. Also — tiny legal note to ourselves — there aren’t any fresh 2024–26 scoops in our file, so we’re riffing on the favourites the papers kept writing about. Back in the day (and in the archives), Dromana did its thing very well.

Morning here used to mean Miller’s Bread Kitchen — the rustic bakery that started in an old car wash in the industrial estate and, yes, was named one of Victoria’s best for sausage rolls. We’d wander the Dromana Habitat at 40 Collins Road where about 15 makers set up shop: Little Rebel Roastery roasting beans on site (they even served a Black Sheep blend), Two Bays Brewing Co at 2 Trewhitt Court, and Bass & Flinders doing gin‑making experiences and G&T paddles. It was the kind of industrial‑estate‑turned‑tourist precinct that made sense once you’d found the carpark.

There was always a calendar of near‑misses: the Dromana Habitat launch party got postponed; the pier got scheduled for a major glow‑up; and, honestly, the Dromana 3 Drive In — open since 1962 on Nepean Hwy — kept reminding us that sometimes the past refuses to be politely renovated. Meanwhile Crittenden Restaurant had its shiny little comeback after a major upgrade and a return to family ownership, proving the Peninsula likes its reinventions tidy.

Who would hate living here? If you despise scenic drives, artisanal industrial estates, or the emotional whiplash of seeing a 1960s drive‑in in the same postcode as experimental gin bars, Dromana will feel like a mood board you can’t close. Also, if you believe beaches should be elbowed and noisy, the “far more chill” vibe reported about our bayside stretches might make you twitch.

Who’d love it? Lovers of beaches and piers, weekend road‑trippers, anyone who appreciates a bakery that started in a garage, people who cheer on community projects from the Dromana Community House and the Dromana Community Garden on Marna Street, and those who think a drive‑in and a gin bar can happily coexist. That’s us — proud, a little theatrical, and stubborn about our pier views.
