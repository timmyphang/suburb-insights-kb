# Brighton East (NSW)

Slug: `brighton-east-nsw`

We know Brighton East like the back of our sunhat — the bit that’s always got sand in it. Back in the day we used to start Sundays at Dendy Park, where the Hank Marvin Market kicked off at 306 Dendy Street and turned the oval into a parade of pickers, bakers and people pretending they were on holiday. If you were the sort of person who liked newsletters and linen, Bespoke Botanics would sort you out with a subscription bouquet and a smug smile.

Our Hawthorn Road renaissance was quietly led by Fran Roberts and Gabby Evans — you might remember when they poured their Marmalade Cafe passion into Primrose Hill Deli and gave this end of town a food-shaped identity crisis. Broadsheet catalogued it like it was a civic achievement and we treated it like a local rite of passage.

We’ve had our fair share of seaside show-offs too: Ian Curley’s 81 Bay Wine Bar and the plans for Baix were the kind of fine-diner ambitions that made us check our hair before the tram. Henrys arrived with a Riviera-meets-’70s vibe and the sort of pasta that made us pretend we’d always eaten like that. Royale Brothers — now Royale — and the B.East have fed our post-beach burger needs and given us something to brag about on group chats.

There isn’t a lot of scandal in the clippings — that’s the point. We’re more into tasteful patios than turf wars. Sitchu still points out the family-friendly streets and the haunted reputation for trick-or-treating on Carpenter Street; yes, we take Halloween decorations very seriously here, thank you.

Who would hate living here? If you thrive on late-night chaos, inner-city grit or the thrill of never knowing where your nearest good coffee is, you’d be miserable. Our crimes are civility, great schools and a beach with very particular ideas about how you may display a striped bathing box.

Who would love it here? Families who like tidy parks, weekend markets and cafes with provenance; beach people who prefer sunsets with a side of curated seafood; anyone who enjoys living somewhere that looks good on postcards and reads like a well-edited guide. We’ll keep the salt on the air and the roses on subscription — you bring the sandals.
