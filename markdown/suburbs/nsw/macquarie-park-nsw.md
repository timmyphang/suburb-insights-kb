# Macquarie Park (NSW)

Slug: `macquarie-park-nsw`

We wake up to the comforting hum of someone else’s commute — either the uni crowd marching across the footbridge or a parcel driver making friends with our letterbox — and decide today is for small victories: plants, ice, and a rooftop view.

Back in the day we’d start at Eden Gardens on Lane Cove Road. It’s the kind of 2.5‑acre nursery that doubles as an aspirational weekend, complete with a café, florist and display gardens where we pretend we know what we’re doing with succulents. From there it was a short trundle past the Macquarie Centre — the shopping precinct that used to be the thing to redevelop, and where Lululemon and Passiontree Velvet got a mention in the glossy roundups — and then to the Macquarie Ice Rink on Level 1 of the centre for a spin (they gave it a proper glow‑up, remember?).

If we’re feeling civic we’ll pop into the Macquarie University History Museum to gawp at ancient artefacts — yes, there are Egyptian papyri — and remind ourselves our suburb is not just offices and lecture halls, despite what the brochure says.

For sundown theatrics we remember when The Governor opened: four levels, a garden terrace and Ess on the rooftop with 360‑degree views and a Japanese robata grill. We’ve watched corporate crowds migrate there, cheered on a rooftop Two‑Up on Anzac Day (the Lindfield Soccer Club had a hand in that) and felt very grown‑up about our skyline.

Who would hate it here? If you want a hamlet of cottage cafés that smell exclusively of artisanal nothingness, or you loathe shopping centres and rooftop bars, you’ll find Macquarie Park a compromise too far. Also, if you cannot abide students, ice skaters and people who buy plants with reckless optimism — maybe don’t.

Who would love it? Students who appreciate a station on campus and short walks to lectures, families who once queued at Playtime Macquarie Park at The Bridge Church on Herring Road, plant nerds who treat Eden Gardens like a pilgrimage, and anyone who likes the odd sky‑high sundowner at Ess or a cheeky skate at the rink. We’ll take the office towers and the malls if it gets us a decent view and a place to park our potted delusions.
