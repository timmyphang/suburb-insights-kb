# Croydon North (NSW)

Slug: `croydon-north-nsw`

We’ll be honest: our suburb’s peak hour is really peak sparkle. Back in the day Mulgrave Way used to go full North Pole from December 1–26 — music, coordinated LEDs and maybe even Santa between 8.30pm and 10.30pm — so much so that Urban List and Time Out name‑checked it as one of Melbourne’s must-see displays. We still tell visitors to time their drive past for maximum carol-induced shame.

Weekends here are the sort of thing where you swap stories over a fence about whose speaker is playing Mariah louder. We remember there being a Yarra Valley Water precautionary warning — Broadsheet listed Croydon North among 88 suburbs told to boil their water after storm-related electrical damage — so yes, we’ve had the “bring a kettle” conversation at more barbecues than any of us would admit.

We’re not all tinsel and kettle advice. The local community scene has long been doing the heavy lifting: the Croydon Virtual Family Hub (CVFH) is the kind of resource you point new parents to, and local research — like the dissertation flagged on the Croydon family hub site — has pulled some uncomfortable truths about hunger, behaviour and safety into the light. We don’t romanticise that; we talk about it, we organise, we try to fix it. That’s our not-so-glamorous civic glitter.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe people singing off-key beneath your kitchen window, if you think a neighbourhood display is a municipal embarrassment rather than a communal rite, or if you demand total anonymity from your neighbours — you’ll find Croydon North a touch claustrophobic.

Who would love it? Families who want local things to actually exist (the CVFH, lists of nurseries and childminding services show there’s structure here), night‑drive romantics who judge suburbs by their Christmas displays, and anyone who prefers a suburb that cares enough to research and talk about real problems rather than pretend they’re not there.

We’re loud at Christmas, practical with our kettles, and stubbornly community-minded. Bring a torch, bring a thermos, and don’t park on Mulgrave Way in December unless you’re ready to be dazzled.
