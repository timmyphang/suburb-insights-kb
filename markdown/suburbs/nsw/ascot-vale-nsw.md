# Ascot Vale (NSW)

Slug: `ascot-vale-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed and wander down Union Street like it’s our own little festival parade — not because we’re fancy, but because Broadsheet once pointed out that most of our best cafes sit in a neat walking strip and we took that as gospel. We’d queue for ramen at Cafe Ogawa (yes, actual iekei and tonkotsu with a master stock brought from Japan — Broadsheet wrote the whole sob story), pop into Ascot Food Store at 320 Ascot Vale Road when it felt necessary, or begrudgingly applaud a new bakery saying “bonjour” — Gordon Street Bakery’s rum-spiked almond croissants were the sort of thing Broadsheet reported on with a wink.

Weekends blurred into community yin and yang. Concrete Playground catalogued The Ascot Lot turning a former car yard on Mt Alexander Road into a rotating food-truck beer garden with live music (and dogs, of course). Time Out told us the Laurel Hotel was wearing a tasteful refurbishment, while The Vale Bar & Eatery was doing its dog-friendly, share-plate shtick to sleepy perfection. Union Wine Store kept a wall of boutique wines that made us feel cultured when we couldn’t pronounce anything on the label.

There isn’t a juicy local feud in our press clippings — the coverage reads like a love letter from people who can’t decide if we’re riverside and leafy or quietly becoming the city’s snack cupboard. So if you’re after scandal, our archives will disappoint; what we have is a steady reel of cafés, bakeries, bars and community centres documenting our slow glow-up.

Who would hate it here? People who loathe dogs (we’re dog-friendly enough to make Time Out nod), absolute silence (The Ascot Lot’s live music won’t care for your grievances), or anyone allergic to being conveniently connected to riverside trails like the Maribyrnong River and parks such as Fairbairn Park and Walter Street Reserve.

Who would love it? Families who need playgroups and a neighbourhood centre (hello, Ascot Vale Neighbourhood Centre and Dwell at Ascot Vale Church of Christ), food obsessives chasing Kömür’s Adana kebab or ramen at Cafe Ogawa, and anyone who appreciates a historic venue — Ascot House and the Laurel Hotel have that old-world polish. As Broadsheet, Time Out and Concrete Playground remind us: we’re modest, greasy-with-goodness, and proud of it.
