# South Hurstville (NSW)

Slug: `south-hurstville-nsw`

We know the smell of Connells Point Road before our eyes are even open — cinnamon, oregano and that unmistakable waft of baking that used to send folk from across Sydney to queue at Oregano Bakery (56 Connells Point Road). As Broadsheet and Concrete Playground reminded us, our scrolls were the thing people came by the dozen for, long before anyone thought to Instagram them.

A Saturday here used to look like this: a run to the South Hurstville shopping village or St George Fruit Market, a detour past The Pinnacle RSL off King Georges Road for someone’s post-gardening pie, and an obligatory consolatory pastry from Piccola Cannoli Bar — which Urban List once crowned Sydney’s first cannoli bar — for the kids (or the adults, look, same thing). Broadsheet’s Hurstville dossier and Time Out’s local round-ups always focused on food: Hurstville Chinese Restaurant and Golden Sands on Forest Road, the yum cha stalwarts; Phu Cuong King’s banh mi getting its own nod in Time Out, and even niche things like the $1 manoush promos that used to pop up at Mt Lewis pizzeria in the area.

If you’re hunting for local drama, our press clippings are disappointingly wholesome — mostly bakery lore, yum cha love letters and property listings that brag “walk to everywhere” (thanks, Sitchu). The real tension here is whether to save the last cinnamon scroll or eat it in the car.

Who would hate it here? If you need a late-night 24/7 dance scene, or you despise the comforting knowledge that the RSL brasserie and the bakery are both in walking distance, South Hurstville will feel like suburbia in a good coat — too sensible, too well-fed, and suspiciously community-minded.

Who would love it? Food obsessives, parents looking for groups like the South Hurstville Mary Andrews intergenerational playgroup at Mary Andrews Village (those meetups used to run a Monday fortnightly), and anyone who appreciates a suburb where your weekend plan can be settled with a scroll or a yum cha booking. We’ll keep the corner of Connells Point Road warm for you — and maybe save you a cannoli.
