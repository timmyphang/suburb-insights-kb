# Subiaco (NSW)

Slug: `subiaco-nsw`

Subiaco, NSW — Saturday mornings here used to feel like a very polite takeover. We’d weave through the stalls at the Subi Farmers Market when Subiaco Primary School folded itself out into a jam of farmers, bakers and people who consider heirloom tomatoes a personality trait (Time Out had it right). 

Our Rokeby Road used to be the kind of strip that made other suburbs jealous: bakeries like Layers, cafés such as Community Coffee Co and Boucla (349 Rokeby Road) all elbowing for table space while the Regal Theatre quietly judged your outfit. Broadsheet and Sitchu catalogued it with the clinical enthusiasm of people who love lists — “best restaurants”, “best cafes” — as if we could pick a favourite child. Spoiler: we can’t.

On any given evening, you could have been torn between a big night at the Subiaco Continental or somewhere cosier like Bar Amelie. Yiamas brought the modern Greek energy to Denis Street and Shui served up prawn crumpets and pork belly until our collective cholesterol shuddered in approval (Urban List / Time Out / Broadsheet). The Vibe Hotel’s rooftop Storehouse used to let us pretend we lived somewhere with views and matching napkins. UnWined Subiaco and Subi Spritz staged our annual permission to drink more than is strictly necessary while calling it “culture” (Time Out / Sitchu).

We’re not immune to nostalgia. For years the Subiaco Playgroup met at St Andrew’s Hall on Barker Road — people who know small humans know the comfort of a routine that involves less Instagram and more glue sticks. The Village Bar and Subiaco Hotel were the reliable aunties of the night scene: same plates, same fireplace, same $10 jug memories (Concrete Playground).

Who would hate living here? If markets, Mediterranean diners, rooftop gnocchi and a calendar full of festivals make you twitch, you’ll find us intolerably smug. We’ll be the ones discussing which empanada from Pinos was the most authentically Chilean while folding laundry.

Who would love it? Families who want playgroups and pastry options, food obsessives who treat menus like weather reports, and anyone who enjoys living somewhere that has a festival for pretty much everything. Back in the day, Subiaco felt like a small city that knew how to throw a decent spritz — and honestly, we miss that chaos with our coffee in hand.
