# Preston (NSW)

Slug: `preston-nsw`

We’re the sort of people who still get our Saturday rhythm from a loud, aggressive fruit stall run by someone who knows our surname better than we do. Back in the day, we’d shuffle through Preston Market — “cheap and loud,” as Time Out put it — chasing paella, doughnuts and a bargain at the Asian grocer, while plotting how the place survived threat after threat of redevelopment. Remember when vendors announced Salta Properties would renew leases and Sam Tarascio suddenly became the man of the hour? That saga (and the later planning amendments that knocked redevelopment plans back) gave us collective whiplash and a new appreciation for stubborn marketplaces.

Morning routine: we used to window-shop at ELK’s boutique nirvana, then pop past Eat Cannoli for a gluten-free contraband treat from Dom and Kate Marzano. Quentin Berthonneau’s Oji House was the baking studio people fussed over — shokupan and sourdough classes, if you wanted to feel artisanal before lunch. If we were being indulgent, Moon Dog World was our cathedral of beer — a secret warehouse with 72 taps, a hidden tiki bar, a lagoon and a waterfall. Yes, a lagoon. We’re not saying we worship hops, but we’ve been caught lighting candles.

Petty local drama: there was the arson scare at Spanian’s kebab truck — horrible, but the owner vowed to be back, and that stubbornness is very Preston. Planning battles over the market and lists of “best suburbs” and “best value” (thanks, Sitchu) give us something to argue about at BARE nights in Wellness Social Club — yes, the same place that tried a nude sauna event. Only in Preston.

Who would hate living here: purists who need quiet and tidy streets without a single kitschy arepa or a 72‑tap shrine to craft beer. Who would love it: families who appreciate Samlesbury Hall, Flipout Preston and Level Preston for the kids; vinyl hunters who worship Poison City Records; bowlers and arcade nostalgics who miss late nights at The Keys; anyone who likes a market that refuses to be a shopping mall.

We’re messy, musical, loud and stubbornly local — and frankly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
