# Galston (NSW)

Slug: `galston-nsw`

We’re the ones who nod at every Galston Road renovation plan like it’s our weekend gossip — and yes, we still remember who said what at the community meeting. We used to duck into Poppy Lane at Shop 4/346 Galston Road for a tempura eggplant that Broadsheet once wrote up; these days we stand on the footpath and watch the plans on the noticeboard grow antlers.

Back in August 2024 the Galston Glenorie & Hills Rural Community News ran a proper piece about a revitalisation — walking and cycling networks, better parking, public toilets prioritised — and Hornsby Shire mums cheered the council’s promise to make the village “better for family to relax, dine, and enjoy.” Then, on Mar 11, 2026, the community site confirmed the Galston Village Public Domain Plan contract has been awarded after all that engagement. The paperwork says construction may be staged over the next two to three years. Translation: bring snacks and patience.

We like to remind newcomers that this place was always a bit different. Time Out once file-noted the Game Farm at 51 Crosslands Rd — yes, the multi-species operation that gave Galston some proper country credentials — and Swayne’s Dural (490 Galston Road, Dural) used to be the sort of 16-hectare rose empire you’d wander through and pretend you understand horticulture while Botanica served coffee you could call “artisan” in a whisper. Those are the local legends we trade stories about while the council draws new bike lines.

Petty drama? The files call it “community engagement” — which, in local-speak, means a dozen opinions and half-a-dozen sticky notes. The real quibble is timing: people who hate staged construction will hate us for the next few years. People who hate change will tut at the new shelter, the traffic-calming and the extra seating.

Who’s wrong for Galston? Anyone who needs instant CBD glamour or zero roadworks. Who’s right for Galston? Families, weekend gardeners, people who like their village semi-rural (as Hornsby Shire Mums put it), and anyone who enjoys a slow-build glow-up. We’ll be here, clipboard in hand, ready to tell you the history while the kerb gets a facelift.
