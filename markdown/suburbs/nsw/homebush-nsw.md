# Homebush (NSW)

Slug: `homebush-nsw`

We know Homebush like the back of our hands — the place where you can watch the sun rise over a hundred-year-old shipwreck and still be in line for dosa by breakfast. As Broadsheet once wrote, “lines snake for brekkie dosa” at New Shakthi; for years we queued at 4am starts and curries made from scratch felt like ancestral rituals rather than weekend plans.

Weekends used to look like this: a Bicentennial Park stroll past wetlands and the SS Ayrfield, a detour along the Parramatta River Walk, then a tactical raid on DFO Homebush (yes, the one tied to those Furla/Coach/Kate Spade promos). If the fam needed chaos, Bounce — the trampoline megazone — and Bounce Inc’s after-dark parties provided it. For culture, we’d catch whatever Concrete Playground flagged at Sydney Olympic Park — from Bubble Planet installations to happy hour deals at RAFI overlooking the man-made lagoon at Urbnsurf.

We’re not strangers to spectacle or irony. The Olympic precinct has hosted the Royal Easter Show and grand sporting seasons since 2000, and Urbnsurf’s surf lagoon came with a Rafi rooftop and Sandy’s poolside burritos — very on-brand for a suburb that once specialised in ship-breaking and now sells rooftop Mai Tais. Meanwhile, Pho Toan Thang and Ram’s held their spots like stubborn local monuments — long queues, fiercely loyal regulars.

Petty local saga? Oh, we had one: fans who parked in the P1 car park by Accor Stadium once came back to find pamphlets with an AI rendering of Nathan Cleary — a pamphlet kerfuffle that made the rounds and then evaporated like a half-time fairy.

Who would hate Homebush? People allergic to lines, bargain-hunting, or the smell of wet grass at 6am. People who dislike the surreal combo of wholesale flower markets, leftover Olympic grandeur and a surf lagoon that isn’t actually the ocean.

Who would love it? Families who like convenience and chaos, food obsessives chasing long-lived pho and dosa legends, and anyone who appreciates a suburb that went from shipyard to sporting precinct and kept its strange, stubborn charm. We wear our quirks proudly — and we still talk about that dosa.
