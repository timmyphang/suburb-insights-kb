# Lesmurdie (NSW)

Slug: `lesmurdie-nsw`

Back in the day we’d pretend we were heading out for a big adventure and then pull up after 30 minutes for a two-kilometre circuit. Welcome to Lesmurdie, where our wild days involve a picnic rug, a lookout and collectively pretending not to notice everyone else taking the exact same sunset photo of the city and Rottnest Island.

Our weekend routine is hospitality-lite: we used to haul the kids to Saturday Morning LEGO at the Lesmurdie Library (9am–12pm, if you were keeping the calendar), then bribe them into a “short walk” at Lesmurdie Falls in Mundy Regional Park. Older guides called it a quick, pretty speccy loop with a 50-metre drop if the heavens had been generous — and a very convincing city skyline if the air was playing nice. Urban List and Time Out were fond of reminding us the falls are best after rain, that the loop is a neat 2km, and that, yes, it can be busy. Concrete Playground also whispered that spring wildflowers (thanks, Welshpool Road East car park route) are worth the soft-shoe shuffle.

There isn’t much local scandal in our file — unless you count the eternal argument of whether the Falls look better from the top lookout or the bridge on the Lesmurdie Falls Track. The practical drama is meteorological: one minute you’ve got a spectacular torrent, the next it’s the “where’s the water?” summer special. Guides warned the falls can dry up in hot weather — nature’s version of a mood swing.

Who would hate it here? If you despise short walks that feel satisfyingly accomplished, or you need your weekends to include concrete and neon, Lesmurdie will bore you rigid. Also, those who’ve sworn off sharing a view with other humans: avoid peak season.

Who’d love it? Teachers planning school excursions (Kanyana Wildlife Rehabilitation Centre at 120 Gilchrist Rd gets a nod), families who valued a library LEGO excuse, hikers after a low-effort win, and anyone who thinks a 30-minute drive for a lookout is a perfectly reasonable life choice. We’re a humble 14 km² pocket tucked among Canning Mills, Carmel, Forrestfield, Kalamunda, Orange Grove, Walliston and Wattle Grove — and we’ll happily share our lookout with you.
