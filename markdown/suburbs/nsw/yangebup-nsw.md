# Yangebup (NSW)

Slug: `yangebup-nsw`

Back in the day we’d say Yangebup is the sort of suburb that greets you with a skatepark, a pocket of paperbark trees and a community noticeboard that’s more reliable than the local weather app. We know every corner of our 6.02 km² postcode 6164 patch — from Dunraven Drive where the Yangebup Family Centre used to be the cultural hub, to the edge of Beeliar Hive at 283 Beeliar Drive where Lagoon Medical Centre used to take care of our sniffles and bulk-billing dramas.

Our weekend ritual used to be embarrassingly simple: drop the kids at the playgroup the Family Centre ran for under‑fives, borrow a book from the street library, then wave at the mums and dads who’d stayed on for the monthly Yangebup Community Eats free dinner. The Centre ran workshops with St John Ambulance and the City of Cockburn, hosted Child and Adolescent Community Health services, and even celebrated milestones — remember their 25th anniversary? We certainly do. We cheered when a YFC educator was named a finalist in the WA Early Childhood Awards (that March 2026 blip in the news felt like ours).

We’re a suburb boxed in by Beeliar, Bibra Lake, Cockburn Central, Munster, South Lake, Spearwood and Success — which is to say we’re perfectly positioned for anyone who likes being near things without being in the thick of them. Population roughly 7,125, LGA Cockburn: quaint, manageable, and quietly persistent.

Who would hate living here? People who equate life with a rotating list of Michelin dreams, 24/7 nightlife or a city that never sleeps. If your idea of community is "discover the 21 best Italian restaurants in Melbourne this weekend", you’ll be restless. We don’t pretend to be a glossy destination; we’re the place that remembers your kid’s name and the exact date of the free community dinner.

Who would love it? Families who want a village feel, anyone who values a community centre that used to hold playgroups and local workshops, and people who prefer a suburb where the street library knows your reading tastes. We’ll keep the lamingtons and the noticeboard; you bring the patience.
