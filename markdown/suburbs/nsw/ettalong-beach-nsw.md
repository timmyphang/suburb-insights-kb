# Ettalong Beach (NSW)

Slug: `ettalong-beach-nsw`

We get off the Palm Beach ferry like it’s a little victory lap — sand in our shoes, sunscreen in our hair, and eyes on the esplanade. As Urban List and Broadsheet have politely reminded everyone within 90 minutes of Sydney, Solina is literally perched on the sand here; we point at the mid‑century seaside house like proud tour guides who also secretly want to live in it.

Our weekend looks predictable and charming: a slow roll along the foreshore (yes, the Twilight program plays live music Friday and Saturday nights from 4–6:30pm — Concrete Playground, take a bow), maybe a bike jaunt to Umina if we’re feeling virtuous (that Ettalong-to-Umina 7.5km route clocks in at about 30 minutes, apparently). Dinner? We’ll tell you straight — Time Out’s write-up of Osteria il Coccia wasn’t lying: fire‑powered Italian by the water is our kind of nonsense. The rest of the foodie gossip — Safran, Bar Toto, Amalfi’s return — reads like a who’s‑who of meals we used to queue for, nodded to in old reviews.

Now the petty stuff, because what seaside village is complete without it: our lovely strip between Picnic Pde and Ferry Rd has been doing the slow coastal fade, and Coast Community News says there’s a $1.8M federal pledge for renourishment that we’re all waiting on (please, before summer). Meanwhile, the Land and Environment Court has given the thumbs up to a five‑storey motel at 43–46 The Esplanade — which is one way to get more visitors to the Twilight sessions, we suppose. The Peninsula Residents Association has even put in for Black Spot funding for the busy pedestrian area at the end of Picnic Pde, because yes, more people = more near‑miss Instagram moments.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t handle impromptu live music on a foreshore, a ferry commute that feels holiday‑adjacent, or the idea that the beach isn’t lifeguarded (Sitchu, don’t say we didn’t warn you), this isn’t your jam.

Who will love it? Anyone who wants coastal village energy with proper dinner options, weekend markets and foreshore quirks, bike rides to Umina, and the kind of houses (hello, JEWEL and 102 The Esplanade listings) that make your relatives whisper. We’ll keep the saxophone on, the fire in Osteria’s kitchen, and our eyes on the sand — just don’t ask us where the funding is hiding.
