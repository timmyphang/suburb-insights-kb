# Drummoyne (NSW)

Slug: `drummoyne-nsw`

Back in the day we’d stroll down Lyons Road like it was our catwalk, except the models were local pups and the runway smelled of battered fish. As Broadsheet noted, Lyons R.A.W. had a loyal bunch of regulars — timber floors, raw brick, and Toby’s Estate on the go — and yes, they famously did a buttery bacon-and-egg pie and a lamb sausage and chickpea hot plate that made queues slightly fashionable.

Morning routine here is very specific: someone lobbed a takeaway from Ocean Foods (Time Out lists it at 154 Lyons Road) into the bike basket, someone else reminisced about a Milloie Cakery cupcake (199 Lyons Road), and others debated whether Zeus’s slow-cooked meats (187–189 Lyons Road) counted as breakfast because they’d been braising for more than 12 hours. Gin & Tonic at 52 Lyons Road used to make us feel like we’d accidentally wandered into a tiny boutique that understood our life choices — Broadsheet loved its personalised vibe.

We own the Bay Run like it’s our communal backyard: Iron Cove views, joggers who look like they’re auditioning for a wellness brand, and the occasional cyclist who treats the seven-kilometre loop like a racetrack. Concrete Playground flagged the Bay Run as the suburb’s scenic selling point, and Birkenhead Point still haunts our outlet-shopping dreams.

There’s no scandal in the data — no council wars, no barista feuds — so our petty drama is existential: do we miss the old corner stores or pretend Hudson Meats (121 Victoria Rd) and Salt Meats Cheese have always been ours? Time Out and Urban List both charted these little additions like they were badges of civilisation.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe water views, family-friendly events (hello, Drummoyne Groovy Grandparents at the Scout Group on 11 Dening Street), playground upgrades mentioned by Inner West Mums, or a local restaurant scene that prefers slow-cooked gyros to loud nightclub beats — this isn’t your postcode.

Who would love it? Families, weekend walkers, anyone who appreciates a seaside suburb that reads like a well-curated local guide — from Ecco at the Sailors Club to the Party People Megastore’s promise of costume drama. We’ll keep the waterfront, the shops, and the tasteful pies — and the rest of Sydney can keep wondering how we made Lyons Road so very placidly charming.
