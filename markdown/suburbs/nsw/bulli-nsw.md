# Bulli (NSW)

Slug: `bulli-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean the only clippings we’ve got in the archive — Bulli was the sort of place where Sundays arrive with a checklist and nobody’s in a hurry to cross anything off it.

We start our Sunday at the Foragers Sunday markets and the Bulli Showground Markets (yes, both are a thing), buying things we don’t actually need and waving at the same people who’ve been selling the same preserves since before we paid rent in Wollongong. If there are kids, they get the real reward: Bulli Beach Playground down on Trinity Row, sliding off sand and declaring they’ve “done” the coast for the week. The escarpment walks — Sublime Walking Track — and the waterfall trails sit there like dramatic relatives you only see once a year, demanding hiking boots and humility. There’s even an Olympic-sized ocean pool if we fancy pretending we’re serious about fitness.

We occasionally reminisce about what used to come and go. Eat at Sandy (323 Princes Highway), which was an offshoot of Sandygoodwhich, took over a former pizzeria and did Middle Eastern–Mediterranean shared plates from a wood-fire oven — until it didn’t. Broadsheet notes it’s permanently closed; we still argue about who ate the last slice.

Then there’s the older, reliable local lore: Resin Brewing, housed in that 130-year-old railway guesthouse, earned awards and brewed things like the Flame Tree amber ale — Concrete Playground wrote nice things about it back in 2020. The Heritage Hotel, built in 1889 and sitting on the State Heritage Register, has been the place reviewers point to when they get nostalgic for burgers and history in the same sentence.

Who would hate Bulli? If you require 24/7 nightlife, a rotation of three different $18 brunches or zero sand in your shoes, you’ll be miserable here. If your idea of a weekend is a rooftop DJ and no walking tracks, move along.

Who would love it? Families who like a weekly market habit, dog-walkers who appreciate a historical pub, hikers plotting the Sublime track, and anyone who thinks a 130-year-old railway guesthouse should be brewing amber ales and winning awards. We know every corner, every market stall, and every playground rule — and honestly, we wouldn’t trade our sandy shoes for your skyline.
