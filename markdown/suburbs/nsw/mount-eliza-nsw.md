# Mount Eliza (NSW)

Slug: `mount-eliza-nsw`

We like to think our Sundays are subtle flexes — a slow stroll through Mount Eliza village, a detour past the Eliza Square shops and a glance toward the bay that says, politely, we’ve got it sorted. Back in the day the local scene was all playgrounds and picnic rugs; these days the headlines have turned up the glamour meter.

Concrete Playground ran a review of Bau Bau Dining as a refined Italian that leaned on its own regenerative farm and Beer Garden Produce — house-made pasta and seafood, apparently with tableside amaro to make you feel like you belong in a tiny, very polite Amalfi. Broadsheet also flagged Bau Bau for lunchtime offerings and chef credits; we read it and nodded like we’d always known. Around the same time, Breadcetera’s coast move was written up — Concrete Playground noted a Mount Eliza store at 26 Ranelagh Drive, and Urban List said Malvern East’s modern market was landing here. Breadcetera was billed as the kind of curated grocer you bring your well-behaved friends to.

We’re not all boutique produce and five-page wine lists. Time Out kept us honest with Sunnyside North Beach — the clothing-optional patch of coastline that makes every morning walk slightly more educational. Little Nook — the kidswear shop at Shop 3, Eliza Square, 85 Mount Eliza Way — was the local mum-and-daughter success story Time Out liked to remind people about. Sitchu’s old profile called us a seaside utopia, family-friendly, with schools like Toorak College and Peninsula Grammar within the orbit; we keep the playgrounds and the PTA drama warm for tourists.

Who would hate living here? If you recoil at the idea of a spontaneous, salty breeze revealing a bit more than you expected at Sunnyside, or you need the pulsating buzz of a city that never sleeps, Mount Eliza will feel like a politely disapproving aunt. Who would love it? Families, weekenders after a two-acre garden with bay views (hello, Mount Eliza Bay Crest Manor), people who like their local markets curated, and anyone who enjoys a village that’s quietly upgraded itself while pretending not to notice.

We’ll keep our beaches, our cheddar (yes, there was Mount Eliza cheddar used in a cheeky cheese course), and our small-town confidence. You’re welcome.
