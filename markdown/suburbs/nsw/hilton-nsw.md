# Hilton (NSW)

Slug: `hilton-nsw`

We start the day the same way everyone in Hilton, NSW does: we type our postcode into the internet and watch the algorithms decide we mean a hotel chain, a pop princess, or a chocolatier from Aotearoa. Back in the day (and by that I mean every article in the feed), "Hilton" mostly belonged to other people — the Glass Brasserie at the Sydney Hilton, Marble Bar tucked beneath the Hilton on George Street, the Parmelia Hilton Perth competitions Broadsheet used to run — all glamour that has nothing to do with our cul‑de‑sacs. As Broadsheet noted, Marble Bar was literally dismantled and reassembled beneath the Hilton Sydney; we nod and wave politely from our street.

Weekend routine: we scroll Concrete Playground for a review of a bar we've never been in, see a write‑up about Hilton Auckland's Fish overlooking Waitematā Harbour and wonder why our weekend plans never include a harbour view. Junkee even found Trevor Hilton — yes, that Trevor — who wrote a jingle that somehow feels like it should be playing on loop here. Pedestrian's reminders that Paris Hilton once dominated headlines are the closest we get to celebrity gossip; she’s glamorous, we are not, and that’s fine.

Petty local drama? There isn't much — mostly a sustained identity crisis. As outsiders keep reminding us, "Hilton" can mean a high‑end lobby cocktail, a promotional sleepover in Perth, or an indigenous chocolatier called Tom Hilton reclaiming identity with Ao Cacao (as Broadsheet wrote). We take our cues from that: ambivalence, with a side of excellent taste.

Who would hate living here: people who move in expecting lobby cocktails, live music beneath marble columns, or concierge services. If you want curated heritage marble and signature cocktails as your backyard, you'll be disappointed.

Who would love it: anyone who enjoys being unnamed on a tourist map, who appreciates that our postcode can inspire confusing search results and weird hotel competitions, and who likes the idea that "Hilton" can also be about handcrafted chocolate and reclaimed identity. We are quietly content with our anonymity — and secretly enjoy telling visitors we live in "Hilton" and watching them Google for a pool.
