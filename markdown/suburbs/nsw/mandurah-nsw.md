# Mandurah (NSW)

Slug: `mandurah-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean the patchwork of travel pieces and local pages we've been hoarding — Mandurah was basically the coast’s best-kept chill secret. We wake up by the Peel–Harvey estuary, pretending the dolphins are here for us (Urban List called us the place to rubberneck the resident dolphins) and wander the eastern foreshore like it’s our living room. As Broadsheet noted, the Crab Festival used to turn that foreshore into a proper pilgrimage for lovers of blue manna crabs — and yes, the crowds were part of the charm.

Our weekend ritual used to be painfully simple: a shoreline stroll, a detour to the Flour Mill Emporium for whatever craft-market momentum was happening, and a slow lap of the Causeway to see if Thomas Dambo’s Giants had been photobombed by a pelican (Time Out raved about the Giants trail). If we were feeling fancy we’d reminisce over seafood pieces like Fleur’s Mandurah blue swimmer crab, which Broadsheet once made sound like haute cuisine stolen from the estuary.

Local drama? Oh, we have the wholesome kind that reads like a true-crime footnote: a property rep in Mandurah was fined for dipping into tenant funds (Pedestrian TV ran that one). And the region’s cameo in other news cycles — remember the Mandurah teenager mentioned in that Junkee roundup? — only added to our small‑town soap opera. We don’t keep scores, we keep stories.

Who would hate living here: people who need neon-lit, 24/7 city chaos. If you crave that, head elsewhere — we’ll wave you off from the boardwalk. Also, anyone allergic to mini golf shouldn’t book the car: Amaze Miniature Park is our sacred putting ground (Urban List did the honors).

Who would love it here: families and art hunters, festival-goers and anyone who thinks a weekend should feature barbecue boats, boardwalk fish-and-chips, or a lazy search for bottlenose dolphins (Time Out’s ode to day trips got that right). We’ve got community staples — ManPAC, the Kwillena Gabi floating pontoons, WA Mums Cottage on Davey Street — and an estuary that still insists on being beautiful. Mandurah isn’t pretending to be Perth; it’s proudly a little bit salt-streaked and properly our kind of coastline.
