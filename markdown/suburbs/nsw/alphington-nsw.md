# Alphington (NSW)

Slug: `alphington-nsw`

Back in the day we’d wander down Heidelberg Road with coffee in one hand and something unnecessarily specific in the other — the kind of suburban wandering that made Alphington feel like a village with a very good appetite.

We used to pop into Apte Cafe on 538 Heidelberg Road for steady regulars and Dukkah Eggs, then somehow end up at Kissaten (538–540 Heidelberg Road) for house‑brewed hojicha lattes and katsu‑style toasties that argued with your expectations. Broadsheet even wrote that our little strip was quietly becoming a culinary enclave — and yes, we smirked and pretended we were surprised.

Weekends meant the Alphington Farmers Market, where the Romu x Shokupan sando pop‑up once took over the kitchen on Sundays and taught us that a tight menu can still cause civic pride. There was also Zero95’s Alphington venue with its piazza and separate Meno Zero entrance — the kind of place that made pizza a communal sport. Venom Brewing had a bottle‑shop outpost here too, because of course we needed an IPA that felt like a local.

You might remember The Alphington Social, an old bank converted into a neighbourhood Italian — woodfired pizza, ricotta gnocchi and an all‑Australian wine list, as Broadsheet kindly catalogued. And yes, we read the whispers: Adam D’Sylva’s Decca was slated to open at Yarrabend in Alphington in 2024, and Pizzateca at 27 Mills Boulevard was the sort of opening Sitchu would file under “now we’re officially a vibe.”

If you’re the sort who hates small‑batch everything, natural wines, and menus that insist on being interesting, you’ll loathe living here. If your dream is endless big‑box convenience and anonymity, this sleepy, foodie‑leaning nook will annoy you with its character.

But if you love weekend markets, thoughtful pizza, Japanese‑inspired cafes and the odd dance studio (Melbourne Bellydance had a second Alphington spot), you’ll fit right in. We’re a suburb that reads Broadsheet like scripture, argues about which pizza is best, and quietly rejoices when a good pop‑up shows up on a Sunday. Come for the food, stay for the farmers market, and pretend you always liked it before it was cool.
