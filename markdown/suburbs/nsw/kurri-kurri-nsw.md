# Kurri Kurri (NSW)

Slug: `kurri-kurri-nsw`

We start our weekends like we start our gossip: loud, proud and with an audience. By 9am we’re strolling the Kurri Kurri Mural Walk — over 60 big, bold pieces that Concrete Playground once pointed out — nodding at the one that looks like it remembers every A‑grade dance from the Nostalgia Festival.

If we’re feeling scenic, we’ll head out towards Narrow Place lookout and pretend we’re on a pilgrimage. As Urban List noted, that view opens up over Quorrobolong to Ellalong, Cessnock and, yes, our patch of glory. Afterwards some of us used to queue for the Kurri Kurri Aquatic and Fitness Centre — Newy with Kids called it the go‑to year‑round pool for the Cessnock shire — because nothing says small‑town flex like indoor lanes and a towel you’ve owned since the 2000s.

We’ve had our moments of pure, glorious spectacle. Pedestrian once ran with the fact that Kurri Kurri’s inaugural Mulletfest went down at the Chelmsford Hotel — exactly what you’re imagining and then some. And for years the Nostalgia Festival rolled through like someone set the 1950s on repeat: classic cars, hot rods, retro fashion and live rock ’n’ roll, as Newy with Kids cheerfully lists.

Not every corner is polished chrome and murals. The Richmond Vale Railway Museum — that love letter to steam and mining between Kurri Kurri and Freeman’s Waterhole — was closed in March 2024 pending safety paperwork, which gave us something to tut about while still admiring the history from afar. And for the parents among us, the Kurri Kurri Samaritan CAPS playgroup turned up on Playgroup NSW’s listings at 118 Lang Road, Tuesdays at 9:30am — proof we can do community as well as we can do a good singalong.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe murals, vintage hot rods, a mullet that’s considered haute couture, or the idea of a town that favours long lunches in the Hunter and public art over anonymous glass towers, this isn’t for you.

Who would love it? Art nerds on day hikes, families who need a reliable indoor pool, and anyone who thinks festivals that lean into their own weirdness are the highest form of hospitality. We’ll see you under the next mural — bring earplugs, a memory for every rock ’n’ roll lyric, and an appreciation for a town that wears its history like a good singlet.
