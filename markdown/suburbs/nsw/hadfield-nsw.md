# Hadfield (NSW)

Slug: `hadfield-nsw`

We’ll be honest: our Monday mornings used to look very modest. We’d shuffle down Middle Street, past the regular strip, and there’d be Middle Street Food & Coffee — Broadsheet once called it a standout — doing the sensible things: Allpress on the drip, Dench pastries in the cabinet, and a mini food emporium bit selling Jonai Farms pork and Warialda beef. As Broadsheet noted, it felt like the place that quietly cared about good things while the rest of the strip just existed.

Weekends were the same comfortable ritual: pick up something for dinner, pretend we’re food critics, and argue about whether the Turkish place on Middle Street deserved Sitchu’s nod. Sitchu’s roundup included a 142 Middle Street entry in their Best Turkish Restaurants list, and yes, we still giggle that our tiny postcode gets name-checked next to kebab prestige.

We don’t have a headline-grabbing scandal to dish. The paper trail is mostly appreciative directories and roundups — a quiet compliment, not national drama — and we’re fine with that. If you want fireworks and front-page soap operas, you’ll be bored here. We also get confused for other Hadfields on the internet; Junkee can’t stop talking about Chris Hadfield in space, and sometimes strangers DM us thinking we’ve got rocket launch tips. We don’t. We have good meat and decent pastries.

Who would hate it here? If you demand buzzy nightlife, a parade of gastropubs, or continuous reinvention, Hadfield will feel like a cardigan: reliable, slightly beige, and not for you. If your aesthetic is rooftop cocktails and neon hashtags, please don’t move here. We’ll sleep better.

Who would love it? People who want a neighbourhood that quietly remembers where its pork and beef come from, who appreciate a locally lauded cafe that once felt like the heart of the strip, and fans of a Turkish place that earned a Sitchu mention. We’re not flashy. We’re not trying to be. We are the suburb you visit when you want food that knows its suppliers and a street that keeps its dignity — even if the internet keeps sending astronauts our way.
