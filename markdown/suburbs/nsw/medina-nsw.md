# Medina (NSW)

Slug: `medina-nsw`

We live in Medina, NSW — and for the record, no, we do not have a UNESCO‑listed 11th‑century tannery in the middle of town. We do, however, get mistaken for one so often that we’ve started charging a tourist surcharge for disappointed globetrotters.

Mornings here don’t involve negotiating a snake of winding souks like Fez (as Broadsheet lovingly documented — “over 9,000 winding streets,” they said; very flattering). Our weekend routine is more “find the nearest coffee” via phone signal that insists we’re three continents away, while someone in Melbourne sips a “Funky Cold Medina” — Broadsheet’s deliciously named cocktail made with Oyster Shell gin and grapefruit — and wonders why we aren’t on the menu.

Back in the day, the only press hits in our dataset were about other Medinas: Broadsheet’s travel essays on Fez, Concrete Playground writing about a Dulwich Hill store called Medina Interior selling Fes mosaics and safi ceramics, and even a Lucy Folk Paddington fit‑out described as a “mini medina” with Tuareg mats. In short: our namesakes are busy being exotic, artisanal or alcoholic. Meanwhile, we’re here being reliably unglamorous.

There’s no local feud to gossip about in the files we’ve been given — only a queer little constellation of medina‑adjacent things. So the petty drama we offer is existential: are we a suburb, a cocktail, a Moroccan labyrinth, or a boutique in Dulwich Hill? The answer is yes, but only in other people’s headlines.

Who would hate living here? If you’re after camel rides, 24/7 souk bargaining or an actual tanneries tour, you’ll be disappointed. Also, people who like their suburbs to have dozens of thinkpieces — frankly, we’re bad for your content calendar.

Who would love it? People who enjoy being quietly underestimated, who get a kick out of confusing Google Maps once in a while, and who can take pride that our name turns up in travel essays, cocktail lists and boutique interiors — even if it’s all about somewhere else. We’ll keep our peace, our humdrum charms, and leave the tagines, tannery tours and backflips to the other Medinas.
