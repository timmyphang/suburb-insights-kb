# Osullivan Beach (NSW)

Slug: `osullivan-beach-nsw`

Back in the day — and yes, there aren’t any recent write‑ups to pretend otherwise — our Saturday starts at the O’Sullivan Beach Boat Ramp, ticket on the dash and the one-eyed seagull judging our launch technique. We used to buy a ramp use ticket to display on the car (or sort a periodic Boat Launching Permit online if we were feeling fancy), then shuffle to the kiosk for bait, tackle, and the kind of coffee and fast food that pairs well with windburn and small triumphs.

We know the corner of Galloway Rd and Marine Dr like it’s a town square: the O’Sullivan Beach Sports & Community Association is there for anyone who needs a hall that seats 150, a commercial kitchen when you’ve suddenly become the person organising the fundraiser, or an oval you can hire when the soccer club decides it’s time to reinvent homework as fitness. The Children’s Centre at 51a Galloway Rd used to be the place you’d drop off a tiny human for sessional kindy or long day care, then feel smug about the “seamless transition” to O’Sullivan Beach Primary School — who knew play‑based learning could be such a stealth curriculum in local pride?

We’re not claiming high drama. The local narrative is mostly functional: MyTime — O’Sullivan Beach showed up as a proper peer support group for parents, grandparents and carers, which is one of the quieter, more impressive things about us. If anyone’s looking for scandal, they’ll be disappointed; the real story is community infrastructure that just does the job.

Who hates it here? People who need constant novelty, boutique everything, or an Instagram mural on every roundabout. If you need late‑night neon and artisanal sorrow, best go elsewhere.

Who loves it here? Families who want community programs and a children’s centre that’s more than a name; anglers who actually read the boat ramp rules; organisers who need a 150‑seat hall and a commercial kitchen ready to be bossed into existence. We’ll keep the ramp ticket on display, the kettle on, and the oval booked for the next barbecue someone insists must be “just a casual thing.”
