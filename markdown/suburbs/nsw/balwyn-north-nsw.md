# Balwyn North (NSW)

Slug: `balwyn-north-nsw`

Back in the day we’d swear Balwyn North was designed by someone who loved hedges, heritage tiles and the polite clink of wine glasses. We know which footpath on Belmore Road gets the best morning sun, which table at Little Nonna (249B Belmore Road) catches the breeze, and where the queue for Mr Hendricks used to be a social ritual rather than a warning sign.

Our weekend routine used to look like this: wander down Belmore for a grilled Bombay sandwich or paneer sanga at Chai Garden, pop next door to Meatsmith Balwyn for something fancy from the Gippsland beef counter, and then stroll off the calories past Maranoa Gardens because yes, we are that predictable. There was always a reason to duck into Hahndorf’s Fine Chocolates and dramatically sample a truffle “for research”, or climb the stairs to Enoteca Boccaccio — the wine bar above Boccaccio Cellars — and pretend we knew the housemade pasta on the menu.

We’re not going to pretend we invented the phrase “leafy, family-friendly” — Sitchu already did. We’re proud of the heritage homes, the elite schools in the zone and the fact that North Balwyn Bowls Club has three well-lit greens and free coaching on Fridays. It’s the suburb where you can smell a slow-cooked ragu and the scent of someone priming their rose bushes in the same breath.

Who would hate it here? People who loathe imported Italian wine lists, communal tables, chocolate that doubles as an emotional support snack, or a brunch that might include a paratha. If you’re allergic to slow Sundays and tasteful restraint, Balwyn North will not forgive you.

Who would love it? Families who like a quiet sweep of lawn, anyone addicted to good bread and cured meat (we see you, Meatsmith), and those who take their wine recommendations from a grocer’s upstairs bar. For years the local listings — Broadsheet, Time Out, Concrete Playground — loved pointing out the same things we brag about at barbecues. We’ll keep pretending we didn’t read those reviews, and you can keep coming back anyway.
