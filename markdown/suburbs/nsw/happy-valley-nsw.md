# Happy Valley (NSW)

Slug: `happy-valley-nsw`

We live in Happy Valley, NSW — yes, that Happy Valley. Before you start googling artisanal terrariums and XO pippies, hear us out.

Morning in our valley is not a Smith Street aesthetic stroll. When tourists ask where the chic little store is, we patiently explain that the Happy Valley everyone raves about used to be the Collingwood boutique run by Chris Crouch on Smith Street — Broadsheet, Time Out and Concrete Playground loved its books, records and hand-picked curios. Meanwhile, another Happy Valley was the name of the restaurant that used to sit inside The Albert Park Hotel, where reviews once promised xiao long bao, prawn toast and chef Ken Yuen’s modern Chinese plates. None of that is actually here. Shocking, we know.

Weekends are quieter than the internet would have you believe. The web is full of misdirects: Urban List keeps lauding the Collingwood shop; Time Out’s listicles drop Happy Valley into roundups about record stores and gift shops; there’s a Happy Valley Brewing Company over in Stafford with 16 rotating taps; and somewhere on the Sunshine Coast there’s a Happy Valley Beach for the wave chasers. Even the telly hasn’t left us alone — Junkee still reminds people that “Happy Valley” as a British crime show is, famously, bleak.

Petty local drama? Our drama is mostly semantic. Do we get annoyed when someone DMs asking if our postcode is the one that starred in a Netflix spot filmed in Fitzroy? Yes. Do we secretly enjoy being mysterious? Also yes. If you moved here expecting curated vinyl, dim sum theatrics or a seaside surf break with a matching postcode, you’ll be disappointed — you’ll be the kind of person who keeps asking for the Smith Street address.

Who would hate living here: collectors of Instagrammable retail experiences and people who need their suburb to double as a lifestyle blog. Who would love it: anyone who enjoys being the real Happy Valley — quietly existing while everyone else fights over the name. We’ll keep the peace, you keep the confusion, and somewhere a Collingwood shop owner probably raises a terrarium in salute.
