# Mitcham (NSW)

Slug: `mitcham-nsw`

We know the bit — a Saturday morning here starts with gumtrees and a creek, not the CBD roar. We’d wander Yarran Dheran Nature Reserve and still be home in time for a coffee that isn’t pretending to be an identity crisis. Back in the day Mitcham’s cafes and markets were the kind of places you recognised: Miss Polly Café and The Mabel — Tim Campbell and Nicole Arnup’s cosy spot with Jasper Coffee — used to feel like a living room with pastries and actual chairs you could sit in without apologising.

If you liked your nights to come with botanicals and a side of provenance, Dutch Rules Distilling Co. showed up on Whitehorse Road and shook things up — the distillery door opened in March 2024 and the kitchen led by Viveik Vinoharan was doing Sri Lankan‑leaning dishes (yes, there was rigatoni with Lankan‑style pig‑skin and wallaby ragu; yes, they used spent spices and pomelo from distillation). Urban List and Concrete Playground both made a fuss about vinyl DJ nights and gin tastings — apparently Mitcham had “entered the chat,” as Sitchu later noted in 2026.

For the patient shopper among us, Point of Difference Liquor — Roger Sauer’s shop — boasted an eye‑watering stock and Friday night tastings that felt like a small, reassuring ritual. And if you liked your venues with a bit of history, Mitcham Social lived inside a former Thatcher steel factory off Rooks Road, all red brick, a kids’ play area and a pool table, and Sundays that stayed loud and generous.

Local drama? The only thing the snippets confirmed was planning talk: Mitcham was named among suburbs proposed for activity‑centre rezoning, with core heights pencilled up to 12 storeys. We won’t pretend to know the neighbour‑ly debates that followed — the paper clipped the plan, we put it on the fridge and made a round of coffees.

Who would hate living here? People who need permanent neon and a rooftop yoga schedule. Who would love it? Walkers who like a creek at their backdoor, gin nerds who appreciate a distillery’s ego, families who need a kids’ menu and a bit of live music, and anyone who likes a suburb that quietly surprises you. We’re Mitcham: not flashy, decidedly ours, and occasionally very, very clever.
