# Pakenham (NSW)

Slug: `pakenham-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start a Saturday like pros: drop the ankle-biters at the Pakenham Baptist Church playgroup on Bentley Court (yes, the Thursday 10:00 slot actually existed — Playgroup Victoria had the details), then reconvene with the local Social Mums Club — Renee used to run the Pakenham chapter, if you remember those cheerful group emails.

Weekends for us were a polite scramble between craft and chaos. If you wanted to try your hand at something that didn’t involve a ball or a screen, Art Tree Creations was listed at 114 Mulcahy Road as the kind of place to get elbow‑deep in resin and make “arty” mistakes with likeminded locals (Sitchu flagged them in a directory). And when the tots finally surrendered to nap time, a Concrete Playground review once tipped us off that Smiley Brewing Co had been claimed as Pakenham’s first microbrewery — founders Trent and Kylie, a taproom vibe, and the not‑so‑humble crowd‑favourite “Pakenham Draught” were all part of that local legend.

We’ll admit: Pakenham has always had an identity crisis. Time Out was saying big things too — a Costco was slated for Pakenham in 2027 — so bargain hunters were already pencilling us into future road trips. Meanwhile, the internet kept confusing us with Pakenham Street in Fremantle, where Bread In Common and Republic of Fremantle do their very polished things. We laughed, we wept, we updated our Google Maps.

Who would hate living here? People who need a city that never slows down or insists on artisanal gin every hour will be left checking their Uber Eats. Who would love it? Families who actually use the playgroups mapped by Playgroup Victoria and the ParentZone Hub, craft‑curious types who find Art Tree Creations, and anyone who treasures a local beer with a hometown name on the tap list.

Look — we’re not pretending this is the new Fitzroy. But for mums, makers and people who like their breweries to feel like they belong to the postcode, Pakenham’s quietly doing its thing. Back in the day — and for anyone still here now — that’s more than enough.
