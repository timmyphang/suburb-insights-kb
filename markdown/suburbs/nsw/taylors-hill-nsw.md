# Taylors Hill (NSW)

Slug: `taylors-hill-nsw`

We’ve got two certainties in Taylors Hill: one, someone on Swanston Court will let inflatable Santa win the neighbourhood lights-off contest every December; and two, the internet about us is mostly coupon code energy.

As Urban List once noted, the Swanston Court display is a luminous wonderland — animated Santa, Mrs Claus, nativity scene and icicles — on every night from 8:30pm to 10pm. We’ll admit it: we park the car, pretend we’re outraged at the traffic, then stand there like kids while the inflatable sleigh does its thing. It’s our version of a summer pilgrimage.

Weekends used to read like a directory: Mi Mai was listed as the go-to hair and skincare salon in Taylors Hill, so a cheeky facial and a blow-dry used to feel like community service. If we weren’t getting primped, LocalFitness had us pencilled in for trial gym passes — Fernwood Fitness Sydenham was one of the nearby clubs they name-checked, and BFT Truganina sneakily pitched the “community” angle in their ads. And when someone needed a lane, LocalFitness pointed us toward Brimbank Aquatic and Wellness Centre at 90 Taylors Rd, Keilor Downs for a swim.

There isn’t much juicy council soap in the files — mostly listings, reviews and “top 10” style pages — so the local drama, if you can call it that, is existential: are we a patch of Melbourne best known for its Christmas lights and free 24/7 gym trial, or a place with a mysterious online identity crisis? Time Out even once covered a hotel called Taylors Hill — in Kandy, Sri Lanka — which we sometimes use as proof the internet thinks we’re an exotic boutique resort until we remind it of Swanston Court.

Who would hate living here? If you crave constant urban theatre and boutique mystery hotels in your postcode, you’ll be frustrated. Who would love it? Families who rate a good lights display above pretension, bargain gym-goers who live for a free pass, and anyone who appreciates a solid local salon were all well-served in the listings. We’re low-key, well-lit (in December) and annoyingly practical — and frankly, we like it that way.
