# Bardia (NSW)

Slug: `bardia-nsw`

On Saturday morning outside Parkside Espresso we are a tableau: a Lululemon-clad mum balancing an oat flat white, a Cavoodle on a rose-gold leash, and a Google Doc of tradie quotes on her phone. That image is Bardia in full colour — neat hedges, a Subaru Outback wedged into the driveway, and a chorus of polite small talk about nap schedules. 

We do ritual here. At 6:00am half of us are at parkrun in Reserve Road or pretending F45 is fun, while the other half are five minutes late to the same parkrun because someone’s doing a three‑point turn in a Toyota Kluger at the school drop-off. The weekly kerfuffle is cinematic: prams, Gymshark tees, a mum filming a 'dangerous parking' reel, and a dad muttering “it’s all about the kids” between Bunnings sausage sizzles. Our Saturday moves in predictable acts — almond croissant from The Village Green Café, midday sausage sizzle at Bunnings, and a playground playdate with sourdough and a thermos of cordial. 

We love our routines so much we document them. There’s a coordinated school-run playlist, Instagram Stories of perfectly mowed lawns, and a monthly “community meeting” at Parkside where dog etiquette and house prices are quoted in the same breath. Petty local drama is our sport. The village green parking wars produce passive‑aggressive notes, the mums’ Facebook group goes viral at roughly 9:12am every Thursday, and a BBQ-adjacent soccer training dispute once escalated to a spreadsheet, a whiteboard pinned to the café, and one magnificently earnest email to the council. The fence debate on Wattle Crescent ran for five months and set new standards for neighbourly diplomacy. 

We are mortgage-glued and proud of it. We shop Willow & Five for cushions, pop into Aldi in the Subaru Outback, and keep our tradie on speed‑dial like an emergency contact. Our Labradoodle is Instagram‑verified in spirit, if not in followers. 

Who would hate this place? The barefoot, vinyl‑collecting creative who loves silent mornings, kombucha and unstructured time. They will be floored by the leaf blowers, trampoline assemblies and relentless WhatsApp schedules. They will not understand why anyone needs a spreadsheet for a soccer ball. 

But who would love Bardia? Thirty‑something first‑home families who want neighbours who’ll lend a ladder and a lemon slice, who value playdates, parkrun fellowships and reliable tradies. If you like community with a side of curated chaos, you’ll fit right in. Welcome.
