# Revesby (NSW)

Slug: `revesby-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start Saturday the only proper way — pretending we were too busy to cook while quietly celebrating that Revesby had choices. We’d wander Marco Avenue and remember when San San opened at Shop 4, 19–29 Marco Avenue, stacking Mortadella and a Smoky BBQ Schnitty on their daily-baked focaccia. Time Out and Concrete Playground made a bit of a fuss, and we basked in that glow like it was our new town square.

If you were feeling less fancy, there was always Awafi — Broadsheet called it one of Sydney’s oldest and best Lebanese charcoal chicken joints, the one with garlic sauce and tabouli that had been feeding us since that original takeaway in 1998. And for the people who liked their bougatsa sans dairy guilt, the Greek Vegan Bakery supposedly began in Revesby before popping up in Newtown; yes, vegan spanakopita was once part of our origin story.

We’re also the kind of suburb with civic infrastructure that doubles as social life: the Revesby Workers’ Club at 2b Brett St has been the venue for more birthdays and bad decisions than we care to count, and the Crown Hotel’s beer garden has hosted entire seasonal wardrobes. Time Out once reminded the city that we’re not exactly inner‑city delicate.

Petty local drama? We’ve had the lot. Time Out flagged us on the proposed flight paths for the new Western Sydney airport — suddenly everyone’s an aviation expert, arguing about whether we’ll live under a parade of A380s. And there was that car‑yard fire that forced evacuations on Gallipolli and Jellicoe streets — a proper reminder that suburban calm can combust at any time.

Who would hate living here: people who desperately need to live somewhere curated by a lifestyle blogger who never eats charcoal chicken or believes every suburb needs a rooftop cocktail bar. Who would love it here: anyone who prizes a solid sandwich, a honest charcoal chook, a long‑running workers’ club, or the comforting knowledge that our local stories get written up — even if the press calls them “now open” or “local knowledge” in slightly smug tones.

We’re not fancy. We’re Revesby: feeding you, ferrying you through controversy, and quietly doing our thing.
