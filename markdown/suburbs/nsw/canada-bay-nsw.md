# Canada Bay (NSW)

Slug: `canada-bay-nsw`

We’re halfway down the Bay Run at sunrise, dodging the faithful joggers and the one bloke pretending he’s on a training plan — you know the type — and we can already hear the chorus: footsteps, river gulls, and the distant hiss of the Drummoyne Swimming Centre heaters keeping its Olympic-sized saltwater pool cosily at 27°C. This is our unofficial triathlon: run, dip, small-voiced moral victory.

Back in the day most of what was written about us felt like a council press release — upgrades, plans, amenity stats — and that’s still our charm. For years the Parramatta-to-Opera House (or Parramatta-to-CBD, depending who’s talking) 91km walk project kept showing up in headlines with a $4.5 million bow on top for Canada Bay area fixes: shared paths, better lighting, raised crossings and easier access to the Bay Run. We’ll take it. Less chance of tripping on a rogue mangrove root.

We used to file our social calendar under “Bowling then Banquet” thanks to the Canada Bay Club: Concord Bowling Club’s four‑hour barefoot-bowls sessions used to be our Saturday religion, with China-meets-carvery support from Canada Bay Phoenix Chinese and Granata’s brasserie for moral reinforcement. On quieter days we’d reminisce about bakery openings in nearby Wareemba — Martha’s had locals talking — and the occasional Sitchu listing that made us wryly proud of our Hen and Chicken Bay vistas.

Public transport buffs love to tell the story of BRIDJ’s on-demand micro-bus pilot that once routed its hopeful little fleet through our streets, linking Rhodes Waterside, Concord General Hospital, Mortlake, Breakfast Point, Cabarita, Burwood and Strathfield. We liked the idea — tiny buses, big ambitions.

There was also that rezoning proposal being assessed with Burwood Council and our own City of Canada Bay Council — the sort of megadevelopment whisper that gives the suburb something to gossip about when we’re not elbow‑deep in prawns at a park bench. The council did, to its credit, get in on pilot programs like Permit/Plug/Play to make hosting street events less ridiculous. Progress, boringly wholesome.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe morning light on water, community running clubs, or people who take their barefoot bowls very, very seriously — move on. Who would love it? Runners, swimmers, bowlers, people who enjoy living somewhere that looks a touch sleepy on the brochure and a little loved in reality. We’ll keep the path warm for you.
