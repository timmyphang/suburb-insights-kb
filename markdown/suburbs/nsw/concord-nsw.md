# Concord (NSW)

Slug: `concord-nsw`

Back in the day we’d amble down Majors Bay Road like it’s our own little High Street of Very Particular Tastes — stopping by Paperboy on Tennyson Road for Single O, queuing at the family-run Espresso Organica for its French toast baklava, then pretending we weren’t going back for wood-fired pizza at Il Tratto Raro (108–110 Majors Bay Road) later that night. Broadsheet and Time Out used to keep us smug with listings; Sitchu reminded the newcomers we still have parklands, foreshore walks and a handy tilt toward Sydney Olympic Park when we want to feel metropolitan without the madness.

We used to celebrate the arrival of places like The Rusty Rabbit and the new Yum Yum Bakery out of Guildford — because nothing says local pride like a haloumi spring roll served next to very earnest napkin-folding. There was talk, too, of Bubooza’s gravity-defying Lebanese booza and its pistachio-ashta pilgrimage; Concrete Playground and Broadsheet nodded approvingly. Cycleworld stocked our sensible cycling fantasies (Shogun, Giant, Bianchi — yes, we’re that kind of suburb), and The Corner Store at Concord was the cosy answer to “who’s bringing the artisanal meal kits?” — shoutout to Inner West mums Lee, Monika and Brooke for that one.

We also have closure receipts: Ruby’s Fried Chicken made the list once and then didn’t (Concrete Playground marked it closed), and Time Out still remembers Concord Squash Courts — also closed. So if you like a suburb that reinvents a few shopfronts while keeping its biggest casseroles intact, this is your stage.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe wood-fired pizza, Mediterranean menus, stretchy ice‑cream or the hum of a bike shop full of very sensible helmets, Concord will test your patience. Also, people expecting late-night neon — this is not your karaoke arena.

Who would love it? Families who appreciate parklands and old houses that haven’t changed much (looking at you, 1 Alton Avenue — first time offered in 40 years), brunch devotees who miss the days Broadsheet and Time Out could map out a weekend for you, and anyone who thinks haloumi is a personality trait. We’re Concord: politely loud, endlessly edible, and quietly stubborn about our dinner reservations.
