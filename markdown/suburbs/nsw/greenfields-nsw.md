# Greenfields (NSW)

Slug: `greenfields-nsw`

We’ll start with a scene you’ve almost certainly seen on someone else’s holiday grid: us, squinting at the sun because our phones insist on capturing “turquoise meeting cream‑white sand” and we’re trying to look nonchalant while Sitchu’s caption does the heavy lifting. For years travel guides have been telling Sydneysiders that a three‑hour drive south gets you to this slice of Jervis Bay paradise — and Concrete Playground even suggests the White Sands Walk and the Scribbly Gum Track if you fancy pretending you earned the view.

Our weekend routine is embarrassingly simple and therefore perfect. We’ve tied up the esky, walked the White Sands loop, lobbed a polite nod at Hyams (yes, you should see it — we know), then wandered on to the more remote Greenfields Beach and Murrays Beach because the guidebooks insisted we do some exploring. Sea kayaking gets a wholehearted thumbs up from Sitchu; you can pretend you’re an intrepid explorer while your toes are literally reviewing the sand.

There’s no juicy petty local drama in the files — which is either because we’re a peaceful bunch or because the travel writers politely left our politics out of it. So the worst scandal we can offer is that visitors keep calling our “remote” beaches a must‑see on their day trips. Fine. We’ll allow it. Just don’t expect us to clap when someone says “hidden gem” while standing ten metres from the carpark.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t stomach a three‑hour commitment to a weekend, or you believe beaches should be Instagram‑exclusive instead of shared, Greenfields will make you twitch. If you need a latte to locate your dignity, you’ll get grumpy waiting for civilisation.

Who would love it here? Sea‑kayakers, sand fetishists, people who treat a Scribbly Gum Track as legitimate cardio, and anyone who enjoys being told by Time Out or Concrete Playground that they’ve chosen wisely. For years the guidebooks have been whispering our name — we’re happy to keep the secret if you promise to pack out the rubbish and leave the sand pristine.
