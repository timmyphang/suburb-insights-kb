# Penrith (NSW)

Slug: `penrith-nsw`

We like to think Saturdays in Penrith begin with river breeze and a plan that involves at least one thing that could get you vertically active before lunch. Back in the day (and in most of the coverage we’ve got on file — pre‑2024 stuff), we’d mosey along the Great River Walk, eye the Blue Mountains over the Nepean and reminisce about that week the man‑made Penrith beach pulled in more than 10,000 people in its first week. Time Out had the receipts.

Mornings used to look like: a Reuben Hills blend at Henri Marc, or hunting down small‑batch drinkery news — Broadsheet once wrote up Go‑Shu, our bush‑side sake brewery — then deciding between Penrith Whitewater for a kayak fix or a lazy paddle at Cables Wake Park. Concrete Playground kept a running list of the food stops we bragged about, and Sinclair’s turning the Log Cabin into something fancy was the sort of thing that made the locals proud (even if we still call it “the Log Cabin”).

Petty local drama? We’ve got both trophy cabinet and tabloid columns. The Panthers have been doing the heavy lifting in the NRL; Pedestrian celebrated our 48.9°C day like it was a medal. There have also been headlines — the kind that remind you we’re not just river walks and ramen — but we prefer the kind written about stadium upgrades and crowds, not the ugly stuff.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to heat, silence or sport. If you can’t stand the idea of a $309 million stadium being your neighbour (and the construction rumpus that came with promises of a 2027 season), or you faint at crowds on a man‑made beach, Penrith will test your patience.

Who would love it: lovers of outdoorsy weekends, Panthers devotees, people who want the Blue Mountains as a backdrop and a growing hospitality scene — Checho’s and other Mexican‑inspired spots were even getting the nod in the guides — and anyone keen on the audacious: Broadsheet and Time Out have both been talking about Winter Sports World coming to town (real snow, long runs — allegedly aiming for 2027–28). We’re loud, hot and endlessly useful for a long weekend. Come for the river; stay for the plans.
