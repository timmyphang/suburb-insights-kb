# Glebe (NSW)

Slug: `glebe-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed and stumble straight to the schoolyard for Markets at Glebe — a chaotic, charming jungle of stalls where you could buy a vintage T‑shirt next to someone selling empanadas. The markets felt like our town square; Pedestrian.TV made a whole saga of it when the owners started feuding and people got very online about whether it was closed, saved or just confused. Classic Glebe.

Our Saturday routine used to look like this: a wary trot down Glebe Point Road to queue for Brune Bakery croissants (Broadsheet raved about them), detour past The Wedge — which Time Out declared a proper all‑day eatery — and then a friendly squabble over whose favourite is better: Hamsi Taverna, Kim’s Bop, Sushi Oe, Nu’u or No. 92 (Concrete Playground and Broadsheet both made lists; take your pick). If you wanted to detox after all that, Nature’s Energy had a bathhouse on Glebe Point Road for a steam and a sulk about rent prices (Urban List and Inner West Mums noted it).

The petty local drama we’ll admit to loving: the markets ownership soap opera. As Pedestrian.TV pointed out, new owners and OG operators kept telling different stories to their followers. We like our neighbourhood with a side of melodrama — you’d be bored without it.

Who would hate living here? People who crave anonymity — we’re the suburb that remembers your ex’s new haircut and where you once left your bike. Folks who can’t tolerate the bohemian, bookshop‑and‑brewery mix (Sappho Books, Staves Brewery, Galluzzo Fruiterers all made the essential lists) will find us slightly exhausting. Also, if you hate good Sunday roasts you might want to move: the Glebe Hotel’s beef and Guinness pie is basically civic duty to taste (Urban List put it on the roast map).

Who would love it here? Creatives, market‑diggers, matcha devotees who used to linger at Mamuki, parents who rely on local playgroups at Benledi House or Glebe TreeHouse, and anyone who likes their neighbourhood loud, cultured and slightly theatrical. We’ll keep the terrace houses, the heritage brasserie that was reborn as Darling Glebe, and the gossip. Bring your reusable bag and your sarcasm — you’ll fit right in.
