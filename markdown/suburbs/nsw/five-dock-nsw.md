# Five Dock (NSW)

Slug: `five-dock-nsw`

We start our Saturdays like everyone else who knows Five Dock: a slow amble down Great North Road, pretending we're just popping in for one thing and emerging two hours later with pastries and opinions. Back in the day we’d queue at Pasticceria Tamborrino for a Roman-style cannoli and a strong espresso, and Buttercrumbs used to be the constant source of flaky croissants that made you forgive almost anything. Broadsheet even had a hub for us — we’re basically famous for our carbs.

If you wanted to be fancy and quietly impress someone, Izakaya Gaku’s Five Dock outpost was the move: a tiny tatami-style room, kamameshi served in an iron pot with Nagano koshihikari rice and a three‑minute sand‑timer to remind you to practise restraint. Urban List gave the ramen a nod too — we knew about it before it was cool to love boiled rice ceremonially.

We’re a village that spends its money on good things: Raineri’s Continental Delicatessan at 97 Great North Road has always been the cut‑to‑order mecca for cured meats, Filicudi used to do the kind of creamy fettuccine that made you cancel other plans, and Akasha Brewing Co on Spencer Street had a rustic vibe with a rotating food‑truck problem solver. Small Joys Gelato gave us popcorn miso caramel and other flavours someone in marketing labelled “inventive,” and Bamiyan quietly offered Afghan food off the main drag.

Council did their part too — a $464,000 Five Dock Park playground build and a $5.72 million upgrade to Charles Heath Reserve made us smug about accessible slides. Livvi’s Place at Timbrell Park still gets mentioned as the inclusive playground that set standards, and Jungle Buddies used to claim the largest indoor play structure in NSW, so child birthday logistics became a spectator sport.

Who would hate living here? People who crave anonymous, relentless nightlife or who think every suburb should resemble an endless rooftop bar. We like our playgroups, our pasta, our deli queues and our future Five Dock Metro Station — which we were already being told to brace for in planning chat.

Who would love it? Families, pastry obsessives, kamameshi nerds, deli devotees and anyone who thinks a four‑day Ferragosto parade of street eats and live music is a civic necessity. We’re small, loud, and reliably delicious.
