# Glenelg (NSW)

Slug: `glenelg-nsw`

We start our weekend like proper locals — toes in sand, sunglasses pretending we don’t know the forecast. Back in the day the brochures and mags all had the same script: Glenelg was “the beach,” and everyone who mattered had an opinion about our promenade. As Time Out put it, Glenelg Beach was Adelaide’s buzzy seaside spot; as Sitchu reminded readers, you could tram in from the city for a sunset and the Glenelg Sunset Markets.

Our morning ritual used to be apologising to our shoes while queuing at Shadow Baking on Colley Terrace — Broadsheet loved to tell the story of Messina alumni Tom Mitchell, Florian Fritsch and Remi Talbot piling pastries into the hands of carb‑hungry punters. If you missed the brioche rush, there was Dante’s Deli for a quick bite (Broadsheet even name‑checked Hark coffee and Boulangerie 113 pastries), or you could brave the tourist swirl around The Beachouse — the waterslides, dodgems and the Skyline Ferris Wheel that Time Out swore were essential family fuel.

Petty local drama? We’ve had it in headline form: food obsessives arguing over whether Oliveti (the Dolly and Lune team’s seaside thing) was Italian enough, and Urban List once lauding The Glenelg Public House as a steak institution on the Highway — which, yes, made someone bring a candlelit Chesterfield to the discussion for no reason. There are ghost stories too; Pedestrian TV even listed Glenelg among haunted beaches, so if anything’s going bump it’s probably the memory of that one high‑tea power move at Stamford Grand Glenelg’s The Promenade (Urban List’s enthusiastic Mum pick).

Who would hate living here? People who secretly prefer silence, beaches without rides, or anyone allergic to queues and sunset selfies. Also anyone who can’t handle a promenade that’s equal parts family daytrip, wine bar terrace and pastry pilgrimage.

Who would love it? Families who need The Beachouse on speed‑dial, anyone who fancies a European‑style balcony and 140 wines (Ballet Wine Bar got that write‑up), and pastry devotees who remember the Shadow Baking line like a local rite. We’re a seaside suburb that wears its past write‑ups like sun‑cream: a bit shiny, occasionally flaky, but stubbornly proud.
