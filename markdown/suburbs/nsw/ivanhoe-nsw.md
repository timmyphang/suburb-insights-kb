# Ivanhoe (NSW)

Slug: `ivanhoe-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean most of the stories in our pile are retrospective — Ivanhoe was the kind of leafy village people put on wedding invitations. We know the scene: tree‑lined streets, period homes and a shopping strip that Broadsheet once dutifully catalogued (Boca Gelato, The Foreigner, Lip Café, Va Tutto — yes, all the names the guide liked to drop).

Our Saturday usually begins at the Ivanhoe Library & Cultural Hub, where playgroups and the Balance Bike Playgroup used to make the place hum with small, victorious squeals. If we’re feeling aspirational we’d nip to Caffe Strada on Upper Heidelberg Road — Urban List once flagged their gluten‑free jam doughnuts — and then walk off the pastry in the 33 hectares of Darebin Parklands Time Out loved to rave about.

Petty local drama? Oh, we had it. Remember when College Dropout Burgers famously drew a cease‑and‑desist from Kanye West (Junkee archived that delightful chaos)? And more recently All Are Welcome’s Ivanhoe East shut up shop, a Broadsheet item noted — bread lovers mourned. We’re not saying our suburb is boring; we’re saying our scandals come with a side of sourdough.

Who would hate living here: people who can’t stand boutique wellness or being within ten minutes of a spa. Urban List and Time Out splashed pages on Relinque — the sort of place that does steam rooms with ice fountains and facials that require ceremony. If you loathe quiet cafés that take their antipasto seriously, or you prefer your nightlife neon and loud, Ivanhoe will feel eternal and faintly judgmental.

Who would love it: families who like community playgroups and local bookshops (Andrew’s Bookshop has been a stalwart), people who enjoy wines poured at cosy spots like Vinoshis in the heritage redbrick, and anyone who thinks a heritage fire station turned restaurant (Barrio) is peak suburban romance. Baba’s Deli’s focaccia launch used to bring a minor rapture to the north‑east — that sort of thing happens here.

We’re fond, we’re a touch precious, and yes — we’ll quietly measure your worth by how you fold your recyclable.
