# Golden Bay (NSW)

Slug: `golden-bay-nsw`

Back in the day — at least according to the clippings we've scavenged — we measured our weekends in Wainui minutes and Patons Rock sunsets. We know the local geography like we know the best place to park for a beach picnic: the northern end of the Abel Tasman Coast Track sits at Wainui Bay, allegedly a 30‑minute drive from Takaka. We use that figure for everything. Need a coffee? Add ten. Existential crisis? Double it.

A proper Golden Bay morning, as the guides used to tell tourists, involved the easy, well‑maintained stroll to Wainui Falls. The path follows the Wainui River through lush nikau and ferns — The Urban List quite reasonably called it a “must‑do.” We’ll take a wet pair of boots and the mossy chorus over a boutique precinct any day. Later, you’d stretch out at Paton’s Rock Beach — a wide, shallow, dog‑friendly stretch, again blessed by The Urban List — and wonder how the view can still surprise you, even if the tide has seen it all before.

Our petty flex (not a drama, because we’re too kind for drama): apparently our clams earned a passport. Concrete Playground once noted Amano was using clams from Golden Bay, which means somewhere between the Wainui River and the café in Takaka a bivalve achieved international dining status. We’re not jealous. We’re proud, and mildly offended they didn’t invite us.

Who would hate living here? People who think “remote” is a brand deficiency, or anyone allergic to nikau fronds, dog walkers, and having strangers on holiday casually rock up to your view because Patons Rock Airbnbs used to promise “you’re literal steps from the sandy stuff.” Who would love it? Families — Time Out highlighted “Karu ō te Kiwi, Golden Bay” in Vrbo’s 2025 list — and anyone who believes life improves with a river walk, a beach that lets your dog run off leash, and a primary school where playgroup actually happens (shoutout to the Golden Bay Primary School playgroup, as a mum blog once put it).

We’re a little rough around the edges, reliably scenic, and frankly smug about our clams. Come for the tracks and beaches; stay because you’ll never want to be anywhere that isn’t smothered in nikau and good, clean sea air.
