# Corrimal (NSW)

Slug: `corrimal-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean the pages Broadsheet and the local playgroup kept warm — Corrimal was the kind of place you could set your watch by: school pick-up at Princes Hwy, coffee at Hevan Espresso, and a vague promise of coastal adventure a couple of kilometres down the road.

We remember Hevan Espresso opening in 2013; as Broadsheet wrote, it was the go-to spot, pouring Single O and serving a roasted pork belly roll the size of a small apology. Muffins were baked fresh every morning and, for a while, that was the cultural summit of Corrimal. Lili J on Corrimal Street used to feel like someone's sunroom — Broadsheet put it at 156 Corrimal Street with Reuben Hills coffee doing the heavy lifting on the bean front. Concrete Playground even notes Pilgrims had a Corrimal outpost once upon a time, because yes, we have a Pilgrims.

We don’t have fresh press to brag about — the local mentions are mostly archived guides and property listings — so consider this a nostalgia tour rather than a weekend itinerary. Sitchu reminds us that Corrimal sits politely on the Wollongong map, close enough for the Grand Pacific Drive, Wollongong Botanic Garden and Jamberoo Action Park to make us feel cultured or at least mildly adventurous.

Our weekday rhythm is public-school-centric: Corrimal Public School sits on Princes Hwy (entrance on Wilga Street), and the kindy playgroup runs out of the school hall next to the canteen — they even hand out a Magic Lunchbox story and the Billy Backpack transition program if you like your community wrapped in paperwork. A property listing for 6 Whiting Crescent bragged about being about 600m from the school, 1.2km from Corrimal Village shopping and roughly 2km from Bellambi Beach — that’s the local geometry; we measure life in metres and muffin crumbs.

Who would hate it here? If you loathe short walks, old-school cafes that earned their rep in the 2010s, or being within striking distance of a beach and a botanic garden, Corrimal will try your patience.

Who would love it? Families who want a proper community, anyone nostalgic for a pork-belly-roll era café scene, and people who like their scenic drives served with a side of small-town pride. We’ll keep the muffins warm.
