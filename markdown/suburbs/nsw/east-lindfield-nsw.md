# East Lindfield (NSW)

Slug: `east-lindfield-nsw`

We know the moment: fog on the Pacific Highway, the train sighs into Lindfield station and we queue like it’s a royal opening — except it’s just us, and Goodfields Eatery used to be there, basking in that light-filled room opposite the station with its white pressed‑metal ceiling and an oversized macramé screen. That was George and Anthony Karnasiotis’ doing — proof that even our village can be familiarly tasteful.

A slow Saturday for us looks like a circuit: stagger past the Lindfield Bookshop on the Pacific Highway (we still nostalgically say “used to pop in on the way to the train”), maybe stand in a doorway pretending the dim, bunker‑cool Blackout Cafe is the height of metropolitan ennui. We’re fond of the small theatricalities that make Lindfield… Lindfield.

We’re not a suburb that ever pretended to be Sydney’s party cousin. For years the guides have told it straight: we’re on the Upper North Shore, about 13 kilometres from the CBD, officially East Lindfield only since 1994. Our edges are bushy — Lane Cove and Garigal National Parks and the Middle Harbour shoreline are the proper show-offs; we just enjoy the proximity and the smugness that comes with it.

Local “drama”? If you call a debate about whether East Lindfield Park is worth a ten‑minute drive a scandal, then we’re scandalous. Even the mums’ blogs shrugged — “nice but not fantastic” — which is peak local honesty. We do have a Community Hall with a stage, PA, kitchen and an enclosed grass area, so if your idea of excitement is a sausage sizzle and a bad rendition of a musical number, we’ll supply the infrastructure.

Who would hate living here? People who need non‑stop lights, late‑night cocktails and rooftop DJs. Also anyone who thinks parks must be spectacular to be useful — a certain kind of perfectionist will be exasperated.

Who would love it here? Families who like decent local loops, people who take their walks seriously, bookish types and anyone who enjoys good Halloween streets like Adelaide Avenue. Oh — and trivia fans: Elle Macpherson grew up here, so when we brag it’s not entirely fictional. Back in the day we were quietly suburban; and honestly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
