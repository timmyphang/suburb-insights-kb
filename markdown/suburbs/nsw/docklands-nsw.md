# Docklands (NSW)

Slug: `docklands-nsw`

Back in the day we’d stagger down to the waterfront pretending we were on holidays while still within eyeshot of the CBD. We’d promenade along New Quay Promenade, Victoria Promenade and Harbour Esplanade like it was our civic duty, stopping at whatever used-to-be hotspot was open that week.

Our morning ritual used to involve a half-serious pilgrimage to Mill & Bakery at Central Pier for something flaky, maybe a trot past Peddler Espresso Shop at One Melbourne Quarter on Collins Street if we felt fancy, and muttering about how The District Docklands had eaten half our weekend for sales and carparks. Quarterhouse at 693 Collins Street used to promise rooftop long lunches; Prison Island at 440 Docklands Drive used to give us puzzles and the existential threat of being locked in a faux cell for entertainment. Not quite the romance of old docks, but it kept us amused.

The precinct has attracted oddities — who could forget the Channel 9 toilet-money caper from 2011? We’re still not sure whether that was peak Docklands or just Tuesday. The Melbourne Star’s slow exit also left us all slightly bereft of something to tut about while crossing the Bolte Bridge.

For a while there were buzzy openings and experiments: From Here by Mike popped up in the lobby of 1 Hotel, Bobby Stop Stealing Our Camping Chairs turned Southern Cross Station into a chaotic music-and-art café, and the Firelight Festival sprawled across the precinct in July 2025 with flame installations and big illuminated sculptures. TotalFusion’s multi-level wellness hub was on everyone’s radar too — shiny promises of magnesium plunge pools and saunas where we could pretend to be healthier.

Who would hate living here? Anyone after laneway grit, local corner-store lore or unpredictable backstreets — Docklands likes its concrete, its curated events and its skyline vistas. If you need "authentic" and slightly ragged, you’ll be pining for somewhere with more gumtrees and less glass.

Who would love it? People who adore harbour-side walks, festival crowds, and the awkward joy of a precinct that’s still figuring itself out. Families who used the Maternal and Child Health Centre and playgroups found here, food-festival wanderers and anyone keen on shiny new wellness hubs will feel right at home. We’ve got views, events and a sense of ongoing reinvention — and that, frankly, suits us just fine.
