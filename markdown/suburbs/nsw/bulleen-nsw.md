# Bulleen (NSW)

Slug: `bulleen-nsw`

We wake up, shuffle our way down Templestowe Road and pretend the sculpture park is our backyard. Back in the day, the Heide Museum of Modern Art at 7 Templestowe Road was the thing that made Bulleen sound fancy on invitations — a sprawling patch of parkland various outlets described as 15 acres, 16 acres or a very polite 6.5 hectares depending on how cultured you’re trying to sound. We know every path past the Rings of Saturn and which bench catches the best late-afternoon light for pretending to read art theory.

Weekends used to look the same: a mosey through the Sculpture Park, a browse at the Heide Makers’ Market (that lovely monthly thing in the grounds), then a meal at the eatery that Mulberry Group reimagined as Heide Kitchen — Laura Bolton even used to talk about how the menu paid homage to whatever exhibition was haunting the house that day. The museum’s healing gardens and haptic play spaces were the kind of places where you could take the kids and still claim it was cultural enrichment. Our local parenting lore includes the Bulleen Kids Early Learning Centre on Manningham Road — small, boutique and very much doing the important early-childcare bits while we argue about who parked across two lines.

Petty local drama? Oh, we’ve got it: Time Out still smugly reminds people that technically Heide sits in Bulleen even though Heidelberg keeps trying to lay claim like it’s a dodgy aunt pinching the good china. And yes, there was a Gateway Festival at Heide that once got a little Live Music Fund love — don’t tell the neighbours it briefly sounded like a festival town.

Who would hate living here? If your main criteria are a throbbing nightlife, a CBD skyline on your horizon or an aversion to public sculptures, you’ll feel distinctly betrayed. We’re not flashy; we are leafy, slightly precious about our gardens and very committed to calling a 15-acre patch “our bit of culture.”

Who would love it? Families who like big blocks and school-belt bragging rights, people who think art and picnic rugs are a balanced lifestyle, and anyone who enjoys claiming they “popped into Heide” as if it’s a tiny Louvre. For years the local guides have been saying it — we know it, you know it, and between the sculptures and the healing garden, we’ll keep calling it home.
