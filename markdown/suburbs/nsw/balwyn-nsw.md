# Balwyn (NSW)

Slug: `balwyn-nsw`

Back in the day we’d saunter down Whitehorse Road like we owned the place — which, judging by the size of some gardens, we probably do. Our weekend protocol used to read like a boutique tourism brochure: a slow orbit past Snow Pony with its hanging flower baskets (Broadsheet and Time Out both winked at that one), a detour to Meatsmith Balwyn for Gippsland cabinets on display, then a philosophical debate about whether Chai Garden’s grilled Bombay sandwich or paneer sanga was the correct accompaniment to masala chai in their courtyard.

We’re the suburb Time Out calls a hidden café culture and Sitchu dubbed “Melbourne’s eastern ambition written in brick, garden and school‑zone cartography.” Translation: lots of immaculate hedges, very particular school runs and more espresso listings on Broadsheet than actual roadside bins.

Civic life here used to mean choosing which small pleasure to frequent: the Scandi‑leaning Mr Hendricks on Whitehorse Road (we never forgot the coconut porridge review), the gussied‑up Enoteca Boccaccio that Time Out once crowned a bright newcomer above Boccaccio Cellars, or a quiet mat‑class at Light Space Yoga run by that mother‑and‑daughter team. Palace Balwyn has always been our classy alibi — rooftop cinema and bar, if you’re pretending your Friday night is cultural not just convenient.

Petty local drama? The archive doesn’t give us any juicy council feuds, so the only scandal we confess to is classical: which deli will get our loyalty this week. Concrete Playground and Urban List gave us the checklist; we pretend that’s drama enough.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to blue‑chip school catchment bragging, or people who believe “quaint shopping strip” is a synonym for “endless tasteful renovations.” Who would love it: families chasing walkability, parks and those elite schools (Balwyn Primary, Fintona Girls’ School) — and anyone who thinks a rooftop cinema is a perfectly reasonable local brag.

We’re proud, slightly smug and fond of our old‑school delights. If you want the full roast, bring a coffee — or at least the Broadsheet directory.
