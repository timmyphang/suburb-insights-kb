# Seabrook (NSW)

Slug: `seabrook-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start the weekend like proper locals: tiptoe past the hammock on the private deck of the Family Retreat Seabrook — yes, the one Urban List lumped into its “Best Kid-Friendly Melbourne Airbnbs” guide — and try not to wake the smalls before the three bathrooms had finished their morning rotation.

Our mornings have a predictable sort of choreography because Seabrook is, frankly, set up for the tiny humans. The Seabrook Community Centre used to be the heart of that routine: bright function rooms, an enclosed outdoor space and a playset where toddlers learned the fine art of dramatic falls and instant recovery. Playgroup Victoria still kept it listed as handy for neighbouring families from Altona Meadows and Point Cook — you could practically time your coffee by the squeals from the playground.

We’re not saying Seabrook is thrilling — we’re saying it’s reliably practical. There’s no shortage of childcare chatter either; Children’s Lighthouse’s Seabrook pages went into grown-up detail about licensed childcare, preschool and after‑school programs, even their Lighthouse Pathways approach (Confidence, Connection, Continuous Improvement, Curriculum). For serious planners they had FAQs and invites to schedule tours; for the rest of us it was reading blog posts like “Crawling Milestones in Babies” and pretending we lived by a curriculum.

Petty drama? The data isn’t giving us a soap opera — just comforting repetition: playgroups, community centre bookings, and Airbnb descriptions that list cots, toys and space for everyone. If you’re after scandal, you’ll have to bring your own.

Who would hate it here: people who think shoulder-room and communal baby gates are a lifestyle downgrade. If you crave nightlife, boutique eccentricity or guerrilla street art, Seabrook’s family-first planning will feel quaintly bureaucratic.

Who’d love it: anyone who secretly enjoys a suburb that is unapologetically for families. We like that our community centres are bright and booked, that there are proper places to get toddlers safely horizontal, and that somewhere, in a neatly described listing, a hammock waits. It isn’t glamorous — it’s useful, loud and oddly comforting. We wouldn’t have it any other way.
