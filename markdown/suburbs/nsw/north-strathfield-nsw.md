# North Strathfield (NSW)

Slug: `north-strathfield-nsw`

We live where George Street smells like kimchi and ambition. By that we mean: back in the day we queued at Myeong Dong (1–3 George Street) for the 12 banchan and Jeolla‑do‑style bibimbap, then wandered down to where Kosuke Ramen’s honten used to promise Tokyo‑style broths made in‑house. That’s our weekend: carbs, spice, and the faint hope of not getting hit by a flying ping‑pong ball from Kingpin.

Full disclosure: our media feed for North Strathfield is mostly older pieces — so this is affectionate nostalgia, not a neighbourhood lifestyle influencer flex. Still, there’s a lot to remember. Abhi’s on Concord Road (163 Concord Road) was the place the BYO guides liked to namecheck; Pron Prohm quietly relocated here before being reinvented as Ron’s Upstairs with a Mediterranean rotisserie twist; and Jongro Hwaro hung on at 4/11 George Street with generous banchan of its own. Time Out even noted Club9 as our loud, tournament‑grade sports bar — pizzas, darts and pool for people who take pub sport very seriously.

Petty local drama? It was short and juicy: Mister Gee — the burger truck-turned-shop on George Street — was listed as permanently closed. We mourned our sauced chips and milkshakes like they were a local mayor.

Who would hate living here? If you crave the polished, brunch‑and‑aesthetic inner‑city lane‑way life, you’ll sigh at our industrial edges and flashes of suburban kitsch. If binge karaoke, bowling, laser tag and escape rooms make you clutch your pearls, Kingpin’s old offerings and Club9’s competitive shuffleboards aren’t for you.

Who would love it? Families and practical people — playgroups are easy to find via Playgroup NSW and local listings on MumsPages — and food obsessives who chase honest Korean, Indian and ramen spots rather than Instagrammable plates. If you like your nights noisy, your dining unapologetic, and your transport stories full of trial routes (remember Bridj’s on‑demand trials here?), North Strathfield was, and still reads like, a perfectly reasonable place to call home. We’ll keep the banchan warm for you — or at least remember where it used to be.
