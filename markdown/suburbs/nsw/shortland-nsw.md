# Shortland (NSW)

Slug: `shortland-nsw`

We like to start our mornings like proper locals: a brisk stroll along Shortland Esplanade toward Newcastle Beach, pretending we’re tourists who only just discovered 61 Shortland Esplanade is even a thing. Back in the day most of what people wrote about our postcode was from older listings and scans — Sitchu had the property pages (10 Marton Street, 19 Conmurra Circuit), MumsPages kept a rolodex of interior designers and kid‑friendly services, and Shine for Kids returned the warmest headline: Shortland Correctional Centre’s first Child & Parent Day was “an unforgettable success.”

So yes, our public profile is a patchwork of real‑estate listings and community service blurbs rather than relentless brunch columns. We’re fine with that. We’ll take being quietly useful over being the next viral café strip any day. Besides, it gives us time to enjoy the weird flex of sharing a surname with Katie Shortland — Broadsheet and Time Out wrote about her RaRa Ramen adventures in Redfern and Newtown — and smugly inform anyone who asks “no, we’re not related” while plotting to blame our enviable noodle energy on Newcastle sea air.

Petty local drama? If you call property developers and listing crawlers drama, we can gossip about new builds appearing on Sitchu and the way every second conversation turns into “so, have you seen the Conmurra Circuit listing?” Otherwise our biggest soap opera is coordinating visiting days and where to park for the Shortland Correctional Centre family events — Shine for Kids’ pieces made it clear those days matter, which is the sort of drama we’ll happily live with.

Who would hate it here: anyone allergic to community obligations, paperwork and the logistics of family visits; someone who expects a glossy lifestyle feature on every corner. Who would love it: families who need real services (MumsPages’ directory exists for a reason), first‑home buyers tracking Sitchu listings, and anyone who prefers a suburb that shows up in news for meaningful community work rather than curated latte art.

We’re not flashy. We’re useful, occasionally smug about a famous Shortland, and quietly proud of a place that makes room for messy, important life.
