# Mylor (NSW)

Slug: `mylor-nsw`

We roll out of our restored 1850s stone cottage, squint at the mist curling off the orchard and decide whether today is a Villetta Porcini kind of mood or a kids‑and‑crepes kind of mood. Back in the day Broadsheet made a fuss about Andre Ursini’s Lunelli Agriturismo and that tiny, fairytale‑like Villetta Porcini on his 20‑acre Mylor property — so yes, we like to pretend we live in a set from an Italian movie, bees included.

Our weekend loop is predictably small‑town: a wander along the Aldgate Valley Nature Walk — Time Out called it “a nice long journey between Aldgate and Mylor,” where you’ll spot southern brown bandicoots, kangaroos, koalas and echidnas if you’re lucky — then a slow drive past the places guidebooks used to rave about. UrbanList and Sitchu both put Mylor Farm on the map as that 15‑acre farmstay about 25 minutes from Adelaide, all restored stone and creekside charm. Sitchu even mentioned “misty mornings” and “long‑table dinners,” so don’t be surprised when half the suburb shows up for supper.

We’ll admit: our village has been written up a lot — Broadsheet, UrbanList, Sitchu — and most of the good press is from the “older” files, so if you’re looking for a current hipster hotspot guide, you’ll find us nostalgia‑forward. Places like Verté (listed at 240 Strathalbyn Rd by UrbanList) and Cherry Bomb Cafe (240 Strathbalyn Road in Sitchu’s notes) were the kind of spots reviewers described with roaring fireplaces, homemade pies and a small library; the articles are older, so they’re best enjoyed as fond memories rather than a live menu.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t handle quiet that’s actually full of wildlife, mist, and unintentionally poetic beehives — or if you need a tram every five minutes — this isn’t your postcode. Who would love it? Food obsessives (As Broadsheet noted Andre Ursini’s influence), families seeking kid‑friendly farmstay vibes (Netmums and UrbanList flagged Mylor Farm), and anyone who’d happily trade a CBD skyline for a stone cottage, an orchard and a packet of hometown hot cross buns from the bakeries reviewers once adored. We’ll keep the kettle warm.
