# Fletcher (NSW)

Slug: `fletcher-nsw`

We start our Saturdays like most Fletcher locals: a tactical sortie to whichever playground the littlies haven't declared off‑limits yet. We used to head to Loch Ness Park Playground in the Hidden Waters Estate — it was renewed in April 2023 and honestly reads like a small children's festival with 41 activities across two levels, a play tower, slides and natural play bits. There’s even a timber jetty over a pond (not fully fenced, so yes, we all practise our rescue‑face) and a water fountain — but no toilets, and mozzies treat us like an all‑you‑can‑bite buffet, so bring repellent and dignity.

When the big kids want real climbing, we’d push on past the Community Centre to The Sanctuary and the Kurraka Drive Playground; the note in the guide said it’s perfect for energetic older kids with a large climbing frame, which is council‑speak for “hope you like climbing walls.” For a proper bush day we’ve explored Blue Gum Hills Regional Park at Minmi/Fletcher — it was upgraded in May 2024 and comes with mining‑history talking points that make us feel like accidental field historians while the kids make volcanoes out of sand.

If you were here for headline‑grabbing council feuds, celebrity restaurants or a viral cafe controversy, the file on Fletcher gave us community centres, playbook pages and park upgrades instead. No scandal, just service directories: the Maryland Multipurpose Centre was listed as providing a safe, supportive environment for Fletcher and Minmi, with Hunter Community Hub links and a nod to funding by the NSW Department of Communities and Justice. Riveting municipal admin, if that’s your kink.

Who would hate living here? People who count themselves urban nightlife connoisseurs, or anyone who’s offended by the even‑tempered soundtrack of kids, birds and the occasional mozzie. Who would love it? Families, bush explorers, anyone who likes their weekends full of playground engineering feats and local community centres that quietly hold the place together.

We’re proud of our modest, kid‑proofed patch: no slick headlines, just upgraded parks, a sturdy community hub and enough climbing frames to tire out a small army. Welcome to Fletcher — bring repellent, bring snacks, leave your expectations of drama at home.
