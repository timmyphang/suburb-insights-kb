# Glenelg North (NSW)

Slug: `glenelg-north-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed, squint at the sea and stroll down to where Glenelg and Glenelg North pretend to be a single, glamorous beachfront. We still point out the corner of Anzac Highway and Alison Street with a kind of smug nostalgia — that’s where Aasha Shaw’s Organic Corner Store, after six years of Thursday markets at the community centre, finally found a bricks‑and‑mortar home. Progress, but we remember the market tents like a badge.

Weekends play out in very familiar choreography: one parent shepherds the kids to Old Gum Tree Playground on MacFarlane Street — two flying foxes, a climbing dome and a bike track, as the mums’ blogs tell us — while the other considers housing envy fueled by Sitchu listings promising “effortless coastal living” and property just steps from Glenelg Beach and the Patawalonga River. We can still point you to 45 Sandison Terrace where Bobbys opened a more grown‑up outpost — if you’re into that sort of thing, it used to be a thing here.

We don’t have any juicy council feuds to gossip about in the files you gave us — just lots of civic contentment. The suburb’s résumé reads like a holiday brochure: Jetty Road and Holdfast Shores Marina, The Beachouse and that skyline ferris wheel Time Out likes to drone on about, Glenelg Golf Club, Glenlea Tennis Club, Novar Gardens Bowling Club, and schools like Immanuel College and St Leonards Primary within minutes. For families there’s Wiggly Worms play cafe on Anzac Highway and Bluey messy play at the community garden/Kibby Reserve — the digital mums’ ecosystem loves it all.

Who would hate it here? People who secretly long for inner‑city anonymity or can’t handle the constant threat of companionable seaside activity. If you despise promenades, high teas (Stamford Grand Glenelg on Jetty Rd gets a mention in the guides) and the idea of living where weekend life is very much visible, Glenelg North will feel like punishment.

Who would love it? Families, seaside nostalgics, and anyone who likes their day punctuated by markets, playgrounds and the smell of the ocean. We’ll keep loving it — and quietly rolling our eyes at anyone who thinks a ferris wheel is an optional amenity.
