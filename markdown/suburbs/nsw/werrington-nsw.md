# Werrington (NSW)

Slug: `werrington-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start the weekend the same way: a quick circuit of Cuckoo’s Hollow to see who’s nesting on the lake, then a slow drift past The Village Centre on Church Street to check if the playgroup door was open. As the Village Playgroup noted, they ran Parent & Toddler sessions and preschool there — the kind of place that even managed an Ofsted Outstanding in 2022, with Emma Turczyn listed as Playgroup Manager. Proper local flex.

Cuckoo’s Hollow is our town’s tiny wild card: a pocket nature reserve with paths you can actually push a buggy around (mostly), a handful of slim bridges that will make you practise your parallel parking skills with a pushchair, and no dedicated car park so you learn the fine art of “park nearby and hope for the best”. It’s pleasant, unpretentious, and great for picnics if you can resist the urge to Instagram a songbird before it files a complaint.

Community life used to run on bake sales and Bingo nights. The playgroup’s fundraising page reads like a who’s who of modest civic theatre — Disco/Bingo nights, a Christmas Fair, Nearly New Sales and the annual Werrington Scout and Guide Carnival. They even had dates pencilled in for events such as a Nearly New Sale (7 November 2026) and a Carnival (27 June 2026), which tells you the organisers were either optimists or excellent forward planners.

Who would hate living here? People who need a 24/7 latte concierge or a permanent sense of urgency. If you require a dedicated car park for every puddle, or you’re allergic to village fetes and small bridges, this might feel like slow TV. We’re not flashy; we’re the sort of place that measures success in successful fundraising totals and whether the ducklings made it to the other side.

Who would love it? Parents who want a proper village rhythm (the playgroup used to run a 9.30–11am session aimed at the littlies), birdwatchers, bargain hunters who get weak at the knees for Nearly New Sales, and anyone who finds comfort in a community that stages discos and Christmas fairs rather than chasing the next trend. We’re low-key, slightly eccentric, and quietly excellent — just how we like it.
