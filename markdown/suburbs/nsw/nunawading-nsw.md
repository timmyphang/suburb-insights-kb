# Nunawading (NSW)

Slug: `nunawading-nsw`

We like to tell people Nunawading is quiet — then point them to the part of town where we used to queue like it was a festival. Back in the day our Saturday started with a slow shuffle past 372 Whitehorse Road, waving at MoPA Nunawading as parents dragged excited small people toward the art room; by mid-morning we’d be arguing whether the cinnamon‑spiced French toast at Station Street Cafe (yes, the one next to Nunawading station) deserved its cult status.

If you were a weekend warrior you could spend an entire day pretending you were fit: bouldering at Hardrock Nunawading (16–18 Varman Court) for about $12 and feeling very heroic when you didn’t fall off the route; then redeem your muscles in one of the many pools Aqualink Box Hill & Nunawading used to boast — proper 25‑metre lanes, indoor and outdoor options, the kind of place that smells faintly of chlorine and community spirit.

We can’t talk about our foodie phase without mentioning the much‑teased bakery drama: as Broadsheet reported, Jess “Lemon” Liemantara was set to open a bakery at 10 Market Street with beginner‑friendly workshops and viennoiseries. There was also Ilza Izakaya — a 25‑seat, Tokyo‑inspired spot run by a husband‑and‑wife team who had outposts in Docklands and Box Hill South — and the Peddler Cafe, the one that used to get name‑checked for pork belly waffles. Urban List even waved a flag for Mr Robertson Cafe in the industrial estate, pouring Axil Coffee Roasters and pretending we were inner‑city.

Our petty drama? It’s small and very suburban: even rappers had opinions. As Junkee archived, Mog.Y once left a blunt Google review on Nunawading Maccas — because of course he did. We wear that review like a badge.

Who would hate Nunawading? If you need 24/7 neon nightlife or another CBD laneway to Instagram, this wasn’t your postcode — we were more about family festivals at the Whitehorse Civic Centre Precinct (remember the Spring Festival superhero run‑around?) and Australia Day concerts with fireworks on the lawn.

Who would love it? Families who wanted museums with sticky floors, climbers who loved a cheap session at Hardrock, and anyone who once dreamed of learning to temper chocolate at a Market Street bakery. We’ll keep the keys by the door — just don’t ask us to move to the city.
