# Grovedale (NSW)

Slug: `grovedale-nsw`

We roll down Surf Coast Highway like we own the place — not that anyone on the Urban List seemed to notice. Back in the day, if we wanted to stretch our legs and pretend we were on a cultural pilgrimage, we wandered the native trees and walkways at the Narana Aboriginal Cultural Centre at 410 Surf Coast Highway. Concrete Playground described it as a welcoming intro to contemporary Indigenous culture, with gardens, an on-site gallery and, yes, a cafe where you could linger without being Instagram-barked at.

Speaking of cafes, Time Out once wrote about Booln Booln Café — listed at the same 410 Surfcoast Highway address — calling it “more than just a café” and praising its bushland vibe and First Nations flavours. We’ll always tell the same story: there was a place on the highway where the coffee and the country felt like they belonged to the same postcode. That was the kind of thing you bragged about when guests asked why we never moved closer to the city.

Petty local drama? Honestly, our biggest scandal is civic invisibility. As Urban List’s search pages make painfully clear, a search for Grovedale didn’t exactly set anyone aflame — their Sydney and Melbourne hubs churn through thousands of results and, as Urban List itself noted, didn’t surface any pages containing the exact term “Grovedale.” We like to think it’s because we’re quietly excellent, not because we’re forgotten.

Who would hate living here: people who need constant validation from magazine lists or who crave laneway bars and climbing gyms plastered across Time Out headlines. If your weekend priority is being on A-lists, we’ll bore you with our local trees and quiet galleries.

Who would love it: families dreaming of space — Sitchu’s old listing for 9 Meadowvale Drive sold the fantasy of roomy, long-term family life — and anyone curious about country culture and First Nations hospitality. For all our low-key vibes and media neglect, we’ve got good bones: a cultural centre, a café that was hailed for its flavours, and the kind of neighbourhood that doesn’t try too hard. We’ll keep the highway, the trees and the quiet pride. You’re welcome.
