# Waitara (NSW)

Slug: `waitara-nsw`

We start our Saturdays like proper Waitarans: bleary-eyed, arguing over whether the weekend deserves spice, and remembering that for years Super Nash Brothers was the thing that put us on the map — that Nashville-style fried chicken with the unmistakable fiery red batter, the Super Nash Sando and thigh boxes that made afternoons suddenly tasteful. It felt like our spicy little flex, even if the write-ups that sang its praises are from a few years back.

Our morning can still be measured in two things: the memory of that red-hued crunch, and the looming matter of The Plan. For what felt like forever a London practice — Níall McLaughlin Architects — was appointed to design a new cathedral precinct for the Diocese of Broken Bay on a sprawling 7.7-hectare site here. The paperwork promised a campus with a parish hall, pastoral centre, clergy residences, diocesan offices and the existing St Leo’s Catholic College slotted into the mix. Big, bold and impossible to ignore on our skyline, whether you’re into architecture or just annoyed by cranes.

There was a quieter kind of drama, too. The closure of Mavka left a gap in Sydney’s Ukrainian food scene — so much so that Concrete Playground talked about a new Kyiv Social filling that void in Chippendale after Soul Kitchen and Mavka shut their doors. Not exactly the stuff of neighborhood riots, but it did give us something to talk about over lunch.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t handle the idea of a 7.7-hectare redevelopment, if spicy fried chicken is a crime in your household, or if you’re allergic to being part of a community that gets written about in lists and then quietly changes the subject — this isn’t for you.

Who loves it? We do. We like a suburb that can be home to a nationally-noted fried-chicken stop, a sizeable architectural project and a pub that made a Sitchu round-up (the place at 55 Pacific Highway got a nod). Humble, a bit spicy, and quietly capable of surprising you — that’s our Waitara, as we remember it.
