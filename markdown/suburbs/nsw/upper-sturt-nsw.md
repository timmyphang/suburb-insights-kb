# Upper Sturt (NSW)

Slug: `upper-sturt-nsw`

We know this bit of the Hills like the back of our flannel — dusty ute-free corners and all. Back in the day we’d pick a Sunday to potter down Upper Sturt Road and pop into the State Flora Nursery in Belair (Sunday, 10–4, if you liked the ritual), where they grew more than a thousand native grasses, shrubs and trees. You could spend an afternoon pretending you were rescuing the planet, then collapse on a picnic blanket in Belair National Park at the corner of Upper Sturt Road and Queens Jubilee Drive and call it culture.

For years the Sturt Upper Reaches Landcare Group carried on the proper work — since 1995 they’d run practical days and keep a Demonstration Site at 171 Upper Sturt Rd. That’s where we learned to love a suspiciously named seedling, or at least to blame the council when the eucalyptus didn’t behave. Next door on the same strip is the sort of civic heart we adore: the Upper Sturt Soldiers Memorial Hall at 173 Upper Sturt Rd — seating for about 80, accessible toilets, and a monthly management committee meeting where the minutes are treated like a community soap opera.

If you have small people, we used to drop them at the Bush School Playgroup — Thursdays, 9–10:30am at Upper Sturt Primary School (118 Upper Sturt Rd) — and, as Playgroup SA put it, “We love going to Upper Sturt Playgroup!” The primary school even had a veggie garden, volunteers reading to kids and OSHC through Happy Haven, all overseen in the old listings by Mr Adam Blakely. Nostalgia alert: it’s the kind of place where the principal’s name was an acceptable hashtag.

Who would hate living here? Nightlife chasers, anyone who insists their Saturday must include a craft cocktail and valet parking, or people who think the only good community hall is one with a neon sign. Also anyone allergic to gumtrees.

Who’d love it? Plant nerds, landcare obsessives, families who like playgroups and veggie gardens, and people who find happiness in an intimate soldiers’ hall and a good Sunday among native seedlings. We’re biased, obviously — but we know every corner of Upper Sturt Rd, and yes, we do measure our weekends in potting mix and picnic rugs.
