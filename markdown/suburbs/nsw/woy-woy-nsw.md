# Woy Woy (NSW)

Slug: `woy-woy-nsw`

Back in the day we’d stagger down to the waterfront and watch the boats go by — not as a metaphor, literally — because Woy Woy’s whole personality was engineered around fishing and ferry timetables. Broadsheet used to remind people that this place had been a fishing destination for 90 years and was one of the first towns hooked up to Sydney by rail; the tourists came for the catch and stayed for the view. 

If you grew up here you knew the routine: a walk through the market to pick your fish at Woy Woy Fishermen’s Wharf, then a long, lazy lunch on the deck while the Cregan‑Clayton family did what they’d been doing for generations. The Old Pub — the Woy Woy Hotel — got a multimillion-dollar glow-up and suddenly even our booziest local felt photogenic. There was a neon‑lit pizza bar in Colonial Plaza too: Tropicana Social Club, which felt like someone tried to import a holiday vibe and left the sunscreen at home. Young Barons was on Blackwall Rd if you wanted pasta instead of waterfront existentialism.

We weren’t all restaurants and refurbs. The Brisbane Water Cycleway between Gosford and Woy Woy got ranked scenic by Time Out — so yes, the town could be gloriously flat and smugly photogenic at the same time. And for a hot minute the Bay to Bay Running Festival put Woy Woy on someone’s fitness calendar, promising a 2km kids run up to a half marathon and the kind of community fundraising that makes local clubs beam.

Petty drama? Oh, we had our moments — Junkee once quoted a tweet about “leadership… to be settled at Woy Woy Maccas carpark tonight.” A town that can be the setting for national political memes will never be boring.

Who would hate it here: people who need 24/7 inner‑city hustle, or anyone allergic to seafood, wide water views, or the idea that a good night includes a beer garden and a long yarn. Who would love it: families (we had playgroups at Woy Woy Public School and baby sessions at Peninsula Community Centre), weekend runners, cyclists on the scenic trail, and big groups who once booked places like LASCALA HOLIDAY HOUSE for a very loud long weekend.

For years the local scene was quietly doing its thing — equal parts fishing village and accidental destination — and honestly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
