# Edmondson Park (NSW)

Slug: `edmondson-park-nsw`

Back in the day we used to mooch down to Ed Square like it was our own little suburban theme park — the town centre that swaggered in, all alfresco Eat Street and open green space, neatly stitched into the train station so you could arrive like a civic hero and immediately queue for charcoal chicken.

Our Saturday ritual? Drop the kids at the Playgroup NSW session at Ed Square (Level 1, 52 Soldiers Parade, opposite Service NSW) at 10am, wave at the TurkuOz crew over at Edmondson Park Public School on Buchan Avenue, then roam the Eat Street that Broadsheet and Concrete Playground told everyone about: more than 20 cuisines, around 90 retailers, a water play park and a six‑screen Event Cinema. For a while it felt like someone had grafted a mini-city onto our postcode and we were all invited to the opening party.

We can still hear the bass from BaaOinkMoo (BOM) — the retro arcade‑meets‑BBQ on Level 1, 52 Soldiers Parade — where neon, DJs and Soju cocktails tried very hard to convince us we’d been transported to Seoul. And if you were doing late-night life hacks, Frango’s first-ever charcoal chicken drive‑thru at 2074 Camden Valley Way was a proud suburban innovation: piri piri smoke, chilli sauce and the distinct thrill of ordering quality charcoal chicken without leaving the car.

The drama? Not the kind you see on TV, more like the slow, relentless ticky-tack of urban growth. Time Out pointed out that our little patch was slated to nearly double — 17,000 new residents, 3,000 homes across a 30‑hectare Town Centre North precinct by 2029 — which, depending on your patience for cranes, is either thrilling or a touch apocalyptic.

Who would hate it here? People who prefer their suburbs sleepy, their cinemas invisible and their dinner not accompanied by K‑Pop DJs. If you loathe kids’ splash parks, drive‑thrus and retro‑neon BBQs, move on.

Who would love it? Young families (hello, playgroups and iPlay), late‑night chicken pilgrims, food obsessives keen to try 20‑plus cuisines, and anyone who likes their suburbs noisy, new and unapologetically trying. We’re not pretending everything’s vintage; we’re the growth story with a water park and a six‑screen cinema, and honestly — we’re having it.
