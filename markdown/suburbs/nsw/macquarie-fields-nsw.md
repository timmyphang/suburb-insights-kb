# Macquarie Fields (NSW)

Slug: `macquarie-fields-nsw`

Back in the day we’d call a weekend properly started only after a questionable detour to Simmo’s Beach — yes, Simmo’s Beach Macquarie Fields — that inland “beach” the Urban List cheekily slapped on the swimming-bucket-list. Unsupervised, a bit wild, and genuinely refreshing if you like your water with a side of bushland and NSW National Parks alerts.

Our morning ritual was simpler than the suburbs further east: someone packs the esky, someone argues about which park has the least graffiti, and someone else pretends Macquarie Fields SkatePark is cutting-edge. Time Out called it “a little lacklustre – all grey concrete – but this park has some decent-sized banks and humps. And lights!” and honestly, that’s peak local honesty.

Then there’s the train station, our unofficial stage for melodrama. Time Out reported the figure people claim to see — “a girl or woman dressed in white, screaming or sobbing on the tracks” — and Junkee gleefully pointed out how the media went bonkers over our haunted platform. We’ve watched the ghost story grow from a late-night dare into mainstream clickbait and, in the meantime, kept lobbying for practical upgrades: Time Out even listed Macquarie Fields Station in the accessibility package, promising lifts, a footbridge and more after years of community campaigning.

Petty local drama? Apart from the occasional headline, our real disputes are about community things: the Leisure Centre upgrades that Western Sydney Mums cheered for (new pools, splash pads and better gyms), the Friday playgroup at Glenquarie Library on 12 Brooks Street, and whether the indoor sports centre on Fields Road still remembers our kids’ soccer shin pads.

Who would hate living here? If you need artisanal everything, constant nightlife and a skyline view, you’ll be disappointed. Who would love it? Families, folk who value practical upgrades like better pools and lifts, and anyone who enjoys a messy, real suburban community — with a haunted train station story on the side.

We’re proud of our half-concrete skatepark, our inland beach, and our noisy, improving local services (hello, Ignite Early Learning Centre on Fourth Avenue and Macquarie Fields High School on Harold Street). Come for the Simmo’s Beach swim, stay for the legends.
