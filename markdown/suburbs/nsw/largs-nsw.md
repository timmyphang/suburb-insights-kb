# Largs (NSW)

Slug: `largs-nsw`

We’re from Largs, NSW, but before you tsk us for being quaint, know this: when we went looking for local gossip the internet kept pointing to other Largs. Back in the day Broadsheet wrote about Queenies Store on Jetty Road in Largs Bay — a pizza-and-tapas spot turned daytime cafe — and Time Out made a fuss about Le Room being the go-to gift shop for stylistas. So yes, the world is confused, but we’re not offended. We just like the idea of being accidentally famous.

Our weekend routine (in theory): we imagine an Esplanade where someone once offered a happy hour listing at 198 Esplanade (Sitchu, remember them?), and where the Largs Pier Hotel was lauded as the sensible next stop after a bracing beach walk — oversized armchairs, a roaring fire and seasonal menus. That’s the vibe we sign up for, even if the reviews are about Largs Bay rather than our postcode.

There’s not much local tabloid drama to rehearse — the public stuff we found is mostly civic and useful: a Fire Service station at 2 Willochra St, Largs North (SAcommunity), an RSL sub-branch at 17 Carnarvon Tce, and the Largs Bay Health Hub’s class passes and “ALL ACCESS” memberships floating around on Momence. It’s a suburb that’s more into committees and membership tiers than feuds, which, frankly, suits us.

Who would hate living here? People who need constant reopening announcements and the latest city restaurant drop in their feed. Concrete Playground is busy with Chapel Street and Fitzroy energy; that’s not our pace. We’re low-on-hype, high-on-quiet-coastal-pride.

Who would love it? Investors, apparently — Sitchu pointed out Largs Bay returned capital gains of almost 18% in a year, with a median home price around $653,000 and rental yield just shy of 4%. And anyone who adores a good local tradition: gift shops like Le Room used to draw crowds from across Adelaide, the Pier Hotel was the winter cosy pick, and family homes on streets like Wandilla (remember that three-bed listing?) make it feel properly residential.

So yes, we’re a little anonymous online. We’ll keep our beachfront armchairs warm and our community groups organised — and if the rest of the internet wants to swap our postcode with someone else’s, fine. We’ll keep the kettle on.
