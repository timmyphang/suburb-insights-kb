# Sunshine North (NSW)

Slug: `sunshine-north-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean the write‑ups that predate 2024 — Sunshine North read like a travel brochure typed up between a sausage sizzle and a poker night. We know this place; we can tell you where the dodgem cars went to retire and which family still makes the best chilenitos.

Mornings used to start with the idea of Persian breakfast at Cafe Sunshine & Salamatea, run by Hamed Allahyari and Jen Morillas — Broadsheet wrote up their date omelette (khorma), ghormeh sabzi and house teas (sour‑cherry and saffron), and even praised their zero‑waste habits. Later you could swing by Panaderia Sud Americana on 76 Warwick Rd, where the Segovia family were busy turning out rosa con chicharrón, cheese empanadas and Chilean chilenitos. If you preferred raspberries to pastries, Sunny Creek Organic — the Rowe family farm — had pick‑your‑own raspberries, gooseberries, chestnuts and persimmons when season allowed.

We also remember the nights: Double Deuce on 264 McIntyre Road, all rockabilly petrol signs, vintage vehicles and billiards; and the Westend Market Hotel doing the reliable rotation of poker nights, Saturday sausage sizzles and monthly rock'n'roll gigs. Time Out even called the wider area a multicultural hub centred on Hampshire Road — which, frankly, feels about right.

The local drama we actually had was literal drama — a big one. Broadsheet reported a massive blaze at the Fun City go‑kart complex on Ballarat Road: more than 80 firefighters, over 30 trucks, the complex “totally involved” and a town watching smoke stack up like a poorly aligned flag. Nothing like a community bonding moment when the fire brigade turns up bigger than the town’s collective appetite for a kebab.

Who would hate living here? Folks who demand immaculate inner‑city pretence and restaurants that rename things more than they serve them. If you want that, you’ll get bored. Who would love it? Families, weekend pick‑your‑own teams, people who love to eat well without a side of pretense, and anyone with a soft spot for retro bars and honest sausage sizzles. For years the local listings told that story — we’re just the ones still telling it with a chuckle.
