# Roselands (NSW)

Slug: `roselands-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean everything we could find in the archives of Broadsheet, Urban List and Concrete Playground — Roselands made a quiet, very specific kind of claim to fame. There’s a brick warehouse on Chapel Street (67 Chapel Street, if you’re the kind of person who bookmarks addresses) where Duo Duo set up a retail counter before the whole taro-glazed-doughnut-and-pandan-deep-fried-ice-cream circus graduated to a polished Strathfield flagship. Kuki even spun out of that story, if you remember reading about soft‑serve and cookies in Darling Square.

Our weekend vignette, then, is less brunch-table flex and more industrial-chic nostalgia: imagine the ghost of a food truck pulling up outside Chapel Street, the smell of something gloriously sugary wafting past 93 Chapel Street (where Chebbo’s Burgers made its name as a much‑loved smashed burger spot) and a dossier of duonuts on your phone because, yes, we had duonuts before it was a lifestyle choice. Urban List and Concrete Playground both nodded to these moments — we’re not making this up.

Petty local twist: the place that helped launch a tiny dessert empire ended up with the shiny flagship across the river in Strathfield. We’ll let that simmer on your conscience.

Who would hate living here? People who need their suburbs to be famous for one single, consistent brand identity — because here you get industrial warehouse origins, viral burger energy (Chebbo), and gyros courtesy of Little Greece tucked into the same postcode. Also, if you loathe practical weekend logistics, know that Concrete Playground once reminded us shuttle buses run from Roselands to the Ramadan Night Markets — so we’re inconveniently useful.

Who would love it here? Families. MumsPages lists the usual suspects — gymnastics classes (think Le Ray Gymnastics Academy), occupational-therapy and cake-maker directories, and Roselands Community Nursery & Playschool along with Roselands Primary School’s Around the Clock Club. We’re a suburb that quietly does the things families need, then produces a deep-fried dessert that ends up in a glossy guide.

So yes, we’re not Bondi. We’re Chapel Street, 67 and 93, a few duonuts, a burger with cult status, and the kind of suburb that ages into charm rather than hype. We like it that way.
