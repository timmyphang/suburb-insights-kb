# Christies Beach (NSW)

Slug: `christies-beach-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean the bits the papers wrote about between 2024 and 2026 — Christies Beach has been quietly doing its own thing while Adelaide proper fusses over lane closures. We’re the people who get sand in our shoes and treat the red sandstone cliffs like local art.

Mornings here are a particular ritual: we used to clock the sunrise from The Esplanade while the Christies Beach Surf Life Saving Club kept an eye on the water (their listings noted beach patrols on weekends and public holidays each season, and the Nippers are a proper thing). If we’re feeling smug about our cardio, we’ll mention the Witton Bluff Base Trail — Time Out wrote about it joining Christies Beach to Port Noarlunga with that 1.1‑kilometre elevated boardwalk when stages opened through 2024–25 — and yes, we walked it and took the photos, thanks.

For visitors who liked to pitch a tent and not feel judged, the Christies Beach Tourist Park at 39 Sydney Crescent was the boutique option in listings: camp kitchen, pedal carts, the lot — Sitchu and SAcommunity had the full spec. And when we needed to usefully organise ourselves, the Christies Beach Community Group and the Sports & Social Club at Bice Oval, Christie Ave, turned up in the directories doing the small‑town admin stuff that keeps a place human.

We’d be remiss not to mention Viscous Coffee on Beach Road — Broadsheet gave the iced concoction 'The Asskicker' a column for its sheer audacity and caffeine workrate. As Broadsheet noted, it was a thing people talked about, responsibly sold and slightly terrified by.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe sea air, coastal cliffs, weekend Nippers, and the idea that someone in your postcode can walk a scenic 1.1‑kilometre trail and come back still smelling of sunscreen, you’ll be miserable. If you need central‑city prestige, move on.

Who would love it? Families who want clean sand and community clubs, campers who want a friendly park by the water, mums who’ve used Keeping Mum sessions at The SAFE Centre on Fowey Street, and anyone who likes a coastline that’s slightly less selfie‑congested than the city. We’ll keep the sand, the surf, and the quiet smugness — you bring the sunscreen.
