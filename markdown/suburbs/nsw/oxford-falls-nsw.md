# Oxford Falls (NSW)

Slug: `oxford-falls-nsw`

You know you live in Oxford Falls when your morning commute doubles as a ghost story and a gardening column.

We start the day like locals do: half of us used to drag prams to The Falls Cafe and Play at C3 SYD, 2 Dreadnought Road — North Shore Mums even recommended it for mothers’ groups — until it announced it will permanently close after Saturday 9 May. So now our “meet-at-the-cafe” habits are in mourning and our playdate logistics are officially a group project.

If you like your suburb low-key but with character, we have that in spades. Broadsheet once noted Valley Ranch Succulents — a third‑generation nursery established in the 1960s — so yes, there’s a pedigree of people who talk about ferns like they’re neighbours. St Pius X College’s playing fields are tucked in here too, the old guides saying they’re about a 20‑minute bus ride from Chatswood, and Oxford Falls Grammar was described as set on beautiful green acreage at 1078 Oxford Falls Road. Rural vibes, minus the sheep.

We also have live theatre: nature edition. Pedestrian TV reported a bushfire on Meatworks Avenue that was later downgraded, and they’ve written about Wakehurst Parkway’s haunted rep — apparently the pedestrian crossing at Oxford Falls once flashed its green man at 3am with no one around. It’s comforting to know our streetlights have dramatic tendencies.

If you need practical help, directories like MumsPages list handymen, plumbers, irrigation services, school‑holiday activities and family support — which is to say, if your houseplant collection fails you or your child needs something to do while The Falls calls it quits, there’s a roster of real‑world help.

Who would hate living here? If you need a glossy high‑street with endless cafés and curated brunch photography, you’ll find our quiet green acreage and disappearing cafés deeply inconvenient. Who’d love it? Families who prize space, mums’ groups who actually used to meet in the play café, and anyone who enjoys telling guests their local crossing once ghosted them at 3am.

We’ll keep the plants, the playing fields and the supernatural streetlights. The café’s gone, but Oxford Falls will still be stubbornly our kind of sleepy, slightly spooky paradise.
