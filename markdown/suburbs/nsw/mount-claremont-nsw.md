# Mount Claremont (NSW)

Slug: `mount-claremont-nsw`

Back in the day — and by “back in the day” we mean everything we’ve got in the archives — Saturdays in Mount Claremont were a very specific ritual. We’d wobble down to the Mount Claremont Farmers Market at 7:30am, queue for bacon-and-egg rolls or crepes from one of the 45-ish stalls, and watch Filament Cold Brew Coffee do its nitro thing while the dog walkers circled the oval like it was the centre of the universe. The market ran until 11:30am, which, frankly, felt like a reasonable amount of civic commitment.

Our weekend walk usually takes us across Mount Claremont oval and on to Lake Claremont — the kind of leafy circuit Sitchu proudly name-checks when someone wants to sell you a “neat & tidy” villa on Alfred Road. If you’re into pottery (and who isn’t pretending not to be?), Potteroo used to be the place we’d nudge friends toward: workshops, exhibitions and the odd kids’ class, all under a name that nods to the Gilbert’s Potoroo. Culture, but with clay under your nails.

We had small triumphs and small losses. Deli Chicchi was the deli-cafe everyone cited for its bacon-and-egg sandwich and pumpkin waffles — it even got a liquor licence, because why not pair seasonal brekky with a cheeky wine? Typika Artisan Roasters used to be the house-roasted-beans hero of the suburb’s breakfast scene before it was later marked Permanently Closed on Broadsheet. Nearby Claremont even saw Avalon open inside The Grove Residences, because you can never have too many Mediterranean white-tablecloth fantasies within driving distance.

Who would hate living here? If your vibe is “non-stop nightlife,” “late-night ramen on every corner,” or “I require a 24/7 espresso crawl,” Mount Claremont will leave you yawning. We’re quiet, leafy and stubborn about our weekend routines.

Who would love it? Families, dog people, pottery students, morning market devotees and anyone who thinks an actual oval that connects to a lake is a selling point. We’re understated, green and slightly smug about it — but we’ll share a market sausage sizzle if you bring the napkins.
