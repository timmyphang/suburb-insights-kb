# Carine (NSW)

Slug: `carine-nsw`

Saturday morning in Carine feels like someone hit slow‑motion on suburbia and added a baby‑pink accent wall. We used to shuffle into Regular Coffee Company — the little spot Jacob Chapman opened with his mum Ann‑Marie — because where else would we get Twin Peaks beans, Teassential tea and a cafe that bragged about an RO water system like it was a personality trait?

Back in the day the all‑day menu read like suburban comfort gospel: smashed avo, pancakes and even a brisket burger if we were feeling wild. Urban List loved the place for its light, plants and that baby‑pink wall, and honestly, it made our post‑school‑run selfies look intentional. There were even talky plans to roast and expand; ambition tasted like espresso shots and future press releases.

For our midweek social lives, The Carine was the nearest thing to a proper outing — Time Out once listed an Oktoberfest there and even Tim Rogers (You Am I) played a gig for the memory lane crowd. It’s not Coachella, but when The Carine had a night on you could count on midweek laughs and drink specials to keep us entertained without crossing the river.

We also had our quieter indulgences. Ember Bathhouse popped up on lists of Perth’s best day spas — a place where phones weren’t allowed in communal bathing because silence is apparently the new luxury. And when Christmas rolled around, Tom’s Pines in Carine was the family‑run tree option for people who still liked the smell of pine more than social media content.

On the more earnest side of town, Carine Robin of The Montessori Family reminded us that this place produces proper educators too; she’s written about the absorbent mind and blending yoga with early years learning — so yes, we’re practical and slightly smug about our parenting credentials.

Who would hate it here? If you need late‑night neon and a global street food crawl at 2am, you’ll find us quaint and infuriating. Who would love it? Families after calm routines, people who prize a decent local coffee (or once did), spa devotees and anyone who enjoys a reliably lovely, low‑pressure suburb with a weirdly proud pink wall. We know our lane — and we’ll keep it.
