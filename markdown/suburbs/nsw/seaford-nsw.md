# Seaford (NSW)

Slug: `seaford-nsw`

We love Seaford, even if the internet mostly fell asleep on the job and only sent us dusty press clippings. For years the archives tell the tale: a seaside suburb where you can either be very calm or very hangry, depending on whether Semla the Bakery and Gelato UNOU are still handing out consolation buns and scoops. Time Out once nodded at the Edithvale–Seaford Wetlands Trail as one of the country’s scenic bike rides — so yes, our morning runs come with birdwatching and the smug satisfaction of not being stuck in the city.

A weekend for us used to start (and this is strictly historical — the listings are older) with a wander past 56 Eel Race Road and the neat rows at Ocean 200, then a detour to where Red Gum At Home reportedly set up shop at 21 Rutherford Road and 38 South Bar and Café quietly did its brunch-by-day, tapas-by-night pivot on 1/131 Nepean Highway. Mr Banks Brewing Co got a name-check in nearby guides (12 High-Tech Pl) for when you needed an IPA and an excuse to stay a little too long.

Community life, as the older listings show, was never far away: the Belvedere Community Centre hosted classes and the Seaford Community Centre on Beechwood Grove ran playgroups, while the Seaford Recreation Centre welcomed the curious at the Five and Dime Kids Market every first Sunday (620 Grand Boulevard — gold-coin entry, apparently). Adelaide’s mum groups even mentioned LEGO Club at Seaford Library, so you can imagine the level of organised chaos at 3:30pm on a school day.

Petty local drama? The files are mercifully light — mostly property listings and bakery raves — so we’ll invent nothing. If you hate slow mornings, wetlands, and the faint possibility of artisan cardamom buns, you’ll despise living here. If you love a suburb that quietly balances beer halls, coastal bike trails and community markets — and can forgive an archive that hasn’t been asked for an update in a while — you’ll fit right in. Back in the day, Seaford was quietly doing its thing. We still are, even if the internet took a nap telling the story.
