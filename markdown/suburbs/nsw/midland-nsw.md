# Midland (NSW)

Slug: `midland-nsw`

Midland, NSW — back in the day the internet mostly forgot we existed, so we made our own myth. We’ll admit it: when we scroll news clippings about “Midland” we’re usually reading about someone else’s weekend — Crooked Spire Coffee House pouring Veneziano and showing murals by Brenton See, Steve Brown and Ariel Katzir; Syd & Phyllis cooking seasonal WA produce at the redeveloped Railway Workshops; or a festival called Midlandia putting acrobats and comedians on our imagined main street.

Our morning routine is therefore a bit surreal. We brew whatever’s in the pantry and pretend Broadsheet has finally noticed us, only to learn they were talking about peanut-butter brownies that vanish in Perth. Urban List dazzles with a shiny METRONET Midland Station — 800+ bay car park, 12-stand bus interchange and a preserved clock — and we applaud from the couch. Then Sitchu sends us regional dining notes about Bar Midland and Time Out brags about a Big4 tourist park next to John Forrest National Park. We nod, sip, and remind ourselves someone else gets the murals and the cider trail.

There’s no juicy local soap in the archive to roast — no council scandals, no barista betrayals — just a long, confusing catalogue of other Midlands: a Taco Bell launch with a Handbrake mural, Last Slice at 226 Great Eastern Hwy being Perth’s cheeky sports-pizza joint, Highland Bakery’s sausage rolls, JD Penangke’s work on Midland Gate. So our petty drama is existential: we’re furious at being the name people misplace on their maps.

Who would hate living here? People who measure a suburb’s worth by how many things it has been Instagrammed for. They’ll be cross that our press clippings mostly point to Perth, Wellington and the occasional US mums’ club.

Who would love it here? Anybody who likes being delightfully anonymous, who prefers to be the local no travel blog can quite pin down. We’re the Midland that doesn’t need Yelp to tell us we’re fine. If you enjoy being underrated, we’ll take you in — we’ll even show you how to pretend the murals, festivals and sausage rolls were always ours.
