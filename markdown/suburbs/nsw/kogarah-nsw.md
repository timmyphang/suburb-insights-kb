# Kogarah (NSW)

Slug: `kogarah-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start a Saturday in Kogarah like it was our civic duty: queue at Pino’s Dolce Vita on President Avenue for cannoli (yes, Pino’s rebuilt after that fire and we still pretended it was the same one our nonna loved), duck into Sugarloaf Patisserie for churros, then argue about whether Panlasang Pinoy on Regent Street did the best weekend barbecue. It was a compact food tour you could finish before the buses remembered to turn up.

We loved how Kogarah could swing from artisan deli to full-on boodle fight in one block — Filipino Fiesta’s grocer-restaurant was famous for halo-halo and those communal feasts that, as Broadsheet noted, started at around $25. And if you were feeling avant-garde there was always a food truck twist: French Street Tacos & Crepes popped up in coverage as a memorable oddball, and MLK Deli tried to tempt us with its Risky Brisket in Kogarah Bay.

Local life kept us entertained in small, very specific ways. Concrete Playground staged the Uncontained Arts Festival down Belgrave Street — immersive lights, shipping-container artworks and food trucks — and for a weekend the town transformed into something performative and slightly smug. Then there were the headline-ready moments: Kogarah Bakery publicly calling out a Daily Telegraph report it said was false, three escaped horses first spotted near Kogarah (yes, horses), and that one regrettable redback bite on Civic Avenue that made Pedestrian TV’s news feed for all the wrong reasons. Only in Kogarah do your public art and your tabloid moments coexist so harmoniously.

Who would hate it here? People who can’t cope with a suburb that’s content being small but interesting: lovers of beige sameness, or anyone who gets flustered by pop-up culture, weekend festivals and the occasional runaway horse sighting.

Who would love it? Families who want community — Kogarah Community Services at 49 English Street has been doing the heavy lifting since 1975 — food obsessives who will happily debate Pino’s versus the bakery, and anyone who enjoys a neighbourhood that’s equal parts old-school deli, Filipino feasts and mildly chaotic local news. We’re not trying to be cool. We just are — and we’ll eat you out of house and home while we tell you the story.
