# Edithvale (NSW)

Slug: `edithvale-nsw`

We wake to sand in our shoes and the comforting knowledge that nobody has invented a better excuse to leave the city than Edithvale. Back in the day the guidebooks made a fuss: Urban List put Edithvale Beach on its best‑beaches list (the piece even recommends the dunes and paddle‑boarding), and The Esplanade was one of the locales listed for those cheerful “Santa Beach Photos Melbourne” sessions. We’ll never tell you how many questionable reindeer poses we endured.

Mornings used to look like this: a quick sweep down Nepean Highway to 265 Nepean Highway where Soul Press earned a mention in Broadsheet for its cold‑pressed routine — smoothies, pressed juices and the kind of chia puddings you’d post about if you had a good camera. Saturday errands might finish at Edithvale General Store, which, since it opened in September 2019, was doing double duty as a milk‑bar‑style community hub selling slushies, staples and all‑day brunch in the same breath. That was the era of local small wins.

There’s no tabloid feud to report — just facts: Time Out kept pointing us at the Edithvale‑Seaford Wetlands Trail as one of Australia’s top scenic bike rides, and Concrete Playground lumped us into Kingston’s 13‑kilometre coastal playground. The local mum directories listed pre‑school options and party venues like they were stocking shelves with optimism; Chelsea Heights Community Centre even ran more than 30 activities and, as their site showed, had a Term 2 2026 timetable up once upon a time. Nostalgia, not scandal.

Who’d hate it here? Nightlife seekers who insist on 2am cocktails and neon, or anyone allergic to dunes, white sand and people who actually use bike trails for their intended purpose. Who’d love it? Families, weekend cyclists, paddle‑boarders and anyone who likes their beach with a side of community hub — the kind that used to be written up in Broadsheet and Urban List.

We’re proud of our low‑key coast. For years the local pages sang our praises; we kept the sand, the walks and the small victories. Come for the calm, stay for the sunset — and if you must, get your Santa photo on The Esplanade.
