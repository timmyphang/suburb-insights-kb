# Kealba (NSW)

Slug: `kealba-nsw`

We wake up on a Saturday and, honestly, our suburb’s vibe is already decided: someone’s in spangly leotards, someone’s lugging a foam pit (yes, a foam pit), and the noticeboard has more drama than the footy club ever did. Back in the day — because all the write-ups we’ve got are older listings — Gymnastics Unlimited’s 20‑year celebration was the kind of thing that told you everything about Kealba: family-first, community-heavy and mildly covered in chalk.

Our weekend routine used to be unapologetically simple. Drop the kids at a Gymnastics Unlimited event (trampolines, pro coaches — the works), swing past the Kealba Community Notice Board online to see who’s running bake sales or playgroups, and scroll MumsPages to compare local classes and health services. If you were feeling crafty, you’d find the Melbourne Crochet Club listed among the usual suspects, because yes, we stitch in public and we’re proud of it. Even the Melbourne Shopping Centres guides made sure parents knew where the cheapest spring school-holiday distractions were hiding.

Petty local drama? There isn’t any headline-grabbing scandal in our files — mostly just the gentle, persistent hum of community organisation. The squabbles live where the data says they do: on Find It Locally posts and the community noticeboards, debating who’s running which kids’ class and which school-holiday event made the “hot or not” cut. Riveting stuff, if your idea of high art is a well-organised playdate.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t stand a suburb that’s officially all about “classes and activities for kids”, community directories and event listings, then Kealba will feel like a long series of PTA meetings. If silence and zero schedules are your thing, move along.

Who’d love it? Parents hunting for local programs, craft lovers who want a crochet circle that actually updates its events, and anyone who measures a good Sunday by how many trampolines are in use. For us, Kealba’s charm is exactly what the listings promise: small, busy, and fiercely organised — and proud of every foam block.
