# Waterloo (NSW)

Slug: `waterloo-nsw`

We like to start the day like locals used to: stand on Bourke Street with a dubious half-formed plan and let Waterloo decide our fate. Back in the day the big warehouses on Danks Street were where your mate fixed a van; then they were the places we raided for dinner — ŌRA’s revolving-door sushi raw bar, Mezzanino’s giant Italian room, Allegra Dining — all showed up and made the old industrial bits feel strangely photogenic. 

If you remember the yellowfin tuna cheeseburger from Josh Niland’s Fish Butchery on Bourke Street, you know what I mean — we chewed through trend cycles in one walk down the strip. OzHarvest even opened a “take what you need, give if you can” market on Wellington Street, which put the “neighbourhood” back in neighbourhood. And yes, the shiny Waterloo Metro station and Metro Quarter rolled in, got architecture awards, and quietly changed how we judged a suburb’s worth by how fast we could trolley home from the city.

There’s a tug‑of‑war vibe here that we secretly love. Sitchu used to call it “juxtapositions” — boutique design stores and galleries rubbing shoulders with the estate that motivated the We Live Here neon window project. The Waterloo Estate renewal and Waterloo South plans promised thousands of new homes and a lot of talk about social and affordable housing; it’s the sort of big, slow rewrite of a place that gives you plenty to gossip about while you wait for your train.

Who would hate living here? If you require absolute calm, no cranes in your skyline and a zero-percent chance of bumping into someone carrying a skateboard at Fernside we’ll say upfront: this isn’t your suburb. If you hate choice, you’ll grumble at the options — from omakase to Italian to whatever pastry experiment landed from Pastry Club back when we were still pretending to be surprised by a croissant‑infused iced latte.

Who would love it? Students, young professionals, food obsessives and anyone who enjoys living in a place that still remembers its edges even as it gets polished. We’ll take the contrast, the markets, the sushi bars and the skate park over beige any day — Waterloo has always been messy, loud and deliciously ours.
