# Kings Park (NSW)

Slug: `kings-park-nsw`

We wake up on a Saturday like everyone else here — esky half-packed, shoes still sandy from last week’s run — and head straight for Pines Picnic Area because yes, the view over the Swan River still makes us feel like we live in the cinema finale of a tourism ad. Back in the day (all our intel’s from the pre-2024 guides, FYI), Kings Park was crowed a national heavyweight: Time Out reminded everyone it’s a 399.9-hectare oasis with 3,000-odd WA-native species, and Tripadvisor named the Botanic Garden Australia’s top attraction in 2024. We still like saying that out loud.

Morning routine: a loop that passes Mount Street, Jacobs Ladder, the Kokoda Track and the DNA Tower — you know, the seven-kilometre-ish torture-tour runners love — then a gentle cool-down on Saw Avenue Picnic Area while the kids reanimate themselves on the Ivey Watson Playground or Lycopod Island at May Drive Parkland. Urban List used to point to Moonlight Cinema at May Drive as the place where beanbags and bad decisions met the skyline; Concrete Playground listed the 2024–25 season too, so our summer evenings have historically involved a blanket and someone crying during a romcom.

We’ve played host to theatrical lighting (Lightscape’s bug-eyes and glowing trees), and in festival years Spilt Milk set up shop among the gums for a “house party” where very urban music met very park-y acoustics. Ministry of Sound Classical even booked a date once — because nothing says cultured like thumping classical under the stars.

Who would hate it here? People who need flat runs, quiet weekends, or a park without lookouts — the Law Walk Loop and Mount Eliza are not subtle. Also, if you loathe tourists who read TripAdvisor aloud, this place will test your patience.

Who’ll love it? Runners who chase hills, families who need playgrounds on rotation, picnic strategists who can claim Synergy Parkland or the Lotterywest Family Area as their tactical base, and anyone who secretly enjoys a light installation pretending the trees are celebrities. For years the guides have sung our praises — we’ll keep being the park that makes Perth look good.
