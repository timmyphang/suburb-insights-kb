# Lyndhurst (NSW)

Slug: `lyndhurst-nsw`

We live in Lyndhurst, NSW, and honestly, when we Google ourselves it’s like a bad identity parade. No recent write-ups — most of what turns up is older snippets, other Lyndhursts doing all the heavy lifting for our internet vanity. So: welcome to the suburb everyone forgets exists until a postcode swap is needed.

Mornings for us are less “cafes on the high street” and more checking what the world thinks of our name. The front page results include Time Out and Urban List entries about Lyndhurst Terrace in Central, Hong Kong — yes, that Lyndhurst — where Tai Cheong Bakery sits at 35 Lyndhurst Terrace and Matchali once had an exclusive iced banana-bread matcha latte. There’s even a Goose Island Taproom and a games-heavy bar called HQ over there. We wave at them across the internet and go make a cup of tea.

The local inbox is a patchwork of parenting guides and directories. For years our online face has been MumsPages listings — “Builders in Lyndhurst (3975)”, hair removal services, and NDIS provider entries that throw up Josh Care and Caring247 Nursing Agency. Netmums keeps trotting out “things to do with kids in Lyndhurst” and seasonal kids’ activities, while Mumsnet nostalgia insists there’s a Lyndhurst in the New Forest with a New Park Manor on Lyndhurst Rd. Nobody’s lied, they’ve just decided to share our name with a village and a terrace that look far more photogenic.

Petty local drama? There’s no spicy council feud in these snippets; the biggest insult is existential: are we a postcode, a UK village, or a Hong Kong terrace? We choose to lean into it.

Who would hate living here: anyone who needs to be “on trend” on Instagram — the web will keep redirecting them to egg tarts and taprooms that aren’t ours. Who’d love it: families who just want practical listings and local services — the same Netmums and MumsPages guides that clutter our search results would make a mum’s life easier, even if they’re older references.

So yes, we know what people say. They also missed the bit where we’re still here, quietly Lyndhursty and perfectly fine with being Internet-adjacent.
