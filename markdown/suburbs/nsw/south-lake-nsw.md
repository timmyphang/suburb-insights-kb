# South Lake (NSW)

Slug: `south-lake-nsw`

Back in the day, when the internet still liked lists, Urban List tucked “South Lake Jindabyne” into a feature called Soak Up The Serenity — which is our favourite polite way of saying: yes, we’re mostly a lake with excellent excuses for muddy shoes and questionable hair-dryer choices.

We start weekends like everybody who actually lives near a body of water: we’re up early, loading kayaks and bait (or snacks — it’s hard to tell the difference) and heading to Lake Jindabyne because Urban List recommended it for kayaking, fishing and lakeside camping. There’s a specific kind of pride here in being able to say “we camped by the lake” without immediately being asked which resort’s spa you booked. We’re the answer that involves a good thermos and realistic expectations.

People outside our postcode are confused, and fair — the internet doesn’t help. One minute we’re “South Lake Jindabyne” on a NSW lakes guide, the next we’re conflated with Havana Wellness on Berrigan Drive in Perth (that’s a different South Lake entirely), or South Lake Tahoe in Time Out’s travel roundups, or even Southlake in Texas popping up on mum-blog event calendars. We get mistaken identity a lot. We’d like a name change but we also like the way it sounds when the mountains meet the water, so we compromise and keep correcting people with a smile.

No juicy local council feuds to report — if you want drama, head to the forums about parking in a capital city. Our neighbourhood debates are more subtle: where to put the campfire, which cove has fewer midges, and who forgot to bring the marshmallows.

Who would hate living here? Nightclub habitues who need velvet ropes and DJs at 2am. Who would love it? People who prefer a weekend measured in paddle strokes, fish tales and the sort of peace that made Lake Macquarie guides sell this region as a proper getaway. We’re not flashy. We’re the lake you come back to.
