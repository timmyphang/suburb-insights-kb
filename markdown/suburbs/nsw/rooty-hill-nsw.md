# Rooty Hill (NSW)

Slug: `rooty-hill-nsw`

Back in the day we’d tell you Rooty Hill was the kind of place where a theatre that seats 2,000 (the Sydney Coliseum Theatre at 33 Railway Street) rubs shoulders with a weekend suckling pig and a halo‑halo stand. For years the stories about this suburb read like a food-and-family variety show — and honestly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.

A typical weekend used to look like this: morning greenery at Urban Jungle with its triple‑stacked buttermilk pancakes and eggs Benedict with yuzu hollandaise while the trio of canaries pretended to be a live soundtrack; mid‑day stops at Mix Mix Co for halo‑halo topped with corn, sugar banana or avocado (yes, avocado — don’t @ us) or a warm taho silken‑tofu pudding; and for dinner the Will Mahusay‑led Mesa, which used to tempt us with pancit, five‑spice beef pares and that infamous weekend‑only lechon. If you wanted Filipino all‑day breakfasts, A‑Team’s Kitchen gave us tapsilog, chorsilog and pork belly with crackling — the kind of hearty stuff Western Sydney writes home about.

Local theatre and sport keep us honest. West HQ — the ambitious dining, fitness and entertainment hub — and the Coliseum have put Rooty Hill on the map (yes, even the MAFS 2023 wedding happened there). The Woodstock — reborn from the Carousel Inn after a Solotel refresh — tried very hard to be everyone’s local: sports bar, beer garden, playground, and, allegedly, $15 Tooheys jugs for State of Origin nights. Anzac Day two‑up in the old carpark? Big deal here. Big, loud and reliably local.

Petty gossip? Mostly practical: venues come and go. Made By The Hill was even marked CLOSED in the listings — a reminder that our hospitality scene can be as volatile as a summer gig at the Coliseum.

Who would hate living here? People who want every cafe to pretend it’s in Surry Hills and can’t handle good, loud communal life. Who would love it here? Families (hello playgrounds, Timezone & Zone Bowling), anyone chasing honest Filipino food, gig‑goers and anyone who thinks a 2,000‑seat theatre and a weekend lechon make for a perfectly balanced postcode. We’ll keep the backyard barbie and the concert queues — you bring the appetite.
