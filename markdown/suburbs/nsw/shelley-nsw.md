# Shelley (NSW)

Slug: `shelley-nsw`

We like to start our mornings with a brisk scroll through the internet, just to see what the rest of Australia thinks our postcode is. By 8am we've already learned that, according to Broadsheet, the High Country Rail Trail skirts Lake Hume en route to something called Shelley Station — which, to be clear, sounds scenic and Victorian and not at all like the sleepy place we actually live in. As if that wasn’t identity theft enough, the web also offers up a Shelley Beach Campground on Shelley Beach Rd, Kronkup (Urban List’s pick of no-frills WA camping), and a Tribute Coffee and Kitchen at 19 Tribute Street West, Shelley — but that one’s in Perth, so congratulations to our namesakes for having better weather and matcha.

If you Google Shelley hoping for a local café crawl, you’ll mostly get literary cameos. Mary Shelley keeps turning up in Arts pages and film reviews — Concrete Playground and Time Out keep serving us Frankenstein takes like we’re meant to feel honored. Meanwhile, Broadsheet and Junkee are busily using “Shelley” as cultural shorthand: potters, product designers and mournful essays about the Big Prawn all share our name on the byline. Eastern Suburbs Mums even introduced a local mum called Shelley who surfs and does ceramics, presumably proving Shelley is also an acceptable first name if you want people to confuse you with a suburb.

Petty local drama? The archives are mum. There are no council spats, no café coups, no baristas with secret playlists — only an oft-repeated identity crisis where an entire internet keeps mistaking our suburb for either a person, a camping ground, or a station on a trail that’s absolutely not in NSW. We’ll take that over real estate fights any day.

Who would hate living here: people who need their suburb to trend on Twitter every Tuesday. Who would love it here: anyone who enjoys being the quiet punchline of a national search bar — cyclists who appreciate a good rail-trail myth, campers with tenuous WA connections, and anyone who secretly wants to be named after a gothic novelist. We own the confusion. We’re Shelley, NSW — charmingly ambiguous and inexplicably literary.
