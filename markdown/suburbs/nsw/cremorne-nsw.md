# Cremorne (NSW)

Slug: `cremorne-nsw`

We start our Saturday like we’ve always started it: half a mind on the harbour, half a mind on pastry regrets. For years the local “Cremorne crawl” along Military Road was the ritual — hair and nail salons, dog boutiques you definitely don’t need, a slice of Fratelli’s pizza if you’re feeling human — and then, because we aren’t monsters, a film at the Hayden Orpheum Picture Palace to sober up. Time Out once called the Orpheum “without doubt the grandest cinema in Sydney” and honestly, the 1935 Art Deco thing, six auditoria and that genuine Wurlitzer organ still makes our cranky inner critic go soft.

We’ve been name-checked in guides and directories — Broadsheet used to list Best Bagel Co and Shot On Military among our Military Road highlights — which, yes, feeds the ego and the queue. Sitchu’s old profile liked to remind visitors about Cremorne Junction’s cafes and the foreshore walks around Cremorne Point and Willoughby Bay. It’s true: the walk to MacCallum Pool and the lighthouse is the sort of cardio where you can pretend you’re reflecting on life and not just thinking about which bakery you’ll hit on the way back.

Petty local drama? The press would sooner write about our cinema organ than our spats, so we’ll keep it classy: we argue about whose dog looked more scandalised at the boutique, and whether the light at the Point is better at dawn or just less crowded by retirees doing power-walk therapy. Either way, it’s a gentle suburb to bicker in.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to harbour views, heritage cinemas, or the very idea of walking somewhere for something other than an Uber. If you need neon-nightlife or mysterious 24/7 grit, Cremorne will bore you polite.

Who would love it: film nerds who want an organ to match their opinions, families who value foreshore picnics and MacCallum dips, and anyone who thinks Military Road’s little shops and Best Bagel Co’s legacy (as Broadsheet once noted) make for a perfectly curated weekend. We’ll be here, ferry of crumbs in hand, pretending the lighthouse is ours.
