# Beresfield (NSW)

Slug: `beresfield-nsw`

We know Beresfield by the sight of a Hi‑Vis Holden Commodore sliding into a spot beside a navy Ford Ranger, both wearing 'Proud Tradie' stickers like medals, and the servo bloke already calling out, "double‑shot, long black, extra hot, Shane?"  
We queue at the Caltex on Station Street like it's a parish—smoko first, theology of footy and fence posts second.  
We wear Lonsdale shorts on Saturdays without irony and keep a spare roll of gaffer tape in the glovebox because it is, in fact, for everything.  
We take our Staffordshire Bull Terrier down to the oval and introduce him to the bloke who thinks a Bunnings sausage with free onions is Michelin‑level dining.  
Saturday dawn looks like this: we drop the kids at the Under‑8s on Maitland Road, swing past Bunnings for timber and "a quick" tin of paint, then get stuck behind the bloke who only came for a drill bit and emerges with a trolley of timber and a new Makita.  
We queue at the servo for that never‑fussy coffee, or if we're pretending to be fancy we sulkily grab the 7‑Eleven long mac with soy and call it culture.  
We RSVP to three backyard projects, a kids' soccer game and a sausage sizzle and actually manage two of the three DIYs.  
We do our Facebook rounds: tradie group for gear deals, the local page for the Beresfield Railway Station carpark drama, which somehow lasted a week of comments and escalated from "park better" to "whose partner blocked me" in a single reply chain.  
We have rituals: Bunnings is church on a Saturday; the sausage sizzle is holy communion; the PTA now insists on a "proper" barbecue master after last year's sloppily flipped snag scandal—someone swears by Weber, someone swears at the Weber, and the Facebook votes are dramatic.  
We know our neighbours' paint colours and their weekend plans by Tuesday night.  
We mow at 4pm on Sunday to keep the neighbours happy and listen to triple j while we wield a whipper snipper with suspicious pride.  
We are petty about parking and proud about our utes and anthemic about a well‑placed gumboot.  
Who would hate living here? An artisanal vegan influencer, Lycra permanently on, who cycles everywhere, brags about being car‑free while live‑streaming from a café with single‑origin pour‑overs, would be baffled by compulsory sausage sizzle etiquette and offended by fuel‑powered Utes in the main street.  
Who would love it? A 38‑year‑old tradie with a Ford Ranger, Hi‑Vis, Lonsdale shorts, a Staffordshire Bull Terrier and a Bunnings membership card who thinks community is measured in sausages flipped and fences fixed—welcome home.
