# Rose Park (NSW)

Slug: `rose-park-nsw`

We like to think of Rose Park as the suburb that wears bluestone and a linen shirt to the supermarket. Stroll past 36 Kensington Road on a weekend morning and you can almost hear the memory of wood-fire: Orso used to sit on the corner of Kensington Road and Victoria Terrace like it owned the block, and next door Willmott’s Gastronomia occupied that century‑old bluestone deli we all pretended not to photograph. As Broadsheet noted, it was a swish bistro/deli pairing that kept the neighbourhood smelling slightly of Josper smoke and good intentions.

Back in the day we’d file into Loft Meditation Yoga — the tiny studio tucked inside a home at the corner of Hewitt Avenue and Winchester Lane — and attempt to be peaceful for exactly as long as it took to check who’d posted their dinner from Orso. The Stables of Victoria Park had the other vibe: a Southern‑Italian diner in the heritage Bookmakers’ League building at the racecourse entrance, which meant you could follow a rustic pizza with a brisk walk and pretend it was exercise.

If you’re the sort who reads reviews like scripture, Sitchu once rhapsodised about a 400g, three‑week dry‑aged ribeye at Longplay Bistro (also listed at 36 Kensington Road) — proof that around here a steak can be treated like a minor celebrity. And for the new parents among us, Adelaide Mums & Babies Clinic used to hold court at 3/146 Fullarton Road, running classes and even planning a nipple laser treatment rollout slated for August 2025, because of course we have niche medicine.

We should confess: we have no recent scoops — these are fond echoes rather than live gossip. So if you hate heritage bluestone, tiny house‑yoga studios, or restaurants that take food very seriously, you would loathe living here. You’ll be the person asking “Where’s the cheap parking?” while we sip our nostalgic appreciation.

But if you love walking past elegant old buildings, pretending you understand Italian small plates, and feeling slightly superior about local eateries you used to frequent, you’ll fit right in. Welcome to Rose Park — where the charm is vintage and the memories smell faintly of wood smoke.
