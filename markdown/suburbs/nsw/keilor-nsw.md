# Keilor (NSW)

Slug: `keilor-nsw`

Back in the day we thought Keilor was where the city’s backyard met a history lesson — and honestly, we liked it that way. Mornings used to start with a drive out to the blue-and-white deck of Peregrine Social Club, which finally opened after "a 15-year wait" in the middle of Niddrie Quarry (Valley Lake) — a place where you could pretend you’d discovered waterfront living without actually leaving suburbia. By noon we’d be arguing over whether Houston’s Barbecue’s smoked brisket could compete with the view.

Weekends were a choose-your-own-adventure: heritage flex at Overnewton Castle (51 Overnewton Road) — the sort of Scottish-Baronial manor you bring your nan to for high tea — or pretending to be cultured at Arundel Farm Estate, the 151‑year‑old bluestone place with 16 acres of shiraz and a cellar door that opens for tastings. If we were feeling civic, we’d tack on a circuit at Organ Pipes National Park (Organ Pipes Road, Keilor North) past the Tessellated Pavement and the Organ Pipes themselves, because nothing says "we live here" like basalt columns and a picnic rug.

There’s a whisper of growth, too. Estate agents were already purring that Keilor East is "ripe with potential" once the airport rail link brings a station our way — the kind of infrastructure gossip that has people polishing their letterboxes. Meanwhile, Melbourne’s food pages treated us like the west’s best-kept pantry: Sunhands in Carlton even used veggies from Keilor’s Day’s Walk Farm, which made us feel a little famous.

Who would hate living here? If you want inner-city flash, traffic-free trams and a postcode that drinks its own flat whites, you’ll sulk. If the sound of a country manor’s church clock and the occasional barbecue smoke from an industrial-estate legend like Houston’s Barbecue make you twitch, Keilor is not your vibe.

Who would love it? Families who still value playgroups — shoutout to Keilor Park Playgroup Centre, East Keilor Kindergarten and Keilor Downs playgroup — hikers, wine tasters, and anyone who enjoys a castle, a quarry cafe, and a decent brisket in the same weekend. We know every corner, and we’ll defend our little slice of west — even if we do it with a wink and a roast.
