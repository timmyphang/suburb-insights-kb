# Dundas Valley (NSW)

Slug: `dundas-valley-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start the morning at Dundas Park — you know, the Phillip Ruddock water playground/Curtis Oval — watching kids annihilate whatever quiet we hoped to have. As ParraParents politely points out, the place has the full parenting starter pack: shaded play equipment, BBQs, sports fields and that miracle of modern suburban engineering, a water playground that keeps everyone distracted for an hour or three. ParraParents also reminds us we survived asbestos remediation and then got the comforts of improved parking and fencing. Glamour, really.

Weekends were a parade of playground destinations: Sir Thomas Mitchell Reserve got its upgraded swings and boat-play gym, Rapanea Community Forest tucked its neighbourhood playground along Brand Street with street parking and bus 545 access — all the infrastructure essentials for the small army of humans we cart around. If you needed childcare intel, MumsPages was our version of divination, pointing us to listings like Eastwood Blessings Early Learning and Preschool and the usual directory comforts.

We used to pop into Daisys by the Park — industrial decor meets oval view — and sample a breakfast wrap while contemplating whether paying for tea by the teabag was a suburban sin. The blog noted the teabag situation, the price-y reality and that black tea was $5.50. Salt and Butter was the picture-postcard spot to sit and watch the rowing teams, the joggers, the cyclists and the parade of dogs pretending they run this place. Happy, slightly smug suburbia.

Petty drama? Not much — unless you count the great fencing-and-parking renaissance after the asbestos scare. We argue about BBQ real estate, whose kid got the best swing, and whether the water playground is open this week because a parent forum said so. Riveting stuff.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to small people’s shrieks, communal playground politics, or unit blocks that elbow the riverbank. If you crave a nonstop café crawl or a nightlife that never sleeps, Dundas Valley will gently disappoint.

Who would love it: families who treat parks as second living rooms, people who enjoy a proper riverside watch of rowers and walkers, and anyone who knows where to find the nearest playground when peace is a rare commodity. We’ll see you at the barbecue shelter — bring paper plates.
