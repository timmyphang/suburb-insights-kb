# Tuggerawong (NSW)

Slug: `tuggerawong-nsw`

Back in the day — as in the mid‑2020s when our council press releases were still the hottest thing on the local noticeboard — we learned Tuggerawong is either quietly ambitious or dangerously overcommitted to swings. We walk that sentence like it’s a loop: the Tuggerah Lakes loop.

We’re the lot who’ll tell you exactly where the new playspace sits — 326–328 Tuggerawong Rd — and which bit of equipment will break your back (the multi‑level climbing structure, obviously). The place came with pendulum swing, a three‑bay swing set, slides, a mouse house and new shade shelters and seating tucked by the shared pathway. It was built with community input, which is council speak for “someone else got to pick the paint”.

Back in the day the re‑elected Albanese Government pledged $4.77 million to stitch together missing links on the Tuggerah Lakes pathway. Emma McBride even turned up to join the Tuggerawong Pathway Group for the announcement, as if promenade‑inspecting was now an Olympic sport. For a while we were all very civic-minded about it — until the sausage sizzle warmed the backs of our jackets and we remembered we were just there for the snags.

There was also talk (again, back in the day) of new public preschools: one earmarked for the grounds of Tuggerawong Public School, part of a 100‑site rollout the NSW Government promised. Liesl Tesch called it “fantastic”, which, to be fair, is the exact level of enthusiasm a preschool announcement deserves.

Who would hate living here? People who regard swings as a luxury and think “shared pathway” means shared personal space. Also anyone allergic to community consultation — it takes a village, and we’re very into committees.

Who would love it? Families who want a properly ambitious playground at 326–328 Tuggerawong Rd. Walkers who remember the Tuggerah Lakes loop as our unofficial picnic runway. Men who prefer to talk their feelings on Thursday evenings at the Men’s Walk and Talk and then reward themselves with a sausage sizzle. In short: if you like clean lakeside walking, well‑planned play equipment and the soothing bureaucracy of local progress, you’ll feel right at home.
