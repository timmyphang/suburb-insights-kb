# Booragul (NSW)

Slug: `booragul-nsw`

We’re halfway along the Warners Bay walk, shoes dusty, picnic rug in the boot and whatever passed for civilisation a polite distance away — because for the record, Junkee did say the Warners Bay walk links Eleebana and Booragul and clocks in at 8.9km, and we take that very seriously. Back in the day — or at least in the guides we’ve got — Junkee recommended an afternoon picnic on the Warners Bay foreshore and honestly, who are we to argue?

Our Saturday usually looks like this: a detour to the MAC yapang Sculpture Park to see which piece has been claimed by a seagull, follow the ReLeaf Tree Trail from the Gateway Sculpture (yes, the trail has a start point near the Gateway Sculpture — sustainable types love that bit), then a slow loop past the Tiny Forest at 2A Five Islands Road (access from Lake Crescent, Teralba) where locals once turned up for community planting sessions. The Woodrising, Booragul and Marmong Point Sustainable Neighbourhood Group still meets — they’ve been organising Clean Up Australia Day events and, for those who like a good throwback, once ran a 2010 Community Swap Party. We wear our badges of composting like medals.

GKR Karate used to run classes at Booragul Public School on Aspinall Street — and if you’ve got kids, you’ll spot the flyers or hear the karate kicks echoing down the street. Playgroup NSW listings have been the quiet backbone for new parents, which is nice because we like our noise to be organised.

Local life here is announced on the Booragul Community Notice Board — Find It Locally groups list us alongside Awaba, Barnsley, Blackalls Park, Bolton Point, Fassifern, Fennell Bay, Killingworth, Marmong Point, Teralba, Toronto, Wakefield, Woodrising, Heaton & Ryhope. That’s our catchment: a scattered family of suburbs that like to borrow each other’s tools and never return them.

Who would hate it here? If you need every weekend to be a new restaurant launch or your aesthetic requires imported city drama, this won’t do. Who’d love it? Families, walkers, anyone into tiny forests, sculpture parks, community clean-ups and karate classes — we’re quietly proud, a bit green-thumbed, and perfectly happy with our foreshore picnics.
