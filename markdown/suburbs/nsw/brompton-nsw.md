# Brompton (NSW)

Slug: `brompton-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed and follow the smell of spit‑cooked roast like it was a church bell. The Brompton Hotel was our landmark — Broadsheet loved its revival under Duxton Pubs and even name‑checked chef Andrew Wandless for turning bistro classics into Euro‑tinged comfort food (yes, beef Wellington and bangers and mash made the cut). It felt like someone put a heritage pub in a time capsule and fed it fancy share plates.

Our weekend ritual used to be staggeringly simple: queue for a Sunday roast, loiter by 36 Chief Street where the town’s fireplaces get all the cosy attention, then wander past 19 on Green — the old Bowden‑Brompton community centre — to see what meetings were on. We’ve even got practical stuff: the Brompton Licensed Post Office at the shopping centre (Shop 6, 89–91 Torrens Rd) was the sort of place you could actually organise your life — as SAcommunity listed, it handled banking and foreign currency (mon–fri, small hours, the whole bureaucratic romance).

If you wanted a hit of caffeine, Sitchu once tipped FIX Specialty Coffee at 1/28a Hawker Street as a local pick, so we used to count that among our reliable life‑support systems. For anyone tallying eldercare options, Ridleyton Greek Home for the Aged at 89 Hawker St appeared in the community listings — because Brompton is that rare mix of heritage pub glamour and quietly sensible services.

Petty drama? Nothing juicy in the files. Our biggest argument tends to be whether the Brompton Hotel’s Euro menu is trying too hard — as Broadsheet observed when they wrote about the hotel’s relaunch — which, frankly, feels like debating whether Victoria Street should be renamed “Very European Lane.”

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to nostalgia, fireplaces and community noticeboards. You’ll shriek if you crave neon nightlife or a constantly changing food truck scene. Who would love it: people who want roast dinners, sturdy post offices and a community centre that still runs actual meetings. We’re small, a bit fuddy, and quietly proud — and that’s exactly why we wouldn’t trade our Brompton afternoons for anything.
