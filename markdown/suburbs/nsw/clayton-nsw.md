# Clayton (NSW)

Slug: `clayton-nsw`

Back in the day we’d tell people Clayton was the place that quietly refused to be basic — and yeah, our scrapbook of older clippings proves it. As Broadsheet once noted, Clayton even had its own sold‑out food tours (Uyghur kebabs, Cantonese roast duck, that upside‑down Indonesian coffee trick) — which, frankly, summed up our tastebuds better than anything in the guidebook.

Our morning routine used to look like this: shuffle through Monash University’s Clayton campus — the big local landmark, as Time Out liked to remind everyone — duck into Church of Secular Coffee on campus for something that felt important, and reminisce about For Heaven’s Steaks at 1465 Centre Road when someone mentioned paninis. If the kids were with us, the playgroup at Clayton Community Centre had us covered while the dog did laps at Clayton Reserve — Concrete Playground used to call it small but welcoming, and they weren’t wrong.

Petty local drama? Oh, we had one: chef Clayton Wells stepping away from Public Hospitality made the rounds — Broadsheet ran that headline and we all pretended not to care while secretly Googling his next move. There was also the quietly competitive business of Christmas lights on Yalta Court; Urban List and Sitchu both praised 4 Yalta Court for two decades of nightly displays, so yes, if you’re into festive one‑upmanship, this is basically our sport.

Who would hate living here: anyone who thinks a suburb’s worth is measured by aperitifs on a rooftop or being “on trend” the minute a critic tweets. If you can’t be soothed by a sold‑out food tour memory or the knowledge that the Australian Synchrotron at 800 Blackburn Rd exists (tourists love saying it), then maybe head back to the inner ring.

Who would love it: students surviving the cost‑of‑living juggle, families who rely on the community playgroups, food nerds who remember Taiwan Yes at 162 Clayton Road, and anyone who thinks a neighbourhood that quietly collects eccentricities is winning at life. We’ll keep celebrating that — even if our sources are a little nostalgic.
