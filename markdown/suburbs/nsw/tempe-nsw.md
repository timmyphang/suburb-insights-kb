# Tempe (NSW)

Slug: `tempe-nsw`

Back in the day we used to trot down Mary Street for a tasting at Willie The Boatman’s little bar — Broadsheet even wrote about the crew who set up shop there — and you could still smell the Pepe Saya churns if you timed it right when the factory doors were open to the public. Tempe’s always been the kind of place where artisan butter and a pale ale called something like ‘Tempe Tim’ elbow up against a very practical highway.

Our weekends were a proper mixed bag: Renzo’s woodfired pizza truck rolled up by the Princes Highway every Wednesday and you judged the whole truck on the margherita (Broadsheet said as much). If you wanted something tangy and beergarden-adjacent, Koh Chang Thai at 703 Princes Highway was on the list (Sitchu noted that tangerine colour scheme and its beergarden). And Time Out once reminded us that Lil Franky Pizzeria is the sort of local favourite that makes people write lists — Tempe got name-checked on a few of those city guides.

Petty local drama? We had our moment: IKEA Tempe was big enough to make headlines — Pedestrian TV reported a COVID health alert that turned us briefly into the country’s most suspicious hotspot, and the store’s sleepovers and take-back pop-ups were the kind of Melbourne-versus-south debate fodder you’d expect. Meanwhile, the Tempe Tip was proudly listed as the op-shop hunting ground (Time Out marked it closed, which felt like the end of an era).

Who would hate living here? People who want their suburbs to smell only of lemon myrtle and boutique candles, who can’t handle a Pepe Saya factory next to an Ikea carpark, or who get offended by pizza trucks being the height of local culture.

Who’ll love it? Folks who like their weekends eclectic — craft beer and butter tours (as Broadsheet chronicled), emergency puncture repairs at Bikebug opposite IKEA (Concrete Playground called them out), midweek woodfired pizzas by the highway, and a sports centre that actually has 11 outdoor netball courts if you want community chaos (Time Out noted Robyn Webster Sports Centre). We’re practical, delicious, and a little bit loud — and proud of it.
