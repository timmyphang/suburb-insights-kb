# Tullamarine (NSW)

Slug: `tullamarine-nsw`

We know the landing strip life here — the hiss of a plane climbing over the Tullamarine Freeway, and the comforting smell of something cheesy wafting from a shopfront you could miss if you blink. Back in the day Broadsheet wrote about the Alba Cheese Factory and its steaming hot ricotta that used to draw the parish; we’d swing by on weekday mornings and pretend we were connoisseurs.

Weekends were an oddball mix. If we weren’t gawking at the two‑hectare lagoon at Urbnsurf — Time Out catalogued our inland surf park and its Wavegarden tech — we were arguing whether Three Blue Ducks, which Urban List and Concrete Playground said set up camp overlooking the surf lagoon, was a proper dinner out or just an Instagramable excuse to eat something rotisserie‑adjacent. Pasta Al Dente was where we solved life’s problems with wood‑fired panini and a freezer full of something resembling culinary commitment. Melbourne Arancini? We knew their Tullamarine kitchen’s assembly line by heart: Mondays prepping, Fridays frying.

Petty local drama? We’ve had a few headline moments. Pedestrian.TV ran the horrible footage of that sign collapse on the Tullamarine Freeway — the one that crushed a car and landed someone in hospital — and they’ve also pointed out how the airport’s car park rakes in an obscene amount per day. Concrete Playground even reported plans to rip out kerbside pick‑ups at the airport and funnel everyone into an elevated T123 hub. Meanwhile, depending on which list you read, our airport was either Australia–Pacific’s top performer or ranked worst for convenience. We’re versatile like that.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to planes, unexpected engineering drama, or paying to park their feelings (and their car). People who live for kerbside goodbyes will grizzle.

Who would love it: surf‑obsessed types who prefer a faux ocean in their postcode; families who remembered Just Kids Playcentre & Cafe as a handy local pitstop; lovers of honest Italian grocers and deli culture — Broadsheet’s lists make it clear we kept that tradition — and anyone who thinks wedding venues at 309 Melrose Drive sounded like a grand plan.

We’ll keep loving our weird little crossroads: part airport, part surf park, part deli strip, all of it gloriously our own.
