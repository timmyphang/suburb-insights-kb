# Menai (NSW)

Slug: `menai-nsw`

Back in the day we used to brag that Menai was the place you discovered when you took a wrong turn and then decided you quite liked where you’d parked. Weekends looked a bit like this: we’d grab something from Little Bonny’s — yes, the rotisserie that arrived with a brass spit imported from France and all the airs of a city chicken shop — and head to Menai Park Bushland Reserve for a picnic. Little Bonny’s was the shiny moment that suggested Menai might be getting a culinary glow-up; Concrete Playground even called it a “refined” chicken shop. Those were the days.

If you wanted something a touch fancier, there was Rocksalt — Urban List’s very own “Menai’s answer to tapas and wine” — tucked around Allison Crescent (Shop 11, 72–80 Allison Crescent, if you like specifics). Time Out also remembers Rocksalt as an elegant, family-run spot with an open-air vibe and a mezzanine for functions. And when you wanted a proper, late-night pub atmosphere, Club Central Menai on Allison Crescent was the place for pints and live sport.

We’ll admit: the food scene here once had the collective ambition of a sleepy houseplant. Concrete Playground described Menai as something of a food wasteland until Little Bonny’s rocked up in 2017. The glow-up was short-lived in parts — reviews later noted Little Bonny’s as closed — which, yes, is the sort of suburban tragedy we hold a candle for and then stubbornly deny at Christmas.

Who would hate living here? If you’re allergic to community-sized ambitions, hungry for endless restaurant lists, or you measure a suburb by how many new openings Instagram can monetise in a week, you’ll find Menai a bit too serene. If you fret about missing out on every tiny festival and pop-up, this isn’t your preferred postcode.

Who would love it here? Families who like reliable local venues, people who appreciate a solid pub, anyone who enjoys packing a park picnic with rotisserie chicken memories, and those delighted that Pasticceria Caruso will deliver a decent cannoli to our corner of Sydney when nostalgia kicks in. We’ll take the quiet, the quirks, and the occasional culinary cameo — and call it home.
