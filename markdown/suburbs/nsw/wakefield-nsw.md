# Wakefield (NSW)

Slug: `wakefield-nsw`

We are Wakefield, NSW — the Wakefield nobody quite meant to click on.

Our morning routine is simple: we make a cup of tea, open Google, and watch the internet quietly decide we are someone else. Broadsheet prefers telling the tale of Dural’s Vicar of Wakefield — which, back in the day, was reimagined as a multi-venue spot with a restaurant called Wakefield’s, a wine bar named Mrs Goldsmith and chef Leigh McDivitt at the pass. Sitchu thinks Wakefield’s Bar and Wine is a buzzy Canberra thing. Concrete Playground and Urban List keep sending people to Wakefield Street in Wellington — where Mama Brown, Apache and Nikau apparently run the real party. Nelson has Wakefield Quay. The Wakefield Mumbler in the UK runs an events calendar and even a Wakefield Armed Forces Day at Pontefract Park. We get lost on the world map a lot, but at least we’re internationally confusing.

Back in the day these write-ups were charmingly specific about other Wakefields. They taught us two lessons: one, we are part of a crowd of excellent impostors; two, if you want press, you should probably move to Wellington Street or open a Mrs Goldsmith.

The petty local drama? It’s mostly a long-standing identity crisis. We don’t bicker about bins or bike lanes — we bicker about which Wakefield was meant when someone tags us in a review. You haven’t lived until you’ve explained, for the twentieth time, that no, we do not serve the Nikau courtyard brunch and, no, we are not hosting Apache’s French-Vietnamese sharing plates.

Who would hate living here: people who need to be on every food guide or crave a suburb that turns up first in search results. If your top priority is being on Urban List or Concrete Playground, you’ll resent being the Wakefield that becomes a Wikipedia game of “which one?”

Who would love it here: anyone who likes being delightfully untrendy and quietly stubborn. We’re the Wakefield that wears its anonymity like a badge. While the rest of the world fights over Wakefield Streets and Wakefield Quays, we’ll keep doing our thing — low-key, un-Googled and very ours.
