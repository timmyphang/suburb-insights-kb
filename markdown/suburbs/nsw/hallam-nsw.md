# Hallam (NSW)

Slug: `hallam-nsw`

We’re standing at the corner of memory and Google search, coffee-less but opinionated, watching Hallam do its quietly useful thing. Mornings here used to mean dodging a pram or two on the way to the Hallam Gardens Children’s Centre — the Playgroup Victoria listing used to rave about its big indoor room and shaded playground at 26–28 Nettle Drive — and waving at whoever’s running Precious Seeds Early Learning that week. If you’ve got kids, we’ve got venues that were always on the program.

Weekends were the real flex: as Broadsheet once noted, Cha’s Cabin in Spring Square was the place for an all-you-can-eat Sri Lankan buffet and karaoke that gave the whole suburb a soundtrack. And for the pretentious skaters among us, Broadsheet also wrote about Tony Hallam’s skateboard collection — described as one of the most comprehensive in the world — which is the sort of local trivia we trot out to sound cultured at barbecues.

Yes, Hallam pops up in headlines of a different stripe, too. There were counter‑terrorism searches carried out in our postcode, and Victoria Police featured in stories about raids that included houses in Hallam — sober reminders that small places get big news sometimes. We don’t make jokes about that; we just lock our gate and take the long way home.

Who would hate living here? If you need an endless parade of boutique cafés on every corner, or if your idea of a Saturday is a 45-minute wait for a $22 curiosity latte, Hallam will fret you. The glossy lists don’t exactly queue up on our streets — so if you’re allergic to suburbia-lite, best keep scrolling.

Who loves it? Families, for the playgroups and the school-hall listings at Hallam Primary; folk who like a solid local story — think second-generation family pizza places that Time Out once tipped as beloved Hallam institutions — and anyone who enjoys a place that looks unbothered while quietly having a life. Nick Hallam’s Positive Posters project even started round here, if you’re into community design lore. We’ll keep the barbecue warm, the kid’s scooter in the driveway, and the karaoke mic polished — whether Cha’s Cabin is still singing or just a headline in our rearview.
