# Sunshine West (NSW)

Slug: `sunshine-west-nsw`

We know the exact moment you realise you’ve arrived in Sunshine West: the air tastes faintly of charcoal and someone’s chocolate bar is being unwrapped. Back in the day the write-ups we’ve got were nostalgic — none of this fresh-off-the-press stuff — so indulge us while we riff on what used to put us on the map.

Morning here could mean a drive past 64 Glengala Road, where Sunshine Social used to make a neighbourhood out of a converted petrol station. As Broadsheet noted, Greg Fee and Gareth Crawford set up an upcycled barbecue joint with rotisserie chicken and a kid‑friendly beer garden — the kind of place where the outdoor cubby house mattered as much as the smoke ring. Mount Zero’s zero-waste market was another weekend ritual at Mount Zero Warehouse, 6 Law Court, a quarterly thing where you’d buy olives, bulk grains and bakeries in recyclable jar-sized quantities. And if you had a sweet tooth, Urban List once pointed folks to Choced at 101 Proximity Drive for that viral Dubai chocolate — yes, the marbled pistachio-baklava sort, two bars for about $40.

We don’t have any recent scoops to fight about, so the local drama is low-key: a lovingly repurposed petrol bowser, a warehouse market, and a rotisserie that smells like someone’s doing something right. Time Out even flagged bigger dreams for the area in the past — a Sunshine Energy Park was slated near Ballarat Road and the Western Ring Road, and Sunshine Station was earmarked for a huge upgrade to become a transport superhub. Fancy plans, long timelines — we’ve been patient.

Who would hate it here? If you can’t stand smoke in the evenings, dislike converted industrial charm, or insist every weekend must be boutique‑market‑to‑bougie‑café, this won’t be your scene. We’re not pretending we’re the CBD.

Who would love it? Families who like a kid‑friendly beer-garden vibe, zero‑waste shoppers who actually bring jars to 6 Law Court, and anyone who takes charcoal chicken very, very seriously. We’re happy with our smoky little corner — a bit worn, a bit practical, and honestly, a lot tastier than people expect.
