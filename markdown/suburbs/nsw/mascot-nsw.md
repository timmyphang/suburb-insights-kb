# Mascot (NSW)

Slug: `mascot-nsw`

Back in the day we’d stand in a Hong Ha Bakery queue and try not to judge the bloke who thought three types of pork in a banh mi was an act of culinary patriotism. Time Out and Broadsheet both made us feel fancy for it — apparently Mascot’s banh mi scene is a thing, and we’ll accept the accolade while planes roar past.

Mornings here used to be a very particular mood: grab a roll from Destination Roll at Mascot Central, eye the mural at La Boca on O'Riordan Street and wonder whether you were in an Argentinian restaurant or an airport mural contest. If you were feeling artisanal you could once get ridiculous with a five‑cheese toastie from Three Blind Mice — Daniel Hughes and Milan Majstorovic’s provedore — and pretend the jet noise was part of the acoustic.

Weekends were either full of takeout geography — grk Souvlaki on King Street for the hangover recovery special — or small-town spectacle: kids learning to skate at Ice Zoo on Coward Street, VR zombies at Zero Latency, or families crowding Unity Mascot’s Meriton precinct like it was a new civic square. Concrete Playground taught us the subtle joy of plane spotting at “The Beach” near the old control tower; yes, planes pass mere metres from the fence and yes, it never gets old.

There isn’t any juicy local council soap in the clippings — no scandalous town-hall brawls, just a lot of very good food and a healthy airport soundtrack. That’s the point: Mascot isn’t trying to be Paddington with better lighting. It’s a suburban hub that accidentally became a food crawl with entertainment options (party costumes at the Sydney Party Warehouse megastore, anyone?) and an international terminal to make your neighbour’s holiday pics perpetually smug.

Who would hate it? Anyone who sleeps on noise and likes their skyline unaccompanied by landing lights. Who would love it? Plane spotters, banh mi evangelists, VR zombies, ice‑skate disco kids and anyone who likes their evenings punctuated by a souvlaki at 203 King Street and a mural at 241 O’Riordan.

We’re Mascot — not glamorous, but reliably interesting, and yes, we’ll still take the banh mi over your sympathy any day.
