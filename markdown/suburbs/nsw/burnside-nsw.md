# Burnside (NSW)

Slug: `burnside-nsw`

Back in the day we’d tell visitors to bring sensible shoes and an appetite for quiet pleasures — the kind of place where a Sunday looks like this: we drag the kids to the George Bolton Swimming Centre tucked into Hazelwood Park for a lap in the heated 50‑metre while the toddlers wage war in the learners’ pool, then spread ourselves on the lawn for a picnic under the gums.

After that, we used to queue — politely, of course — for The Village Baker at Burnside Village. Ben Sachse’s pies and sourdough were the kind of culinary flex that didn’t need shouting about. If we fancied something a touch more Mediterranean, there was Ballaboosta Burnside on Glynburn Road with its woodfired breakfasts and Levantine comfort food (350 Glynburn Road, if you like directions). For when we felt urbane, Yuki in Burnside offered multi‑course omakase vibes that made us feel faintly like we’d left the suburbs for Tokyo for the evening. And yes, Lockwood General lived in a restored 19th‑century corner store — rustic charm with a providore wall we could admire between sips.

When we wanted culture, the Jazz Room at the Burnside Ballroom was our small‑room secret: ticket‑only, intimate and smugly knowledgeable. When someone wanted to watch sport and drown their feelings in choice beers, The Cremorne on Glynburn Road was the reliable go‑to with 18 taps and a beer garden.

We’re also the kind of place that runs on committees and classes: the Burnside Community Centre (401 Greenhill Rd, Tusmore) hosts everything from English language programs and Kumon tutoring to patchwork and mahjong; our library kept unreasonable hours on a Thursday night for the dedicated (Thurs 9:30am–9pm, if you must know). The Burnside Historical Society — established 1980 — meets at the community centre and quietly preserves our stained‑glass stories.

Who would hate living here? If you crave a neon nightclub, a pierced‑moped parade or a postcode that never goes to bed, we’ll bore you to tears. Who would love it? Families who like pools and picnics, food obsessives who worship local bakers and woodfire breakfasts, jazz spotters and anyone who prefers stately streets and tidy pleasures over chaos. We know what they say about us — and we also know where to get the best pie when you need to prove them wrong.
