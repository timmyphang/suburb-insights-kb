# Eltham North (NSW)

Slug: `eltham-north-nsw`

Back in the day we’d tell people Eltham North was the sort of place where the weekend begins when you find a shady spot in the park and refuse to move until someone offers you a sausage. Time Out even pointed out the Eltham North Parklands — undeveloped, honest, with a running and cycling track, sheltered barbecues and a small playground — basically our communal excuse to linger outdoors 20km north‑east of the CBD.

We know every quiet corner: the patch of grass where the pram brigade and the dog‑walker truce hold, the loop where you pretend you’re training for something serious, and the picnic shelters that have seen more tupperware than a novelty shop. For years the local charm wasn’t about neon signs or cocktail menus; it was about these parklands doing what suburbs do best — giving us space to breathe and an open invitation to be low‑fuss.

Back in the day we’d brag about the hidden Zen Den — Urban List called it one of Eltham’s best secrets, the sort of café that looks like an old run‑down house but hides funky décor and hanging plants — and the postcard‑ready Eltham Hotel, with its towering palms and retro paint job, the sort of country pub photo that got shared every time someone discovered a weekend escape in the north. We used to point them out like easter eggs to city friends.

Concrete Playground once ran a proper spa‑fantasy on us: a 5,000‑square‑foot wellness centre called THERAPY, pairing gut‑health therapies with a 60‑seat café and services like hydrogen therapy, cryotherapy, infrared saunas and a hyperbaric oxygen chamber — it was slated to open in October, according to their piece. Fancy, yes — and a touch aspirational for our quiet streets.

Who would hate living here? If you need neon nightlife, hourly trams to the theatre, or constantly curated brunch reels, this isn’t your backdrop. Who’d love it? Families, walkers, slightly smug picnic enthusiasts and anyone who likes their weekends unpolished and persistent. We’re not flashy. We’re the laid‑back cousin of Melbourne suburbs — and honestly, we prefer it that way.
