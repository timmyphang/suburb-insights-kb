# Norwood (NSW)

Slug: `norwood-nsw`

Back in the day — or at least according to every lifestyle list we still paste to our fridge — Norwood was where “heritage meets hip” and we’d brag about it until someone ratted us out to Time Out for being smug (they ranked us in their 2024 list, if you’re keeping score).

Our weekend used to look like this: amble down The Parade, admire the terrace roofs, stop at Argo on the Parade for coffee and zeppole (Time Out had that on file), and wonder how Boris The Cuttery had been holding court on The Parade for roughly forever. By arvo we’d orbit the Norwood Hotel — the heritage-listed pub with Finn McCool’s hiding inside — and argue about whether Bar Madrid’s free tapas and acorn‑fed jamón ibérico made you feel cultured or just very hungry (Broadsheet did a first look).

The real flex was the contradiction. We had Arkhe — Jake Kellie’s four‑tonne wood oven jammed into a heritage sandstone building, according to Broadsheet and Time Out — rubbing shoulders with East End Cellars’ bottle‑shop‑meets‑brasserie vibes. Johnny di Francesco’s 400 Gradi got a mention on Parade too, because of course it did: Norwood never met a pizzaiolo it didn’t want to queue for (Urban List, naturally).

There’s not much local scandal in the files — no juicy feuds, just lots of menus and a festival calendar. Sitchu even penciled in the Norwood Food & Wine Festival for April 12, 2026, which explains the sudden uptick in people practising “cheffy” hand gestures on the footpath.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe boutique chaos, can’t stand a salon with an award, or despise heritage pubs that double as social hubs, Norwood will make you twitch. Also, people allergic to good bread and decent wine should steer clear.

Who would love it? Savvy shoppers, families who like convenience, and anyone who enjoys living where alfresco dining used to be a competitive sport. We’ll keep polishing the terrace, queueing at Argo, and pretending we weren’t the ones who told Time Out we were cool.
