# Monbulk (NSW)

Slug: `monbulk-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean our source material is all charmingly pre‑2024 or undated — Monbulk felt like a slow‑motion romcom where the supporting cast are lyrebirds and tulips. We’ll own that: we’re the sort of place where your big Saturday morning decision is whether to stretch your legs at Grant’s Picnic Ground (70 Monbulk Rd) and pretend you’re on a nature documentary while crimson rosellas photobomb your selfie, or wander the fields at Tesselaar Tulip Farm (357 Monbulk Road, Silvan) when it used to host the KaBloom/Tulip festivals.

Mornings here used to be that specific kind of gentle chaos. Families would pile into the park, someone would suggest Grants on Sherbrooke for “light meals, coffee and scones,” and someone else would remember the Direct Radiology appointment at 66 Main Road. The local online mums’ pages and The Parents Hub used to be the unofficial town noticeboard — Masquerade Gala recollections, Repower Festival plans at Monbulk Primary School (the inaugural one apparently on 23 March 2025), and cheeky breathwork sessions at Nurture Creek, 110 Rankins Road. Community is practically our middle name.

There’s not much tabloid petty drama in the files we have — no council brawls, no pub scandals — just quiet civic pride. Even the nearby Belgrave mentions are genteel: Killik Handcrafted Rum (64 Monbulk Road, Belgrave) used to double as a daytripper cafe, and the venerable Micawber Tavern (65/71 Monbulk Rd, Belgrave) popped up in headlines about ownership changes. It’s all very “tea and a sensible chat.”

Who would hate living here? If you need late‑night glitter, constant city buzz, or you’re allergic to birds that act like they own the place (lyrebirds, cockatoos, rainbow lorikeets — they do), Monbulk will test your patience. Also, bring gardening gloves: the tulip post‑cards are not an optical illusion.

Who would love it? Families, festival lovers, people who measure happiness in scones and community fetes, and anyone who thinks a breathwork session at Nurture Creek sounds like prime weekend real estate. We’re small, a bit floral, and quietly stubborn — and honestly, we’d have it no other way.
