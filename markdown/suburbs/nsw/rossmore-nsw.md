# Rossmore (NSW)

Slug: `rossmore-nsw`

Back in the day we treated Rossmore like our private botanical speakeasy — an hour’s drive from the CBD, the kind of semi-rural idyll that answers the question “Where did all the succulents go?” with a shrug and a half-acre shrug more. Our Saturday ritual used to be appointment-only pilgrimages to Rare Plants and Succulents Nursery, where Margy — sorry, Margaret Cumurovic, depending on how formal you were feeling — presided over rows of cacti, agave and plants with names we could barely pronounce but loved anyway.

Yes, it was appointment-only (they were generally open Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday and Saturday), which made every visit feel like we’d mastered some exclusive members’ club. You rocked up with cash? Great. Prices started from $5, so you left pretending you were only buying one, then some 300 varieties later you were wheeling a boot full of prickly commitments back to civilisation.

If Rossmore has a personality, it’s Margy’s nursery: legendary and oh-so-knowledgeable, as Urban List put it. Broadsheet did the maths on our little patch of plant obsession too, noting the scope — half an acre of open-air plant hoarding that made us feel both smug and suspiciously green-thumbed.

Petty local drama? Only the kind that blooms when appointments clash and you discover your mate has nabbed the last rare agave. Nobody called the council; we argued over pot sizes instead. It’s low-stakes, earnest and mostly about whether you should have bought that $5 cactus named something dramatic.

Who would hate it here: people who need a shopping mall on every corner, someone who thinks half an acre is “unmaintainable” or anyone allergic to the idea of leaves that look like art declarations. If your weekends require cocktails and curated nightlife, Rossmore will reply with a cactus and a knowing look.

Who would love it: plant fiends, weekend escape artists, and anyone who thinks a legendary nursery run by Margy is the precise kind of eccentricity worth driving an hour for. We’ll always brag that our semi-rural corner had the kind of plant selection that made city folk jealous — and quietly, we’ll keep the appointments exclusive.
