# Strathmore (NSW)

Slug: `strathmore-nsw`

We know every turning of Woodland Street because for years we used to stroll down it like it was our main strip — heading to Pizzicotto at 68–70 Woodland Street, Manuel Masia’s cosy woodfired place that Broadsheet wrote up back in 2018. It felt like the sort of room where the pizza oven does the small talk and the traditional desserts quietly judge your life choices. Broadsheet even listed the place with its Tue–Sun evening hours, so yes, we remember when we pretended to be very cultured on a Tuesday.

Weekends had a quieter half‑life. For a while the restorative pottery space on 68 B Bulla Road was our excuse to muck about with clay and call it mindfulness: one‑off beginner sessions, four‑week intro courses, eight‑week Mindful Clay programs and the occasional Clay Retreat day when we needed to reset our pace before returning to real life. Sitchu wrote about it in that long list of nice things to do in Melbourne, and we kept turning up because it was cheaper than a therapist and came with actual mud.

If you read the roundups — yes, Sitchu even lumped us in with Ascot Vale, Moonee Ponds & Footscray when discussing Italian food — you’ll see what people think of Strathmore: quietly respectable, a touch domestic, and unexpectedly useful. Back in the day we were the suburb people drove through to get to somewhere else; now we like being the place people quietly remember fondly after an uninterrupted dinner and a pottery bowl that didn’t collapse.

Who would hate living here? If you want neon nightlife, rave‑level unpredictability or an identity that screams from every corner, you’ll be bored stiff. We’re not trying to be edgy; we’re trying to be steady — like a favourite pair of slippers, but the ones you’d admit to owning.

Who would love it here? Folks who enjoy a cosy woodfired pizza, a slow Saturday getting pleasantly messy with clay, and the kind of neighbourhood that’s content to be quietly good rather than loudly famous. We’ll keep Woodland Street’s oven warm for you — just don’t expect it to be brand‑new news.
