# Belgrave (NSW)

Slug: `belgrave-nsw`

Back in the day — and yes, we’re saying that because all our sources are older listings, so this is equal parts nostalgia and local gossip — we were the town at the end of the train line that Melbourne pretended not to know it loved. We’d pile off the Puffing Billy tourists, breathe in Sherbrooke Forest, and argue about whether the Cameo Outdoor Cinema was peak romance or peak damp picnic. The train’s glow-up for the Train of Lights (they ran a 2026 season, if you like glittered nostalgia) only made us smugder.

A weekend used to run like this: brunch at Earthly Pleasures Cafe in that 1930s stone building (our laptop people’s favourite in the old reviews), wander past where the Belgrave Emporium once housed ninety local makers until it was listed “permanently closed”, then catch a flick at Cameo or a walk on Dandenong Ranges tracks near where Killik Handcrafted Rum set up shop. Babaji’s Kitchen, the Kerala-run spot, cropped up in the write-ups as a bright, street‑food addition to town — all of which reads like a brochure we keep in a shoebox marked “why we stayed.”

Petty drama? We’ll take the humble catalogues: Micawber Tavern was sold to Julien Moussi’s Only Hospitality Group — plans for a refurb were announced in the pieces, so yes, the century-old boozer was due for a makeover. And the Emporium’s closure is still a local sore spot in the files — artisan dreams collide with rent ledgers, as ever.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to gumtrees, slow trains, community-run cinemas and festivals that insist on being alcohol‑free (we’re looking at you, Survival Day). If you need 24/7 city convenience, you’ll resent our quiet weirdness.

Who would love it: families who grew up at Belgrave Lake Park Cottage Playgroup, people who cheer at Puffing Billy events, weekenders who wanted the Dandenongs for a 30‑minute escape, and anyone who thinks a village with independent cinemas, lantern festivals and a rum distillery sounds exactly like the antidote to chain-store life.

We’ll keep tending the gumtrees and the odd indie cinema seat. Come for the steam train; stay because you forgot how to rush.
