# Balgownie (NSW)

Slug: `balgownie-nsw`

We know Balgownie by the sight of a Subaru Outback with a dusty paddleboard yawned on its roof rack, idling outside The Fig & Bean while a Cavoodle called Marmite sniffs the footpath like it's an unfolding novel. We do the 7am school run to Balgownie Public School in our Toyota RAV4, secreting a piccolo (oat milk) from The Fig & Bean between laps of the roundabout, and we will absolutely, loudly, discuss whether the new junior footy coach is "too intense." We wear Lululemon to the playground and call it ‘practical’; we drive Hyundai Konas with surf racks that have never seen saltwater and we keep spreadsheets for canteen duty like it's fiscal forecasting.

Our Saturday is a ritual in three acts: 6:30am coastal walk with Marmite along Woonona‑Balgownie Road, piccolo in hand; 9am junior soccer where we referee with the same intensity we reserve for PTA votes; then brunch at Balgownie Bakehouse where someone will politely correct your coffee origins while you nod and try not to spill avocado. By 3pm the Subaru’s paddleboard is still dusty and the backyard is half‑DIYed into a cricket-pitch fantasy we swear will be perfect by summer.

Our local drama could be its own reality series. There was the petition, printed and nailed to lamp posts on Hillcrest Avenue, after commuters started parking on residential streets to access the beach — heated comments and GIFs included. There was the two‑week Facebook meltdown when a passerby dared give The Fig & Bean three stars for “slow service”; regulars retaliated with a flood of five‑star reviews, barista selfies and strongly worded memes. And yes, bin collection is a sport here: leave your recycling out on a Tuesday and you’ll be publicly shamed by the strata WhatsApp by Wednesday.

Who would hate living here? The 25‑year‑old fixed‑gear inner‑city bar aficionado who cycles through King Street sneering at anyone in activewear, who finds driveway BBQs ‘very small‑town’ and cannot fathom PTA text chains or communal fury over a three‑star espresso. They’d be baffled by mulch debates and faint at the sight of a petition about parking.

Who would love it? The mid‑30s tidy‑townhouse owner with an underused surfboard on a roof rack, a Cavoodle named Marmite, Lululemon for weekends and a spreadsheet for volunteer days — who loves the gentle suburban policing, the coffee conversations, and the exacting, comforting smallness of our patch by the sea. Welcome.
