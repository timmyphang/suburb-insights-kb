# Cranbourne East (NSW)

Slug: `cranbourne-east-nsw`

We know the bit: Saturday morning, the prams line up like it's a small-town parade and someone is nursing a thermos they swear is essential. Back in the day our weekend ritual involved Heathland Circuit Playground — the very spot Wild Child Nature Play ran its Term 3 Cranbourne Bush Playgroup and those pop‑up bush sessions for 12‑month‑to‑6‑year‑olds. If you wanted to find them there, the booking page and the cheerful contact (wildchildnatureplay@outlook.com) used to be the only thing stopping us from turning the playground into a forest school permanent residency.

For years the local listings were the kind of thing Concrete Playground would tuck into their “find events in Cranbourne East” roundup — the vague, hopeful guide to bars, restaurants and cafes that made you imagine a night out that looked nothing like a school fundraiser. MumsPages, equally dutiful, has our postcode (3977) bookmarked across everything from baby support and play to upholstery services and even science lessons for kids. Yes, science lessons. Somewhere between the bush playgroup and the upholstery guy we learned about states of matter and thread tension.

And we cannot let our own name go unsaid: Royal Botanic Gardens Cranbourne — home of the Australian Garden, with its ponds, walkways and the very photogenic Red Sand Garden — was even named among the world’s top ten gardens. Cranbourne sits about 50 kilometres southeast of Melbourne, which is our polite way of saying we’re close enough for an escape, far enough that you’ll only see the city’s flashing lights on a special occasion.

Who would hate living here? People who can’t cope with kids’ chatter, community-run nature programs, or the idea that “a night out” might involve watching toddlers discover mud. Who would love it? Families who appreciate bush playgroups, parents who read MumsPages like scripture, and anyone who takes pride in having a world‑class garden on the end of their postcode.

We’re Cranbourne East — a bit earnest, a bit outdoorsy, and quietly smug about our Red Sand.
