# Girrawheen (NSW)

Slug: `girrawheen-nsw`

We live on the Marangaroo Drive beat — you know the one: where the lunch queue used to be a serious cultural ritual. As Broadsheet noted, BMT at 70 Marangaroo Drive was the family-run shop people swore by for banh mi thit, tri-coloured jelly and nem chua, and Urban List named Trang’s Vietnamese Cafe & Noodle House (Shop 11/70 Marangaroo Dr) as the pho that made Girrawheen feel like Perth’s unofficial noodle annex. We brag about that like it’s our civic duty.

A typical morning for us was a tactical mission: swing by 70 Marangaroo for a roll (Bánh Mì Vũng Tàu also got a name-check in the guides), nod at whoever was restocking SUPA VALU on 60 Marangaroo Drive, and dodge the takeaway traffic around the Hawaiian Centres where Wutang Fried Chicken earned cult-fave status. If we were feeling culinary ambitious, someone would mention Mirrabooka Village’s Hu Tieu Thanh Liem — Broadsheet even grouped Mirrabooka and Girrawheen in their Asian-eats map, so we winked and accepted our gastronomic destiny.

We’re not exactly tabloids of scandal — there’s no mayoral soap opera in the snippets — but the local storyline reads like community programming: playgroups flagged by Playgroup WA, parenting sessions at the Girrawheen Hub (Nom! Families, Circle of Security, INFANT) and library antics at 6 Patrick Court that once hosted Christmas Grooving and an Imagination Station. The Recycled Fairies used to lure us into miniature-garden envy. Even the school at 39 Calvert Way gets name-dropped on local listings.

Who would hate it here? People who need Michelin stars and valet parking — the headlines we have are about pho, playgroups, and public projects. Also anyone allergic to the idea of a 760-square-metre community precinct — Hawaiian’s Newpark Community Corner was approved with a full public basketball court, Firewise Garden, water play and nature play and was expected to open in September 2026 — because there will be noise, kids, and very public joy.

Who’ll love it here? Families using the Hub programs, students near Girrawheen Senior High, bargain shoppers at SUPA VALU, and anyone who believes a great life includes really good pho, a DIY fairy garden, and a library that stages a groovy sing-along. We’ll keep defending our Marangaroo strip with the fierce, fond loyalty of people who know where the best bowl of soup used to be.
