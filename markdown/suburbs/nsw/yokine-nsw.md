# Yokine (NSW)

Slug: `yokine-nsw`

We know the sound of Saturday at Yokine: kids squabbling over a new toy at the community hall, someone queuing for a bagel, and the faint, irresistible whiff of deep‑fried chickpea from the soccer pitch. Back in the day Broadsheet was right to single out Falafel Omisi — Yaakov Omisi’s family recipe that traced back to 1961 — tucked into Joe Rosen Oval beside the Maccabi teams. It was gloriously unlikely: a fast‑casual kiosk at a suburban pitch that somehow made hummus feel like a trophy.

Our weekend ritual used to be painfully specific. Morning: drop the kids off at Yokine Community Playgroup (26 Ranger Rd, corner of Blythe Ave) — they had a “new toy alert!” and the venue-hire rules you could quote at dinner. Midday: limp to Flinders Square on Wiluna Street for groceries; the shopping centre once housed Coles, Baker’s Delight and even a Pet Barn, which made walkies conveniently boring. If you wanted late‑night comfort food, Urban List remembered The Mighty Quinn at 112 Wanneroo Rd for those greasy Irish‑Chinese spice boxes that make hangovers feel like a lifestyle choice.

We’re not all suburban idylls and perfect brows — though Nakisa Yokine did get a tick from Urban List for eyebrow framing, because obviously our arch game is on point. And yes, we have our tabloid moments: Pedestrian ran a story about a police search in Yokine connected to an Olympic silver medallist. For a suburb that gets name‑checked for bagels and brow specialists, we like our headlines varied.

Who would hate living here? People who need their nightlife curated by neon and DJs. Folks allergic to hummus, spice bags or the faint whiff of goalkeeper sweat. Perfectionists who can’t tolerate a shopping centre that doubles as a community hub.

Who would love it? Families who value a proper playgroup and cheap, honest grub; food obsessives who remember Falafel Omisi’s punchy tahini; anyone who appreciates a suburb that quietly stacks authentic bagels, spice bags and community noticeboards without trying too hard. We’re Yokine — gloriously ordinary, blessedly specific, and stubborn about our pita.
