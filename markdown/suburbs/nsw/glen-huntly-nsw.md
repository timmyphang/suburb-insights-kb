# Glen Huntly (NSW)

Slug: `glen-huntly-nsw`

Back in the day our Saturday mornings used to look like a travel brochure written by someone who’d eaten one too many vanilla slices. We’d amble down Glen Huntly Road, past the places lifestyle sites used to fuss over: Broadsheet once wrote up Workshop Specialty Coffee at 1146 Glenhuntly Road, Urban List loved Antique Bar at 218 Glen Huntly Road, and Upsy Daisy at 1062 Glen Huntly Road was the family-friendly stop near Booran Reserve that bloggers gushed about.

As Time Out reminded readers, the 67 tram and the old Glenhuntly train station make us feel annoyingly well-connected — eleven kilometres feels like “close enough” when you can catch a tram to Monash Caulfield or hop the Frankston line. Sitchu used to sell the dream of parks and playgroups: Booran Reserve, Glen Huntly Park, and the Maternal and Child Health Centre all made us sound like suburbia’s answer to wellbeing.

Yes, we’ve had headlines too. Reports about incidents at Glenhuntly train station landed in Pedestrian TV and made national eyebrows raise. We don’t pretend that’s a fun chapter — it’s the sort of thing that gives locals a wry look and a “remember when” at the pub, not the sort of gossip you turn into a hashtag.

If you hate a bit of change, you’ll hate us: Concrete Playground pointed out Glen Huntly on a government map pencilled in for taller buildings — up to 12 storeys in spots. If you like your streets low, quiet and devoid of anyone daring to open a new bagel or cake shop, move on.

Who would love it here? The families who queue for playgroups at St Anthony’s or the Maternal and Child Health Centre, the students who like tram rides to Monash, the people nostalgic for a strip where Aviv’s decades-long bagel reputation and Quality Groceries used to be the sort of local brag that made guides write long, affectionate lists. We might not be flashy, but for people who want parks, public transport and a main street full of remembered favourites, Glen Huntly’s our quietly confident little stage.
