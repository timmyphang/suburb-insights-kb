# Leeming (NSW)

Slug: `leeming-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start the weekend like every sensible Leeming local: drop the small humans at Leeming Playgroup (the one that runs at Leeming Heights Community Hall on Westall Terrace), wave like we own the school pickup rota, then consult the blessed listicles to decide how fancy we can pretend to be.

As Broadsheet once noted, our most rebellious culinary flex was Ichirin — a tiny Japanese restaurant shoehorned into a former fish-and-chip shop that, somehow, served a $60 six-course omakase. Chef-owner Shiro Okuchi (yes, the ex-Nobu connection gets name-checked) turned suburban Leeming into a place that could embarrass inner-city diners... at least on paper. Urban List and Time Out have both flicked a torch this way in past roundups, which is our version of the Oscars: brief, slightly awkward acknowledgment, then back to the kids’ sport.

When it rains and morale dips, the one true saviour has been Inflatable World at 55 Farrington Road — the kind of indoor chaos that wipes a fortnight’s worth of whining off your calendar. If you need proof adults can survive suburbia, ask someone who’s negotiated a birthday party there.

Our local logistics are wonderfully unglamorous: there's the Leeming Lottery Centre & Newsagency keeping the Christmas cards hopeful and the spreadsheets of our lives stocked, Leeming Child Care Centre on 71 Calley Drive quietly doing the heavy lifting, and the Leeming Sports Association running things down at John Connell Reserve — home turf for anyone who takes their Saturday seriously.

Petty drama? The media attention tug-of-war over whether we’re “tucked away” or “underrated” is as close as we get. For years the local press has oscillated between calling Ichirin a hidden gem and wondering why anyone would travel outside the suburbs for dinner.

Who would hate living here: night-owl clubbers craving threeam kebabs and mood lighting on every corner. Who would love it: families, sports clubs, people who secretly enjoy a bargain omakase story to tell at barbecues, and anyone who appreciates that suburban quiet is just organised patience with better parking.

We’re Leeming. Not flashy. Slightly smug about our small triumphs. And yes — we’ll happily point you to the indoor playground when you visit.
