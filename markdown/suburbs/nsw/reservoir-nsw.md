# Reservoir (NSW)

Slug: `reservoir-nsw`

We know the exact soundtrack of a Reservoir morning: the ducks at Edwardes Lake Park doing whatever ducks do, someone stubbing a toe on a footpath while lugging a barbecue, and the distant rattle of a tram that promises civilisation. Back in the day the guides loved to tell that story — Time Out wrote up Edwardes Lake Park’s wetlands and barbecue spots, Broadsheet ran a hub listing La Pinta and Cono Gelateria, and Sitchu called us “the northside reality check” — affordable, with backyard space and train access.

Weekends used to be a gentle relay race of local comforts. We’d amble to the lake for a stroll, then remember Broadsheet’s old write-ups about La Pinta — the chalked-up, frequently changing tapas menu and that buzzy horseshoe bar (yes, getting a seat could be a mission). If we were feeling indulgent, we’d queue like everyone else for ice-cream: Broadsheet noted Cono Gelateria’s retro vibes and that locally made pink terrazzo bench, and there was even news about Luther’s Scoops opening a production-kitchen-style outpost in Reservoir to feed their other shops. Those pieces are from the archives now, so this is nostalgia, not a listing.

We don’t have an outrageous local feud to spill (the papers didn’t oblige), but we do have our civic pride. Reservoir Neighbourhood House gets a shout — Playgroup Victoria flagged it as a real community hub, accessible and unflashy, and St Stephen’s Playgroup has been doing the quiet, crucial work of wrangling toddlers for years. Sitchu’s nod to us as family-friendly and close to Preston Market wasn’t wrong.

Who would hate living here? People who want inner-north clickbait on speed dial, or anyone allergic to parks, honest old shops and the occasional production kitchen hum. Who would love it? Young families, picnic obsessive types, and anyone who appreciates a suburb that’s more substance than headline.

For years the local press and guides have told our story in bits and back pages — so if you think Reservoir is just a name on a map, come back with a picnic rug and an appetite for ice‑cream queues. We’ll be by the lake.
