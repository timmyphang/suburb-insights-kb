# Elwood (NSW)

Slug: `elwood-nsw`

For years we’ve loved Elwood like a slightly sandy cardigan you can’t quite throw out — sun-creased, a bit precious, but undeniably ours. Because the pieces we can find about the place are mostly from the archives, this is more a fond memory tour than a current events bulletin. Consider it Elwood: a greatest hits cassette, not the live album.

Weekends used to begin with a loop down Ormond Road Village: we'd duck into The Leaf Store at 111 Ormond Road for flowers, watch kids trail sand through Jerry’s Milk Bar on Barkly Street, and marvel at Arlo’s aquamarine windows and neon signage while thinking “yes, chicken waffles with kimchi butter was on a menu somewhere.” Repeat Offender — that Latin-ish, gluten-free experiment on the Ormond & St Kilda corner — felt like someone’s very tasteful holiday fling. Elwood Bathers sat on the foreshore doing what it does best: being a bistro that remembers the ocean exists.

Petty local drama? The internet had us on the map for a few awkward cameos: Pedestrian TV once put Elwood Beach on the “beaches to avoid” list for long weekends, and we briefly flirted with environmental notoriety when a vegetable oil spill story named our sand. And yes, Lune apparently started here in 2021 before becoming a headline elsewhere — we were quietly proud, then smug.

Who would hate living here: people who crave anonymous suburban blandness, those allergic to Art Deco charm and promenade promenading, and anyone who thinks off-leash dogs and a village vibe are character flaws (Concrete Playground did point out the dog-friendly stretch, so consider your tolerance level). If you can’t stand the idea of crowds on a sunny afternoon, Pedestrian’s long-weekend warning was probably your cue to move inland.

Who would love it here: small-family types who appreciate a playgroup run by volunteers (shoutout to Elwood Community Playgroup), beach lovers who prefer low waves and a foreshore lifesaving club, and anyone nostalgic for a place where the café scene, seaside bistro and art-deco apartments rub shoulders without much fuss. We’ll keep our sandy cardigan, thanks — sunburnt, slightly smug, and full of local stories that are best told over something sweet and very, very buttery (we know someone who started with croissants).
