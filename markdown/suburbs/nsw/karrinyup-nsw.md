# Karrinyup (NSW)

Slug: `karrinyup-nsw`

Back in the day when the papers were still writing about it, we could tell you the precise spot where daylight finally gave up and turned into retail: the West Deck at Karrinyup Shopping Centre. We remember when the centre’s big, new‑look roll‑out — the one Broadsheet called an ~$800 million makeover — felt like a small city arriving on our doorstep, complete with Sephora, Zara, H&M and Uniqlo elbowing for space.

We used to spend weekend arvos wandering the food precinct like it was a theme park. You could roll past Spice Market Karrinyup for the kind of seafood plates that make you Instagram remorseful, grab ramen from Super Souper Ramen opposite Super Tetsudo, and then argue with your group about whether Ciao Dandy (or Dandelion, depending which listing you read) was the rooftop you wanted for a drink with ocean views. The West Deck even had arcade energy — Holey Moley Karrinyup, Strike bowling and Archie Brothers Cirque Electriq made the shopping centre feel less like retail and more like a slightly smug indoor carnival.

For a while the place tried being everything at once: a wellness precinct with Alchemy Saunas, designer boutiques beckoned by Broadsheet and Sitchu, and yes, a July luggage flagship that made us feel unusually well‑packed. There was even a HOYTS APEX screen that prompted Concrete Playground to mention our multiplex pedigree. Families had Playdates Kids Cafe on speed‑dial and Yo! Hot Pot at 200 Karrinyup Rd for collective broth ambition. Out past the centre, Time Out remembered Karrinyup Waters Resort as a caravan‑friendly option.

We’ll admit: if you hate fluorescent lighting, queued elevators and the existential decision between Zara or Uniqlo, you’ll loathe living here. If your weekend joy is finding a quiet suburban lane with zero ambient mall muzak, this isn’t your postcode.

But if you love variety, three‑course indecision, and the comforting knowledge that you can go from hot pot to hole‑in‑one without getting in a car, you used to be our people. Just a heads up — our notes are from older pieces, so consider this more a nostalgia tour than a current listing. Still, we wear that mall‑born confidence like a local badge.
