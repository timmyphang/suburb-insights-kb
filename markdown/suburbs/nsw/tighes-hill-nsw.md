# Tighes Hill (NSW)

Slug: `tighes-hill-nsw`

Sunday mornings here still smell like sunscreen and zucchini seedlings. Back in the day, our ritual walk was a circuit that started and finished at Gross Street Reserve — the place the parenting blogs keep calling a "hidden gem." As NewyWithKids noted, we get two playgrounds in one reserve, a proper toddler area, undercover picnic tables and shade from mature trees. It’s the kind of local flex you silently brag about to visitors who think Newcastle suburbs are all coastline and cafés.

We also used to potter through the Tighes Hill Community Garden, pretending we planted heirloom tomatoes and actually remembering to water them twice a season. And if you ever received a box of improbably perfect pears, that was probably from An Apple A Day — listed at 173 Maitland Road in the old write‑ups — which offered fresh fruit and veggie boxes and door delivery for the wider Newcastle area. Those delivery subscriptions felt like a tiny, smug victory for people who buy vegetables on a Tuesday.

Official stuff: Tighes Hill Public School is the local K–6 school the neighbourhood orbits, and Playgroup NSW gets name‑checked when parents want group activities for under‑schoolers. KindiCare’s old suburb profile also reminded us there aren’t a glut of childcare centres here, so you learn your fellow parent’s coffee order by name because you’re at drop‑off together every week.

Petty local drama? The archives don’t give us a juicy council kerfuffle, so the worst scandal we can muster is passive‑aggressive ownership of the park bbq on weekends. The data’s older, so for all we know the fence has been painted and everyone’s moved on.

Who would hate it: anyone who needs endless retail, late‑night bars or the anonymity of a big suburb. Who would love it: small families, playgroup converts, gardeners with patience, and anyone who thinks two playgrounds in one reserve is peak civic ambition.

For now, our view of Tighes Hill comes from those earlier guides — a cosy, leafy pocket that’s quietly proud of its playgrounds, community garden and hometown delivery boxes. We’ll keep tending the seedlings and the gossip.
