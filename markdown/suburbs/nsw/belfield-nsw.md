# Belfield (NSW)

Slug: `belfield-nsw`

We know Belfield by the sight of a Lululemon jacket slung over a pram, an oat piccolo balanced in one hand from the Punchbowl Rd Café, and a Mazda CX‑5 idling politely in the drop zone like it’s doing community service. We stroll our Cavoodles to Belfield Park at 6:15am and compare weekend market finds like they’re heirlooms; we post a photo of kale and sourdough and call it culture. We’re soft‑inner‑west adjacent: earnest captions, practical SUVs.

Our morning is a ritual — piccolo at 8am from the little Punchbowl strip, a quick scroll through the Facebook mum groups, then a strategic shuffle into a Subaru Outback for school drop‑offs by 8:45. At 9:30 the kids are on the soccer field, at 11:00 someone announces “Bunnings run” in the group chat and suddenly we need a new hose and three timber screws. We finish the day with sushi and a neighbourly wine on the driveway while someone grills the lamingtons like it’s an Olympic sport.

The local drama is deliciously petty. Punchbowl Rd isn’t a battlefield until 8:05am when the double‑parkers appear and Granny’s spot becomes contested territory. There was the Great Sticky Note Campaign — colour‑coded Post‑its on windscreens declaring ‘illegally long shopping trip’ in fluorescent pink, then a passive‑aggressive flyer drop targeting a family who dared to park across two spaces. The café review thread on Facebook? That descended into weeks of ‘reply all’ virtue signalling, screenshots of receipts and an unofficial petition for timed parking that now lives in the community files next to the F45 group roster.

We have habits: activewear is business attire, our phones are full of school group chats and weekend market threads, and we will defend the sacredism of a properly pulled flat white. We position our cars with military precision near the Belfield Bakery for maximum croissant access and pretend to be outraged when anyone eats avocado with a knife and fork.

Who would hate Belfield? The late‑night experimental DJ who cycles everywhere, drinks black coffee and wants a year‑round calendar of underground art shows. They’d be baffled by our 7pm curfew on noise complaints, our almost spiritual reverence for efficient bin collection, and how any conversation can be rerouted into school canteen politics within three sentences.

And who would love it? The mid‑30s project manager with a Subaru Outback, an F45 membership, a Cavoodle and a relentless devotion to weekend markets and barbecues — because here the coffee is reliable, the neighbours are nosy but kind, and there is always a spare sausage on someone’s driveway. Welcome.
