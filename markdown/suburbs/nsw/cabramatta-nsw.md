# Cabramatta (NSW)

Slug: `cabramatta-nsw`

Back in the day we’d dodge the lunchtime crush and do a proper DIY food tour through Cabramatta’s maze of arcades and alleys — Time Out called it an immersion into Sydney’s biggest Vietnamese community and they weren’t wrong. We’d start on John Street: maybe grab something from KK Bakery or queue for Vinata’s Hot Bread (they’ve been around since 1988, which makes our memories vintage) and wander toward Pho Tau Bay for a bowl the guides swear has been slurped since the ’80s (as Sitchu reminds us).

We remember the tofu-making ritual at Hoa Hung Tofu, every batch a little ceremony, and when Kreamu first opened here with its tamarind and sticky-rice gelato, Broadsheet had us feeling proud that our suburb could birth a gelateria with punchy flavours. Air Lab’s souffle pancakes used to tempt the indecisive, and Rivareno’s gelato turned up on lists for best ice cream in Sydney — which, to be honest, is a daily argument at the local train platform.

Petty local drama? Oh yes — we had influencers and a restaurant called Silver Peal turn into a courtroom subplot, with accusations of faked food poisoning and a $364 bill getting more airtime than anyone wanted. And our past hasn’t always been Instagram-friendly: Junkee outlines the harsher chapters — the supervised injecting centre era — and we don’t pretend that was small potatoes. We own it, because it’s part of the story that made the place fiercely protective and, frankly, interesting.

Who would hate living here? Anyone allergic to noise, spice or cultural variety. If you like your suburb to come with one kind of takeaway and a single footpath, Cabramatta will make you twitch. If you love food that’s worth queuing for, festivals that turn the town centre into a proper cultural hub (yes, the Moon Festival — Concrete Playground marked its 25th anniversary), and playgroups for every language of childhood (shoutout to the Chinese and Vietnamese playgroups at Cabramatta Public School on Playgroup NSW), you’ll adore it.

We’re loud, delicious and a bit stubborn. Come for the banh mi and pho, stay for the stories — even the messy ones.
