# Berowra (NSW)

Slug: `berowra-nsw`

We can tell you it’s Berowra by the Prado with a kayak on the roof, a Cavoodle in a designer lead, and a Lululemon-clad mum saying “it’s practical” as she orders an oat flat white from the Berowra Station kiosk.  
We queue, we nod, we pretend the air is misty and not just last night’s ute exhaust.  
We wear Lorna Jane or Align leggings with Merrells, and call flannel a mood rather than a shirt.  
We call the ridge “our sunrise” and post the 6:15am snap like a manifesto.

Our weekend runs on ritual.  
6:30am oat flat white in hand, we wobble past the station carpark gladiators — Prado versus Forester is a spectator sport and double-parking at 7:45am is peak theatre.  
By 7:00am we’re on the 8km loop through Berowra Valley Regional Park, Kelpie and Cavoodle leading the pace, Hornsby Council notices pinging on our phones about track closures like friendly bureaucratic gossip.  
9:30am is sacred hardware time: Bunnings for rope, tarps, and that new swag of barbecue tools we absolutely need.  
Afternoon: smoked brisket, a crate of pale ale, and three neighbours arguing over sourdough starters on the driveway.  
Evening: plotting the sausage sizzle for the school fundraiser while someone demonstrates gazebo-folding technique.

Our small-town soap opera runs on Facebook.  
A “please pick up after your dog” post morphs into full-on declarations of who “saved the bushland” last summer.  
We’ve seen a single thread escalate from civility to the digital equivalent of someone waving a muddy boot.  
Neighbourly disputes about whether dogs should be on or off-lead on the walking tracks are more ferocious than any council meeting.  
We patrol the station carpark etiquette like it’s a sport — you can tell who’s been to Bunnings by how aggressively they measure parking lines.

Who would hate living here?  
The misfit who needs 24/7 cafes, late-night gigs, trams and a single-speed bike would shrivel.  
They refuse to own a car, think a commute should never include mud, and would weep at the sight of a kayak on a roof-rack.  
They want cosmopolitan anonymity; we want neighbourly know-how — someone to fold a gazebo without swearing.

Who would love Berowra?  
The mid-30s parent with a Prado, a Cavoodle and a Kelpie, who shops at Bunnings on Saturdays, wears Lululemon to the school gate, and swears by the morning bush reset.  
Welcome — we’ll lend you a rope, a sourdough tip, and a place by the barbecue.
