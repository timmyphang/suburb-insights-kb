# Minmi (NSW)

Slug: `minmi-nsw`

We roll out of bed to the same soundtrack every weekend: a caravan of mums, an occasional footy whistle and the distant clink of schooners at the pub. If you listen closely you can hear Woodford Street arranging the day's business — the Minmi Hotel (156 Woodford Street) still holds its country-pub crown, as Findglocal cheerfully listed, and the takeaway spot R U Hungry Minmi used to be the place we pretended was “just for the kids” (94 Woodford Street — yes, we all know where that is).

Back in the day there wasn't much press on us — so we lean into that. The property ad for 23 Bell Street reminded everyone that Minmi Public School is roughly a kilometre away, there's a local Aldi nearby, and Newcastle is about 16km if you fancy a proper shopping day. For us that means drop-off, a short drive, and the comforting knowledge that major link roads will get you to Cameron Park, Fletcher or Maryland without too many existential detours.

Our weekend ritual: a play at Minmi Reserve on McInnes Street (opened mid-2022 for the under‑8s), then a bush ramble in Blue Gum Hills Regional Park — the park that tells the story of the mining past and, yes, had an upgrade in May 2024 with a maze and an observation deck. The kids get worn out; we pretend the pub’s undercover beer garden is for "fresh air".

The closest thing we have to soap opera is the local Facebook feed. As Findglocal noted, Minmi FC at 96 Woodford Street keeps everyone emotionally invested with junior-football draws and facility feedback requests. Thrilling stuff. The Minmi Courthouse Restaurant used to pop up on lists too (40 Church Street, Newcastle) — nostalgia for those who like to remember places by their listings.

Who would hate living here? If you crave a booming cafe strip, Michelin‑level sushi scenes, or a late‑night neon skyline, you’ll find us gloriously underwhelming. Who would love it? Young families, bushwalkers, footy parents and anyone who appreciates a proper country pub, a playground that actually wears the kids out, and a town content with being its small, stubborn self. We’d argue that’s more than enough.
