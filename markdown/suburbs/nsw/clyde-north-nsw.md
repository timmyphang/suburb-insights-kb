# Clyde North (NSW)

Slug: `clyde-north-nsw`

We know Clyde North by the squeak of a swing and the gentle distant hum of estate developers, because most of the stories we’ve got are from “back in the day” — older write-ups, playgroup yarns and a curious golf-simulator flex. So forgive us if this reads like a nostalgia tour with a punchline.

Mornings here used to look identical in every suburban parenting column: we’d herd small humans to Banjo Park’s waterplay — pumps, balancing logs and all — then wander the newish green spaces. Orana Park was the one with the Skywalk and a giant slide, plus those sky-high cubbies and sculptural bits by Geoffrey Ricardo & Shlomit Moria. Clara Park cropped up in the Smiths Lane development with undercover BBQs and, memorably, a nearby café called The Humble Merchant. Lacey Park even stole identity points by being inspired by the native Lace Monitor Goanna. As Playgroup Victoria noted, the Selandra Community Hub watched farm paddocks turn into kinders and community life; Selandra Playgroup itself grew from three mothers in April 2015 to a proper waiting-list ménage.

If you like your local news tidy and wholesome, our “drama” reads like a brochure. Urban List once reported a very earnest headline-crossover: X-Golf in Clyde North opened — a 611 sq m complex with six golf simulators, a lively bar and a full kitchen. This is the only time our suburb’s name shared a sentence with “world-class” and “coaching” and we cherish that ambition, even if the articles we have are older.

Who would hate living here? People who measure a suburb’s worth in late-night laneway bars or anonymous 24/7 neon. Also anyone allergic to playgrounds, community hubs, and art on a roundabout.

Who would love it? Families, first-home hopefuls who like a backyard and a BBQ shelter, anyone who remembers when a playgroup could change a person’s life (thanks, Selandra Playgroup), and golfers who prefer simulators to wet weather excuses.

We’re not pretending this is the new Fitzroy — our clips are from earlier seasons — but if you want earnest parks, ambitious public art and a community that grew out of playgroups, Clyde North has been quietly, proudly doing that work for years.
