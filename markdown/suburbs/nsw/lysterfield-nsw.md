# Lysterfield (NSW)

Slug: `lysterfield-nsw`

Back in the day, travel guides used to gush about our patch of scrub and water — and honestly, we don’t blame them. We drag ourselves out of bed on the weekend, head to Lysterfield Lake for a paddle in one of the two protected swimming bays, then scrape the mud off our shoes and point the tyres into Lysterfield Park, which Concrete Playground once described as roughly 1,400 hectares of mountain‑bike trails, walking tracks and bushland. Time Out also noted the park’s around 20 kilometres of bike trails and that the lake was the go‑to for sailing and canoeing. Sounds humble. Feels huge.

Our weekday ritual used to look more like chauffeuring small humans: MumsPages listed school holiday camps and activities in Lysterfield, and CareforKids’ listings showed there were 40 childcare services in the postcode, with an average cost — yes, people do talk about this — of about $147.78 a day. There were also 17 pre‑schools and kindergartens on the books, and the listing said 9 of those had vacancies. If you’re keeping score, that’s a lot of snacks and a lot of forms.

We don’t have a dramatic town square argument to spill — no council strikes, no scandalous cafe wars in the files we were given — so our local soap opera is mainly about who claims the best launch spot for a canoe. The nearest organised playgroup the snippets mention is Taylors Lane Playgroup in Rowville, which has always been happy to welcome families from Lysterfield. Practical, neighbourly, slightly damp at the knees.

Who would hate living here? People who crave neon nightlife, endless restaurant choices, or manicured city parks where you can’t find a stick to poke the water with. Who would love it? Anyone who thinks a day that ends with mud on your tyres, a quiet paddle at sunset, and a kid who can’t stop talking about “the big climb” is absolute perfection.

We know what they say about outer‑edge suburbs — but they missed the bit where we have real bush, real water, and enough childcare options that the argument over who does pick‑up is a weekly sport.
