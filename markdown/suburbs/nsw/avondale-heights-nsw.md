# Avondale Heights (NSW)

Slug: `avondale-heights-nsw`

We wake up in Avondale Heights to the comforting whirr of someone rolling a 36‑hour‑fermented dough down the street — yes, Impasto Forno Antico used to be our bakery landmark, the sort of place where the oven smelled like proper Italian commitment. Our Riviera Road was never loud, except for that one tiny miracle: Melbourne’s very own Cannoli Bar at 23 Riviera Rd. Back in the day the place made something like 1,000 cannoli a day, and yes, Freddy’s Pizza on Chapel Street used to admit it relied on Cannoli Bar for the shells. We know that, because Broadsheet and Concrete Playground told us so — we’re not making the cannoli connection up.

Full disclosure: our sources are all pre‑2024, so this is nostalgia with receipts. Ether at 51 Military Road used to be the spot for indulgent brunch experiments and speciality coffee; Particle Cafe cropped up in those guides as the local 100%‑vegan option — the sort of place you’d stumble out of the Maribyrnong River Trail and into after pretending to be fit. The Avondale Heights Community Gardens were also a recommended pit stop on that trail; we like to tell visitors it’s where our green thumbs go to swap seedlings and gossip.

Our community has its quieter, earnest side too. The Avondale Heights Grandparents Playgroup was celebrated enough to get a Hansard shoutout — Elizabeth Vescio’s group even inspired a parliamentary mention from Ben Carroll on 10 August 2017. We’re proud of that: grandparents running playgroups with waiting lists is peak Avondale Heights.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe Sicilian desserts, slow‑leavened loaves, community gardens and the idea of grandparents running a social calendar, you’ll be miserable. Also, if you prefer your suburbs to come with a louder personality or fewer generous cannoli shells, maybe keep driving.

Who would love it here? Families, folks who like old‑school Italian bakery skills, cyclists doing the Maribyrnong loop, and anyone who appreciates a neighbourhood that quietly supplies Chapel Street with dessert shells. Avondale Heights: low drama, excellent pastries (at least according to the archives), and enough community pride to make even the grandparents eligible for a round of applause.
