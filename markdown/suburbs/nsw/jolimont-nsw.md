# Jolimont (NSW)

Slug: `jolimont-nsw`

We live in Jolimont, NSW — but honestly, try Googling us and the internet keeps apologising and pointing you to someone else. The Jolimont everyone writes about is either the neat little pocket in Subiaco (Bishop Street, postcode 6014) or the Jolimont station that’s forever being name‑checked as the closest stop to Rod Laver Arena. We get mistaken for a postcode that does reformer Pilates and Classique Dance, and for a train stop that’s on every concert guide between Flinders Street and Richmond. Classic.

Mornings here feel like a civil identity crisis. We commute with the smug satisfaction of people who’ve lived in a place long enough to know the real corners, but if we mention Jolimont to out‑of‑towners they immediately start talking about tram routes 70, 75 and the Flinders Street walk past Federation Square. As Urban List and Time Out kept reminding everyone back in the day, Jolimont Station is the convenient little sibling to Richmond and Flinders Street for Rod Laver Arena events — which means our name gets shouted in concert logistics more than in local newsletters.

There isn’t much spicy local drama in the archives — mostly useful entries: Concrete Playground once flagged an Agnes Street apartment block in development plans, and Perth mums were extolling a Freemove Pilates studio on Bishop Street. So no council coups or barista battles to regale you with. The real story is the sameness of it all: our identity is a map pin that tourism and music writers keep borrowing.

Who would hate living here? People who crave being unique; folks who hate queuing after a big show when every guide drops “Jolimont Station” into their travel tips. Who would love it? If you like being quietly useful — the place other suburbs borrow when they need a handy walk, a station, or a cameo in a concert guide — you’ll find us charmingly anonymous.

Back in the day, Sitchu and the concert guides made us sound like a scenic stop on a Flinders Street to Jolimont stroll. We’ll take that. We’ll keep being the suburb everyone borrows and no one quite owns.
