# Heathmont (NSW)

Slug: `heathmont-nsw`

We start our Saturdays like proper Heathmont locals: bleary-eyed, folding a playmat in the car while checking the Playgroup Victoria listing to remember whether the session’s at Heathmont Baptist Church or the Heathmont kindergarten. For years those Venues were the heartbeat of our weekend — toddler chaos, earnest fundraising bake sales and someone inevitably asking, “Is that the PlayConnect+ list?” (As the PDF once showed, yes, we were on it.)

Back in the day Broadsheet dragged us into the metropolitan drama too — Yarra Valley Water’s precautionary boil-water warning included Heathmont among 88 suburbs, which briefly turned us into connoisseurs of boiled tea and canned-mineral-water etiquette. We handled it, like we handle most things: with a shrug and extra biscuits.

Food-wise, we quietly flex our provenance. Our Place — the Heathmont bakery Broadsheet mentioned — once teamed up with Sebastian Pasinetti on a purple Pride fruit loaf (pick-up details? yes, at the Heathmont shop). Our loaves have pedigree: Concrete Playground noted our Our Place bread turned up alongside Blanc Bakery at Breadcetera, and Chrystalla’s even served casseroles with crusty bread from Our Place. If you find artisanal pastry crumbs in the glovebox, you’re not alone.

Sitchu once pointed out 157 Canterbury Road as part of our dining map, and the same site tipped us off to local makers like Heathmont Honey — golden jars of morning smugness for toast and market bragging rights.

Who would hate living here? People who can’t stand being recognised at the farmers’ stall, who loathe warm, sticky honey jars, or who get twitchy at the thought of a church hall doubling as a community playgroup. Also, anyone allergic to the very idea of a suburban gym list — LocalFitness used to profile our top gyms, pools and personal trainers for exactly that reason.

Who would love it here? Families who value playgroups and kindergartens, bread obsessives who remember the Our Place x Pasinetti loaf, and anyone who considers a modest water-warning anecdote a good story for the next pub trivia. We’ll take our small-town weirdness and artisanal crumbs any day.
