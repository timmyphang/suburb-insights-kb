# Auburn (NSW)

Slug: `auburn-nsw`

We stand at the corner where the garlic-sauce haze meets the Botanic Gardens’ morning light — a Prado idling, a Cavoodle on a retractable lead, and the baker on Auburn Road slipping two almond croissants into a paper bag like it’s a state secret. We brag about single-origin espresso and then tuck a sachet of instant Nescafé into our tote because honesty and convenience are both things we enjoy. We are the people who call ourselves “low-key foodies” while ordering a piccolo and smashed avo on artisan sourdough, then defending the best pide like it’s a World Cup.

Our Saturday starts with a sunrise walk through Auburn Botanic Gardens, the kids chasing ducks, and us pretending the eucalyptus breeze is part of our mindfulness routine. We swing by the bakery opposite the gardens for two almond croissants, flash our loyalty card like a badge, then file through an IKEA run at noon because the Billy shelf finally needs a sibling. By afternoon we’re on the sideline of the kids’ soccer pitch, thermos in one hand, a speaker playing a productivity podcast in the other, and a Toyota Prado parked three-quarters over the footpath — stage presence is real.

Our daily rites are charmingly specific. Morning piccolo. Quick scroll of the Auburn community Facebook group for the latest kerfuffle. Twice-weekly Bunnings reconnaissance for perfect pavers. Drop-off at soccer training. Repeat. Drama in our feed is theatre: a full-blown thread about who stole the kerbside parking space morphs into a planning-free debate on roundabouts and tyre marks. Someone tags the councillor. Someone else calls for a referendum on tree pruning. A local influencer loses their mind when a lane of Auburn Road is closed for roadworks and half the suburb declares a culinary emergency. There are bin disputes that could be made into a three-act play, and strata battles over whether the balcony tiles are “in keeping” with the neighbourhood vibe.

We keep it petty and fiercely defended because that’s how we show we care. We will argue about five minutes of brunch lateness like it’s a national emergency and we will fiercely protect our favourite baker’s recipe with the gentle ferocity of people who have opinions and carbs.

Who would hate living here? The barefoot minimalist cyclist with one tote, a Brompton and two shirts would last twelve minutes; they hate idling SUVs, crave quiet lanes and think cars are a lifestyle choice. Here, cars are culture, convenience is king and delivery runs at 2am are a feature, not a bug — they’d be exiled to the inner-city laneway by lunchtime.

Who would love it? Young families who own matching activewear, loyalty cards and a Prado; night-shift bakers; anyone who believes garlic sauce solves most problems. Welcome — you’ll never be bored, and you’ll never be far from a croissant.
