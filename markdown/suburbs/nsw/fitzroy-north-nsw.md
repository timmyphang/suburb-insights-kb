# Fitzroy North (NSW)

Slug: `fitzroy-north-nsw`

We’ll be honest: when we walk out our door in Fitzroy North we half expect a brass band and a picnic blanket to materialise. Back in the day — or, more accurately, in the collection of guides and reviews we could dig up — this place reads like Melbourne’s boutique village with a drinking problem and very good pies.

Morning routine: we squirrel the pram past Edinburgh Gardens, drop the rug at Bargoonga Nganjin (the North Fitzroy Library) for storytime or playgroup, and if someone’s feeling fancy there used to be a queue for Lune’s rare delivery when it covered Fitzroy North. For lunch we’d confess to having once ordered a pie from the North Fitzroy Arms — Broadsheet and Sitchu both tip them for comfort food — and yes, Concrete Playground noted the Arms got a slick bistro refresh while the old front bar stayed stubbornly itself. The Arms’ menu was even flirting with apricot chicken and sticky date pudding as nods to history. We’re not saying we’re cultured, but we can pronounce “Bargoonga Nganjin” without looking it up.

There’s local reinvention too: the Pinnacle reopened under new owners and, according to Broadsheet, treated the neighbourhood to $25 pot‑and‑parma nights. The Royal Oak (442 Nicholson Street) earned praise in the Urban List pub round‑up, and Neighbourhood Wine and Public Wine Shop — the latter with sommelier Campbell Burton and chef Ali Currey‑Voumard historically attached — keep the after‑work wine chat thriving. Vintage hunters: Error404 has been pointed out by Sitchu for dragging old glamour into our lanes.

Petty local drama? Mostly polite: a banana statue once caused more world news than any council meeting ever will (John Oliver even offered to buy it), and the Arms’ facelift was discussed like a family inheritance. If you’re after shouting match energy or neon club hours, you’ll be bored. If you hate people who garden, do life drawing or get enthusiastic about community bee workshops at Rushall Community Garden, this isn’t your postcode.

Who hates us: night-shift ravers, anonymity enthusiasts, and anyone allergic to terraces. Who loves us: families who use Bargoonga Nganjin, wine nerds, vintage browsers, and anyone who likes their pubs a little storied and their parks enormous. We’ll keep the village charm; you bring the rug.
