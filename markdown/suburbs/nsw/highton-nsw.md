# Highton (NSW)

Slug: `highton-nsw`

We know Highton by the way the sun hits Barabool Road at 8:30am and how long it takes a toddler from Roslyn Playgroup to lose interest in the toy box. We’ve clocked those small, repeatable moments so often they feel like civic rituals.

Back in the day, as Broadsheet noted, 63 Degrees on 87 Barabool Road was the rustic, vintage heartbeat of our brunch economy — the kind of place you’d point to and say “that’s Highton” while pretending you weren’t secretly timing the queue. We’ll admit it: a large part of our civic pride is quietly knowing where the best parking is on Barabool Road (no, we’re not telling).

Our week is punctuated by community practicalities. Roslyn Playgroup, which used to run weekly two‑hour sessions for little ones from the multipurpose room at the Highton Child and Family Centre (74 Bellevue Ave), left an imprint on mornings here — the FAQs even once mandated adults stay, supervise and turn up to the beloved toy wash night. If you’ve ever judged a playgroup timetable with the intensity of a match review, you’ve been one of us.

We’re also keeping an eye on the wider Geelong glow-up. Concrete Playground’s chatter about the new Crowne Plaza and Chin Chin’s one‑year pop‑up at Kardinia Park reminds us Highton is no island — footy fans and out‑of‑towners ripple through our streets, and we secretly enjoy the spillover.

Who would hate living here? If your ideal day is perpetual rooftop cocktails, a Michelin crawl, or a 24/7 nightclub, Highton will feel like a polite timeout. If you demand constant novelty, the steady, dependable rhythms of Bellevue Ave and Barabool Road will feel… gently aggravating.

Who’ll love it? Families who understand the sacredness of a toy wash night, people who appreciate a quiet street you can actually park on, and anyone who enjoys being close enough to Geelong’s city ambitions to reap the benefits — without having to explain what “Kardinia Park” is to visiting relatives.

We’re proud of our small rituals, our local institutions (even the ones chronicled in old listings), and the way Highton still feels like a place that knows its own name.
