# Stockton (NSW)

Slug: `stockton-nsw`

We know Stockton like the soles of our sand-scraped thongs. Back in the day the guidebooks treated us like a secret — a short walk to Stockton Beach for swims and slow mornings, a hop over from Newcastle via the ferry terminal and suddenly the city's noise is just a memory. The papers loved the headline bits: Stockton Beach is a 32‑kilometre stretch, the dunes are officially “the largest moving coastal dunes in the Southern Hemisphere,” and some of them crest up to 40 metres high. Yes, we live in the shadow of a sand mountain — and we wouldn’t have it any other way.

Morning routine: we’d amble out to the Worimi Sand Dunes, past the Worimi Forest Walk if we were feeling virtuous, and either watch the light hit the ripples or join the chaos — sandboarding, quad riding, the lot. Urban List and Time Out have been telling this story for years; Urban List even name‑checked Sand Dune Adventures for the full bush‑to‑beach thrill. If you’re itching to drive where the sand meets the horizon, the park’s Recreational Vehicle Area gives four‑wheel‑drivers access to more than 19km of beach and over 350 hectares of dune driving. It’s gloriously, gloriously messy.

Petty local drama? Our press files are mercifully light — mostly dune porn and activity round‑ups. There’s a “stockton rush” tag on Pedestrian.TV if you’re hunting for clicks, but no mayoral scandals or thousand‑member Facebook fights in the archives we’ve seen. For years the story about Stockton has been: dunes, dunes, and more dunes.

Who would hate living here: anyone who prefers their carpets sand‑free, their shoes clean and their weekends predictable. Also people who think a “walk to the shops” shouldn’t involve negotiating a dune.

Who would love it: the sand addicts, the sandboarders, the people who buy a sunscreen the size of their head and never regret it, the day‑trip hunters from Sydney and the ferry commuters to Newcastle who like the easy escape. For years the travel pages have been right about one thing — Stockton is unapologetically about sand, space and the kind of small‑town saltiness that’s impossible to tidy away.
