# Wakeley (NSW)

Slug: `wakeley-nsw`

Back in the day our Saturday ritual was gloriously unrefined: we’d roll out of bed, argue about beetroot on burgers, and make our way to Bulls Road where Superior Burger opened in Wakeley in 2016. Time Out remembered the place as a quiet pocket of outer western Sydney with neon signs, plastic palm trees and 1970s wood‑panelled walls; Broadsheet loved that the Wakeley shop was “10 minutes away from our farmer,” and Concrete Playground cheered when Jovan Curic took over that old takeaway shop. Those days felt like our little nostalgia bubble — potato scallops and dry‑aged beef, the whole lot.

Full disclosure: all the mentions we found are older pieces, so we’re reminiscing rather than giving you a live events guide. Superior even featured in Burgapalooza delivery lists once, and its creator kept the Wakeley core menu alive while doing things like the Superior Express bus and weekly residencies elsewhere — Pub Life Kitchen closing didn’t mean Superior disappeared from conversation; it just moved the drama to different parts of town.

We’re not all burgers and neon. Families know this postcode for the practical stuff: Wakeley Early Learning Centre on Humphries Road was listed with No Vacancies, a 4.2 rating and fees shown around $88/day on CareforKids, while aggregated listings note dozens of childcare and preschool options across the 2176 patch (CareforKids counted 42 services, average cost about $110/day). If you’ve got kids, Wakeley has the infrastructure — and if you want them doing kata before school, Kenyukan Okinawa Goju Ryu Karate‑Kobudo runs out of King Park School at 31 Humphries Rd.

We also have the small-business backbone: pest controllers like EMK Termite & Pest Control and a whole directory of cake makers and pregnancy‑massage listings that mean we can sort births, birthdays and bugs without leaving suburbia.

Who would hate it here? If you live for buzzy inner‑city cafe strips and boutique everything, Wakeley — described as a quiet outer‑west pocket — will feel like a long bus trip to excitement. Who’d love it? Families wanting practical services, burger nostalgics who remember the neon and scallops, and anyone who likes a suburb that does the ordinary things very well.

And yes, we remember the grim headlines too — serious stuff happened here, and it’s part of our patchwork. But mostly, we’re proud of our humble little slice of 2176.
