# Bronte (NSW)

Slug: `bronte-nsw`

We know Bronte like the backs of our salty hands. We wake to the waft of Iggy’s Bread on Macpherson Street, shuffle through Bronte Park with a thermos we’ll probably forget, then convince ourselves a dip in Bronte Baths will fix whatever mood we brought to the beach.

Back in the day the guidebooks were unanimous: Macpherson Street was our high street—Locale Bronte, The Bogey Hole Cafe, Favoloso Espresso Bar & Deli, Coop and the long-standing Bronte General Store all got namechecked as the kind of places you’d bump into someone you vaguely know and pretend you were trying to avoid. Table Manners arrived as a shiny Euro-leaning neighbour, Moxhe and Vacanza kept the seafood faithful enthused, and Bronte Road Bistro reemerged as Bronte Road Fish with tuna smash burgers, katsu prawn burgers and a laneway soft-serve window that made post-swim decisions very easy.

Our coastline has always done the scenic strolls better than anyone—Bondi to Bronte and the rest of the Bondi–Coogee route drag a steady stream of walkers past our cliffs and the Baths. It’s also given us civic projects to gossip about: one story after another followed the roughly $24 million overhaul of Bronte Surf Life Saving Club, a makeover that promised better lifeguard digs, a rooftop garden and a new lease on local pride. Waverley Council even asked the neighbourhood about upgrades to the Bronte Ocean Pool; there was a rock slip at North Bronte that reminded everyone the cliffs don’t care if you’re on your phone.

We’ll admit the suburb isn’t perfect. People who hate coastal clambering, construction dust, or the idea of sharing a patch of sand with enthusiastic walkers and families will find it trying. And the place has had its ugly headlines—there was an incident on the beach that ended with a man charged over allegedly taking intimate images; reminders about safety and vigilance aren’t just council talk, they’re real life here.

Who loves Bronte? Families, swimmers, anyone who lives for a sunrise lap at the Baths, seafood fans and the kind of people who’ll argue the merits of a soft-serve cone versus a post-walk aperitif. We’ll keep walking, swimming and buying bread from Iggy’s—because where else would we rather be?
