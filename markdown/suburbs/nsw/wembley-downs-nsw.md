# Wembley Downs (NSW)

Slug: `wembley-downs-nsw`

Back in the day, on a Saturday that somehow felt like everyone’s idea of “quiet suburbia”, we’d wander past Weaponess Road and pretend we hadn’t come for the spa appointments and the mini golf selfies.

Mornings in Wembley Downs used to involve a very specific circuit: someone booked into Spa Society at 136a Weaponess Road for eyebrows and existential calm, the other half vanished into Ocean Cosmetics (also at 136A) for “consultations”, and by noon we were pretending the round of mini golf at 200 The Blvd was sport. The nearby 300 Acres — tucked into the middle of the Wembley Golf Course at 200 The Boulevard — was where you’d sip something vaguely restorative and agree that the greens make everything look expensive.

We’d queue up for pizza at Monsterella, the white‑walled joint Broadsheet praised for being effortlessly suburban and family-friendly, then slide along to Mummucc, the 60‑seater sibling that “stretched even further” than the mothership. There was comfort in familiarity: The Stanley was, as Broadsheet put it, “laid-back, welcoming and completely lacking in pretention.” And then, in classic suburban plot twist, The Stanley closed in February and Albert’s arrived as the new neighbourhood small bar — locals took the Stanley’s disappearance personally, chalking it up as another Covid casualty.

The petty local drama? That melodrama over whether the town had a proper small bar scene. We watched the handwringing, the “where will we drink?” columns and then silently judged anyone who treated bar closures like the end of civilisation. We’ve had our fair share of replacement acts.

Who would hate living here? People chasing nightly cocktail trends or avant‑garde nightclub theatrics — as Broadsheet promised, this place never tried to be pretentious. Also, if you loathe playgroups, you’ll despair: for years the Wembley Playgroup ran sessions out of the Community Centre on 40 Alexander Street and even scored a Lotterywest grant to build a nature playground, so mornings are still full of prams and singalongs.

Who would love it? Families who want community, gentle small‑bar politics, and a mini golf selfie to prove they left the house. We’ll take the spa appointments, the pizzas, and the awkward local drama any day — it’s our kind of quiet chaos.
