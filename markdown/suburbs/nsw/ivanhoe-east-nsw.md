# Ivanhoe East (NSW)

Slug: `ivanhoe-east-nsw`

We know Ivanhoe East like the back of our hand — the slow, leafy kind of suburb that insists on heritage facades and a polite nod before you cross the street. Sunday mornings used to be a ritual: a wander through East Ivanhoe Village past the little boutique shops Time Out loved to namecheck, then a stop where All Are Welcome once set up shop for its hulking sausage rolls and kouign‑amanns. As Broadsheet noted, that bakery became a local favourite — and then, heartbreakingly, it called it quits and the Northcote, Thornbury and Ivanhoe East stores shut (Broadsheet updated June 19, 2025). We still miss the smell.

For years we’d pop into the old heritage building for a glass at Vinoshis — Broadsheet even quoted Chef Elias Salomonsson saying it “nailed the vibe.” It felt proper: natural drops from here and Europe, charcuterie, the kind of low‑key sophistication that says, “Yes, we like a good wine and a seat with a view of the elm tree.” Time Out’s writeups about East Ivanhoe Village summed it up — unique cafés, restaurants and historic architecture, all quietly doing their thing.

The petty local drama, then, becomes the gentle kind: shops that quietly vanish, new openings that promise comfort. Sitchu recorded Baba’s Deli arriving in Ivanhoe (soft opening noted as Wednesday 13 May 2026 at 229 Upper Heidelberg Road) with free focaccia — proof that the Village keeps trying to replace the bakery-shaped hole in our hearts.

Who would hate living here? People who want late‑night chaos, neon signs and a tramkerb-level soundtrack — Ivanhoe East prefers steam rooms and pilates at Relinque, weekend playgroups at the Ivanhoe Library & Cultural Hub (Playgroup Victoria listings attest to the regular sessions), and quiet cricket on the green. If you can’t handle a Thursday night that’s indistinguishable from Sunday lunchtime, this isn’t your postcode.

Who’d love it? Families who value playgroups and community rooms, wine lovers who fondly remember Vinoshis’ pours, anyone who thrills at period homes and a village that still cares about tradition. We’ll raise a glass — in memory of All Are Welcome’s kouign‑amann, and in hope of the next small shop that makes us queue on a Sunday.
