# Wangi Wangi (NSW)

Slug: `wangi-wangi-nsw`

We know the exact hour the peninsula exhales: that golden five minutes when the houseboats line up at 287 Watkins Road and everyone pretends they didn't spend the afternoon arguing about whether to stay in for the RSL raffle. We are Wangi — properly Wangi Wangi — and yes, we call it Wangi.

Weekend ritual: we pile into the car and drive past Hotel Wangi on 230 Watkins Road (it’s been feeding generations, if FindGlocal is to be believed), check the WANGI RSL posts for Sunday live music and the membership draw (May 2026, very specific, very lucrative if you like small-town suspense), then shuffle down toward the water. If you’re on a houseboat with Wangi and Beyond Houseboats you’ll be the one parked at 287 Watkins Rd being yelled at by somebody’s phone alarm because the booking rules demand you get there at least 3.5 hours before sunset. Yes, really — bring a book, or three.

Back in the day Newy with Kids talked up Dobell Park, Wangi Point and the Kent Place boat ramp — the kind of family-guide stuff that makes us smile and roll our eyes. Those places have long been where kids learned to fish, families picnicked, and kayaks disappeared toward Pulbah Island. That write-up belongs in the scrapbook, not the noticeboard, but it explains why grandparents still point and say, “There, that’s the lake.”

Petty local drama? The RSL’s raffles and weekly promos (they post them; we read them) are our soap opera. And boat-booking rules make for surprisingly earnest online debates in the community page — which, full disclosure, is the heart of Wangi: posts, news and the kind of updates only a narrow peninsula can produce.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to holiday crowds on a narrow spit of land, or who wants a suburb that never has an excuse for a live band on a Sunday. Who would love it: families, lake-lovers, and anyone who enjoys telling visitors that the town has an artistic claim — William Dobell lives in the conversation — and that we are, definitively, big water.
