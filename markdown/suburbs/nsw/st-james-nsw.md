# St James (NSW)

Slug: `st-james-nsw`

Back in the day we’d surface from St James Station, blink into Hyde Park’s trees and wonder whether breakfast belonged on a sunlit terrace or in a church crypt. That was the paradox of our little sandstone patch: pious columns on one side, the kind of subterranean drama that makes tourists whisper.

We used to duck into Jardin St James, which set up in the forecourt and crypt of St James Anglican Church on King/Macquarie Street — yes, the crypt — and pretended that a Niçoise tasted classier when eaten beneath Georgian architecture. Metro St James used to sit like a Parisian impostor against the station’s heritage sandstone, with views over Hyde Park and a barista counter that made the morning commute feel slightly less like exile. Broadsheet and Concrete Playground wrote about it like we were a curated mood board; we rolled our eyes and took another croissant.

The real local soap opera, though, has always been subterranean. Time Out and Sitchu told us about the century‑old tunnels 20 metres beneath the CBD: Bradfield’s ghosts, WWII air‑raid shelters, and a $1‑million restoration that promised guided immersive tours. The NSW Government even floated turning disused platforms and tunnels into a dining precinct — Time Out said they were seeking expressions of interest — which had us picturing fine dining with damp brick and a side of echo. It’s the kind of council-to-developer fantasy that gives our local identity its theatrical bent.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who can’t deal with people insisting breakfast is “so atmospheric” when the venue used to be a crypt, or who recoils at the idea of culinary experiences pitched against 1920s tunnel masonry. Also, if you’re allergic to sandstone and polite civic hubbub, you’ll find St James trying your patience.

Who would love it? Heritage nerds, secret‑tour believers, and anyone who thinks Hyde Park views paired with a cheeky hotel bar is a proper city life — Time Out’s listing of the hulking St James Hotel at 114 Castlereagh Street reminds us we’ve always had somewhere to go for budget-friendly drinks after an overly earnest tour. We know our quirks: we’re the suburb that wears its history like a badge, and if you like your brunch with a side of crypt and your nightlife with a slab of sandstone, you’ll feel right at home.
