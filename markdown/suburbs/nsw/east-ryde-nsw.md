# East Ryde (NSW)

Slug: `east-ryde-nsw`

We know East Ryde by smell and sight: sunscreen, barbecue smoke and, if you’re lucky, the faint whiff of butter pastry from a patisserie that felt like it’d been airlifted from Angers. As Broadsheet once noted, Choco Cannelle had a map of France on the wall — Jean‑François Perron’s subtle way of saying “we’re fancy, but only until school pick‑up”.

Our Saturday looks the same in memory: drop the kids at Heatley Reserve on Sager Place — the fully fenced playground with its shade sail and merry‑go‑round is where we pretend toddler chaos is ‘structured play’ (shoutout Ryde District Mums for the intel). Then we decamp to the big green: Time Out used to call Lane Cove National Park a picnicker’s paradise. We’ll never tire of the Lane Cove River threading through everything, the idea that you could hire a rowboat and look like you planned it all along.

Once a month we’d drift down to the Ryde Wharf Market at Ryde Wharf Reserve — Time Out’s listing — pretending we’d buy one artisanal thing. Crêpes, bao, arancini, pho and dumplings did most of the convincing while the Ryde Bridge posed for our photos.

Property chat? Sitchu made us beam and groan in equal measure when 16 Fox Road came up: a mid‑century on a whopping ~923sqm, on the market for the first time in 55 years. It’s the kind of block that makes us simultaneously swoon and panic‑renovation. We still share postcode 2113 with Macquarie Park, which means we get big‑town conveniences and small‑town gossip.

Local logistics: the 1st East Ryde Scout Group ran a proper car boot sale (21 September 2025) at the corner of Badajoz and Twin Roads in North Ryde, and our Scout Hall has a kitchen that will outlast your party. Ryde District Mums keep the neighbourhood calendar honest.

Who’d hate living here? If you crave tramlines of avocado‑toast cool and late‑night cocktail bars, you’ll find our pace infuriatingly wholesome. Who’d love it? Families, picnic obsessives, anyone who prizes green space and community stalls over being first to the latest inner‑city fad.

Back in the day the press catalogued our charms. We’re still here, slightly rumpled, plenty of gumption and ready to make you a cuppa — if you can resist the pastry.
