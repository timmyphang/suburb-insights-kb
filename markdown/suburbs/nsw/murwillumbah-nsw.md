# Murwillumbah (NSW)

Slug: `murwillumbah-nsw`

Back in the day — and in all the weekend guides that keep sending people our way — we start Saturdays the same: a slow wobble down to the Murwillumbah Farmers' Market at the Showground (Wednesdays, 7–11am, for those who like to pretend they plan ahead). Then there’s the practical detour through Sunnyside Mall to Coles when the fancy produce has eaten our grocery budget.

We’re the town that makes an art project out of resilience. The Murwillumbah Art Trail came back singing “Moving On” after the 2017 floods, and the Tweed Regional Gallery & Margaret Olley Art Centre still gets people to travel for a peek at the studio and the collection. Once a year the Regent Theatre hosts the LAVA Arts Festival and, suddenly, our sleepy main street looks like it’s wearing its best frock.

For a weekend we were the kind of place restaurant lists liked to brag about: Bistro Livi — an intimate, Melbourne-style wine bar in an art-deco building — was celebrated for fish-forward plates and a mousse-y chocolate cake that made city folk tut and book a road trip. Tweed River House used to be the riverside manor everyone wrote about, its restored rooms and weekend lawn bar activations splashed across Time Out and Urban List. Even our brewery got a mention: Ventura Brewing’s taproom was noted for Wollumbin views and a lineup that included beer, kombucha and non-alcoholic options.

We have a trailhead, not a motorway: the Northern Rivers Rail Trail starts from 284 Tweed Valley Way and the Murwillumbah–Crabbes Creek stretch is a neat 24km with the 500m Burringbar Range Tunnel — two to three hours if you’re trying to impress someone with Lycra.

We also briefly hosted TV camps — yes, the “I’m A Celebrity” campsite was set up here, which taught locals how to say “hello” to bewildered celebs who’d never met a small-town Friday night.

Who would hate it here? If your idea of fun is an endless rotation of late-night city restaurants, or if you can’t cope with an art festival, a farmers’ market morning, or a 36‑kilometre lie between us and the Gold Coast airport, this probably isn’t your tempo.

Who’d love it? Art lovers, cyclists, families who use the Murwillumbah Community Centre and rock up to Carols in the Park at Knox Park, and anyone who thinks a restored riverside manor or a quirky local brewery are good reasons to stay a little longer. We’ll keep being quietly brilliant — and quietly roasty about it.
