# Craigmore (NSW)

Slug: `craigmore-nsw`

We know Craigmore like the back of our hands — or at least like the back of a letter you once had to take to the post office. Back in the day our biggest outing was a pilgrimage to the Craigmore Licensed Post Office at Craigmore Village Shopping Centre (Shop 1/176–240 Yorktown Rd), for passports, TFN stuff and foreign currency, because apparently we were all planning very showy holidays we never took. As SAcommunity noted, that LPO handled everything from PO boxes to ID photos — the Swiss Army knife of civic errands.

Weekends used to look suspiciously wholesome. Drop the kids at Craigmore Kindergarten on 2 Carinya St for sessional kinder or playgroup, then swing by the aged-care hubs on Adams Rd. SAcommunity’s listings tell it straight: Estia Health Craigmore at 150 Adams Rd and Resthaven Craigmore at 200 Adams Rd were doing the heavy lifting with low and high-level care, respite rooms, dining areas, gardens and even a sensory garden — which, frankly, is where half the neighbourhood’s calm comes from.

There is no juicy local scandal in our files — the internet archive of Craigmore is mostly service directories and helpful contact details — which is either a testament to our decency or the internet’s boredom with suburbia. Even more confusing: our name has gone international. Findglocal prefers to talk about Craigmore poultry farms in Cape Town and Stellenbosch, so if you ever wanted your suburb to have a secret life as a supplier of day-old chicks, congratulations — someone else nailed it.

Who would hate living here? If you need rooftop bars, late-night street art or a never-ending stream of boutique everything, Craigmore’s online record won’t thrill you. We don’t scandalise; we facilitate forms, kinder drops and good gardening.

Who would love it here? Families who like predictable services, carers who appreciate nearby aged-care facilities, and people who prefer a neighbourhood that’s quietly competent rather than theatrically Instagrammable. We’ll keep the LPO running in memory and the sensory garden blooming — and if you’re passing through with a passport form, we’ll nod like we’ve seen it all before.
