# Burns Beach (NSW)

Slug: `burns-beach-nsw`

Back in the day we’d tell you Burns Beach was the sort of place where your weekend decisions were basically: coast walk or coast walk with snacks. We still ride the Marmion-to-Burns-Beach stretch along West Coast Drive — Broadsheet reckoned the 14‑kilometre run from Marmion to Burns Beach is the scenic bit — and yes, it does take between one and three hours depending on how many times we stop to read the tasteful little signs about native coastal shrub and pretend we know the flora and fauna.

For years the local press has been embarrassingly proud of our marine résumé. Urban List called our little bay a snorkelling spot — there’s that large sheltered rocky reef north of the sand where reef fish pop by and, if you’re lucky (and have cheeks of steel), you might glimpse dhufish. The Ritz‑Carlton’s Hearth even grilled abalone that were from Burns Beach, which is either a flex for us or a quiet reminder that our ocean is doing freelance work for five‑star hotels.

We used to have reports about Monelli opening here — Urban List and Sitchu said Monelli was perched in the breezy enclave, with Eleonora Patrizi and family and chef Luca Minetti bringing Roman flavours to the coast. Lot One cropped up in the round‑ups too, as one of those cafés people recommend after a walk. These were stories from the quieter chapter of our life; treat them like postcards rather than current intel.

And yes, we have a caravan park — Burns Beach Sunset Village — listed for campers who like waking up to views of the Indian Ocean and, on a good day, a tip of Rottnest. Local directories like MumsPages and the Joondalup Family Centre kept the mums and the kids organised, because someone needs to run the after‑walk sports roster.

Who’d hate living here? People who think “quiet coastal enclave” means “endless entertainment”. If you need neon lights and a tram every five minutes, you’ll curse our limestone cliffs and our well‑labelled shrubs.

Who’d love it? Anyone who wants a sensible slice of the Sunset Coast: beach, reef, a 14‑kilometre excuse to get outside, and enough local pride to make a hotel chef name‑check our abalone. We’ll save you a bench on the coastal path.
