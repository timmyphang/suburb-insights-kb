# Windsor Gardens (NSW)

Slug: `windsor-gardens-nsw`

We are the Windsor Gardens people who keep getting identity-swapped by the internet and frankly, we’re over it. On any given morning we could be the sleepy dead end Time Out called “a dull little dead end in W9” (that’s Paddington’s Windsor Gardens, not ours), the subject of a Paddington movie shooting schedule that hijacked Primrose Hill streets, or the place Broadsheet casually linked to a coffee roaster named Monjava via a Payneham South cafe profile. It’s like being famous by bureaucratic error.

Back in the day the press loved to borrow our name and scatter it across other people’s glory: Time Out staged tourists boarding a pretend train to “Windsor Gardens” at County Hall for the Paddington Bear Experience; Urban List waxed rhapsodic about the Windsor Wine Room on Chapel Street — a Euro-centric wine bar from Mamas Dining Group overseen by Michael Stolley, with talk of another project called Suzie Q. Meanwhile a Sitchu rental listing quietly put 4/112 Pitman Road in the same sentence as Windsor Gardens SA. It’s a shame we can’t all split the royalties.

Our weekend routine is therefore mostly spent correcting strangers. We don’t have a red carpet or a cosy Chapel Street vino den — those belong to the Urban List Windsor Wine Room — and we’re not the Payneham South cafe, though Broadsheet did mention Five Little Figs and that Monjava roaster connection once upon a time. So no, we won’t be your film set, your European wine bar, or your artisanal fig brunch. We’re the place that keeps turning up in other people’s travelogues like an extra who keeps getting promoted to lead.

Who would hate living here? Anyone allergic to ambiguity or who needs their suburb to have a single, consistent backstory. If you want your postcode to mean only one thing, move to somewhere that hasn’t been used as a spare prop in a family film, a wine-bar feature, and a rental ad all in the same internet week.

Who would love it here? People who enjoy being underestimated, who prefer postcode mystique to postcode prestige. We’re modest, pleasantly unremarkable, and mysteriously versatile — good enough to be Paddington’s old postcode, useful enough for a Chapel Street headline, and bland enough to be called a “dull little dead end” by Time Out. We’ll take it.
