# South Perth (NSW)

Slug: `south-perth-nsw`

We start our Saturday as we always have — ambling down the South Perth Foreshore, past Sir James Mitchell Park, pretending the skyline is our personal postcard and not a background for someone else’s wedding photos. Back in the day we’d wave to the ferries at Mends Street Jetty and keep an eye out for who’d scored a bench with an actual view.

Weekends used to have a rhythm: a riverside loop, a detour to Mends Street for whatever the local guides were fawning over, and a family pitstop. Perth Mums Group used to rave about The Secret Garden Café and Sprolo as kid-friendly brunch options; Playgroup faithful met at John McGrath Hall on Ernest Johnson Oval, and Studio 64 was touted as a bit of a one-stop convenience hub. We weren’t short on picnic spots either — McCallum Park and the foreshore were perennial favourites for a rug and a smuggled sandwich.

Our dining gossip was never subtle. Urban List used to list Ludo, Ciao Italia and Miso Hangry among the local heavy-hitters; Nextdoor on Angelo Street got its neighbourhood reputation for being the butcher-to-plate spot. Broadsheet wrote about Corner 33 on Canning Highway — that butter‑yellow, cherry‑red corner that tried a Japanese twist on diner classics and promised to be a community space. The Station precinct — yes, the old 1908 federation-style police station remade into a multi-venue thing — also made the rounds in the previews. Even Embargo, an old shipping container turned rooftop, had its own little legend.

Petty local drama? Oh, we had that too. There was a moment when somebody thought we’d be better off married to Victoria Park as “South Park” — because nothing says romance like being subsumed in a council rebrand. We muttered, signed a petition or two (maybe), and went back to arguing about whose turn it was to pick which riverside bench.

Who would hate it here? If you loathe slow-moving stroller traffic, weekend walkers who treat the foreshore like a runway, or the idea that your neighbour knows every school fundraiser, this place will try your patience.

But if you love river views, a tight-knit playgroup scene, old-school eateries, and the comfort of a suburb that still feels like a village pretending to be a postcard — well, that was always the point. We wouldn’t trade it for the skyline.
