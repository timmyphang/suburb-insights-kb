# Claremont Meadows (NSW)

Slug: `claremont-meadows-nsw`

We like to think Claremont Meadows is the sort of place where your weekend begins with a timetable and a thermos. Back in the day the Mobile Playvan swung by our own Claremont Meadows Community Centre on Sunflower Drive—Fridays at 9:30am, bookings essential—run as a free service through Penrith City Council and the NSW Department of Education. As Penrith City Childcare noted, it was the reliable little caravan of chaos for under-sixes (and the parents who needed it).

If you’re picturing grills and grand plans, the council did give us a proper flex: the Gipps Street Recreation Precinct opened in late August 2024 and turned 32 hectares of what used to be “quiet” into a small kingdom of concrete ollies, pump-track glory and a whole lot of floodlit sport. Western Sydney Mums wrote about the youth zone—skate park, a 245-metre pump track and parkour—plus multi-sport courts, netball courts, cricket practice facilities, a dog park, outdoor fitness equipment, water play and a children’s playspace. Fancy for a suburb that’s still learning to queue politely.

Our cultural calendar used to be delightfully modest: Children’s Week on 24 October brought the Mobile Playvan again, music therapy, animals and reptile displays if you were brave. There were early-childhood art classes running in five-week cycles (they charged $75 per five weeks), and a Build-Your-Own World diorama workshop that ran on a Monday 14th July at the community centre (172–180 Sunflower Drive) — because nothing says “local arts” like glue, glitter and suspiciously enthusiastic parents.

Who would hate living here? Nightlife chasers and anyone who measures a suburb’s worth by cocktail bars per square kilometre. If your ideal Saturday is rubbing shoulders with a rooftop DJ, we’ll wave you off with a kindly smirk.

Who would love it? Families, dog walkers, skate kids, and anyone who likes planning their week around the Playvan, pump track runs and a reliably good children’s program. We’ve got space, sensible programming and a pride that doesn’t need a skyline to prove itself.
