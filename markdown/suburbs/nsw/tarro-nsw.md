# Tarro (NSW)

Slug: `tarro-nsw`

We stand at the Tarro community notice board like it's a town square with screws in it — thumbs through photocopied flyers, squinting at a fax-era font and thinking, yes, this is us. For years the only glossy thing about Tarro, postcode 2322, has been the “Tarro” line buried in regional community pages — and frankly we wear that like a badge.

Our morning is checking the Find It Locally hub and muttering the official grouping out loud: "Thornton, Beresfield, Berry Park, Chisholm, Duckenfield, Metford, Millers Forest, Tarro, Woodberry & Phoenix Park Community Group (1388)". We say it as one long sentence like an incantation convincing everyone they’ve turned up to the right meeting. If you’re here before 9am, you’ll see us swapping tips on where the next sausage sizzle might appear, or who’s put a notice up about the lost cat that’s probably just at the neighbour’s back porch.

Petty drama? The data won’t bless us with juicy council fights or barista scandals, so our scandal is simpler: we’re the town that never made anyone’s lifestyle roundup. Urban List loves Auckland’s “Hot Tarro Bread” and Concrete Playground runs long lists about pubs and special boards in Sydney, but when it comes to Tarro — we’re mostly content to be cooperative annex in a regional noticeboard chorus. There’s a certain smug comfort in that anonymity.

Who would hate living here? People who need to be in every weekend feature and who think local lore only counts if it’s been photographed for an Instagram grid. If you measure a suburb by how loudly it screams for attention, Tarro whispers and then offers you a cup of whatever’s been reheated.

Who’d love it? Anyone who likes neighbours who actually say hello, who appreciates a community group that covers ten towns with one mailing list, and who finds solace knowing some places are quietly doing their thing while the rest of the internet chases trending tags. We’re small, slightly forgotten, and perfectly content to stay that way — except when the notice board gets a new flyer, then we’re all mildly scandalised and delighted.
