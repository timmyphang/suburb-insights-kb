# Belair (NSW)

Slug: `belair-nsw`

Back in the day our Saturday started with the kind of small-town ceremony the travel writers liked to gossip about: a paper in one hand, a pastry in the other, and a plan to get as far into the gumtrees as our legs would let us. We used to brag — and the papers used to list it — that Banana Boogie Bakery (yes, the one with the award-winning sausage roll and hot cross bun) was worth the detour. We remember the glow of their display like a beacon for anyone who takes pastry seriously.

After that, it was off to Belair National Park for a picnic — the guides said it had playgrounds, lakes, picnic tables and hireable tennis courts — ideal when you wanted the kids to tire themselves out before 3pm screen time. For the horticulturally obsessed among us, State Flora Nursery Belair was the place to pretend we knew the difference between a drought‑tolerant shrub and an arrangement that’s just wilting dramatically for Instagram.

There was also the local pub lore: The Belair Hotel’s lamb shoulder roast got namechecked in the roundups, and if we were feeling sophisticated we’d complain about the slow service in the same breath we admired the garlic-crusted roast potatoes. And for those pretending to be sommeliers, Belair Fine Wines used to be our excuse to buy something with a fancy label — complete with a ‘Gin City’ wall and a ‘Great Wall of Beer’ to gawp at while you argued about tannins.

Yes, we remember the odd viral moment too — like that train clip Pedestrian covered where a St John’s Grammar teacher was stood down after a tense encounter on the way to Belair. We’re not proud of viral indignation, but we’re honest about it.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t handle a postcode that talks about sausage rolls with the same reverence others reserve for sports finals, or if native plant nurseries make you yawn, move on. This place is low on thumping nightlife and high on weekend rituals.

Who would love it? Families who value a playground that actually survives the weekend onslaught, gardeners with a soft spot for native species, and anyone who believes a bakery can be a cultural institution. For what it’s worth, most of what we’re remembering comes from older write‑ups — so consider this a fond, slightly toasted postcard rather than a live events guide.
