# Point Clare (NSW)

Slug: `point-clare-nsw`

Back in the day our Thursday mornings had a rhythm: small shoes by the door, a thermos that wasn’t really ours, and the slow migration to Point Clare Community Centre at 49 Brisbane Water Drive. We used to file in for Tascott Point Clare — Thursdays at 9:30am, every week — which is basically the unofficial heartbeat of this suburb. If you wanted to find us any weekday, check the playgroup notices; they were the closest thing we had to a local newspaper.

Our village is proudly organised around tiny humans and people who really like organised noise. Erina Epico even used to brag about free barista coffee at its Monday 9:30am meet-ups (yes, the grown-ups get lured in, too). MyTime Erina listed Tascott Point Clare as a nearby spot for carers of kids with disability — not a headline, but the kind of detail that explains why we look out for one another. Peninsula Villages and Don Leggett House made the intergenerational vibe official, pairing silver hair with glitter glue.

We aren’t all story-time and cordial biscuits. Point Clare Scout Hall hosted Huntrix K‑Pop dance camps for the 3–7s, and the Point Clare Public School hall once held an Imagination Dance Frozen camp for 4–8 year olds — yes, we’ve staged entire economies around Elsa. For the slightly more disciplined, Central Coast Karate For Kids (19–21 Takari Ave) accepted three-year-olds who are far too eager to show you their punch. Even Games2U listed Point Clare in its service area; our parties could be outsourced and still go off without a hitch.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who needs a thumping nightclub or a skyline view. If K‑Pop choreography at 9:30am and weekly playgroup democracy make you homicidal, this isn’t your postcode.

Who would love it? Families, carers, grandparents, and people who like their community with a side of craft glue and organised chaos. We’re small, we keep meeting, and we look after each other — mostly because someone’s already signed up to run the next camp.
