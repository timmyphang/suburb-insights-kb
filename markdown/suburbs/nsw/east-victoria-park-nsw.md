# East Victoria Park (NSW)

Slug: `east-victoria-park-nsw`

We roll out of bed in East Victoria Park, NSW like we own the place — which is probably true if you count every noodle stall, mozzarellaria and family arcade we've fed into. Full disclosure: the clippings we dug up are mostly older features and listings, so consider this a fondly nostalgic roast rather than an up‑to‑the‑minute guide. Back in the day (and in those write‑ups), our Saturday started with a detour to La Delizia Latticini for bocconcini, then a circuit of the Albany Highway legends.

We used to queue for Mei’s Noodle tucked in the industrial area for Crossing the Bridge noodles, and brag that Yip had the rare Guangzhou “bamboo” noodles with roast pork and handmade wontons — as Broadsheet noted. If you were feeling adventurous you’d bounce between Sedap Place’s nasi lemak and the comforting chaos of Dragon Hotpot, Frank’s Barbecue, Ramen Samurai and Royal’s Chicken & Burgers — all names the listings loved to parade.

There was a time the papers got very excited about shiny things: Sitchu ran with Baillie Hill as a $25 million multi‑venue dining mecca built around Elizabeth Baillie House. We watched that announcement like it was our suburb getting a new haircut — dramatic, expensive, and somehow still our mum’s slippers under the bed. Broadsheet also reported Palace Arcade as the family‑friendly alternative when the kids had energy to burn: about 50 rotating gaming machines, New York‑style pizzas and cocktails. Very wholesome, very neon.

Who would hate it here? People who need their suburb to be one vibe only — the sort who pick a lane and refuse to leave it. If you can’t handle a place where you might queue for Hokkaido milk soft serve from Pupu Desserts one weekend and a plate of Yunnan lentil jelly the next, this isn’t your scene.

Who would love it? Food obsessives, bargain‑hunters for boutique mozzarella, parents who appreciate a proper arcade, and anyone who thinks a $25 million dining precinct is a neighbourly conversation starter. We’ll keep defending our little stretch of Albany Highway — loudly, with chopsticks in one hand and a napkin full of pastry in the other.
