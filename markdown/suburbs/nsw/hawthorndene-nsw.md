# Hawthorndene (NSW)

Slug: `hawthorndene-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean the community listings we dug up rather than any hip new feed — Hawthorndene was the kind of place where your weekend itinerary reads like a primary school newsletter.

We start Saturday mornings at Apex Park (26 Watahuna Ave), the fenced park “at the bottom of the hills” with undercover seating, tables, BBQ facilities and that impossibly tall spiral slide kids tell stories about. The playground list also keeps us honest: Archibald Reserve Playground on Renfrew Dr and Suffolk Road Reserve Playground on Suffolk Rd are the neighbourhood trifecta, and yes, the Mitcham Council contact details appear on every playground page, like a cautious parent lurking in the background.

For years the local childcare and schooling listings were the town’s social calendar. Hawthorndene Primary School on Suffolk Rd — the directory even names Mrs Andrea Burnside as principal in the old entry — offered OSHC with strict hours: Before School Care 7am–8.30am, After School Care 3.10pm–6.15pm and Vacation Care 7am–6.15pm. The OSHC and care pages remind us there were about 12 childcare services in postcode 5051, five of them reportedly with vacancies, and an average cost floating at $142.44/day. Hawthorndene Kindergarten at 1 Kindergarten Ave, run by Ms Louise Montesi in the listing, scored an NQS rating of “Exceeding” and even had a Friday playgroup — the kind of brag you stick on the fridge next to the soccer ephemera.

Petty local drama? The archives are mercifully thin. If anything, our quarrels were over which park had the better spiral slide and whether the tennis courts should host another family day.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to kids, fenced parks, or an unofficial municipal obsession with slides and BBQs. Also, if you need an ever-changing café scene, this isn’t your inner-city hustle.

Who would love it: families who like reliability — tidy playgrounds, a kindergarten with an “Exceeding” rating, OSHC that runs by the book — and people who measure neighbourhood success in slides per suburb. Consider this a fond roast of a place that still reads like a family newsletter, and yes, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
