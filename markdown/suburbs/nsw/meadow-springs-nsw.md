# Meadow Springs (NSW)

Slug: `meadow-springs-nsw`

Back in the day we’d convince ourselves Meadow Springs was a secret worth keeping — then forget where we hid it. Weekend mornings meant a family census, a failed attempt at navigation and finally emerging, sheepish, at the Quarry Adventure Park — yes, the one tucked at the corner of Pebble Beach Rd and Wincrest Way. Perth Mums Group even warned us: “the only bad thing… is finding it.” They weren’t wrong. It’s the kind of park you find by accident and then lie about like it’s an exclusive.

For years the local parenting websites — MumsPages and Perth Mums Group — did the heavy lifting: lists of businesses, meet posts, and earnest “Welcome To My Suburb” love letters. Meadow Springs cropped up as the sensible subplot in Mandurah’s expansion: popular with families because of our proximity to the Mandurah train line and the City of Mandurah. Translation: we can commute, we can cart kids, and we can still call it suburban bliss.

Family life here used to be reliably choreographed. Jellybeans Meadow Springs was the kind of childcare centre that got name-checked on every playdate plan — long daycare, kindy programs for kids from six weeks to school age, bike tracks, sandpits and a “natural stream” to convince the babies they were outdoorsy. The online listings promised excursions and community connections; we promised to pack a spare t-shirt and forgot it anyway.

We’re not big on drama — at least not the tabloid kind — so the local headlines read like a parenting forum: Easter egg hunts at Quarry Adventure Park (remember 2017? $5 entry, sausage sizzle and a drink included), meet-and-greets and directory listings that made life look organised even when it wasn’t.

Who would hate living here? City slickers who need neon buzz, late-night bars and footpath cafes with ironic names. Who would love it? Families who like train-line convenience, parks you can claim as your secret, and the comfort of a suburb chronicled by every mums’ blog from MumsPages to Perth Mums Group. We’re low-key, a bit hidden, and very useful — especially when someone needs a last-minute egg hunt venue.
