# Hamlyn Heights (NSW)

Slug: `hamlyn-heights-nsw`

We wake up in Hamlyn Heights and somewhere between the aroma of someone’s reheated takeaway and the sound of kids catching the 8am bus, we remember: we’re a suburb that quietly does community better than it does hype.

Back in the day Broadsheet wrote up Eddy and Wills — they turned an old butcher into a pared-back cafe with polished concrete floors, timber details and a small dog-friendly courtyard. As Broadsheet noted, the menu rotated through eggs Benedict and a breakfast tasting plate and the beans were Code Black from Brunswick — very much grown-up suburbia. That was the sort of thing that made us smug for a week.

Our real heart, though, is more Vines Road than espresso machine. Vines Road Community Centre has long been the place where ham-and-veg-soup-level neighbourliness gets scheduled: it’s co-located with Western Heights Secondary College and a branch of Geelong Regional Libraries, and you could always check the centre’s “What’s On” schedule or their Facebook for the next knitting, class or community meet-up. The centre’s contact details even live in the archive: 37–61 Vines Road and a phone number that’s been doing its job for years. MumsPages, for its part, reminds us that there’s a steady rotation of kids’ classes, sports and services that keep the suburb from dissolving into breakfast-only culture.

Petty local drama? The historical record is suspiciously calm. Our news clips are more “community timetable” than “council kerfuffle,” and honestly we like it that way. If you live here because you crave constant novelty and a rotating roster of boutique openings, you’ll probably hate how steady and useful our amenities are.

Who loves Hamlyn Heights? Families who want things that work, people who like real community centres, parents trawling MumsPages for kids’ classes, and anyone who appreciates a neighbourhood that prefers bin-hire adverts (yes, Mr Bins Geelong makes the list) to being in the foodie spotlight. We’ll keep our polished concrete floors, our library arm, and our “What’s On” calendar — and we’ll be quietly proud about it.
