# Geelong East (NSW)

Slug: `geelong-east-nsw`

We like to start the day down at Eastern Beach, even if our idea of "beach chic" is a towel and a sensible sun hat. Back in the day the guides told us to begin mornings there and then drift into the city for coffee, galleries and a bit of genteel wandering — and honestly, that’s still our vibe when we’re not pretending to be too busy.

By mid-morning we’ll amble up Garden Street, past the places Broadsheet once wrote about. Goose at 91 Garden St was called “a long-standing pillar of Geelong’s retail landscape” — and Frank and Dolly’s at 72a Garden Street used to be the sort of ethically made, twee oasis you either adored or rolled your eyes at. We’ll admit we miss poking through their curated homewares and wondering why we never actually need three more throw cushions.

If you’re into urban reinvention, we’ve got a glow-up story. Concrete Playground liked to point out Geelong’s pivot from heavy industry to galleries and small bars; Powerhouse Geelong (the old power station) now wears murals like a statement necklace, and the Geelong Library and Heritage Centre — “The Dome” — gives our civic pride something to show off at family gatherings. Urban List even teased a shiny waterfront revamp, which makes us very optimistic about future promenades and slightly wary about the new Instagram angles.

Petty local drama? The papers were mostly kinder to us than that. We’ve had the slow-motion culture clash of families and professionals moving in, and the city quietly swapping smokestacks for brunch recommendations. It’s not explosive; it’s the sort of gentle friction that yields a farmers’ market and a rooftop bar rather than banner headlines.

Who would hate living here: people who need a relentless 2am party precinct or think a “weekend away” must cross state lines to feel exotic. If you need Soho-level energy, Little Malop and Pakington Street’s foodie cred still reads like a polite invitation rather than a drum circle.

Who’d love it: families, the low-key culture scouts, anyone who values Eastern Park, the waterfront and being a short hop from Melbourne while keeping our small-city charms. We’ll keep the Dome, the murals and the boutiques — and you can have the frenetic nightlife.
