# Preston West (NSW)

Slug: `preston-west-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean the snippets we could dig up from before 2024 — Preston West felt like a suburb that couldn’t decide whether it wanted to be a bustling market precinct or a secret warehouse for extremely dedicated beer-drinkers. We know the reading: Preston Market, the 53‑year‑old institution that Salta Properties promised to keep at its site for at least another five years, loomed large in our weekends. As Broadsheet noted, it’s where you could score specialty coffee and a sense of municipal history at once.

Our morning stroll used to look like this: wander past stalls at the market, then debate whether today’s plan is Arepa Days tucked off Preston Street or something cannoli-shaped from Eat Cannoli (Dom and Kate Marzano’s little operation, as Urban List remembered). If we were feeling loud and celebratory, we’d point our shoes toward Moon Dog World — the place that, per Broadsheet and Concrete Playground, was decked out with a pool, tiki disco bar, giant beer garden and even an indoor waterfall. You’ve got venue drama and waterfall aesthetics — what more do you want?

Petty local drama? We’ve had it in spades: the looming redevelopment threat to the market (briefly very public, then leases renewed), and the odd headline — like Spanian’s kebab truck suffering a suspected arson attack, with reports that Spanian’s Kebabs in Preston looked set to reopen quickly (Pedestrian.TV covered that one). And yes, Jamsheed’s old cellar‑door energy got a tweak when John Cassie came on board as head chef, promising nostalgic comfort dishes (Broadsheet).

Who would hate living here: the person who needs everything to be minimalist and anonymously curated; those folks will grumble at the market chaos, the giant beer‑garden revelry and the fact Preston is, as Time Out put it, a “broad expanse” of eclectic pockets.

Who’d love it: market people, folks who like a 72‑tap option and an indoor waterfall (again, Moon Dog’s flex), shoppers bound for Northland, and anyone who enjoys an honest jumble of cafes and cuisines — Sartoria on Plenty Road and the long list of little breakfast spots that outlets used to collect into guides. We’ll keep the market banter alive — even if the latest headlines are, well, from a few years back.
