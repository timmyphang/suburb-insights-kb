# Randwick (NSW)

Slug: `randwick-nsw`

We know Randwick by the little rituals: a twilight queue at the Ritz, arguing about whether the race track noise is ambience or assault, and pretending the light rail makes everything “so accessible.”

Weekend morning you’ll find us at the Ritz Cinema — that glorious Art Deco dive the Time Out lot can’t stop raving about — and then wandering The Spot to see if the council’s latest plan has landscaped it into a theme park or a tasteful postcard. On race days Royal Randwick Racecourse owns the mood (and occasionally our ringtone), and when the carnival rolls in there’s even a Riders Club pop‑up to keep us fed — as Broadsheet previewed for the Autumn 2025 dates.

Food-wise, we’re refreshingly indecisive. We cheered when Corner 75 reopened on March 6, 2025: Broadsheet reminded us the pork schnitzel survived the revamp, like a sentimental uncle who’s had a new haircut. And for a greasy, inventive moment, Concrete Playground gave us Umami House at 49 Perouse Rd in 2025 — shiitake‑pushed burgers that make you reconsider basic burger logic.

Local drama? Oh, we’re full of it, but it reads like council paperwork. In 2024 Randwick Council publicly pushed back against the NSW housing reforms — Eastern Suburbs Mums covered the rejection — and more recently Concrete Playground flagged a proposal to scrap free beach parking across seven beaches in our area. So yes, we’ll lobby for live music and heritage cinemas, but don’t take our free parking without a fight.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to racehorses (literal or cultural), people who need absolute silence between 9am and 5pm, or someone who thinks heritage cinemas are just “old buildings.” We’re noisy, territorial about our beaches, and suspicious of fast policy changes.

Who will love it: cinephiles, race‑day romantics, people who like a surprisingly diverse food map (Sitchu’s 2026 roundups even name Corner 75 and Sella Vinoteca among local favourites), and anyone who thinks a fortnightly live set at The Spot or Coogee is the civic equivalent of a warm hug. We’ll keep loving it — even while grumbling about the meter at the beach.
