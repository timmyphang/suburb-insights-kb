# Seaforth (NSW)

Slug: `seaforth-nsw`

We know the hilltops, the harbour sneaks, and exactly where the parking app lies. Back in the day Broadsheet introduced us to Serva at 1/538 Sydney Road — the little cafe‑meets‑providore where Miles Ackermans made preserves, pickles and a very memorable egg‑and‑cheese English muffin, and where a blueberry relish was deemed worth taking home. We’ll also admit Staple Bread & Necessities — James Partington’s sourdough temple — used to make our weekends smell like victory; Broadsheet even noted its RAS NSW sourdough nod and Urban List later celebrated its gooey vanilla slice.

Our Saturday looks like this in memory: someone drops by Staple, clutching a Balgowlah bun or a flaky vanilla slice; someone else brings home a jar of Serva relish and, for reasons no one can fully explain, a viral vodka parm sando story that Urban List once wrote about. If you wanted waffles, Taste Of Belgium in town made the lists; if you needed gluten‑free pizza, Sale Pepe had the nod from way back when. For a quiet schooner and an honest counter meal, Time Out used to point to the Balgowlah RSL.

Local legend? Pedestrian TV reminded us about the Wakehurst Parkway ghost — the spooky tales along the road that links Seaforth to Narrabeen are part of our folklore, perfect for after‑dinner walks when you want a spine tingle with your harbour view. And if you’re packing for visitors, Time Out once wishlisted Spa Serenity Cottage, because apparently our vistas translate well to short stays.

Who would hate it here: people who need a never‑ending, neon‑lit nightlife or crave a city‑block of anonymous convenience stores. This place rewards cottage industry, village habit and the odd boutique kids’ shop (LillyMax used to be part of the directory). We’re not for everyone.

Who would love it: families — MumsPages lists the whole support ecosystem, from camps to baby services — and outdoorsy types who use the Manly Dam bike track via Gibbs Street or Kirkwood Street at Bantry Reserve for escapes. If you like a village that smells of baking, keeps a jar of artisan relish on the shelf and tells ghost stories on the way home, we’re your kind of suburb.
