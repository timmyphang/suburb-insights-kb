# Erina (NSW)

Slug: `erina-nsw`

Saturday mornings in Erina used to look like this: we’d roll out of bed, squint at the creek, and try not to overpromise a “quick” trip into Erina Fair. Back in the day the plan involved Hoyts Erina for a kids’ flick, a detour past the Woodport Hotel (all polished up after that big reno, now with a speakeasy and water views), and a wander to whatever seasonal thing Distillery Botanica was doing with its garden-grown botanicals at 25 Portsmouth Road.

Don’t get us wrong — we’ve always been unashamed about trying to be both country and cosmopolitan. Concrete Playground once wrote about Kew Dining bringing paddock-to-plate vibes to Erina Heights, while the same site praised Bar Botanica as a planty gelato hideout inside Distillery Botanica. Time Out and Sitchu treated us like a stop on the way to someone else’s beach holiday — “pop in for a beer at Six String Brewing” or “it’s an easy 90-minute drive from Sydney,” they said, as if that were a warning rather than a selling point.

There’s not much local soap-opera gossip in the paperwork we’ve got — instead you get restaurant reviews, renovation features and the occasional festival of food writing by Erina Starkey (yes, the name is delightful and yes, she wrote about the Bon Pavilion in Gosford). So the only petty drama here is deciding whether to have a gin-based dessert or a beer-based one. Thrilling, we know.

Who would absolutely hate living here? If you need an inner‑city tram every five minutes, zero‑traffic noise, and shops that never close, Erina’s quaint ambitions — Woodport’s live music, Distillery Botanica’s horticultural cocktails, Six String’s small-batch pours — will feel like chronic underachievement.

Who would love it? Families who actually go to the playgroups (Erina Epico at 4 Ilya Avenue, MyTime at 27 Karalta Road — they used to meet weekly), people who like a shopping complex with a cinema, and anyone who appreciates a town that politely stages its own renaissance: a renovated hotel, a gin distillery with a gelato stall, a paddock-to-plate spot in the heights. We’re not pretentious. We’re just quietly trying to be worth the 90-minute drive.
