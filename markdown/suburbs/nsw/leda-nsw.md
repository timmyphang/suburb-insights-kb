# Leda (NSW)

Slug: `leda-nsw`

We start our mornings at the Leda Community Notice Board Hub 6170 like it's a seaside pilgrimage — except it's a corkboard, not a cliff. For years the local Find It Locally page has been our civic Tinder: a place to swipe right on "dining, health, tradies, clubs" and all the minor commitments that make a suburb feel like a suburb. You can tell a lot about us by what people pin: a flyer for a Pilates class, a "looking for a footy teammate" post, a laminated card from a local business hoping someone needs a dog-sitter at 2pm.

Back in the day — or at least back when the internet still liked local noticeboards — we organised meetings, swapped tools, and advertised small businesses through that very Hub. It’s a kind of analogue humility for a postcode that refuses to be flashy. We are not about neon laneways or being in every listicle; we are about showing up and stapling your hopes to a board.

We like to joke that the suburb shares a name with a certain cinematic Leda — Olivia Colman’s brooding professor who spends a lot of time "peering at the ocean", as Concrete Playground put it. We’re not peering at any ocean here, but we do spend an inordinate amount of time peering at the noticeboard to see who’s offering a babysitting swap or selling second-hand lawn mowers.

Petty local drama? The data is mercifully thin. The biggest controversy we could find is whether to list something under "dining" or "clubs" — and honestly, that’s the sort of fight that keeps us civil. If you needed headlines, you’ll have to look elsewhere; we're more into small kindnesses and hand-written A4 flyers than viral outrage.

Who would hate living here: anyone who needs a curated brunch crawl and an influencer moment every weekend. Who would love it here: community people who prefer a real noticeboard over an algorithm, small-business owners who want their leaflet actually read, and anyone who enjoys a suburb that wears its uncomplicatedness like a good flannelette shirt.
