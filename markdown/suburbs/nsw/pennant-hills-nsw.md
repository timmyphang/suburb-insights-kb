# Pennant Hills (NSW)

Slug: `pennant-hills-nsw`

Back in the day — or at least in the stories we could find — Pennant Hills felt like the suburb that quietly refused to be boring. We can picture a Sunday: the Wollundry Park playground, freshly patched after the vandalism that finished it off in December 2023, filling with kids while the DownTown Markets set up opposite the library every third Sunday (Hornsby Shire Mums tells us this all kicked off in 2024). Parents vote on nursery-rhyme themed equipment. We clap like it’s a community musical.

Our morning ritual used to be: a quick hop from Pennant Hills Station (Sitchu notes some houses are about 550m away), a stroll past the fenced playground, then a choice between curry for brunch or something convincingly pan-Asian. Broadsheet used to whisper about Blue Elephant’s secret Sri Lankan menu and hopper nights, and that old-school Malaysian stalwart Istana — slightly over in Thornleigh — still gets a nod for Hainan chicken. Hornsby Shire Mums introduced us to BB Chi on Hillcrest Road, which felt like the kind of place you bring the kids to and still get fed well.

The local drama we actually have receipts for: Wollundry Park got vandalised, the council scrambled, residents voted on new designs and the park was rebuilt by mid‑June 2024. Community spirit: 1. Vandals: 0. Also, Time Out once put West Pennant Hills on a list for toll rebate claims — 1,007 claims averaging $305 — so if your idea of paradise is toll-free motoring, maybe don’t move here.

Who would hate it here? People who need the CBD on demand, or who can’t handle koalas. We, however, don’t mind that Koala Park Sanctuary in West Pennant Hills lets punters cuddle koalas and ogle emus and wombats (Time Out’s listing — yes, really). Bushwalkers love that Berowra Creek begins around these parts, and Concrete Playground points to Crosslands Reserve and the Benowie Walking Track for a proper dip or hike. Cumberland State Forest is the full forest experience if you prefer ziplining or tree-top chaos to suburban calm.

Who’d love it? Families who want fenced playgrounds, weekend markets and a pub that used to run a bouncy-castle Sunday lunch (Time Out’s Hotel Pennant Hills), foodies who enjoyed Blue Elephant, Istana or Okami’s all-you-can-eat habit — and anyone who secretly likes a suburb with a bit of history, a lot of trees and the occasional koala photobomb.
