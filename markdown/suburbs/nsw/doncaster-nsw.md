# Doncaster (NSW)

Slug: `doncaster-nsw`

Back in the day, we’d start our Saturday like proper locals: a sluggish shuffle down Doncaster Road to where Meno Zero gelato used to tempt us with 24 flavours (yes, even that pizza Caprese one Broadsheet wrote about). The papers we get quoted are mostly older guides — Broadsheet, Time Out, Sitchu — so this is part nostalgia, part humblebrag.

Weekends then meant the big civic centrepiece: Westfield Doncaster’s much-talked-about makeover. Concrete Playground called it a $30‑million rooftop food precinct, complete with a DIY okonomiyaki joint and names like Dohtonbori and Betty’s Burgers elbowing for space. Urban List even crowed about Sushi Jiro’s 30.6‑metre circular sushi train — allegedly the longest in the world — because nothing screams community cohesion like a conveyor belt of salmon and ambition. Pop Mart and Top Tea also popped up in the write-ups, proof we can be kawaii and caffeinated when required.

Local life wasn’t just malls. Time Out and Sitchu remind us we were the kind of place families pitched tents in the suburbs: South Surrey Park and Surrey Gardens got name checks, Sitchu lauded our big blocks and school‑belt aspirations, and a curated listing even hawked an “exception” three‑bed at 25 Gedye Street. Nicks Wine Merchants came up as the reliable old guard — family‑run since 1958, if you fancy an anecdote with your chardonnay.

There isn’t much petty local drama in these archives — the biggest argument seems to be whether rooftop yum‑cha makes us cosmopolitan or merely hungry. If you’re the sort who hates queues, finds rooftop dining tedious, or believes 30‑metre sushi trains are a cry for help, you’ll grouse here.

But who would love it? Young families (Sitchu’s verdict), anyone who thinks mall gastronomy is an experience, devotees of yum cha and teppanyaki (Time Out still name‑checks Plume and Kobe Teppanyaki for special occasions), plant‑based eaters who want Vegie Mum at 27 Village Avenue on rotation, and anyone who enjoys a well‑stocked local bottle shop with history: that’s Nicks.

We know our patch through older headlines and comfortable memories — and frankly, we wouldn’t have it any other suburbanly sensible way.
