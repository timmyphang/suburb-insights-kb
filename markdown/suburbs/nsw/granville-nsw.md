# Granville (NSW)

Slug: `granville-nsw`

We get up early in Granville to the comforting smell of things being expertly charred and expertly wrapped — not a zen retreat, more a garlic-scented alarm clock. Back in the day the magazines noticed: as Broadsheet said, we’ve long been the manoush capital, and you could practically map our week by what was coming out of the ovens on South Street and Railway Parade.

A proper weekend here used to look like this: a detour past Beirut Bakery at 66 South Street for bread still warm enough to apologise for, then a pilgrimage to Abla’s Pastries (48–52 Railway Parade) for sweets the size of a small decision. El Jannah — the one that started it all in Granville in 1998 — used to put us on the culinary map with its charcoal chicken and garlic sauce that arrives like a full personality. Time Out and Concrete Playground wrote love letters about it; we just wrote grease on our shirts.

Our geography includes Meehan Street Park / Wallawa Reserve, which had a shiny new playground upgrade for the little ones, and community staples like the South Granville supported playgroup at the Community Centre — the kind of local things ParraParents and Playgroup NSW dutifully list when they want you to know we’re not just takeaways and baklava. Even the more indulgent suggestions — Ottoman Turkish Bath at Level 2/46 Wellington Road, South Granville — were already on the spa maps.

If you’re the sort who hates smoke, strong garlic, lively weekend crowds and honest, old-school bakeries that don’t pretend to care about aesthetics, you will loathe Granville. You’ll call it noisy, aromatic and inconveniently alive.

But if you love unapologetic food culture, community playgroups, sprintcar racing lore from the Sydney Speedway and nights where live performances and food trucks used to take over the streets (Granville Nights, remember?), this town has your name embroidered on a bread bag. We’ll keep being the place that magazines keep writing about — even if those write-ups are mostly from “back in the day.” Come for the manoush, stay for the stories, and bring something to wipe your hands on.
