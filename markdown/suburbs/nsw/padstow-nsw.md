# Padstow (NSW)

Slug: `padstow-nsw`

We know Padstow like the back of our hands — the kind of place where your Saturday starts with a plan and ends with slightly more dancing than you intended.

Back in the day, Urban List wrote up Elatte Café at 101 Villiers Road as the kind of spot that fuels our unhealthy attachment to Allpress espresso. We remember the walls dressed in cutesy art and the feeling that somewhere between the second latte and the third scroll you’d committed to a weekend of sensible errands.

If you’d asked Time Out, they’d have told you Padstow was “packed with great things to do and places to go” — which reads like travel brochure optimism and also our collective dream of having actual nightlife that doesn’t close at sensible o’clock. Junkee once reminded everyone not to underestimate us either: an LJ Hooker Padstow real-estate clip went viral for being filmed like an R&B music video, complete with dancing. Yes, our houses have rhythm. No, we didn’t ask for the global attention, but we took it.

Even our institutions make a low-key fuss: Playgroup NSW listed a weekly Tuesday 9:30am playgroup at Padstow Heights Public School on Chamberlain Road — evidence that families are the backbone of the place, and that Carpark Olympics is a weekly spectator sport.

And in the media merry-go-round, Broadsheet noted JDA Hotels — the group behind the General Gordon and our very own Padstow Park Hotel — when they bought a different pub (The Unicorn in Paddington). So while the headlines chased demolition dramas in Paddington, we nodded, sipped, and kept our own pubs in the local orbit of someone else’s refit saga.

Who would hate living here? People who need boutique everything, endless dinners out, or someone else to collect the mail while they’re off finding themselves in Newtown. We’re not Paris; we’re the comfortable middle sibling who actually pays their rates on time.

Who would love it here? Families who appreciate a Tuesday morning routine, anyone who thinks a viral real-estate dance-off is peak suburbia, and those who like their espresso grounded, literal and from Allpress — as Urban List once cheerfully observed. We’re low-key, reliably human, and occasionally embarrassingly viral. What’s not to like?
