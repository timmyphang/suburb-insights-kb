# Tuart Hill (NSW)

Slug: `tuart-hill-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed and wander Lawley Street like it’s our civic duty — because Tuart Hill has always been that small, smug pocket where sandwiches feel like a religion. As Broadsheet cheekily put it, Strollio’s Luncheonette was a love letter to the Aussie deli, and yes, we remember the tuna-and-artichoke melt and the pastries that came from “Alexander’s oven.” Urban List even gave Strollio’s a tick at 68 Lawley St, and Broadsheet’s custard-filled doughnut (brioche-style, four-ingredient custard) was the sort of thing you’d queue for like it’s a public holiday.

If breakfast is your vibe, Time Out had our backs — Little Donkey Corner (4/91 Wanneroo Rd) got the nod for Turkish eggs, chilli cheesy eggs and those hot-chocolate waffles that feel like a dare. Local Bird shows up in the lists too for its big crispy fried-chicken burgers and, crucially, the hot chips that Urban List readers swore by for that umami seasoning. For anyone nostalgic for specialty groceries, Time Out once pointed out Kings Euro Foods as the boutique deli where you can elbow past jars of things you’ll pretend to know how to cook.

We’re not pretending there’s feral nightlife here — it’s more sandwiches by day, naps by 3pm. So who would hate living here? If you loathe bread, breakfast lists, or people who treat a lunchtime sanga like a Tinder date, you’ll be miserable. If you require your suburb to have a permanently buzzing late-night scene, move along.

Who would love Tuart Hill? Food obsessives who read lists, parents who appreciated the practical stuff — MumsPages had the local childcare and family-day-care directories, and Playgroup WA’s finder was the kind of resource that made mid-morning coffees possible. We’re the sort of place where you can argue about which cafe made the best doughnut, then take your kid to after-school art at Galleria and call it a balanced life. Short on pretence, long on good eating — and honestly, we prefer it that way.
