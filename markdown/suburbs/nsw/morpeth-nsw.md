# Morpeth (NSW)

Slug: `morpeth-nsw`

Back in the day, if you wanted to find us you followed the Hunter River until the 19th‑century buildings started giving you side‑eye. We’re that 1,500‑person village in the Hunter Valley everyone keeps writing about when they need a quaint escape or a spooky headline.

Mornings used to begin with a slow wander down to the riverfront, past sandstone that’s older than half the travel writers who’ve written about us. If you read the old guides, one of the winter‑friendly brekkies in town used to be at Common Grounds — you’ll find mentions of house‑made spicy beans and black sesame‑crusted avocado in the archives of Concrete Playground. Later, journalists would trot out the Morpeth ghost and heritage tour as proof we’re “pretty much Australia’s most haunted town”; Urban List made that claim with the sort of certainty usually reserved for espresso shots and horoscopes.

Here’s the delightful contradiction we enjoy: one paragraph we’re spectral, the next we’re culinary. Concrete Playground even name‑checks our red kale in a Port Stephens food round‑up, and Sitchu’s Hunter Valley winery guide gives a polite nod to Boydell’s Vineyard when they want to remind readers there’s more than ghosts around here. Nearby getaways like The Cottage at Berry House have been suggested as pet‑friendly bases for exploring our sleepy streets, which is exactly the kind of wholesome brag we tolerate.

There’s no real scandal to report — mostly the local lore tilts between “historic charm” and “ethereal tourism” — which suits us. We don’t need manufactured feuds; our contradiction does the work: imagine a place where you can sip wine sourced from Boydell’s vines while bargaining philosophically with a ghost about parking.

Who would hate it here? People who need a 24‑hour neon buzz or who want every laneway plastered in street art. Who would love it? History nerds, Hunter Valley wine hunters, couples looking for a romantic weekend, and anyone keen on a heritage stroll that might come with a spectral accompaniment.

For years the travel pieces have loved to tell our story. We’re fine with that — we’ll keep being photogenic, haunted, and quietly smug by the river.
