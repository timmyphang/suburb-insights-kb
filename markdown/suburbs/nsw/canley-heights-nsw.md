# Canley Heights (NSW)

Slug: `canley-heights-nsw`

We start our day by pretending we live on Canley Vale Road: back in the day we'd amble along the strip that Broadsheet split into Canley Vale and Canley Heights and argue about where to queue. Time Out even dubbed the Canleys “Little Asia,” which was generous, but also accurate enough to get us through breakfast.

Morning ritual used to mean a choice between Gia Hội for the bun bo hue (Broadsheet’s local-knowledge pick), Weston Eatery for the matcha hotcakes and burgers (owners Hung Le and David Duong were name-checked), or a no-fuss feed at Tan Viet Noodle House. Fat Panda was cheekily described as a gem on Canley Vale Road; Twelve Spices sat over on 197 St Johns Rd. If we were feeling nostalgic, we’d remind each other Madera Kafe first ran here from 1997–2006 — the sort of local lore you trot out at laundromats.

Full disclosure: the snippets we’ve got are all older — there aren’t any recent articles in the pack — so we’re writing in the past-tense like a proud historian. Time Out’s pieces from the archive still stuff our mouths: they talked up the Canleys as an emerging nightlife hotspot and flagged Fairfield Council’s plans for Special Entertainment Precincts. In short, we’ve been quietly working on our night-owl cred while the city slept.

Petty drama? The records don’t give us a council brawl or a barista scandal. If there’s heat, it’s our own: which place gets the takeaway tonight — Kickin’Inn had a Canley Heights outpost, and the bubble tea listings once noted Canley Heights among the suburbs with bobas. Riveting, we know.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to late-night noodle fumes, garlic on everything or the idea of a dining precinct called “Little Asia.” Who would love it: food nerds with short attention spans and families — Community Kids Canley Heights sits at 122–124 Canley Vale Road, and Playgroup NSW listings make parent life look do-able.

We’re not posh, we’re not pretending to be, and the archives make us look like a well-fed secret. Come for the bun bo hue (Gia Hội), stay for the argument about where to park on Canley Vale Road.
