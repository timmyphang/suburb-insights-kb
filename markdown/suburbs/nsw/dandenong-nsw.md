# Dandenong (NSW)

Slug: `dandenong-nsw`

Back in the day we’d brag that our postcode contained an entire continent’s worth of dinner options and a steam train for good measure. We’re Dandenong — the place Broadsheet once called “diverse” for a reason — and yes, we used to spend Saturdays ducking between stalls at Dandenong Market, queueing for La Caze Mama’s chicken briani and mine frite like it was an AFL final.

Our weekend routine was gloriously simple and slightly smug: market takeaway in one hand, a detour to the Dandenong Ranges when the city felt too beige. Grant’s Picnic Ground and Grants on Sherbrooke were the sort of places you’d recommend to relatives who’d flown in for “the bush” — fern gullies, mountain ash and a cafe that made you feel outdoorsy without actually walking more than 20 minutes. Puffing Billy and the 1000 Steps were the inevitable photo ops; Kalorama Lookout delivered mist and cinematic sunrise views, and the ngurrak barring art trail gave us something to tell visitors that wasn’t just “we have great food.”

Yes, we’ve had our soap-opera episodes. Pedestrian once ran a piece on the so‑called “acai wars” — remember when Munch Spot allegedly copped a vehicle attack? TikTok made it messy, local headlines made it louder, and we kept having to explain to outsiders that no, this wasn’t how every smoothie shop ends. Concrete Playground also chronicled our show-off moments: a Brick Lane brewery opened here with celebrity names attached, and rumours of Wendy’s drive-thrus in Dandenong South kept turning up in job listings and gossip columns.

Who would hate living here? People who loathe tourists at Micawber Tavern, anyone allergic to mist, or the type who cries “too many options” when the market menu takes longer than three minutes to decide.

Who would love it? Families leaning on local community supports — shoutout to the Dandenong Community & Learning Centre and the playgroup listings — plus campers heading to Kurth Kiln, art pilgrims checking out Burnham Beeches, and anyone who still thinks a multicultural market meal beats a polished bistro any day.

We’re a little rough, very varied and louder than the brochure suggests — and honestly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
