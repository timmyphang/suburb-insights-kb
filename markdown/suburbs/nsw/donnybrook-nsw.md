# Donnybrook (NSW)

Slug: `donnybrook-nsw`

We admit it: we’re the sort of place that measures its social calendar in craft-room openings and aqua-aerobics timetables. Back in the day — and yes, most of our clippings are a little dusty — we’d start the weekend at the Donnybrook Recreation Centre on Steere Street, the heated pool where the lanes were more social club than Olympic trial. It was the kind of place you saw twice a week and yet never knew anyone’s surname.

After a proper swim (or a proper sit by the pool pretending to lap), we used to duck into the Donnybrook Artisans at 108 South West Highway, a small hub where members sold ceramics and jam and probably a vase shaped like a sheep. The Arts and Crafts Group even had membership deals that read like a bargain bin for hobbyists: $60 a year, $4 for visitors — and they moved into new club rooms on that very South West Highway. We applaud the commitment to patterned tea-towels.

Then there was the weekend escape we'd brag about without ever actually booking: Donnybrook Eco Retreat — a two-person glamping tent on a 100‑acre farm near Maitland and the Hunter Valley. Perfect for when you wanted to Instagram bucolic views across paddocks while secretly missing your kettle.

We’ve had our moments of genuine drama too. Who could forget the Donnybrook Berries recall? The sewing-needle scandal that sent wastage viral and gave us a reputation as a brand name Hollywood could use to describe a whole-of-town whoops. Not our finest hour, but at least it gave the national press something to chew on.

Who would hate living here? If neon laneways, 24‑hour everything and an inner‑city soundtrack make you sneeze, move along. If the idea of a Biggest Morning Tea or a pottery market makes your palms itch, Donnybrook will not be your scene.

Who would love it? Families who liked an indoor heated pool, crafters who know the value of a $60 annual membership, and couples who dream of a 100‑acre glamping weekend near the Hunter. We’re small, patient and fond of our own weird rhythm — and we’ll never apologise for knowing where every espresso-free meeting spot is.
