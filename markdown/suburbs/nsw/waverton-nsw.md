# Waverton (NSW)

Slug: `waverton-nsw`

Back in the day we’d get up at a reasonable hour, wander down Larkin Street to the lookout and pretend we weren’t five minutes from the CBD while the sun hit the Harbour Bridge just so. Waverton Park and Larkin St Lookout gave us those postcard views — and, as Time Out reminded everyone, that skyline selfie spot never gets old.

Our weekend used to look like this: a slow lap of Balls Head Drive for the picnic crowd (Sitchu put it on the best picnic spots list), a detour through the Coal Loader Centre for Sustainability for whatever rooftop gardening event was on — Concrete Playground once ran the Botanica Gin and Spirits Journey there, and they even hosted Picnic on the Platform — then a polite nod to our bowlo on Woolcott Street where Waverton Bowling Club serves harbour views with your two-hour green (Concrete Playground even noted beginners’ lessons for $12). As Urban List listed, The Grumpy Baker used to be the local bakery outpost, and yes, it was dog-friendly — sorry, not trying to make Fido jealous.

Our most ongoing bit of suburban theatre? The ground beneath our feet. Urban List explained the Western Harbour Tunnel tunnelling kicked off in 2023 and that massive machines are now boring under Waverton en route to a 2027–2028 opening. We like to imagine the TBMs will leave us a latte bar when they’re done.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to harbour views, quiet village vibes or having a neighbour who can actually aim in lawn bowls. If you crave loud chain strips or a main drag that never sleeps, you’ll find our “village-like ambience” (thanks, Sitchu) mildly infuriating.

Who would love it: people who want a short commute (Sitchu says about 4km to the CBD, ten minutes by train), lovers of foreshore walks, rooftop gardeners, picnic planners and anyone who appreciates a good local bowlo lunch (Berry’s Bay Brasserie gets a shout in the listings). Time Out’s Berrys Bay park plans read like something we’ve been waiting on for a century — it’ll only make us smugger. We’re small, leafy and stubbornly harbour-adjacent. We know it — and frankly, we’re fine with that.
