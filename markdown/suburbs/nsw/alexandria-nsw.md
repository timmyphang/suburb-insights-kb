# Alexandria (NSW)

Slug: `alexandria-nsw`

We know the drill in Alexandria: weekend mornings used to be a parade of pastry decisions — Da Orazio Pasticceria, Wild Cockatoo Bakery, or the Ferreira family’s Tuga Pastries with that hush-hush pastel de nata trick. Back in the day our routes read like a mildly smug scavenger hunt: Bitton for crepes and that Moroccan‑spiced chicken burger we pretended was healthy, The Copper Mill tucked into a former printing press on Mitchell Road, and Meet Gerard on 54 Henderson Road serving Coffee Alchemy to the bleary-eyed.

Yes, we were that suburb: warehouses turned into brunch temples and design showrooms. Design By Them at 16 Stokes Ave meant your couch could be both sustainable and unbearably photogenic. Concrete Playground even called ours the place with OTT croissants and “the coolest public library in the city” — which, frankly, we still boast about at parties.

Now for the headline we’ve been pretending not to care about: The Grounds of Alexandria — the one Time Out said was the world’s most Instagrammed café in 2023 and 2024 — has just won a tender to open a new venue in The Domain, on the corner of Art Gallery and St Mary’s roads, on a 25‑year deal. So no, you can’t escape their seasonal wonderlands; they’re simply moving their magic (and the miniature animal farm, and the weekend crowds) into a more historic postcode. As Pedestrian reminders have shown, when Grounds goes full Cinderella or Frozen, it’s a full suburb theatrical.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who loathes queues, child-friendly farms, or the constant background hum of a brewery playlist — Holey Moley Alexandria was even Australia’s largest indoor putt‑putt, for goodness’ sake. If you’re after quiet cul‑de‑sac anonymity, this is the wrong postcode.

Who would love it? Families and caffeine pilgrims, design obsessives and brewery hop‑outs (Yulli’s Brews and Rocks Brewing Co Tap House were staples of our taproom map). We’re the suburb that turned industrial grit into festive festoon lights at Backyard at The Alex, shipping‑container bars and all. Come for the pastries, stay for the spectacle — but bring patience, a camera, and a soft spot for a place that knows how to make reinvention look ridiculous and very, very cosy.
