# Athelstone (NSW)

Slug: `athelstone-nsw`

Back in the day we used to call Athelstone “neat” and mean it – the kind of foothills suburb Adelaide Mums Group describes as picture-perfect if you squint from the car. We’re part of Campbelltown Council, about 25–30 minutes’ drive from the CBD (or roughly 45 if you trust the bus), and yes, we know exactly which way the hills slope.

Our weekend scene used to look like this: parents tiptoe into Tom Packer Reserve for a community planting day with Conservation Volunteers Australia (remember that seedling-planting event on World Environment Day?), kids roll out mat time at Athelstone Kindergym, and the keen ones head to Foxfield Hall on Maryvale Road to bounce a ping-pong ball at Athelstone Table Tennis Club. If tennis is more your vibe, updates used to come from the Athelstone Tennis Club at Max Amber Sportsfield on Range Road — Easter messages and the like — and Saint Ignatius’ College — Fidelis had the school run sorted on Manresa Court.

We’ve got the directory life too: the old business listings name-check Asset Personnel Pty Ltd, RJ’s Sign Services, Custom Brick Builds, Alexpert mobile accessories and Evolve Body Scans. It’s the practical, small-business backbone that keeps the whitegoods and the weekend projects alive.

Petty local drama? The papers don’t give us a scandal so we recycle the classic: whose parking space was that and who cut in line at the planting day. If you want fireworks, you’ll have to bring them — our controversies are mostly about lawn care and who forgot to bring the trowel.

Who would hate living here: anyone who wants tramlines, CBD cocktails at 10pm or an endless stream of late-night restaurants. If you need constant novelty, Athelstone will bore you in the best possible suburban way.

Who would love it: families who value green reserves, folks into community projects, parents who like organised playgroups and anyone who secretly enjoys a good recreational sport roster. For years the locals have quietly kept things humming — and honestly, we wouldn’t trade our little neat foothills for a skyline view.
