# Summer Hill (NSW)

Slug: `summer-hill-nsw`

Back in the day our Saturday started with a slow stumble down Lackey and Smith Streets — the kind of village drag where you’d pretend the traffic noise was “ambience.” We’d wander past the Flour Mill Markets at 18 Flour Mill Way (first and third Sundays, plastic‑free, as Concrete Playground cheerfully noted) with a tote bag already full of artisan nonsense and a sense of smugness that we’d found a farmers’ market inside a heritage flour mill.

If you were raising kids here you’d know Darrell Jackson Gardens like the back of your hand — the park that got an $800,000 playground refresh in 2022, with tennis courts and a skate park that made school pickup feel almost recreational. The suburb’s always leaned into the leafy, heritage‑home vibe: tree‑lined streets, a proper village, and trains that make the CBD feel like a commuter afterthought (Sitchu clocked it at about 7km and a 15‑minute hop to Central).

The foodie theatre used to be the real show. Broadsheet and Time Out catalogued our little renaissance: Solstice (from The New Paradigm Coffee Roasters) bringing a pour‑over menu and Saturday treats from Bloods, Postino Osteria squeezing homestyle Italy into the old One Penny Red post office, and Rio Milk Bar keeping the milk‑bar spirit alive with a festooned courtyard and Southeast Asian/Japanese touches. Apéritif & Co even wound up on Time Out’s radar with an award for Australia’s Best Wine List at 25 Lackey St — yes, our village went full Euro‑chic and we clapped.

We haven’t got much petty tabloid drama to report; the big shifts here were gentler — new wine bars, old milk bars reborn, and a bookshop (Broadsheet’s Rose Read) that made us feel cultured between school drop‑offs.

Who would hate it here? If you loathe village vibes, markets, characterful small bars like The Temperance Society or Vernon's Bar, or the idea that a Sunday should include artisan pastries and argument over which dumpling bar is the best, this isn’t your postcode.

Who would love it? Families, coffee obsessives, small‑bar hoppers and anyone who likes their heritage buildings repurposed with good taste and a bit of cheek. We know every corner — and yes, we’ll happily tell you where the best antipasti used to be.
