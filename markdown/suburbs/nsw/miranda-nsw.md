# Miranda (NSW)

Slug: `miranda-nsw`

Back in the day we’d kick off a Saturday by resigning ourselves to the full Miranda ritual: the kids (or nieces, or our inevitable nephews) drug through Westfield Miranda at 600 Kingsway for a Santa photo, while we pretended we hadn’t forgotten the present budget. As Urban List noted, Westfield used to be the place for seasonal nonsense — and for reasons of parental survival, honestly unbeatable.

For years the local leisure economy read like a children’s party wishlist. Inflatable World Miranda at The Rec Club was the kind of place listed as “one of the biggest inflatable playgrounds in Australia” (two hours for about $25 — glorious exhaustion). The Play Cave on 110 Parraweena Road, Sitchu reminded us, came with a multi-level maze and The Rock Cafe where grown-ups could exist in a separate, less traumatised timeline.

Our after-kids collapse options also looked very Miranda: Sky Zone Miranda in the Westfield was billed as the Southern Hemisphere’s largest trampoline park (enduro-Ninja-warrior-type stuff), and Urban List once crowned the Miranda Hotel a south-Sydney favourite — live music, pub feed staples and a new small bar called The Lane. Dining arguments used to centre on The Italian Kitchen Miranda — Danny Russo’s place that Urban List flagged for squid ink tagliatelle, market mushroom gnocchi and garlic prawn pizza — which, for a suburb that doubles as a family funpark, felt delightfully grown-up and mildly pretentious.

We’ll admit: if you despise the sound of joyful chaos — the patchwork roar of trampolines, the queue for Santa, the small debate about which pizza won last Tuesday’s dinner argument — Miranda isn’t your quiet cul-de-sac dream. But if you love places that can do both high-energy kids’ burnouts and genuinely decent Italian without apologising, you’ll fit right in.

Transit buffs (or those with patient grandparents) used to be told to take the train to Engadine then the 993 bus to Miranda via Woronora Heights, which sums up our vibe: slightly inconvenient, deeply useful, and full of local stories that always end in snacks. We’ll take the chaos, thanks.
