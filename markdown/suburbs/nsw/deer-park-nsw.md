# Deer Park (NSW)

Slug: `deer-park-nsw`

We still wake up like we own half the postcode — not because we do, but because someone in our house is already arguing about where the morning bread came from. Back in the day Broadsheet told the story: Latin Foods and Wine moved out this way, expanded into a deli, bakery, bottle shop and cafe when Maria De La Plaza swapped ANZ life for something with more empanadas. The Villanis were part of our carb heritage too — Impasto Forno Antico’s family had bakeries in Deer Park once upon a time, so yes, we know our sourdough origins.

Weekends here are somewhere between “bring a reusable bag” and “where’s the laksa?” — Concrete Playground has long thumbed a satisfied nod at Chef Lagenda in Deer Park, the franchise that’s been dishing out curry laksa since 2003. We’d argue it pairs nicely with a browse of a deli counter and the kind of small-town gossip that’s been doing rounds longer than any café seating chart.

We’ve had headlines — Pedestrian TV reported an incident in March where a woman was threatened in an underground shopping-centre car park — and like any place with history we file that under “keep an eye out” rather than “everything’s doom.” What keeps us rooted is quieter: Playgroup Victoria lists Deer Park Playgroup Centre and Deer Park North Primary School as hubs, and the PlayConnect+ listings remind us there’s a cluster of families who actually know each other by first name.

Then there’s the fancy bit the city begrudgingly loved — Time Out wrote up Deer Park Station’s Hassell redesign, the 2023 rebuild that later scored in the 2025 National Landscape Architecture Awards, with a rooftop garden and a cultural collaboration with Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Elders. It’s proof we can be both grassroots and surprisingly architecturally smug.

Who would hate it here? If you want a boutique brunch strip with curated playlists and sidewalk seating that looks like a magazine, you’ll find the vibe a bit earnest. Who would love it? Families who actually use playgroups, laksa faithful, people who treasure a proper deli-bakery backstory, and anyone quietly thrilled that our train station now has a rooftop garden to brag about. We’ll keep the empanadas warm and the local stories louder.
