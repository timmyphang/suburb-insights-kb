# Fairview Park (NSW)

Slug: `fairview-park-nsw`

Fairview Park, NSW — we tell visitors it’s the quiet little postcode where the loudest thing is the school bell, but if you Google us you mostly get two things: old community listings and a very glamorous case of mistaken identity.

Morning routine: we used to describe the school run by pointing to the SAcommunity entries for Fairview Park Primary School at 7 Nepondi Crt (that listing ties the preschool and OSHC to the primary school), and the Fairview Park Preschool that serves 0–5s and was listed as operating within the primary school grounds. The kindergarten entry noted accessibility features — wheelchair access, accessible toilets and parking — and Gowrie SA Kindy Care was listed as operating at the kindergarten (7 Cotton St) with before‑ and after‑school care (phone 08 8352 5144, lyndsay@gowriesa.org.au). All of those were older listings, so we say “used to be listed” rather than “pop in for a latte.”

Petty local drama: there isn’t much recorded. The internet’s take on Fairview Park is either practical preschool directories or Time Out Hong Kong’s much more dramatic Fairview Park — a 1976 private estate in Yuen Long with low‑rise houses, on‑site schools, a country club and a 15,000‑square‑metre artificial lake (and yes, even a dedicated sewage treatment plant made the roundups). So our scandal is identity theft by geography.

Who would hate it here: anyone who moved for restaurant clout or a spot on Broadsheet’s Hot List. Broadsheet has plenty to say about Melbourne and Sydney openings, but not much about our corner of the map. If your vibe is boutique buzzy nightlife and constant new openings, you’ll feel like you’ve been invited to a dinner party that’s actually a PTA meeting.

Who would love it: families who value a proper school run, accessible facilities and reliable before/after‑school care. If you want placid streets, practical services and the thrilling knowledge that the internet thinks you might also be in South Australia or Hong Kong, you’ll feel right at home.

We’re proud of our low-key life. We’ll take the school bell and the sensible parking over hype any day — and we’ll keep correcting the GPS with a smile.
