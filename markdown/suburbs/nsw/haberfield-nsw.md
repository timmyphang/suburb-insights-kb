# Haberfield (NSW)

Slug: `haberfield-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start Saturday with a ritual: dodge prams on the wide, leafy streets, then join the queue for Pasticceria Papa’s ricotta cheesecake like it was the last tram to the city. Broadsheet and Time Out both called us a little slice of Little Italy, and they weren’t wrong — Ramsay Street (and yes, the shopping strip gets name-checked everywhere) used to be where you could inhale the scent of baking and think, “We did the old country proud.”

For years the local press loved cataloguing our edible virtues: Cassaniti Bakery famously had people lining up for rosetta rolls, Zanetti 5 Star Deli stacked shelves with Parmigiano-Reggiano and truffle dreams, and Urban List still remembers Paesanella on 88 Ramsay Street among the best delis. Happyfield — the bright yellow corner café that Time Out profiled — was the place for fluffy pancakes (Broadsheet mentioned those Instagram queues too). Little West’s smoked brisket sausage rolls and muffuletta made the food writers smile. La Disfida’s thin-crust pizza and the Regatta Club at the UTS Haberfield Rowers Club were the go-to fixes when someone admitted Haberfield “didn’t have a pub” in the old stories.

We’ll admit it: we’re small, heritage-obsessed and a tad smug about our Federation-era houses and manicured gardens — Sitchu called us “heritage-listed” and “villagey,” and the real estate write-ups on Sitchu remind you that many homes sit on roomy blocks (Inner West Mums mentioned averages around 700sqm). That’s why we’ve got perfect nonna-vibes and more pantry-providores than footy pubs.

Petty local drama? The articles are mostly guides and mouth-watering menus, not council scandals — so the fiercest tussle in these pages is over who gets the last ricotta cannoli.

Who would hate living here? If you crave high-rise anonymity, a 7am siren and zero opinions on your garden fence, Haberfield will feel like a museum of tasteful judgment. Who would love it? Families who want space, anyone who worships proper Italian delis, and food lovers who remember (or wish they’d experienced) the long queues for bakery glory. We’ll keep breathing in the bakery steam and pretending the Bay Run was made just for our jogging selfies.
