# Narre Warren North (NSW)

Slug: `narre-warren-north-nsw`

Back in the day our Thursday mornings looked like a convoy of prams and the occasional tiny sock rebellion — because Thursdays were Narre Warren Kidz Playgroup days. We went for the roomy indoor play centre the committee insisted on (they switched to it to dodge the weather, apparently — history page nods to that), and stayed for the sheltered sandpit, soft‑play area and the kids’ play kitchen that made everyone momentarily believe play food could solve the week.

We’re the sort of suburb where the local internet footprint is mostly mums’ directories and community service pages — MumsPages lines up our Write Start Education options alongside SESI Care Services and even Ultimate Demolitions (yes, that last one is a listing; you can compare reviews if you like). The Mum Blog spells out the practical stuff: sessions for 0–5s, a Thursday slot, and even a note that fees were set at $45 per term per family for 2026 on the session page — which we’ll file under “community budgeting, but cuter.”

There isn’t a juicy council scandal in these snippets — the real cliffhanger is committee turnover. As the Narre Warren Kidz Playgroup history reminds us, leadership has shuffled over the years like library chairs. Riveting stuff. We’ll admit we prefer it that way: less drama, more organisation (and more room for the sandpit).

Who would hate living here? If you need to be 10 minutes from the CBD on a whim, this might not be for you — Wikipedia helpfully notes we’re about 36 km south‑east of Melbourne’s centre. If you crave a suburb soundtrack of late‑night neon and rooftop clubs, we’re probably not your scene.

Who would love it here? Families, clearly. The ABS QuickStats shows a population that skews settled (median age 43; population 8,033 in 2021), and services like Casey North CISS are handy for the practical side of life (they offer support and list a contact on their site: 03 9705 6699). If you like sensible committees, roomy play spaces and a community that lists everything from facial treatments to in‑home care on MumsPages, you’ll fit right in.

We’re not flashy. We’re reliably useful. And if you judge us, we’ll invite you to play kitchen for tea.
