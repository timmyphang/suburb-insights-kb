# Matraville (NSW)

Slug: `matraville-nsw`

We stand on our corner of Matraville and watch the sun sink, because Broadsheet liked to point out we’re only a 20-minute drive from the CBD — but also like to remind people we might as well be on another planet. Back in the day the planet’s hobby was neat hedges, tidy streets and, come early December, a full-on neighbourhood audition for the Christmas lights Olympics. The whole suburb turns into a silent disco of flashing Santas and inflatable penguins; it’s our annual civic pride and passive-aggressive decoration contest.

Our weekend rhythm has a gentle, practical cadence. For years the local ritual involved Heffron Park — the Matraville Sports Centre at 441 Bunnerong Rd — which Time Out once described as the kind of place with 12 tennis courts (day/night hire), squash, indoor netball and the rest. Somewhere between the courts and the carpark you’ll find the playgroup crowd: the Matraville Intergenerational sessions at St James Villa on 60 Lawson Street used to list Thursdays at 2:30pm, a lovely loop where toddlers and seniors swapped stories and crisply packed biscuits.

We’re not trendy. We’re the suburb you mention when someone says “quieter than Marrickville” — and Concrete Playground, bless it, had plenty to say about Marrickville’s breweries and libraries while we kept being charmingly suburban. Even Brooklyn Boy Bagels closed up shop in Matraville in 2016, according to Urban List, and decamped to markets with plans in Marrickville — we waved them off with a bemused smile and a thermos.

Who would hate living here? People who need a boutique on every corner, someone who measures life in craft beers per postcode or can’t comprehend a sunset interrupted only by the occasional ute reversing. Also anyone allergic to the kind of calm that’s actually loud in its refusal to be buzzy.

Who’d love it? Families who still think community centres are a feature, seniors who like a playgroup that doubles as social reconnaissance, and anyone who believes a suburban December can be more magical than a festival. We’ll keep our neat hedges and our overachieving Christmas displays — and if you want Marrickville’s heat, you can always drive 20 minutes.
