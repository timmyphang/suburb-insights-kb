# Liverpool (NSW)

Slug: `liverpool-nsw`

We’re the lot who know where the council carols end and the Paper Mill’s fairy lights begin — yes, we live in Liverpool, and we’ll happily tell you where to park your cynicism.

Back in the day, if you wanted to start your weekend like a local you’d stroll past the Yellamundie Civic Place Library and Art Gallery — that six‑level beast that opened on December 3, 2023, with something like 85,000 items and two kilometres of shelving. It made civic pride look like a hobby we suddenly took seriously. On a good day we’d then wander riverside to the heritage building that used to be the Paper Mill Food precinct; it relaunched in 2025 after Platinum Hospitality Group stepped in, promising three restaurants, a cocktail bar and room for up to 600 guests. Big promises. Big chairs.

If you’re after cuisine with character, Broadsheet once had us pencilled in for Fiji Style Desi Dhaba on Railway Street — a spot serving Fijian and curry mash‑ups that made our weeknights less predictable. That’s the kind of thing that keeps us humble and slightly full.

The official narrative says Liverpool is “almost too vast to pin down,” and we’re not mad about that — we’ve got room for your ambitions, your awkward first apartment and that inexplicable karaoke habit. There’s also the looming Bradfield City Centre next to the Western Sydney Airport; Time Out noted it could deliver 200,000 new jobs. Which is either a miracle or a very long commute, depending on how many coffee shops survive the transition.

Public transport nerds, take note: Urban List reported a set of Western Sydney bus routes due to start on 5 July 2026, including routes 825 and 860 to Western Sydney International Airport. Whether that will make us feel metropolitan or like we’re on an express tour of every roundabout in the west remains to be seen.

Who would hate living here? If you need a postcode that smells faintly of curated gentility and predictability, move along. Who would love it? People who like a place that’s reinventing itself, has a library you can get lost in for three hours, and will feed you curry on a Tuesday like it’s a public service.

We’re Liverpool — loud, evolving, and stubbornly our own.
