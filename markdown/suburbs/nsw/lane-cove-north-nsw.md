# Lane Cove North (NSW)

Slug: `lane-cove-north-nsw`

Back in the day a Saturday in Lane Cove North meant we’d loiter like it was an artform — stroller parking at the Meeting House on Stokes Street, then a strategic reconnaissance to 72 Helen Street to see if the Sydney Daifuku Store’s pillowy daifuku had managed not to sell out yet. Broadsheet wrote the mochi was gone daily; Chisato Nakayama’s five fillings became our unofficial village currency.

We used to brag about new arrivals too: Barrel One rolled in with its New‑York‑style bagels (their fifth site, if you were counting), and The Alcott swaggered onto the scene with a spritz bar and a terrace you could Instagram from every angle. Hummerston’s Gourmet Butcher quietly did what it’s always done — 40‑year pedigree, preservative‑free sausages and the sort of free delivery that feels like being looked after by a very particular uncle.

If your weekend plan involves competitive picnic real estate, Time Out’s 2025 liveability hailstorm made sense — Lane Cove National Park is literally the place you drive to when you want a serious barbecue and a rowboat for the ‘gram. The Canopy precinct got a name check too, because if a place earns a precinct, we must be doing something right.

We don’t have much petty council drama in the files here, so instead we’ll own the small contradictions: we’re quaint and villagey and also very into bottomless mussels — Urban List remembered Garçon’s all‑you‑can‑eat mussels-and-fries Wednesdays (around $36) like it was a civic duty.

Who would hate living here? Anyone allergic to queues for dessert, to people having opinions about bagel authenticity, or to your weekend being jointly controlled by playgroup timetables and national‑park weather forecasts. If you can’t handle being gently outclassed by a 40‑year butcher, this is not for you.

Who would love it? Families — Sitchu even notes the weekend village takeover — nature folks who want picnic options and rowboats, and food obsessives who remember when daifuku were the scarce local treasure. We’re leafy, a little precious, and quietly obsessed with our own good taste — and honestly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
