# Strathfield (NSW)

Slug: `strathfield-nsw`

We know exactly how a Strathfield morning used to unfold: first there was the festival buzz in Strathfield Square — we still talk about that Strathfield Festival (May 2–4, 2024) that turned the place into three nights of multicultural eats and entertainment — then a slow drift down The Boulevarde, past all the places that put us on any food map.

Back in the day we queued for com tam and banh canh cua at Saigon Things, reminisced about Duo Duo’s Vietnamese iced coffee gelato and their deep‑fried ice cream at 11 The Boulevarde, and argued whether Jang Ta Bal at 48A The Boulevarde was the OG of Strathfield K‑BBQ. We used to point out Hansang’s homestyle Korean soups, Ho Jiak’s Penang snacks, Abhi’s Indian dining, Biwon’s jajangmyeon and tangsuyuk, The Mandoo’s buzzy vibe, and even Kosuke Ramen’s honten over in North Strathfield when someone claimed we were light on options. Mister Gee and Club9 in North Strathfield gave the post‑dinner crowd somewhere to be loudly competitive about pool and darts. Property porn? Sitchu listings made sure our garden pools and basement cinemas got their moments too.

The petty local drama? Oh, we had that in spades — council motions about foreign‑language shopfront signage (the one moved by Independent councillor Matthew Blackmore) turned our multilingual strip into headline fodder, and Danny Lim’s long history around the fountain — from fines to being a local character — kept local conversations colourful. Trains being everyone’s mortal enemy surfaced too: that fallen power line near Strathfield station that hit a passing train and sent commuters into chaos was the kind of day we all gripe about at the top of the stairs. Real estate churn gave us stories as well — burned houses and sludge‑filled pools auctioned for eye‑watering sums — because why wouldn’t Strathfield be dramatic?

Who would hate living here? If you crave anonymity, pristine predictability or a suburb without its own characters and council meetings to gossip about, this isn’t for you.

Who would love it? Food obsessives, festival folk, families using local services (hello, RoboThink and all the mums’ directories) and anyone happy to live where the streets come with opinion, flavour and the occasional scandal — that’s us, proudly.
