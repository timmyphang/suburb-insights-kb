# Bexley (NSW)

Slug: `bexley-nsw`

Back in the day we’d shuffle out for a weekend walk that always ended in someone else’s dinner: a cream doughnut from My Mother’s Cousin, then a consolation gyros while we debated whether Self Raised’s sourdough could be considered a personality trait. As Broadsheet noted, Sugo arrived like a retro cousin at Shop 3, 8–20 Sarsfield Circuit — a neighbourhood pasta bar spun off from the same My Mother’s Cousin crew — and for a while our biggest decision was noodle shape, not mortgage structure.

We used to time our mornings around where the queues were shortest. Time Out loved Gyradiko for its generous pork and chicken gyros (and even cited that simple gyros once went for $7.80), so the corner takeaway was a reliable bottleneck. Ken’s Sushi Bar Dining? Bookings were the local currency — as Time Out said, dinner without a reservation was a fantasy. Self Raised’s retro bakery was the etiquette school of pastry: sourdough, focaccia, NYC-style sangas and, yes, the Snack Shoppe kiosk to soothe the more impatient of us during peak panic-buying hours (Concrete Playground covered the behind-the-scenes hustle).

Our petty local drama was never about council fights; it was whether My Mother’s Cousin sold out yet — Urban List reported it was selling out every night when it opened — and whether Sweet Beirut’s two-level, 170-sweet stronghold had anything left by the time you’d finished the school run. The Doll Hospital — established 1913, because of course we have a century-old doll clinic — sits there like proof that our strip can be charmingly weird and stubbornly old-fashioned at once.

Who would hate Bexley? People who hate queues, carbs, and the idea that a local pizza-and-wing operation might be more exciting than their latest app. People who require a rooftop bar and neon for validation would be bored.

Who would love it? Food obsessive families, nostalgic bakery hoarders, anyone who appreciates a corner shop with history and a pizzeria that treats dough like a slow hobby. We’re not flashy, we’re functional and delicious — and that’s our whole vibe.
