# Mount Lawley (NSW)

Slug: `mount-lawley-nsw`

Back in the day, we’d stroll down Beaufort Street like it owed us money — coffee in hand (or at least the memory of one) — and stop by the Seinfeld mural by the 2nd Avenue IGA to take the touristy photo our relatives would insist on. Mount Lawley used to feel like a compact, sun-drenched village that had borrowed a European accent and a New York playlist: Soho Lane’s pink-hued brunch moods, Mary Street Bakery pastries that made you forgive parking, and Planet Books keeping late-night bibliophiles company after the Astor showed a Leone double bill.

For years the local food scene read like a tasteful mixtape. We had Mame with its five‑generation tofu story and humble obanzai sets, Testun’s playful Italian energy from the Threecoins crew, Le Rebelle doing Paris-with-beer vibes, and Mack Daddy’s serving up New York–size pizza that you needed a friend — or a small forklift — to finish. Local & Aesthetic popped up in a heritage tiled doorway and felt like the kind of project two hospitality founders would dream up between perfecting their lighting and curating WA-made ceramics. Cheol’s rebrand into a Korean-style wine bar was part of our slow cultural glow-up.

Then there was the plan: the ECU Mount Lawley Redevelopment Master Plan was finalised with talk of up to 1,100 new homes, a Creative Industries Hub, a proposed urban primary school and keeping WAAPA where it belongs. We watched the promises of green corridors and walkability like a soap opera — exciting, slightly alarming, and definitely worth a drink at the Inglewood Hotel afterwards. The Inglewood has been holding court since 1936; it’s where the screens are honest and the menu reliably stodgy.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe leafy streets, independent bookshops, packed small bars and the comforting smell of fresh soy milk from a dedicated tofu spot — look elsewhere. If you can’t handle the possibility of bumping into playgroup parents at Banks Reserve or the Highgate Playgroup in Forrest Park, Mount Lawley will test your social stamina.

Who would love it? Families who like organised chaos (hello Highgate and Menora playgroups), food obsessives who collect pasta shapes like stamps, and anyone who wants their suburb to be both characterful and capable of surprising you with a nine-dish obanzai set. Mount Lawley: small enough to know, big enough to keep us entertained.
