# Queenstown (NSW)

Slug: `queenstown-nsw`

We are the Queenstown everyone forgets to fact-check. Every family group chat that starts with “which Queenstown?” ends with a pilgrimage to Lake Wakatipu, Coronet Peak and Fergburger — and we sit there like, yes, but which one?

Back in the day the travel pages were unanimous: the internet’s idea of Queenstown involves a skyline gondola up Bob’s Peak, luge runs, Onsen Pools and world‑class ski fields like Coronet Peak and The Remarkables. Broadsheet wrote about Eichardt’s Private Hotel and its lake‑view suites; Urban List did lists of “best restaurants” (looking at you, The Grille by Eichardt’s and Boardwalk). Time Out and Concrete Playground keep selling gondolas and panoramic views as if that’s our backyard. Meanwhile, Sitchu’s pieces keep sending misty‑eyed readers to a different Queenstown entirely — the Tasmanian moonscape with Empire Hotel, Tracks Cafe and Moonscape Wine Bar, and adventures on the West Coast Wilderness Railway.

Our weekend routine, as imagined by national outlets, involves wine trails, ski passes and a breakfast by Lake Wakatipu. The reality? We watch other Queenstowns hog the headlines while people tag us in photos of Nelson Falls and the Basket of Dreams on Queenstown Hill Time Walk and expect the same from our postcode. Concrete Playground even announced a shiny new Coronet Ridge Resort — not here, but you’d forgive people for assuming.

There’s no petty council drama in the clippings — just a relentless case of mistaken identity. The only local spat is whether Fergburger is overrated, according to several itineraries. We don’t mind. Own the mix‑up with pride. It’s flattering that people think we have gondolas, gourmet grills and steam‑train dining.

Who would hate living here? If you’re allergic to confusion, can’t handle tourists asking for Nelson Falls directions, or insist every travel guide must be geopolitically accurate, this repeated identity crisis will drive you mad.

Who would love it? If you’re the sort who enjoys being part of a club with multiple famous cousins — alpine lakes, moonscapes, steam trains and all — we’re your kind of place. Come for the mystery; stay for the smug satisfaction when you tell visitors, “Wrong Queenstown, mate — but nice try.”
