# Northcote (NSW)

Slug: `northcote-nsw`

Back in the day we’d saunter up High Street with coffee-in-hand (or at least the idea of one), passing the directory listings that made Northcote sound like our own little global village. High Street was even crowned the “coolest street” in the world in 2024 — yes, we basked in that headline and pretended it wasn’t the thing we’d been telling interstate visitors for years.

Our weekend ritual used to be gloriously simple: linger through the markets the mums’ pages listed (remember the Northcote Autumn Weekend Market and that Northcote Night Market that popped up in the 2025 listings?), argue over whether Estelle or Va Penne was the better night-out compromise, and file into the Northcote Social Club for a gig. The Social Club has been doing the heavy lifting for live music forever — Concrete Playground chronicled its two‑decade birthday program and we were there, drunk on nostalgia and kebab sauce. We also watched the Northcote Aquatic & Recreation Centre reopen after that lavish 18‑month upgrade and silently judged anyone who swam laps without making eye contact.

Petty local drama? Oh, we had our blackout — the one Pedestrian called #Hipstergeddon when parts of Northcote and Brunswick West went dark for six hours. We staged candlelit vinyl listening parties like it was cultural resistance. There was also talk of a half-billion-dollar makeover for Northcote Plaza — big renderings by Woods Bagot, bricks nodding to the old brickworks — which made us simultaneously thrilled and suspicious of anything with “mixed-use” on it.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe live music, markets, and tram noise, Northcote will feel like a personality clash. If “heritage theatre turned creative space” and a town hall full of indie vinyl stalls sounds like a headache, move along — we won’t cry.

Who would love it? Artists, families, music nerds and anyone who likes a suburb that still remembers Merri Creek and also dreams in gig posters. We used to queue for croissant wheels when Calle announced it was coming to High Street, and while some things change, the mix of trattorias, Syrian banquet spots like Shamiat, train‑station‑themed eateries such as STN and the stubborn, glorious noise of live music at Northcote Theatre and the Social Club kept us coming back. We’re biased, loud and oddly proud — and that’s exactly how we like it.
