# Parramatta (NSW)

Slug: `parramatta-nsw`

Back in the day we used to joke that Parramatta was Sydney’s cheeky little sibling — now everyone else is trying to copy our homework. We still start Sundays by pretending we’re on a riverside pilgrimage: a stroll along the Parramatta River, a detour through Parramatta Park past Old Government House, and a nostalgic nod to anything that smells faintly of history or natural wine.

As Broadsheet once put it, Parramatta has "unpretentious cafes" and immigrant cuisine — and we wear both like a badge. We remember when Misc in Parramatta Park was the place for communal Mediterranean plates (focaccia, sujuk, smoked labneh — you know the drill). Westfield Parramatta still hums, the River City gets its theatre fix at Riverside Theatre (yes, we saw Shrek there), and Harvey’s Hot Sandwiches in Parramatta Square made the internet pay attention to our snack game.

There’s been buzz — some might call it a glow-up, others a civic identity crisis. Time Out wrote up the 25‑year Parramatta 2050 plan and the council has been floating a new Chinatown precinct and a civic square to revamp Church Street. Urban List teed up Powerhouse Parramatta, slated to open in 2026, promising to make the west’s cultural cred harder to ignore. We’re equal parts excited and wary — every artist’s laneway needs a good espresso and a queue.

Who hates it here? If you’re allergic to festivals (we had Good Things at Parramatta Park), people on bikes, or multiple playgroup choices — All Saints Anglican Church’s Parramatta Playtime, PHIVE’s Parramatta Penguins, James Ruse Reserve play sessions — then Parra will try your patience. If your dream suburb is a static postcard, ours is a slideshow on fast-forward.

Who loves it? Families who use ParraParents guides and Ollie Webb Reserve’s Livvi’s Place; food hunters following Urban List and Concrete Playground’s tips; history buffs who can spend a day bouncing from heritage-listed sandstone to Badu Mangrove Boardwalk and Lake Parramatta. We’re noisy, proudly caught between past and what’s-next, and frankly, we like it that way. If you can handle ambition, kebabs at midnight and a museum opening in 2026, you’ll fit right in. As Broadsheet said: come for the food, stay for the river — and don’t be surprised if you never leave.
