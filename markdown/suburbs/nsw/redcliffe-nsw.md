# Redcliffe (NSW)

Slug: `redcliffe-nsw`

Back in the day we’d tell Brisbane mates “come north for the day” and point them at the usual suspects — Redcliffe Jetty, Settlement Cove Lagoon and a walk along Marine Parade before you cross the Ted Smout Memorial Bridge back to civvy street. We still brag that our foreshore looks like a postcard someone mildly edited in the holiday brochures. 

Our weekend rhythm used to be unmistakable: sleep in, stagger down to the Jetty Markets for a browse (and a laugh at the same old stalls), watch the pelicans get their feed at Pelican Park, then pretend the Bee Gees Way isn’t the town’s best-kept pop-culture flex. If nostalgia’s your thing, you could do worse than remembering Undersea Putt & Play — the underwater-themed 18-hole mini golf that made us feel like pirates for an afternoon.

We’ve had festivals that make us proud and slightly exhausted: Redcliffe Festival, the Festival of Frights that turned Redcliffe Parade into a pirate-friendly spookfest, Christmas Twilight Markets that stretched past dusk, and even Easter pop-ups along the jetty. Once there was word that the world’s softball eyes would be on us — the Women’s Softball World Cup finals were announced for Talobilla Park in 2027 — and we clapped politely while checking the calendar.

We’re not all seaside serenity. For years there’s been the practical stuff in the background — Redcliffe Hospital, the IGA within walking distance, property listings up and down Anzac Avenue and John Street reminding us someone always wants to flip waterfront views into numbers on a spreadsheet. And the charities quietly doing the heavy lifting — shout-out to Redcliffe Mums Inc and the Miracle Mums Movement for keeping neighbourhoods stitched together when the sun goes down.

Who’d hate living here? People who need late-night city theatrics, or anyone allergic to pelican choreography. Also, anyone who insists their weekend must include underground techno.

Who’d love it? Families who want lagoons and jetties on tap, day-trippers chasing scenic drives, and anyone who thinks a Bee Gees mural is a legitimate reason to move. We’re a bit sleepy, a bit earnest, possibly obsessed with markets — and honestly, that’s half the charm.
