# Ormond (NSW)

Slug: `ormond-nsw`

We start our weekend on the hill at Point Ormond Lookout, pretending the city skyline is our screensaver and not the thing we commute past every week. For years that little rise has been our sunset theatre — Junkee and Sitchu called it a selfie-friendly stopping point, and honestly, we’d miss the light show more than the espresso.

Back in the day our Saturday ritual involved a battered bike and a house-made scone. Broadsheet once did a whole profile on Velo Espresso — the cafe that doubled as a bike workshop, Velo Rapport, run by Chris Savage and Lisa Rush — where you could sip and wait while someone tightened your gears. Meanwhile, Common Ground used to roast on an old Probat and sell beans from a communal table before it was listed as permanently closed; Time Out and Urban List noted the roastery vibes at 750 North Rd. If you remember that smell of coffee and roast oil, you know what we mean.

Our village hums along Ormond Road (yes, the one that bleeds into Elwood), where Repeat Offender and Foxes Den cropped up in reviews — Concrete Playground and Urban List charted their evolution from mornings to mezcal nights. The Commons Collective took over the bluestone Ormond Hall with garden bars and a Rosé Garden designed by Paul Bangay, if you like your outdoor drinking with a side of landscaping pedigree. Ormond Hall itself has been the backdrop to everything from weddings to festivals since 1891, according to Time Out — classy enough to make us feel cultured when we’re really just chasing free parking.

Petty domestic drama? Not much in the clips we’ve got — mostly cafe turnover and venue repackaging. That’s our drama: willing landlords, new menus, the occasional seasonal closure and the eternal question of which outdoor garden is objectively best for a rosé.

Who would hate it here? People who loathe sunsets, heritage halls, communal tables and the logistics of bike maintenance. Who would love it? Cyclists nostalgic for Velo Rapport, families who remember the Montessori-centred Ormond Childcare, and anyone who enjoys niche garden bars and a proper view of the bay. We’re a small village with a stubbornly good sunset — and we’re not about to say sorry for it.
