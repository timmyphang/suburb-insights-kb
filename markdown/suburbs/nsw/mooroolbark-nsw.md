# Mooroolbark (NSW)

Slug: `mooroolbark-nsw`

We know Mooroolbark by sound and by lighting — the hum of a miniature steam train on a Saturday morning, the distant rattle of someone inflating a three‑metre reindeer down the street, the shout from a playgroup leader counting off kiddie instruments. We’re the ones who nod at neighbours on Brice Avenue and pretend we don’t notice the inflatable Santa sleigh next door.

Our weekend routine? We used to wheel the pram past the Mooroolbark Community Centre at 125 Brice Avenue — Time Out even listed it and noted the Red Earth Gallery living there — and wonder if the latest exhibition pairs well with fluorescent lighting. The Parents Hub on Manchester Rd gets name‑checked for its Free Supported Playgroup and hub perks, and Melbourne Mums have bookmarked Phoenix Hair Design at Shop 11, 66–74 Brice Avenue as the family‑friendly salon for those days when we need more than a trim.

Petty local drama, you say? Well, we do have stories. Pedestrian TV once reported a bloke losing his licence after taking a motorised esky for a spin through Mooroolbark — $1,100 in fines and a lesson about horsepower we’ll never forget. And every Christmas, Boyd Close used to turn into a tacky masterpiece: roof displays, a snow machine and inflatables galore — Sitchu described the life‑size sleigh and 3m reindeer — all to raise funds for the Mooroolbark CFA. We clap and donate; we also judge the tack.

The misfits: people who despise community effort, seasonal sparkle or tiny trains will hate us here. If you loathe $3 train rides (yes, the Mooroolbark Miniature Railway ran rides for that fee and told families to pre‑book), or cannot abide Halloween trains turned into "graveyards of tracks", move on. If you roll your eyes at Kids’ music sessions, you’ll find the Mooroolbark Manor Intergenerational Music Program — Playgroup Victoria listed it on Apr 28, 2026 — painfully wholesome.

Who’d love it? Families who want neighbourly chaos, volunteers who enjoy fundraising for the CFA, folks who think a community centre, a miniature railway and a noisy playgroup make a proper cultural precinct. We’ll happily trade Melbourne flash for Mooroolbark heart — inflatable reindeer and all.
