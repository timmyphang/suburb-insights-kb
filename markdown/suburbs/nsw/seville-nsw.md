# Seville (NSW)

Slug: `seville-nsw`

Seville, NSW — back in the day the only Seville we could find in the papers was the one that lived five hours away in the Upper Yarra Valley, so if you’re scrolling for local gossip we’ll be honest: our file on Seville, NSW is basically a postcard from Seville Estate at 65 Linwood Road.

We know the Sunday routine as it was written about: a slow drive out to paddocks, a table with a slow‑cooked short‑rib on the menu and a barrel‑fermented chardonnay in hand. Broadsheet called Seville Estate the “epicentre of Victorian food”; they weren’t shy about the red soils, the old‑vine shiraz or the views across paddocks to the Baw‑Baw ranges. Sitchu even noted the restored homestead, the pool and native gardens — and that the place was crowned 2019 Winery of the Year. Fancy-sounding, yes, but we’ll take humble rows of vines over another rooftop bar with lipstick-stained air-con.

Petty drama? The archives only give us culinary quarrels: are you booking the Italian classics at Killara Estate in Seville East, or joining The Shortest Lunch at Seville Hill? Concrete Playground planted those two as your two choices for a weekend argument — both very civil, very vinous, and ending with someone admitting the other’s pick was “actually great.”

Who would hate it here: people who need flashing lights, flat‑out nightlife and immediate Uber Eats delivery. If your idea of excitement is a midnight kebab after the club, Seville will give you crickets and a cellar door card instead.

Who would love it here: we’d recommend it to anyone who smells like a good vintage and likes their dining rooms to look out over paddocks rather than parking meters. Folks who prize multi‑generation winemaking, on‑site accommodation for a family getaway, and the kind of menu that shows visiting chefs‑in‑residence actually cared — that’s your people. Even James Cornwall’s Tenable Dining links (Young Hearts, Bonny Fitzroy and Seville Estate) suggest Seville has pedigree, if not postcode clarity.

So yeah — our Seville, NSW mood board is mostly Victorian vineyards in the archives. Still, if you want quiet, quality and a very good bottle on a verandah, we’ll pretend it’s in our postcode and pass you the corkscrew.
