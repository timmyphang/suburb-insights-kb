# Springvale (NSW)

Slug: `springvale-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean the only write‑ups we’ve got are the slightly older ones — Springvale was the place we’d brag about to inner‑city friends while secretly hoping they never move here. We know the routine: queue for a pork roll at Bun Bun Bakery (Time Out said lines snake out the door and Concrete Playground practically worshipped the house‑baked rolls), then wander through Springvale Market Square for cheap produce and the kind of snacks that make your mum ring you five times asking for the recipe. As Broadsheet noted, more than 69% of residents were born overseas, and you could taste that in every bite.

Mornings used to look like this in our heads — clutching a banh mi from Bun Bun (Urban List alleged they churned out over 700 a day), picking up flaky goods from Tabac Bakery or Spring Hot Bread, and plotting our pho pilgrimage to Pho Hung Vuong because someone always swore that hole‑in‑the‑wall did broth best (Concrete Playground made us believe it). For dessert? Bok’s rice cakes and mochi were a thing of legend — Hana Yoo’s bakery had more than ten types, back when the write‑ups were writing.

Local drama, such as it was, came in planning documents and council maps: we were one of ten suburbs eyed for higher‑density activity centre zoning (Concrete Playground, remember?). Translation: the skyline might one day have had opinions. Or not. Either way, it gave us something to complain about while holding a sandwich.

Who would hate Springvale? If you loathe queues, variety, or anywhere that isn’t a single artisanal latte away from being indistinguishable, you’ll find it vexing. If you want suburbia to come with only one kind of takeaway and a strict quiet hour at 5pm, move on — we’ll miss the smugness.

Who would love it here? Food pilgrims, bakers of backpacks, families who remember kids’ holiday chaos at Springvale Indoor Sports or the Homemaker Centre’s Summer Wonderland, and anyone who finds a bus tour of Springvale Botanical Cemetery surprisingly civilised. We wear our multicultural chaos like a badge — a little messy, very delicious, and undeniably ours.
