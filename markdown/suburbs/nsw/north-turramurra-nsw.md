# North Turramurra (NSW)

Slug: `north-turramurra-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start Saturday like a very civilised cliché: sticky fingers from a Flour Shop pastry and a Single O pour-over in hand. As Broadsheet put it, Flour Shop was in the business of doing three things well — bread, pastries and coffee — and for a while that was our unofficial civic anthem.

We wander the strip that used to feel like a village by accident: Woolworths Metro opened on 3 November (North Shore Mums gave us the proud ribbon-cut), the Orange Green Playground reopened in July with inclusive gear the kids took to like it was their right, and Turramurra Kindergarten has been the boutique daycare we point to when explaining why we can still pronounce “playdate” with dignity. For the plant-obsessed among us, Honeysuckle’s Turramurra outpost has long been a green-fingered lifeline — Broadsheet and Sitchu both note it as a proper nursery stop.

If you like your meat with provenance and a side of old-school charm, Exclusive Meats at 288 Bobbin Head Road was the family-run butcher we bragged about — Nuremberg bratwurst, continental frankfurters, the lot. For a quiet celebration, Tom’s Cellars used to be the boutique bottle‑o we’d whisper about; Concrete Playground even called it ‘a journey through wine’. And if someone fancied feeling slightly more metropolitan, Kipling’s Garage Bar over in Turramurra (2 Eastern Road) got a nod for industrial-chic wine and tapas less than a minute from the station.

No, the archives don’t have a juicy council scandal for us today — shockingly wholesome, we know. The real personality here is the bush: national parks, Bobbin Head and the Lane Cove River Walk that make our backyard a very scenic way to humble any city slicker’s stride.

Who would hate it here? Nightlife hunters and people who think “walkable” means 24-hour ramen and neon. Who would love it? Families, gardeners, walkers and anyone who rates a good croissant, a proper sausage, and a calm train trip into the city over clinking barstools. We’re North Turramurra: quietly smug, very green, and strangely smug about it.
