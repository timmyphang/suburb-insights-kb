# Osterley (NSW)

Slug: `osterley-nsw`

We live in Osterley, NSW — which is a lovely sentence to say until you Google it and the internet politely suggests you meant something else.

Morning routine: we make a cup of tea, open the laptop, and watch search results roll out a very confusing family tree. First up, Time Out wants to show us Osterley Park and House — an 18th‑century Georgian palace in London that apparently does candle‑lit Full Moon Balls and once heard a cuckoo after 20 years. Then Broadsheet hands us "Oster" — not us, an osteria in Richmond with Lombardy pasta and a deconstructed lemon meringue tart. And if that wasn’t disorienting enough, there’s a Sitchu listing for 1 Osterley Road in Yeronga, QLD, plus St Mary’s Osterley Playgroup files from Isleworth that read like a volunteer committee saga. We’re flattered, honestly — we just never realised our suburb was so cosmopolitan.

Back in the day (and still, it seems), the best local news we can scrounge up is other people’s Osterleys. Time Out rhapsodises about Georgian interiors; Broadsheet waxes lyrical about filled pasta; Mumsnet argues about parking at Osterley tube station and whether the Piccadilly line will finish your commute for you. Meanwhile, us? We get mistaken for a National Trust property and a Melbourne restaurant in the same breath. Peak identity crisis.

Who would hate living here: anyone who’s bought tickets to a Bridgerton‑style weekend expecting state apartments and Robert Adam plasterwork. Who would love it: people who like being delightfully anonymous, who prefer their suburb to be the one that comes as a surprise when you finally visit — and who enjoy the quiet thrill of telling visitors “No, not that Osterley” as they blink at their phone.

For years the local narrative has been outsourced — to London, to Richmond, to Isleworth. And honestly? We kind of like being the underdog Osterley. Let them have the ballrooms and the risotto. We’ll keep the mystery.
