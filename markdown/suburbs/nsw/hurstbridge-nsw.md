# Hurstbridge (NSW)

Slug: `hurstbridge-nsw`

Back in the day — or at least according to the archives we've got — Hurstbridge felt like a country postcard that accidentally grew a Michelin-sized ego. We know every quiet corner: the place is named after Henry Hurst, who famously built a log bridge across Diamond Creek, and for years the town has been a tidy mix of commuters, farmers and artists (Time Out’s words, not ours — but we’ll take the compliment).

Our weekend ritual used to involve pretending we were too busy to leave the postcode while secretly booking tiny-table flexes. Greasy Zoe’s was the village flex: an eight‑seat, multi‑course shrine run by Zoe Birch and Lachlan Gardner (Shop 3/850 Heidelberg–Kinglake Road), lauded by Time Out, Sitchu and Broadsheet as the sort of place you make a road trip for. Restaurant St Lawrence likewise cropped up in Broadsheet as a 25‑seat diner with a monthly menu and weekend bakeshop — the sort of locally obsessed place that makes you feel smug about your postcode.

If you’re waiting for petty local drama, our files are disappointingly wholesome. The biggest scandal seems to be whether the Hurstbridge trio — the process engineer, mechatronics engineer and environmental scientist who brewed the Chargrilled Roo for Kangabrew — used enough PepperBerry. Junkee called it delightfully weird, and honestly, that’s our level of controversy.

Who would hate Hurstbridge? People who need a tram every five minutes, or anyone allergic to intimacy at dinner — the eight‑seat degustation model is not for loquacious loners. Commuters who like the bright lights might grumble about being at the end of the Hurstbridge line (yes, that line that links North Richmond, Westgarth, Heidelberg and Eltham), where suburbia politely hands over to paddocks.

Who would love it? Food obsessives who enjoy booking windows of time rather than tables, road‑trippers following Broadsheet’s recommendations, and anyone charmed by a town that still celebrates its Wattle Festival and thinks local produce is a personality trait. For years the local press has painted us as small and slightly precious — and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
