# Port Kembla (NSW)

Slug: `port-kembla-nsw`

We stand at the lookout and squint at our own skyline — red dust, cranes and a coastline that hasn’t bothered to apologise for being useful. Back in the day (our sources are mostly older, so we’re talking fond flashbacks, not travel-advice), Port Kembla was the kind of place that made other beach towns tidy up their pastel cushions.

Mornings here used to (and for many, still) look like this: we climb Mount Keira — the Dreamtime story of the Wadi Wadi and Geera sits heavy in the air — pass the Six Daughters of the West Wind sculpture, and roll down toward a coastline Time Out cheerfully called “grungey industrial.” For the kinetic among us, there was talk of Kembla Mountain Bike Trails and even tandem flights from Bald Hill or Port Kembla for anyone who prefers adrenaline with their sea breeze (Sitchu mentioned that one).

Petty local drama? Oh, the paperwork. As Time Out and Urban List reported, big-picture plans kept knocking at our gate: bulk port operations from Glebe Island slated to shift here by 2030, and an “advanced manufacturing hub” that was forecast to bring around 30,000 jobs. Concrete Playground told us we’d become the most in-demand coastal suburb in NSW (AVM about $1.01m, 13% uplift in enquiries) — which is either growth or the creeping gentrification we pretend not to notice. Junkee also reminded everyone that ports like ours feature in NSW protest laws, so even our harbour has rules for behaving.

Who would hate living here? People who want their beach trips to come without forklifts or history — if you hate the “grungey industrial” charm, the steelworks cameo in Jimmy Barnes’ old clip, or a skyline that doubles as a working port, you’ll probably pack up your cushions and go.

Who would love it? Creatives and makers. Wonderwalls brought murals big enough to stop traffic; Sarah McCloskey’s festival pieces were the sort you brag about having seen. Films and records rolled through — Broadsheet even noted Beast shot here, Junkee reported music recorded in our studios — so if you fancy art with a ship’s horn soundtrack, we’re your people.

We’re loud, useful and stubbornly beautiful. Come for the murals, stay because the cranes have nowhere else to go.
