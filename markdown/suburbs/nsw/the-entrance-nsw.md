# The Entrance (NSW)

Slug: `the-entrance-nsw`

We wake up in The Entrance like we’re at the climax of a holiday ad: sun half-committed, seagulls negotiating breakfast, and someone somewhere already stirring the pram. Back in the day we’d drag the little ones down to MyTime The Entrance at the Community Centre on Battley Ave — Thursdays, 9:30am — and swap survival tips like they were Olympic training regimes (Playgroup NSW had the details, if you’re nostalgic).

Our weekend ritual used to be the 7.6km Coast to Lake walk — a proper circuit that trots you past all the hits and misses of town. It’s the sort of walk Time Out and Sitchu recommended when Tuggerah was being billed as the launching point for our little seaside pilgrimage to The Entrance and Shelly Beach. If you liked lighthouses, Concrete Playground reminded us that Norah Head Lighthouse sits on the headland north of The Entrance, stoic and photogenic like a pensioner at bingo.

We haven’t forgotten the ways we entertained visiting relatives: a sweaty trot around Memorial Park for the Central Coast Chilli Festival, which Concrete Playground noted used to return in May and had a chilli-eating competition on Sunday, June 15. Nothing says family bonding like watching Auntie suspect she’s been poisoned by a serrano while Uncle insists it’s “flavour.”

Long Jetty used to flirt with being cultured — Glass Onion Society at 308 The Entrance Road and The Savoy Long Jetty at 391 The Entrance Road were the spots people name-checked when they wanted to sound like they’d read a review (Concrete Playground did the reviewing). Erina’s Woodport Hotel at 207 The Entrance Road got a post-renovation shout-out too, just to prove the area could host a decent night out when it wanted to.

Who would hate living here? People who need 24/7 pretension, rooftop infinity pools, or a city skyline to show off. Who would love it? Families who actually like sand in their shoes, festival-goers who’ll brave chilli fumes in Memorial Park, and anyone who thinks a lighthouse counts as ambience. For years the local guides told our story — and we’re still telling it, seagull soundtrack and all.
