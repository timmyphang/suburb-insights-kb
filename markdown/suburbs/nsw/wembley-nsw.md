# Wembley (NSW)

Slug: `wembley-nsw`

We start our weekend the way locals do: a circuit around Lake Monger — the trees are doing the heavy lifting for Wembley’s civic pride, and we’ll happily let them. Sitchu called us one of Perth’s leafiest neighbourhoods for a reason: Herdsman Lake on one side, Lake Monger on the other, and paths that make you forget there’s a city at all.

Back in the day the foodies moved in and made it charmingly complicated. Broadsheet loved crowing about Monsterella — the family‑run pizza joint on Grantham Street that Time Out praised for its wood‑fired pies — and its younger sibling Mummucc, a 60‑seat nuovo‑Italian wine bar. Albert’s popped up at 294 Cambridge Street after The Stanley closed (Broadsheet’s headline read like a soap opera). We’re proud of our pedigree; we just prefer our pasta with fewer dramatics and more napkins.

Our small‑town soap opera is surprisingly mild. The Stanley’s permanent closure was the most dramatic plot twist we got; Albert’s reopening felt like a season finale and a new chapter all at once. Meanwhile, the Wembley Hotel at 344 Cambridge Street kept doing what pubs do best — showing the game on a big screen and promising Grand Final energy, as Urban List pointed out. Not exactly high council intrigue, but it’s ours.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe parks, think green is overrated and prefer concrete views over duck ponds, Wembley will bruise your soul. Likewise, if the idea of a neighbourhood where Monsterella and a vintage shop like Bluebird Vintage get equal billing makes you twitch, this isn’t your postcode.

Who loves it? Families and small‑business devotees. Our playgroup at the Wembley Community Centre on 40 Alexander Street got a Lotterywest boost to build a nature playground and ran inclusive sessions — Playgroup WA even highlighted their projects. Wellness buffs had Reclab on Nanson Street on their radar for saunas and ice baths, and 300 Acres at 200 The Boulevard gave golf‑view dining an almost respectable vibe.

We’re leafy, low‑key and a bit smug about good pizza. We’ll take the lakes, the small bars and the playgroup morning teas — and if you’re passing through expecting glitter, we’ll happily point you back to the path around Lake Monger.
