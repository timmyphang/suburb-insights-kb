# Beaconsfield (NSW)

Slug: `beaconsfield-nsw`

Back in the day we used to brag about having an actual address on the map: 56 Victoria Street. Urban List once wrote about NU 56 — yes, at that very number — and how it served modern regional Italian breakfasts (black truffle scrambled eggs, breakfast bruschetta). We tell that story now in past tense because, like most things about our patch, the coverage is nostalgia-heavy and a touch confused.

If you’re scrolling the internet trying to find our local drama, prepare to be distracted. Broadsheet wants you to drive 45 minutes to O.My (Cardinia farm, moody dining room), Concrete Playground keeps recommending Pipi’s Kiosk down on Beaconsfield Parade (129a) and the Bleakhouse Hotel (97 Beaconsfield Parade), and Perth gets all the coastal cred with Ripple Brewing, Little Hawk and Beaconsfield Wine Bar on Lefroy Rd. Even Time Out’s busy ranking has a very posh Beaconsfield over in Buckinghamshire. We’re basically the understudy in a play where three other Beaconsfields keep stealing the lead.

Our biggest petty scandal? The internet can’t tell which Beaconsfield it’s gushing about. One minute we’re a truffle-scrambling, bruschetta-serving dot on Victoria Street; the next we’re a seaside wine-bar fantasy or a country degustation 45 minutes from Melbourne. It’s flattering, in a confusing way.

Who would hate living here: anyone who needs constant clout, weekly “best of” lists, or a local scene that’s always trending. If your life depends on being in the latest round-up, you’ll be disappointed — the web keeps sending the hype to other postcodes.

Who would love it here: people who like the idea of a suburb with a proper name and relatively little fuss; the kind of folk who enjoy telling visiting friends “we used to have NU 56 at 56 Victoria Street” and watching them try to locate it among O.My, Pipi’s Kiosk, Ripple Brewing and the rest. We’re quietly proud that the name Beaconsfield gets around — even if it’s rarely talking about us.
