# Barrack Heights (NSW)

Slug: `barrack-heights-nsw`

We’re the lot who know every footpath between Carrington St and Gipps Cres — and no, we don’t all own matching garden gnomes. Back in the day the local scene was less about boutique this-or-that and more about whether Park & Play was on at Family Services House in Shellharbour City Centre, because that’s where the real social calendar lived.

Morning routine: we’d argue over the best park for the toddler convoy. The Parents Guide used to list Stewart Park on Stewart Place and Jock Brown Oval on Gipps Cres as the obvious contenders, and yes, we kept score. The Barrack Heights Community Preschool at 10 Carrington St had a profile too — the listing cheerfully noted a director with a “Big Fat Smile” for 14 years, which felt strangely official and comforting at 9am when everyone’s hair is a suggestion.

If you like your suburb with a community-services footnote, this is our thing. The Family Services Illawarra annual report from 2015–16 actually name-checked Barrack Heights PS (and Warilla North PS and Mt Warrig...) as partners for Park & Play and parenting programs — so our parenting hacks aren’t just gossip, they were part of someone’s strategy document. There’s a whole brochure about supporting families across Illawarra and Shoalhaven that mentions this sort of work; not glamorous, but solid.

Petty local drama? We’ll admit one: the eternal debate over which playground made it into the “best of Shellharbour” list alongside Oak Flats. It’s not a council scandal, it’s a morale issue. Otherwise the big controversies involved swings and whether the oval had enough shade.

Who would hate it here: anyone craving glittery nightlife, rooftop tapas or a cafe that pronounces beans like a sommelier. Who would love it here: families who appreciate long-serving preschool staff, Park & Play community mornings, and parks where kids can exhaust themselves properly. We wear our low-keyness like a jumper in winter — not flashy, but dependable. If you’re into real-life community where programs and parks outweigh headlines, Barrack Heights has quietly been doing the job for years.
