# Corio (NSW)

Slug: `corio-nsw`

We wake up here with Corio Bay in the background and a shrug — not because anything dramatic happens every morning, but because the town wears its odd bits proudly. Back in the day the papers liked to remind people we had industrial romance: as Broadsheet noted, the old Corio Distillery was built in 1928, churned out whisky that critics once joked smelled a little like petrol, and finally closed in the 1980s. We still tell that story like it’s a family legend.

A weekend look-back around here involves shoreline things and big, slightly odd structures. The Crowne Plaza Geelong once opened right on the edge of Corio Bay, the waterfront got a shiny revamp (Urban List covered that), and Concrete Playground gossiped fondly about the Powerhouse Geelong — a hulking, 3000-square-metre playground of staircases, long-disused rooms and even old cars that locals have turned into legal street art. For the outdoorsy among us, Sitchu used to recommend scenic flights with Adventure Flight Co to trace the curve of Corio Bay or a climb of Flinders Peak in the You Yangs for the same view we pretend we’re tired of.

Petty local drama? We had a perfect one: Broadsheet reported a stolen food truck — Rahaf Al Khatib’s Falastini rig — taken from Preston and, in a twist too cinematic for us, located in Corio with iPads, ovens, fridges and stock gone walkabout. It made us proud and a little bewildered, which is peak Corio energy.

Who would hate living here? People who need their suburb to smell exclusively of artisan citrus and curated candle smoke. People who can’t get jazzed about refinery-chic history, big skies, or legal graffiti on former industrial bones.

Who would love it? Folks who like a shoreline with stories: the old distillery lore, oyster-reef restoration projects for Corio Bay mentioned in conservation write-ups, a revamped waterfront and the You Yangs at arm’s reach. We’re not trying to be everywhere; we’re content being ourselves, a little rough around the edges and impossibly stubborn about our view of the bay.
