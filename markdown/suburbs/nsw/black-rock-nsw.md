# Black Rock (NSW)

Slug: `black-rock-nsw`

We like to think of Black Rock as the place you arrive at when you’ve decided the city is too loud and the sea breeze is the only filter you need. Back in the day the local reads were all about Bluff Road cafés and Half Moon Bay postcards — Time Out loved the HMVS Cerberus wreck, Broadsheet was always waving a flag for our beachside eateries, and Sitchu described us as that tidy, slightly affluent coastal village with Bay Trail walks and foreshore BBQs.

A weekend for us used to be gloriously predictable: a slow shuffle past The Colonel’s Son opposite Black Rock Beach (holiday‑house vibes, dog‑friendly courtyard), a detour for artisan gelato at Davey Mac’s — which, as Broadsheet put it, can “transport you to the heart of Italy” — and a wistful read about Elichi turning up at 6/8 Bluff Road from the Babu Ji team, promising a South‑coast/Australiana spin on Indian food. Winston’s even staged a cultural coup, swapping an edgy dive‑bar past for a city‑coded European bistro courtesy of Bonne and Kevan. We watched it happen and still pretended we weren’t impressed.

There isn’t much juicy local scandal in our clippings — mostly lifestyle notes, beach rankings and venue transformations. Urban List happily called our beach a proper dunk (heads up: steep face, gets deep fast), Concrete Playground recommended nearby snorkel spots and Sitchu kept reminding people we’ve got schools, golf and the Bay Trail for when you need to feel very lifestyley.

Who would hate living here? If you crave an all-night city roar, 24/7 curiosity and sand that gently lulls toddlers all day — bad news: Urban List warned our bay drops off quickly and isn’t ideal for very young children. Also, anyone allergic to the smell of salt, artisan ice‑cream and people who own flannelette throw rugs should steer clear.

Who would love it? People who like history with their morning coffee (HMVS Cerberus, hello), swimmers who want a proper dunk, diners who remember Elichi and Winston’s signifying something special, and those who appreciate a village that reads like a well‑curated travel column. We’ll keep the esplanade tidy; you bring the sensible shoes and an appetite for gelato.
