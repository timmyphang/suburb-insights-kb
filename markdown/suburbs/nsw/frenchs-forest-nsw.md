# Frenchs Forest (NSW)

Slug: `frenchs-forest-nsw`

Back in the day our Sunday mornings actually felt like a hobby: we’d mosey down to the Frenchs Forest Organic Markets in the Parkway Hotel carpark and pretend we were foodies while juggling free‑range eggs and a tote bag that said “support local”. Time Out once even listed those markets — we were smug about that for months.

If you liked routine, we had it: Feels Pilates had a local outpost so your mid‑week stretch was sorted, Gyronimo’s in Frenchs Forest was the long‑running gyro place people name‑checked like it’s a heritage site, and Dan Murphy’s Frenchs Forest even earned visits from a roaming Sorbet wine truck — because yes, this is where convenience meets aspirational snacking. Reuben Republic used to turn up at our markets too, slinging pastramis at weekend crowds like a sandwich pop‑up with grand ambitions.

We weren’t all markets and mat work. For culture fixes the Glen Street Theatre in nearby Belrose was our smoky‑theatre‑in‑the‑bush fantasy, and Time Out once wrote up Frenchs Fair at 7 Skyline Pl — artisan pastries and custard cruffins included, if you wanted to feel both virtuous and slightly over‑caffeinated. The Northern Beaches Hospital precinct and Forest Kitchen gave the suburb a grown‑up pulse, and local directories even listed Ogalo and childcare options like MindChamps and Explore and Develop (run by Cherie and Lilli) so parents could breathe, briefly.

Petty drama? We mostly argued about market stall queues and which Pilates class had the best playlist. Plans for a Northern Beaches rail line have been discussed for ages, so if you need a train on‑tap, you’ll understand our collective patience.

Who would hate it here: nightlife hunters and people who measure a suburb by footy‑club culture — we’re more farmers‑market, Pilates, school‑pickup, and dog‑parade. Who would love it: families who value bigger blocks, people who like weekly markets and long walks in nearby reserves, the kind who follow @sunshine.garden.playgroup for playdate inspo, and anyone who wants a suburb that quietly collects conveniences and calls it a lifestyle. We know our lanes, our markets and our mat classes — and we’re not ashamed to brag about it.
