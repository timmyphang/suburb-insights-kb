# Bangholme (NSW)

Slug: `bangholme-nsw`

We start our weekend like locals used to: shoes dusty from Riverend Road and the car full of folding chairs that never quite fit in the boot. For years the local mentions are a bit charmingly practical — directories, kids’ classes and the kind of festive enterprise that sends you home with pine needles in your jeans.

Back in the day, if we were feeling adventurous you could find Shanx Mini Golf at 5 Riverend Road inside the Melbourne Cable Park — Urban List called it a “fresh, adrenaline-packed 18‑hole course” with loop‑de‑loops, sneaky jumps and hazards to test your skills. If that sounds wildly out of character for Bangholme, well, so are we. Nearby, Sitchu once tipped Evergreen Christmas Trees at 240 Thames Promenade as the family-owned farm with rows of fresh trees, an on-site “Enchanted Forest” and prices from roughly $50–$190. We still argue about which tree had the most character.

The rest of what the papers archive about us reads like a parenting economy: CareforKids listed 66 childcare services in Bangholme — 50 of them reportedly had vacancies and the average was about $126.70/day — and MumsPages cheerfully catalogued party and event providers plus Aspistars Gymnastics Club at 65 Riverend Road (Cornish College), affiliates with Gymnastics Victoria. Cornish College’s early learning centre at 65 Riverend Road even made a note for parents: it had no vacancies at the time. If you want to organise a toddler’s birthday, this is where the research muscle gets a proper workout.

There’s not much juicy local drama in the clippings — no council soap operas, no turf wars — mostly useful lists and nostalgia. So our biggest civic quarrel tends to be practical: who’s on drop-off duty and who remembered the spare nappies.

Who would hate living here? People after trendy laneway cafés and late-night bars — the data suggests we’re more about gymnastics classes, childcare and the occasional adrenaline-packed mini-golf than boutique lattes. Who would love it? Families, tree-hunters, club coaches and anyone who appreciates spreadsheets of childcare options. We’ll keep the pines, the loop-de-loops and the sensible directories — and the rest of Melbourne can keep its buzz.
