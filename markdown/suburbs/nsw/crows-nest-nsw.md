# Crows Nest (NSW)

Slug: `crows-nest-nsw`

Back in the day we’d get up and wander Willoughby Road like it’s our personal strip mall runway — past St Thomas’ Rest Park (yes, the gothic cemetery that makes our Sunday walk feel cultured) and the takeaway queue outside Crescent Croissanterie, where Elly Kim’s plain croissant used to be the kind of pastry that made you apologise to strangers for enjoying it so loudly. 

We watched the Nakano Darling crew land Ramen Auru with its tatami room and ticket machine and thought: finally, a queue we can judge properly. Yurippi moved in not long after, grilling yakitori over konro charcoal and pretending our gas bills aren’t affected by it. And for the nights we wanted to be somehow both civilised and rowdy, the Crows Nest Hotel got a $1.6‑million makeover — slow‑roasted lamb by day, spicy margaritas and dance‑floor bangers until 6am by night. Small Bar and The Crow Bar kept the boutique‑wine and tapas energy humming like a very polite rave.

Petty local drama? Oh, we’ve had our moments. There was that surreal metro clip where a Sydney Metro train’s doors stayed open mid‑ride and rolled into Crows Nest station — the kind of thing that makes you check the timetable and your life choices. El Jannah’s arrival north of the bridge was also a talking point; the chook mafia expanded and we applauded, because garlic sauce is practically a civic amenity.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to decent coffee, because Only Coffee Project in Crows Nest climbed a list and made other suburbs feel inadequate; or anyone who can’t cope with yakitori smoke, croissant crumbs, and the existential dilemma of choosing between Lees Fortuna Court’s Peking duck or a ramen bowl. Also, if you want silence after 9pm, this village will keep disappointing you.

Who’d love it: food obsessives, parents who enjoy the Mum Club Sydney vibe or a Bub dance class on a weekday, bargain costume hunters at Fancy That!, and anyone who thinks a bit of history (hi, St Thomas’ Rest Park) pairs perfectly with a night of tapas and boutique wine. We’re a bit loud, a bit hungry, and very proud.
