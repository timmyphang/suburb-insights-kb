# Gooseberry Hill (NSW)

Slug: `gooseberry-hill-nsw`

We drive the Zig Zag Scenic Drive like it’s a ritual: windows down, eye on the lookout, pretending we didn’t just stop for the perfect shot of Perth City sinking into the Swan Coastal Plain. As Sitchu wrote in 2025 and again in 2026, the Zig Zag at Gooseberry Hill National Park is one of those places you keep telling visitors about until even your in-laws get bored of sunsets.

Weekend routine: a slow roll along the one-way Zig Zag Drive, pause at the lookouts for the panoramic views everyone brags about (Urban List and Sitchu both have receipts), then a picnic — yes, there’s an Urban List article that literally says “Pack the picnic basket.” If we’re feeling indulgent we reminisce about the quieter days when Annasha Day Spa Retreat at 120 Williams Street used to be tucked away for “recharge your mind and body” afternoons, or when Embers Wood Fired at 169 Railway Road used to be the answer to “who’s bringing pizza?” — those are fond pasts, not current recommendations.

Petty local drama? The official feud is between sunset chasers and anyone who’s actually trying to take their dog for a walk without getting photobombed by a tripod. Photographers and picnic baskets fight for the best lookout like it’s a competitive sport — Urban List made sure to point out the photography opportunities, so don’t act surprised when half the carpark looks like a pop-up studio.

Who would hate living here: folks who like flat horizons and zero incline. If you loathe curves, lookout pull-ins and the occasional queue of people admiring the view, Gooseberry Hill will frustrate you. Also, anyone allergic to nature playgroups — Playgroup WA’s activities once made this place feel like an ongoing art project involving leaves, spiders (the pretend ones) and glitter.

Who would love it here: people who treat sunsets as a weekly event, families who enjoy the nature-inspired play the old Playgroup WA posts celebrated, and anyone who feels instantly calmer after a Zig Zag drive. We’ll keep showing up at the lookouts, because the view never gets old — even if our picnic blanket does.
