# Albion (NSW)

Slug: `albion-nsw`

We live in Albion, NSW — and honestly, we’re used to being the one no one can quite find on a map. Back in the day the internet had very firm opinions about “Albion”, but they were mostly about other Albions: Broadsheet happily catalogued Albion (Brisbane) with Ruby’s Wine Shop and Time & Temp (cardamom buns that used to sell out by 9am), and even wrote up a Bathhouse Albion with its two 38‑degree vitality pools and a 12‑degree cold plunge. Meanwhile Time Out was off gossiping about Albion Street in Surry Hills — Bessie’s, Crystalbrook Albion — 70 metres this way, bars that way. You get the picture.

Our morning routine? We make our own. While Urban List was writing features on Olive Thyme in a heritage‑listed Albion and sprout artisan bakery moving whole headquarters into an “Albion” warehouse, we’re the people who quietly turn up to the community things that actually exist nearby: Playgroup NSW lists MyTime Albion Park Rail SaCC (they met Wednesdays at 10am at Albion Park Rail Public School, 18–20 Tongarra Road) — which is the sort of practical detail journalists forget when they’re choosing which Albion is “vibe of the year”.

Petty local drama? There isn’t the juicy kind reporters love. The scandal is existential: are we Albion, Albion Park Rail, or “that place the papers meant when they wrote about craft bakeries and bathhouses”? We’re both pleased and annoyed that Concrete Playground got excited about Craft’d Grounds and a 2,600‑square‑metre dining precinct somewhere called Albion — but didn’t swing by our street.

Who would hate living here: anyone who needs to be on a rooftop bar menu, or who wants their suburb to be the latest glossy feature. You know the type — they’ll move here and moan we’re not as photogenic as Melbourne’s or Brisbane’s Albions.

Who would love it: people who value being reliably under‑reported. If you like actual community things (playgroups with real details in the Playgroup NSW planner), low fuss, and the satisfaction of owning a postcode that confuses journalists, welcome home. We’ll keep the cardamom buns myth alive for you — even if someone else got the cake headline.
