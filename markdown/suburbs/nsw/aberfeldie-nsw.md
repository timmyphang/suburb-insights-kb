# Aberfeldie (NSW)

Slug: `aberfeldie-nsw`

Back in the day we’d stroll out of our postcodes like we owned the place — coffee in one hand, suspiciously earnest reusable bag in the other — and argue about whether 25 Tilba Street was vintage Italian charm or a wistful attempt at being Rome. Broadsheet once wrote about Carlo Mellini’s old‑world leather booths there and Nomadi Coffee Roasters on the bar; Sitchu later had Arnold’s listed at the same address, and 27 Tilba Street cropped up as Aberfeldie Salumeria. It’s been a cosy game of musical chairs with prosciutto.

Our weekend ritual used to involve a detour past Buckley Street. Time Out called Insight Body and Mind at Level 1 & 2, 361A Buckley Street a calm sanctuary — we’d nod solemnly in yoga class, then immediately trade wellness tips like they were footy scores. The Incinerator Gallery on 180 Holmes Road used to be where culture met big industrial bones; remember The Playground Project? It even dug into the history of swings and slides in 2025, which made us feel sophisticated and slightly nostalgic for scraped knees.

Petty local drama? Oh please. We had a real gem when Calmer Cafe threatened legal action after losing a five‑star review because some VCE exam invented a fictional coffee shop. The VCAA apologised; Pedestrian TV reported the cafe bounced back to a gleaming 4.9. Only in Aberfeldie could school exams tangibly affect a café’s Yelp ego. It was peak suburbia: furious parents, defensive baristas, and the town rallying around… a review.

Who would hate it here? People who can’t cope with a suburb that swaps salumerias and southern‑Italian sandwiches for Peking duck and lamb spring rolls at Benyue Kitchen (alumni of Lau’s Family Kitchen). Also, if you’re allergic to low‑key cultural institutions, yoga studios, or even a directory entry for skip‑bin services at 421 Buckley St — this isn’t your postcode.

Who’d love it? Families who like middle‑ring liveability (PwC even puts Essendon–Aberfeldie in the top 20), foodies who appreciate a stubbornly good salumeria or a sincere southern‑Chinese kitchen, art fans who haunt the Incinerator Gallery, and anyone who treats weekend drama as community theatre. We’ll keep polishing our leather booths and arguing about whether Arnold’s ever really left.
