# Maylands (NSW)

Slug: `maylands-nsw`

Back in the day we used to roll out of bed and meander down Whatley Crescent like it was a communal yoga class for people who like good bread and bad decisions. We’d pass Mrs S and The Woodfired Baker at 194 Whatley Crescent, pop a nod to Sherbet Cafe and Bake Shop (Matthew Perroni really did bake daily) and pretend we weren’t judging anyone for ordering the biggest pastry on the menu.

For years the local scene quietly turned into a very particular beast — a working‑class glow‑up, as Urban List put it — so Maylands ended up with everything from converted garages doing relaxed pizza (Rossonero tucked down Lyric Lane) to a neighbourhood aperitivo vibe at Volare. We watched Lyric Lane open and think, “Ah yes, a 170‑cap live‑music spot — now we can complain about soundchecks in stereo.” And there was Peninsula Farm Café, stubbornly idyllic on the bank of the Swan River, making mornings feel like someone’s curated PowerPoint about wholesome coastal living.

The petty drama we couldn’t ignore: Smidge. Broadsheet gave us a first look when the compact bakery opened in December 2023, then, as Sitchu recounted, Maylands locals “cried deeply” when that laneway gem was forced to close and eventually relocate to Leederville. We still talk about it at the pub — The Seasonal Brewing Co., that 300‑seat warehouse‑turned‑brewery (175 Guildford Road, if you like exactitudes), with 16 taps, a big beer garden and a kids’ play area — the kind of place that curates chaos and calls it family‑friendly.

Who would hate living here? People who crave absolute silence, no queues for pizza, or a suburb that refuses to decide between river walks and a live‑music bar opposite the train station (we’re looking at you, fans of hermit life). Swallow — that tiny spot opposite Maylands train station — and the whole Whatley Crescent strip are not for the solitary.

Who would love it? Families who like their beer gardens with a playground, music nerds who want cheap local gigs, ceramics hobbyists who’ve been to The Sunshine Lane, and anyone who appreciates a suburb that’s proudly had a glow‑up and still remembers where it came from. We do.
