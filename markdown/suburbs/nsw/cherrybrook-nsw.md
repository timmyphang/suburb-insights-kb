# Cherrybrook (NSW)

Slug: `cherrybrook-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean according to the local round-up of articles we could find — Cherrybrook still feels like the kind of place where you’ll be recognised by your grocery trolley rather than your nightlife habits. We know the Sunday rhythm here: kids cartwheeling at Darlington Reserve (the dino-themed playground that opened in September 2019 on Darlington Drive), a stroller convoy around the Swing Bridge Loop Walk (entry points at Keighran Place and Fallon Drive Reserve), and someone clutching a paper bag from This Is Us — the sourdough outfit that started in 2020 and went bricks-and-mortar with loaded reubens, chicken-and-leek pies and very pretty cakes (as Broadsheet noted).

We brag about having proper bush walks: the Callicoma Walk’s five-kilometre loop that takes about two hours and is allegedly “medium-grade,” the kind of trail that makes you feel both virtuous and slightly damp. Our sport calendar is full of local institutions — Cherrybrook Little Athletics at Greenway Oval No.2 on Shepherds Drive, the Pennant Hills/Cherrybrook Rugby League Club and the West Pennant Hills Cherrybrook Football Club, “The LIONS.” We’ve got childcare density too: Kindalin in Cherrybrook Village, Wonder Years (formerly Cherrybrook Pre‑School Kindergarten) and Heritage House, which opened in 2010 and had its indoor/outdoor areas revamped.

There isn’t much juicy council drama in the clippings we saw, so for petty local soap we’ll quote the facts: Cherrybrook Village (41–47 Shepherds Drive) used to list retailers like Costi’s Catch and Pattison’s, and the suburb keeps things very much on the community side of town.

Who would hate living here? People who need 24/7 entertainment, an ever-changing parade of trendy bars, or anyone allergic to playgrounds, bushwalks and weekend sports fixtures. Who would love it? Families who actually use drop-off centres, parents who queue for Saturday athletics, walkers who like an honest loop, and carbo-lovers who recall the bakery that made us proud.

We’re not flashy. We’re not anonymous. We’re the suburb where you can find a swing bridge, a dinosaur park and a pie you brag about at work on Monday.
