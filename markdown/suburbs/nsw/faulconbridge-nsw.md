# Faulconbridge (NSW)

Slug: `faulconbridge-nsw`

We wake up to the smell of eucalyptus and the faint promise of scones — or at least we used to brag about that when Sydney was making road-trip checklists. For years Broadsheet told visitors to "sip tea while browsing books at Chapters & Leaves" (Shop 7, 7–9 St Georges Crescent), and yes, that teahouse-and-bookshop made hot scones with house jam sound like a civic virtue. We tell people it’s the kind of place that makes you consider moving here just for the pastry shelf.

Our Saturday looks like a brochure: a gentle stomp through the Sir Henry Parkes Victory Track (3km return, starts on Sir Henry’s Parade, slips into the rainforest of Sassafras Gully) — a Blue Mountains Mums favourite for families — then the kids get thrown at the Jackson Park playground while adults nod knowingly at the Corridor of Oaks. Time Out reminded people about the Norman Lindsay Gallery — Norman Lindsay’s old stone cottage at 14 Norman Lindsay Crescent — and how the place still smells faintly of ink, eccentricity and the sort of art that makes cousins uncomfortable at dinner.

We’ve always been the scenic pause on the way to Katoomba — Sitchu put us on the Greater Blue Mountains Drive map between Springwood and Leura — officially a pit stop, unofficially the bit where you pretend you’re on a culture-packed mini-break. Concrete Playground even listed the Norman Lindsay Vintage Fair as part of the 2026 Australian Heritage Festival, promising retro stalls, classic cars and a vintage fashion parade; honestly, we wear those label-less jumpers with pride.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t stand vintage markets, small-town bookshops, or 10.50am meet-ups on the Faulconbridge Railway Station platform for a songline walk, this place will feel like punishment. If you require a neon-lit strip of options at 10pm, we are not for you.

Who would love it? Families who want playgroups and bushwalks, readers who miss proper scones, art lovers who like wandering through Norman Lindsay’s stone cottage, and anyone who enjoys being the charming detour on a Blue Mountains road trip. We’re quaint, a little smug, and unapologetically fond of our scones.
