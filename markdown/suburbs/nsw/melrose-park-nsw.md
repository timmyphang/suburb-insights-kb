# Melrose Park (NSW)

Slug: `melrose-park-nsw`

We know exactly where the film trucks left tyre marks — the stretch of Melrose Park they dressed up as Gas Town for Furiosa — because we still get asked if our industrial estate comes with a moat and a soundtrack. As Time Out noted, Sydney’s west briefly doubled for Mad Max, and honestly we’ve never felt more cinematic cutting through on a Tuesday.

Our weekend routine is gloriously unspectacular. We drop the kids at the wooden-themed playground on 86 Lancaster Avenue — Ryde District Mums reckoned it best for 2–9 year olds — where the lack of shade sails is the suburb’s unofficial cardio test and the small water feature with its pump-and-water-maze keeps everyone damp and demanding snacks. Then we wander to the Melrose Park Family Fun Day (formerly Market Day) where artisan stalls and gourmet snaffling make the whole place feel like someone hit ‘community’ on the GPS.

Back in the day the property brochures used to thrill us — like that 3-bed, 2-bath at 9 Batten Ave that was spotlighted with a guide price of $1,290,000. We recall open homes with more laminate than drama, and neighbours comparing fence heights like it was Wimbledon.

Petty local drama? We actually don’t have a scandal museum yet. Our fiercest civic debate tends to involve shade sails and whether the water pump needs supervision or a formal permit. It’s riveting. If you’re after council rebellions or heated rallies, you’ll be disappointed — unless you count the furious discussions about who snagged the last pastry at the Family Fun Day.

Who would hate living here: nightlife hunters who need 2am DJs and a sushi omakase on every corner. Who would hate the industrial-chic hum of delivery trucks or people who can’t abide a playground with wooden beams and a bit of grit.

Who would love it: young families, festival-goers, and anyone who quietly prizes being the suburb that doubled for a post-apocalyptic citadel and is also planning a 4,700 sqm street-food precinct called Melrose Central — Time Out said it was due mid‑2026 — promise of Hong Kong, Tokyo, Seoul and Singapore flavours included. We’re low-key, proud, and occasionally cinematic. Come for the Family Fun Day; stay because someone told you Gas Town has great lighting.
