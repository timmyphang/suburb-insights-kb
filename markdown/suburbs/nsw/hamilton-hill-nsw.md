# Hamilton Hill (NSW)

Slug: `hamilton-hill-nsw`

Back in the day we used to measure a good Saturday by two things: whether the kids hadn’t yet peeled off for Dixon Park and whether Everyday Bread still had any hot cross buns left. As Broadsheet remembered them, Everyday Bread’s buns were “for purists” — light, fragrant and gone by 9.30am — and we wore that scarcity like a badge of honour.

Our weekend routine used to look like this: one parent drops the kids at Dixon Park on Ommanney St — yes, the nature playground with log steps, rope play and a BMX pump track that Perth Mumsgroup swore mums loved — while the other queued at the bakery. Then we’d meet at The Pear Tree (1 Kerry Street), which Perth Mumsgroup flagged as a family-friendly café with a play area, so the peace lasted at least as long as toast and small people’s negotiations do.

For dinner we’d brag about Forno Antico’s woodfired margherita — Broadsheet once couldn’t stop thinking about that smoky crust — or pretend we’d hired Ready Eddie’s Cafe’s boodle tray for a party because “you can eat it with gloves or cutlery,” as Broadsheet helpfully noted. When relatives came to town we’d take them to Glowing Rooms for 3D glow-in-the-dark mini golf and pretend the German artists’ paint job made us feel cultured.

There’s no tabloid scandal in our files — just reliable local stuff: Control Pilates and Fitness on Carrington St, Fins Seafood’s Hamilton Hill factory (they even opened a retail store in Bicton Central), a community hub at 1 Starling Street beside the orange basketball building, and the Hamilton Hill Community Group doing its thing since 2011. If that reads like a brag or a CV, we’ll take both.

Who would hate living here? If your vibe is “late-night neon and buzzy laneway bars,” this suburb will politely remind you that it prefers baked goods, basketball courts and nature play. Who’ll love it? Families, amateur bakers, pizza purists, mini-golf party planners and anyone who enjoys a suburb that never tried to be anything other than itself. We’re quietly useful, stubbornly good at food, and unapologetically local — and honestly, we like it that way.
