# Semaphore Park (NSW)

Slug: `semaphore-park-nsw`

We know Semaphore Park by the scrape in the footpath, the community garden hose that never quite behaves, and the way everyone waves like they’ve rehearsed it twice a week. Back in the day Broadsheet flagged our local culinary revolution — Phatsachon “Tiki” Ritnayom opening Tiki’s Thai Cuisine at 5/292 Military Road — and yes, we still tell visitors about the tom yum like it’s a family heirloom. Findglocal even remembers the place posting menu updates and looking for staff, so if you loved small-town launches, you were spoilt for content.

Our weekend looks suspiciously wholesome. We potter (literally) at places listed for ceramics: Sitchu included Polisu at 43 First Avenue in its pottery roundup, and Pottering Around gets a shout for being the paint-your-own kind of therapy — glazes fired, brushes provided, no judgment when you glaze outside the lines. The kids get their energy out at the playground and, for summer gladiators, Semaphore’s waterslide complex on The Esplanade is the kind of seasonal chaos parents whisper about in carparks.

We’re proud of the bits the internet remembers: a community garden that’s “a place of friendship, encouragement and learning together,” and Bower Cottages Community Centre on 200 Bower Road, which the local listings say runs everything from pyrography taster weeks to morning teas about community resilience. It’s the sort of place where you’ll learn pottery and how to run a sausage sizzle in the same fortnight.

Who would hate living here? If you crave a nightclub that’s open past the point where a neighbourhood watch starts planning its rota, you’ll find Semaphore Park a bit... domesticated. If your idea of a Saturday is elbowing for street art in the inner city, our gears are wooden and our vibes are community garden-tier cosy.

Who would love it here? Makers, small-business supporters, parents who judge park shade like it’s a human right, and anyone who thinks ordering takeout from a locally opened Thai counts as a cultural event. We’re low-key, reliably neighbourly, and fond of our little institutions — imperfect, paper-mulched, and exactly as sentimental as the garden signage suggests.
