# Fullarton (NSW)

Slug: `fullarton-nsw`

We live on the kind of suburban strip where the most dramatic thing you can argue about is whether the motel down the road is mid‑century chic or full‑on Las Vegas — and yes, Tim Ross did call it “a little bit of Las Vegas in Adelaide,” so we’ve got receipts. Weekends here used to look like: we’d drive past the Highway Hotel at 150 Glen Osmond Road, where Sitchu said there’s a big garden screen and a buzzing game‑day vibe, and then swing by Glen Osmond Road to see what’s new at 306 (Broadsheet did a whole piece on Noi Vietnamese Eatery moving in back in 2019).

Back in the day Broadsheet fell in love with Noi’s woodfired sugarcane pork and whole‑bird lemongrass chicken — it was billed as Vietnamese‑meets‑Australian, sun‑filled atrium et cetera — and we’d gossip about whether that kind of hybrid cuisine would ever forgive us for living so close to a retro motel with neon potential. Time Out even listed a tasting at the Arkaba Hotel, so our local calendar had a weirdly classy streak sandwiched between thrift runs and beer nights.

If you’re the sort of person who sneers at second‑hand threads, well, Fullarton might sting: Urban List name‑checked 2nd Chance Collective — “2CC” — as jam‑packed with American vintage tees, ’90s sweatshirts and a hefty range of Carhartt jackets. We wear our preloved pride like a badge, and if you hate elbowing for a 1990s jumper, you’ll hate us for it.

We’ll admit we’re not the buzziest postcode in the state, and most coverage about us is a collection of spotlights — a new eatery, a motel photo feature, a hotel tasting — rather than full‑on drama. Even Playgroup SA’s details land on Fullarton Road (their head office listed at 33 Fullarton Road, Kent Town), which is about as domestic as suburbia gets.

Who’d love living here? People who want a weekend that goes from thrift treasure hunting at 2CC to cheering on the game at the Highway Hotel, then finishing with a nostalgic motel sighting and a decent meal (as Broadsheet noted). Who’d hate it? If your idea of excitement is an ever‑changing river of new bars and constant late‑night chaos, Fullarton will feel like Sunday at 11am — delightfully, stubbornly calm.
