# Bankstown (NSW)

Slug: `bankstown-nsw`

Back in the day — or at least in the archives we were handed — Bankstown felt like a very busy lunchtime montage and an ambitious rooftop bar had sky-high ideas. We know the intersection of Bankstown City Plaza and Chapel Road like the grooves on our phone charger: it’s where Phuong 18 set up shop and reportedly shifted around 1,500 pork rolls a day. Yes, 1,500. By lunchtime. We applauded the carb stamina.

Our weekend routine used to involve a tactical pho pilgrimage to AN Restaurant — the family-run place that’s been simmering bone broth since 1987 — and, if we were feeling decadent, popping into Bankstown Sports Club where Marrow & Co brought theatrical steak service (including a 1.5‑kilogram Riverine tomahawk) to a venue that already housed more than a dozen restaurants. For an aerial perspective, Lady Banks Rooftop Bar gave us sweeping views from its lofty perch; for wheels and wobble, Deepwater Pump Track transformed an underused pocket of parkland into a proper BMX playground. We even had an aviation museum at Bankstown Airport — the Australian Aviation Museum — in case anyone wanted a side of plane history with their lunch.

There was always a little suburban theatre: a TikToker fronting court after an alleged hairdresser brawl and whispers about an ‘acai war’ involving Acai Junky. We’re not dramatising — we’re just noting the things that made the place feel alive in the papers and feeds. And yes, we’ve been told about plans and infrastructure, like the Western Sydney airport developments and the leafy 90‑metre plaza at Bankstown Station, but most of our sources are older, so treat this as nostalgic scouting rather than a current events briefing.

Who would hate living here? If you insist on suburbia being all manicured hedges and a single artisanal bakery, you might struggle with our glorious, messy variety: Lithuanian clubs sitting next to halal Nikkei concepts (Tokyo Samba) and banh mi rivalries in Old Town Plaza.

Who would love it? Anyone who wants food that actually tells a story, big community hubs, and a skyline you can toast from. We’d argue Bankstown’s charm has always been its deliciously democratic chaos — and we’d happily queue for another pork roll.
