# Wallsend (NSW)

Slug: `wallsend-nsw`

We know the morning here: we’d ferry the kids to Wallsend Pool, that solar‑heated 50‑metre relic where the free‑swim side becomes a small nation of inflatable crocodiles and sunscreen. Then it was a quick scoot along the Tramway Track — that 3.9km former steam‑tram route to Glendale — because knees, scooters and a sense of civic pride.

Back in the day the City of Newcastle had been busy too: Brickworks Park — the old brickmaking site — got its new playground in 2020, survived the infamous vandalism in December 2022, and then strutted back into service with a new play fort in 2023. Around the corner the Wallsend Active Hub opened in October 2022 in Federal Park beside the pool: a playspace for all ages, a pump track, multi‑sports courts, a rebound wall, climbing wall and fitness stations. We’re not saying we miss lugging helmets, but we do.

Petty local drama? Oh yes — our playground had its moment. Vandalism in 2022 felt like the suburb trying on edginess for size, then promptly returning to the business of community sausage sizzles and scooter races. The Wallsend Children’s Community folks — Paula McCormack, Gill Gray and Darren McGee — kept at it, running surveys and supports. Their 2024 parent survey showed only a third of parents felt stressed or anxious (lower than 2021) and the pupil survey had 826 kids from nine primaries filling in their opinions — 51% boys, 48% girls, three non‑binary students and ten who preferred not to say. We like a neighbourhood that counts everyone, even when the numbers make us sound like a PTA AGM with good lighting.

Who’d hate it here? If you need 24/7 nightlife, Michelin pretence or a tram every five minutes into the CBD, this isn’t your scene — local property listings even brag about a 25‑minute drive to Newcastle CBD and 3.5km to Maryland Shopping Centre and Tavern, like it’s a selling point for people who enjoy a short pilgrimage.

Who’d love it? Families, cyclists, the sort of person who considers a pump track a date idea, and anyone who likes their history with a playground on top: brickworks, tramlines, community organisers who actually do the work. We’re Wallsend — a little rough, mostly charming, and quietly very good at being local.
