# Highett (NSW)

Slug: `highett-nsw`

Back in the day we used to think Highett was the quiet cousin who turned up to family Christmas in sensible shoes. These days, if you squint down Highett Road between the station and Westfield Southland, you can still see the old lanes — but also the fingerprints of a slow, tasteful glow-up.

Our Saturday mornings used to start with a nearly secret pilgrimage to The Flour House, where they milled their own Victorian grain and sold out of the good stuff before we’d finished our second cuppa (as Sitchu lovingly chronicled). Then came the cafés that quietly changed the vibe: The Diplomat — the review that credited it with kickstarting Highett’s transformation — sitting opposite Highett station; Third Wheel, which opened in June 2023 and even snagged a Toby’s Local Legends nod; and the swish Abbiocco, the Broadsheet entry that made pasta sound like a hobby you should take up.

If you like your gentrification with a soundtrack of woodfired pizza, Concrete Playground wrote about Railway Wine Bar opening as a sultry new 60-seat venue, pouring minimal-intervention wines while Teo’s Pizza did the heavy lifting next door. And for those who remember Hot Lips Hacienda, the La Tortilleria team slid into that slot with Chilpa — a neat, local reshuffle The Urban List and Concrete Playground both noticed.

We don’t have a scandal to whisper about — more like a slow-motion suburban opera: cafes swapping hands, a pizzeria perfecting 72-hour dough, and community centres quietly keeping the heart beating. Highett Neighbourhood Community House at 2 Livingston Street still reads like the good uncle of the place — offering Tai Chi, Strength & Balance classes, even a Justice of the Peace service. It’s wholesome, and we like it that way.

Who’d hate living here? Nightlife chasers who measure a suburb by how late the lights stay on. People allergic to community noticeboards, local kindergartens at 1 Livingston Street, and the idea that your bakery might actually mill its own grain.

Who’d love it? Anyone who enjoys artisan bread, well-made pizza, small wine bars, and a neighbourhood with the patience to change gracefully. We’re quietly proud — sensible shoes, upgraded.
