# Broadmeadow (NSW)

Slug: `broadmeadow-nsw`

Back in the day we used to joke that Broadmeadow was either about to become Newcastle’s answer to Las Vegas or forever the place you drive through to get to somewhere better. As Time Out and The Urban List noted, the NSW Government once committed planning money for a 12,000‑seat Newcastle Arena next to McDonald Jones Stadium — the sort of thing that promised hotels, shiny commercial space and better transport connections. We’ve paced that footprint, imagined the queue for shows, and then gone for a quiet walk instead.

Our morning could involve watching the Mariyung inter‑city trains roll through — Time Out gave us a first look when the new fleet’s inaugural service included Broadmeadow — and then swinging by the absurdly specific local innovation: Concrete Playground reported Australia’s first drive‑thru‑only KFC at 80 Lambton Road, with five lanes and that four‑digit app code ritual. It used to feel like visiting a drive‑thru theme park; the article even listed the hours as 10am–midnight daily, which is handy information for late‑night curiosity.

We’re small — about 2.4 km², tucked between Adamstown, Georgetown and Hamilton — but weirdly well‑served on paper. CareforKids used to list eight childcare services in the 2292 postcode, with seven of eight showing vacancies and an average cost of $131.22/day. Little Kindy Broadmeadow on Lang Rd popped up with a $105/day fee and a glowing 5.0 from 17 reviews; Goodstart on Broadmeadow Road was described as a charming cottage setting with a 5.0/5 rating. There’s even allied health at ORS on Newton Street and, yes, a Broadmeadow Children’s Court in the directory — the kind of municipal realism that keeps things grounded.

Who would hate living here? People chasing non‑stop inner‑city glamour; those who need their suburb to be constantly on fire with late‑night bars and artisanal this‑and‑that. Who would love it? Families who like actual childcare options, commuters glad the new trains stopped here, and anyone with a soft spot for a town that’s quietly practical but promises big things on paper.

We know every corner, every planning application and every quirky drive‑thru code. Call us optimistic realists: we’ll queue at the KFC for a look, but we’re still waiting for the arena to tell us what came next.
