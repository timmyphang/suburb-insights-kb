# Mawson Lakes (NSW)

Slug: `mawson-lakes-nsw`

We love Mawson Lakes like you love a suburb you’re still pretending is “in the city” — predictable, tidy, and with enough organised fun to make the in-laws nod approvingly.

Morning routine: we drop the kids at Sea Stars Play Cafe (3/7 Main St) so they can conquer the four-storey playground and the “Messy Play” sensory studio while we actually use the word “networking” and stroll past UniSA Mawson Lakes campus. If you want culture with your canapé, Crowne Plaza Mawson Lakes has the immersive 7 Paintings dinner show running through to September 2026 — yes, we’ll take the plant-based option and still complain about the lighting.

Back in the day Lazyboy Hotpot arrived on Victoria Parade and brought malatang to a quiet residential street, which felt thrillingly rebellious for us. These days our rebellions are more modest: jaywalking to Mawson Lakes Shopping Centre for whatever the week’s essential is, or pretending the lake is Roman.

Petty local drama (short version): the train station gets hot. Pedestrian TV once flagged “extreme heat” at the Mawson Lakes train station and, yes, lifts being out — so if you enjoy sauna-style commutes and a little suspense about whether the lift will return, we’ve got you.

Who would hate living here: anyone who loathes scheduled fun. If you can’t stand four-storey indoor playgrounds, a nine-hole golf course on Mawson Lakes Blvd, or a suburb that earnestly offers planetarium dates (Sitchu once suggested the Adelaide Planetarium at the UniSA campus), this place will feel like a corporate retreat for children.

Who would love it: families, students and professionals — precisely the crowd Sitchu promised when Mawson Lakes was conceived in the late ’90s. We’ve got community staples: Mawson Lakes School, the Mawson Centre at 2–8 Main St with Aroma Cafe, Mawson Lakes Sports Centre (Building B, University Blvd) for actual exercise, and a golf club if your idea of fun includes membership forms.

We’re not flashy. We’re planned, pleasant and quietly smug about it. Come for the planned lakeside walks; stay because your kid prefixed their birthday with “Can we invite the whole play centre?”
