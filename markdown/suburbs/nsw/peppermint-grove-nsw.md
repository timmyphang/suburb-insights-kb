# Peppermint Grove (NSW)

Slug: `peppermint-grove-nsw`

We like to think Peppermint Grove is less a suburb and more a very polite yacht club with hedges. On a Sunday we’ll potter down to Keanes Point Reserve, nod at the tennis clubs like they’re ancient rituals, and quietly assess whose Camellias are winning the suburb’s ongoing horticultural cold war.

Back in the day our “night out” meant wandering down Stirling Highway to Pep’s — Pep’s Wine Bar at 492 Stirling Hwy — where co‑owner Charlie Galbally famously said, “We want this place to feel like our home.” It was housed in a building Broadsheet noted dated back to 1890, served antipasti and a 160‑bottle list, and made even the most modest of us feel like mid‑century sophisticates. We used to pop in after Council fetes, feeling very much like the sort of people who owned furniture from Coco Republic at 496 Stirling Highway.

Coffee culture here had its moments too: Monogram Caffe on Leake Street and Leroy’s drive‑through on 615 Stirling Hwy were the kinds of spots our neighbours would quietly benchmark against each other. And for those who like their homes to smell like private equity, Peppermint Grove Australia’s candles — made, as Sitchu noted, with an in‑house perfumery in the Southern Highlands — gave many of our houses that “yes, we DO source things” aura.

If you’re imagining chaos, you’ve been reading the wrong postcode. Time Out flagged our postcode 6011 (Cottesloe and Peppermint Grove) among Australia’s wealthiest, and you can feel that in the curated calm: top private schools, yacht club mornings, immaculate gardens. It’s all very serene, very tidy, and very strongly scented.

Who would hate living here? If you like street art, late‑night live music or bins that smell like community spirit, this place will bore you into submission. Who would love it? If you enjoy quiet prestige, a good wine list from Pep’s heyday, Coco Republic showrooms, tennis club gossip, and Keanes Point sunsets — welcome home. We’ll save you a spot on the bench and a polite clap at the next Joy Shepherd Performing Arts Centre recital.
