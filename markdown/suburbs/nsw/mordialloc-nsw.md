# Mordialloc (NSW)

Slug: `mordialloc-nsw`

Back in the day we’d pretend the sunrise at Mordi Beach was our little conspiracy — Time Out even put our stretch on the list of best sunrise spots — and for a while it felt true. We’d stroll the walk/bike path that links three suburbs, point at the playground and the foreshore BBQs, and act like we’d discovered bayside living before the rest of Melbourne caught on.

Weekends used to look like a Broadsheet feature: families, dogs and kids drifting through Mordi Village, the open day vibes Time Out wrote about, and the odd industrial-turned-café on the main drag. Hendriks used to occupy a former carpet showroom with big windows overlooking the golf course; Urban Ground used to run the big, family- and dog-friendly food-truck park on the old car‑wrecking site (distillery and all). Milhouse Canteen was, at one point, the new kid in the industrial backstreets. Tommy Ruff moved its fish bar to 564 Main St and got fishier. La Cabra was billed as “coming soon.” Chiki Chan opened in October 2024 and brought neon and high-energy vibes. Broadsheet, Urban List and Concrete Playground did their part cataloguing our eating-and-drinking glow-up.

Then Wall to Wall Festival 2024 turned the Mordi Village Arts and Cultural Precinct into a mural playground — block party, food trucks, DJs, workshops and open studios — which was chaotic and brilliant in equal measure. We liked it. Our laneways had opinions now.

Who would hate living here? Obviously anyone allergic to murals, weekend markets, or a reasonable oyster happy hour (Time Out even flagged a hawker-style spot for cheap oysters). If you crave pristine anonymity or you think bayside suburbs should come with a mandatory Michelin silence, Mordialloc will rile you.

Who would love it? Young families, sunrise stalkers, bargain-seeking diners, and anyone who wants “bayside without the Brighton ego,” as Sitchu put it. For those who appreciate a little grit — an old carpet factory turned café, a reclaimed wrecking yard turned food hub, a main street that keeps reinventing itself — we’re quietly smug that Mordi still makes room for you.
