# Cockatoo (NSW)

Slug: `cockatoo-nsw`

Back in the day — or at least in every guide we’ve devoured — we’d get off the ferry, squint at the skyline and tell visitors: welcome to Cockatoo Island, or Wareamah if you want to sound like you read the brochure. We know the little rituals here: lugging a tent across the Northern Apron Park, deciding whether to book one of those brutalist heritage cottages, and pretending the island’s convict-era shipyard makes our camping vibes ‘authentic’.

For years the island’s been a grab-bag of moods. It was billed as a festival island — remember Mode Festival 2025? — and it was even earmarked for immersive pop-ups like ‘Wednesday Island’ and fear-packed events such as Fear Island. The Harbour Trust once floated a grand vision to turn the place into a sprawling arts precinct; whether that was bold thinking or just a fancy manifesto we’ll never completely agree on is part of the charm.

We also admit the island is secretly primal. Time Out loved reminding us it’s heritage‑listed, with cavernous industrial buildings that double as excellent settings for ghost tours — and Concrete Playground made a living listing the after‑dark ones where spirit detectors meet old convict bricks. Then there’s the wildlife doing its best to upstage us humans: as scientists reported, Sydney’s cockatoos taught each other to open wheelie bins, so expect avian entrepreneurs stealing the show (and your picnic).

Who would hate living here? People who can’t handle ferries, brutalist architecture, or the sound of a cheerfully unhinged bird community schooling humanity on resourcefulness. Also anyone who thinks a campsite and a UNESCO tag are mutually exclusive.

Who would love it? History nerds, festival fiends, cheap-glampers and anyone who likes their skyline with a side of rusting gantries and giant seagull‑adjacent cockatoos. We’ll keep bringing our esky, pitching a tent, and pretending the industrial romance was always ours — because, frankly, it is.
