# Roseville (NSW)

Slug: `roseville-nsw`

We’ll say it straight: back in the day we used to shuffle out of Roseville Train Station like a small, well-behaved congregation — coffee in one hand, library book in the other — and head for the village on the Pacific Highway. There’s a particular kind of North Shore calm here: Lane Cove National Park on the edge, Hill Street and Pacific Highway doing village‑style shopping, and a cinema that refuses to become a multiplex.

Our weekend ritual used to be very specific. Not far from the station was Calibrate Coffee — Sam Gabrielan and Murat Kum’s little crown jewel — all Scandinavian fit‑out vibes and a custom Slayer espresso machine. They made an impression: the Calibrate Bowl, an acai with Hippie Lane RAWnola, the whole specialty-coffee flex that had the suburb pretending to be a Copenhagen satellite for a minute. It put Roseville on a few food‑ie maps, then sent its owners scurrying to other postcodes. We watched proudly.

We also quietly boasted about Sydney’s first permanent cat cafe, Cafe Purrfection (part of the Chatswood Cat Palace). Yes, you could sip and stare and book a session if you wanted your latte judged by a rescue cat — very civilised.

If you’re the sort who needs late-night ragers, neon laneways, or a skyline you can’t breathe, you’ll hate Roseville. We’re not flashy. We’re the sort of place that will host the Alliance Française French Film Festival at Roseville Cinemas and consider that a peak cultural moment. We like our bathhouses, too — Escape Bathhouse was slated for the heritage‑listed Seymour Building at 989 Pacific Highway and got a lot of “ooh, fancy” press when it arrived.

Who would love it here? Families, people who want a green edge with top schools, anyone who prefers a boutique cinema to a stadium, and coffee nerds who once queued for micro‑lot talk. We’re unhurried, village‑minded and slightly smug about it — and honestly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
