# Edwardstown (NSW)

Slug: `edwardstown-nsw`

Edwardstown, NSW — we can still picture the Clarence Gardens Masonic Hall at 146 Avenue Rd Clarence Gardens, even if the listings we found are more "back-in-the-day" than breaking news. As SAcommunity noted, two lodges — Edwardstown Lodge - 168 and Edwardstown Lewis Lodge - 207 — used to book the place like clockwork: last Friday evenings for one, second-Monday evenings for the other. The hall seats 100, which is either perfect for a proper knees-up or for politely ignoring your neighbour across a very large room.

Our weekend ritual (when the internet still felt like a noticeboard) involved a detour to the Rotary Book Exchange at 29 Aberfeldy Ave — listed open Wed–Sat 10am–4pm — to rescue a paperback and pretend we were on a cultured errand. The Rotary Club of Edwardstown itself was listed as meeting twice monthly at the Maid of Auckland Hotel (926 South Rd Edwardstown), first and third Tuesdays, dinner at 6:15pm — so yes, civic duty came with slab-like punctuality and a side of pub lighting.

If you're hunting local drama, the paperwork suggests it comes in the form of scheduling: two lodges, one hall, and everyone with a monthly calendar. Riveting, really — lodge democracy at its finest. The Edwardstown Baptist Church Community Centre (corner of Rothesay Ave and Dorene St, St Marys) was listed as offering venue hire — auditorium and café facilities — so if your idea of excitement is a well-appointed meeting space, our suburb was living the dream.

Who would hate it here? People who need a skyline of tapas bars and late-night DJ drops. If your weekends demand artisanal everything, you’ll find our archival charm a little too much like… actual community.

Who would love it? Folks who like organised gatherings, secondhand book treasure hunts, hall hire that fits a hundred people and the comforting logic of a twice-monthly Rotary meeting. For us, Edwardstown’s appeal has always been that it wears its community on its sleeve — the online listings just happened to be telling their own slightly nostalgic story.
