# Upper Ferntree Gully (NSW)

Slug: `upper-ferntree-gully-nsw`

We start the weekend like proper locals: pretending we're just popping down to the shops, but actually we mean the 1000 Steps. Back in the day Concrete Playground told newcomers to catch the Belgrave line to Upper Ferntree Gully Station, wait for the 688 bus up Mt. Dandenong Tourist Road and then feel very pleased with themselves for getting to the Dandenong Ranges National Park. We still get that smug cardio buzz, even if the public transport remains, ahem, character-building.

For years the local listings and guides name‑checked the usual cast: the 1000 Steps and the 1000 Steps Cafe at the Dandenong Ranges Visitor Information Centre on Burwood Highway; Wholefood Merchants was that slightly woo‑adjacent emporium Broadsheet wrote about, selling organic fruit and vegetables, crystals and tarot cards to people who’d gone for a bush walk and refused to stop being earnest. Time Out even noted Upper Ferntree Gully made Domain’s 2025 “most-viewed” suburbs list — which we like to interpret as “everyone looked at us, then decided to drive.”

There’s no scandal here, only community infrastructure doing its thing. The kids’ footy clubs—Upper Ferntree Gully Junior Football Club at Dobson Park and the Upper Gully Football Club at Kings Park, Willow Road—keep weekends noisy and parents slightly overdressed in club scarves. The CFA brigade posts reminders about driveway access like a kindly neighbour who also happens to roll up in a truck when needed. Medical and pet services were listed in local directories too: Angliss Hospital on Albert Street, Glenfern Dental on Burwood Highway, JD Wilson Optometrist and Ferntree Orthodontics around town, and the Ferntree Gully Veterinary Hospital for the pets who deserve checkups after they stare at us from the backseat.

Who would hate living here? If you require trams every seven minutes, a rooftop cocktail bar on every corner or absolute silence between 8am and 8pm, this place will test your patience. But who’d love it? Hikers who like a proper hill, families who live for Auskick and local footy fixtures, and anyone who enjoys a town that wears its greenery like a slightly damp cardigan. We’re proud of our ferny, slightly eccentric slice of outer suburbia — and we’ll see you at the bottom of the 1000 Steps, pretending the climb was the easy part.
