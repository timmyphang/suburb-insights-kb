# Forest Lodge (NSW)

Slug: `forest-lodge-nsw`

We start Saturdays at the Tramsheds — or at least we used to, back when Broadsheet crowned Forest Lodge “on the up” and listed house‑cured meats, breakfast rolls, burritos and Umami coffee as proof. The old tram depot at 1 Dalgal Way was our outdoor table: Dust’s stone‑milled loaves, Flour Eggs Water’s pappardelle, Gelato Messina for when regret needs cooling and Belles Hot Chicken for when the rest of us need righteous pain. Urban List used to call it a go‑to foodie precinct; we sealed that with a cheeky shared plate at Bodega 1904 or a big, American‑leaning feed at Redline Taphouse and Kitchen.

On weekdays we’d drift down Bridge Rd to Delisia (284 Bridge Rd) for a focaccia sando that Time Out liked, or pick up croissants from Brickfields at 38 Ross Street when Sitchu wrote its love letter to flaky things. If you liked a French espresso martini in the sun, Concrete Playground used to point you towards Garçon in the Tramsheds — all of which makes us sound terribly cultured on Instagram and just a little smug in real life.

We’ve had tabloid moments. Pedestrian TV once reported the Harold Park Hotel’s Sunday acoustic slot getting canned after a single noise complaint — yes, that Harold Park on Ross Street — and the same outlet unfortunately covered serious incidents nearby. Not our finest hours, but we’re not pretending we’re perfect.

Who wouldn’t last here? People who crave eternal silence. The council’s taste for quiet after noise complaints and our propensity for converting heritage buildings into dining temples is a cultural clash waiting to happen. If you hate a short walk to many options, this isn’t your postcode.

Who would love it? Food obsessives, anyone who rates a neighbourhood by its tramshed of choices, families who like that Sitchu notes spots are close to Ross Street Playground and Forest Lodge Public School, and the smug sustainability nerds who remember the Forest Lodge Eco House and its seven‑metre vertical garden. We own our little contradictions: heritage terraces, a converted depot full of treats, and the occasional headline. Still, it’s ours — noisy, flaky, slightly righteous, and impossible to be bored in.
