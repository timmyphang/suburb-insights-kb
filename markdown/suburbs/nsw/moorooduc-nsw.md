# Moorooduc (NSW)

Slug: `moorooduc-nsw`

We like to think Moorooduc is where the Mornington Peninsula remembered how to slow down and then apologised for it. On any given imagined Saturday we picture ourselves leaning on a white picket fence while someone argues about pinot noir like it’s a geopolitical stance — as Broadsheet once wrote, Moorooduc Estate was one of the oldest vineyards on the Peninsula and its cellar door was the sort of place Richard McIntyre got called an elder statesman of “natural wines.” Dreamy, slightly smug, very vine-y.

Back in the day we’d point the car down Stumpy Gully Road and find Stumpy Gully Vineyard — Concrete Playground described it as a family-run spot at 1247 Stumpy Gully Road with a restaurant open Friday–Sunday. If you wanted provenance, there was provenance: Broadsheet noted the Zantvoort family planted their first block in 1989 and leaned hard into pinot noir. Very artisanal. Very intentional. Very, “Do you taste the afternoon?”

We used to bring the kids to the Moorooduc Primary School Hall for playgroup meetups — Playgroup Victoria had the venue listed — and on other weekends Penbank on Rickards Rd would feel like someone had converted a 35-acre postcard into a co-ed school and, occasionally, a festival field: Time Out mentioned Penbank hosted the Between The Bays music festival. The Peninsula Summer Music Festival also used to spread itself around wineries including Moorooduc Estate, if you liked your concerts with a side of chardonnay.

We even had seasonally festive plans: Sitchu once listed the Moorooduc Christmas Tree Farm at 1680 Stumpy Gully Road, a 25‑acre paddock where you could allegedly clamber onto Santa’s sleigh and pay something like $75 for a tree. Rustic, slightly theatrical, peak Moorooduc.

Who would hate it here? People who need neon, 24/7 kebabs and a tram every five minutes. Who’d love it? Anyone who wants vineyards, sheepish local charm, kids’ activities and a festival that used to spread across church gardens and cellar doors. We’re not flashy. We’re the postcode that prefers cultivating vines to cultivating trends — and honestly, we’re fine with that.
