# Camperdown (NSW)

Slug: `camperdown-nsw`

Back in the day we’d stagger past the converted bowling green at Camperdown Commons and still smell the soil from Pocket City Farms before we even decided what to eat. The whole place used to be this delightfully earnest farm‑to‑fork experiment — a dining room with woodfired pizza, a 100‑square‑metre urban farm and Acre (or “acre”/Acre Eatery, depending on which write‑up you read) turning our salad shame into bragging rights. Time Out and Broadsheet loved it; so did our Instagram feeds.

Morning routines here, when we were properly local, involved a slow wobble to Gather on the Green for Mecca coffee and a ploughman’s that felt like a communal cuddle. On Parramatta Road there used to be Deus Ex Machina, where motorbikes and lifestyle merch brushed shoulders with espresso, and a Savers was even slated for 93–99 Parramatta Road — so yes, secondhand glamour was coming. If you wanted a late‑night feed, Derrel’s inside The Lady Hampshire was the bain‑marie curry legend people whispered about after gigs; The Lady Hampshire itself was given a new lease of life when it reopened in 2023 under Public Hospitality, all murals and live music.

We’ve also had our share of curiosities. Junkee once reported locals furious about a burger festival eyeing Camperdown Memorial Rest Park (our dog‑and‑picnic kingdom), and Pedestrian had a go at a lookalike contest that copped council pushback. There was even an escaped ape incident that made the rounds — because of course there was.

Who would hate living here? If you’re into utter quiet, pristine suburbia and zero student energy, you’ll despise the University of Sydney and Royal Prince Alfred Hospital precinct vibe — constant foot traffic, late gigs, and the comforting chaos of medical and academic life. If you loathe topiary attempts at sustainability, the converted bowling club will make you snort.

Who would love it? Families (the Inner West Mums crew can tell you Camperdown Park and the refurbished playground were lifesavers), food obsessives who remember Acre, late‑night curry devotees, motorbike romantics from Deus Ex Machina era, thrift hunters eyeing Savers, and anyone who prizes a suburb that’s equal parts scrappy, brainy and quietly brilliant. We’ll take the mess — it’s ours.
