# Leopold (NSW)

Slug: `leopold-nsw`

We roll out of bed on a Saturday and, in our imaginations, it’s one of those idyllic Leopold mornings — the kind we read about and nod at like it’s our postcard. Back in the day we used to pop into the Community room at the Leopold Community Hub for playgroup: the Leopold Playgroup borrowed toys from the Leopold Toy Library almost every week, ran sensory pirate weeks and held sessions on Mondays, Tuesdays and Fridays (first session free, naturally). Those volunteer committees really did keep the tiny ships afloat.

If you wanted pies, we queued. As Urban List reminded us, Rolling Pin in Leopold often had a line out the door — and Broadsheet even name‑checked Rolling Pin’s 2021 Baking Association of Australia pie win. We liked to pretend we were queuing for culture, but it was really about pastry supremacy.

For the people who liked their weekends with a side of overindulgence, Campbell Point House on Matthews Road was the fantasy: infinity pool, tennis court, day spa and a private jetty. Concrete Playground billed it as the Bellarine’s shiny little luxury escape. And for those who preferred something homely, there was a B&B listing — Dimore le Leopoldine — promising complimentary Wi‑Fi and tour help if you wanted to remain connected while pretending to disconnect.

We’ll admit the name “Leopold” has been busy elsewhere too — Broadsheet once chronicled a Melbourne fashion label called Leopold hopping around Manchester Lane, even doing a capsule called ‘3rd Time’ with Glen Rollason. Not our fault the brand won’t stay put; we can empathise.

Who would hate living here? People allergic to community involvement, boutique pies and tiny local pageantry. If you loathe queues, toddlers in pirate hats, and the faint possibility someone nearby might be getting a massage and an infinity‑pool selfie, this isn’t for you.

Who would love it? Families who relied on the playgroup’s toy library, pastry pilgrims who still whisper Rolling Pin’s name like a talisman, and anyone who once dreamed of arriving at Campbell Point House by private jetty. For years the local story has been quietly wholesome, sometimes luxe, always a little bit proud — and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
