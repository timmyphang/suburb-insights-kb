# Dulwich Hill (NSW)

Slug: `dulwich-hill-nsw`

Back in the day our Saturdays began with the smell of crust from the Dulwich Hill Hot Bread Shop — Luigi Carrieri’s handiwork — and a dawdle down Marrickville Road to whatever pastry cabinet was open that week. We knew where to queue for tamales at Tamaleria & Mexican Deli, where to pick through the vintage trove at PM Store Sydney, and where Strawberry Fields Patisserie would have a tart so pretty it felt rude to eat it.

We’re proud Dully locals: the village felt like a corridor of contradictions — a sausage cellar‑door one block over from a revamp‑ed milk bar, a vegan chocolatier doing handmade takes on Tim Tams and Cherry Ripes, and Zeus Street Greek on Canterbury Road serving pitas that solved argument fatigue. The General Eatery and Supplies and Sideways Deli Cafe were the spots that made school‑run logistics feel gourmet; Sitchu even called us family‑friendly. Transport? We’ve always known the L1 light rail and T3 train had our backs.

Petty local drama, you ask? Oh, we had it — in the form of dessert arms‑races. Tella Ball’s Dulwich Hill location unleashed the Tellabab and a Nutella‑stuffed milkshake war that had Pedestrian TV drafting emergency exit strategies for World Nutella Day pancake challenges. It was all very middle‑aged‑anarchist, and we loved it.

Who would hate living here: the person who wants silence at 8pm on a Saturday. We’re quietly becoming an indie‑music hub — the Relit Festival in May 2025 staged around 85 artists across eight venues over six days, and that was just the overture. If you loathe live music, artisanal sausages and people who think op‑shopping is a sport, Dulwich Hill will test you.

Who’d love it: families who need parks like Hoskins Park and Johnson Park on the doorstep, thrifters who live for PM Store Sydney, food nerds who like a neighbourhood that can swing from baklava to banh mi, and anyone who wants a village where you can hop the tram, grab a loaf (we had Luigi’s), and still catch a six‑day festival without leaving the suburb. We’ll keep our village quirks — you’re welcome.
