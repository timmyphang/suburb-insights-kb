# North Fremantle (NSW)

Slug: `north-fremantle-nsw`

Back in the day we’d stroll down Queen Victoria Street with sand in our shoes and a stubborn sense of superiority — because we lived in North Freo, where eats meet art and the river thinks it’s part of the furniture.

Morning routine: we used to drift past Bib & Tucker for a waterfront meal and then pretend we were heading to Leighton Beach for “exercise” (address: Leighton Beach Boulevard), only to be lured into MRKT SPACE at Freeman Loop for something that looked like charcuterie and felt like a lazy ocean-view picnic. The Bridge House — that 100-year-old cottage turned brunch spot — used to be our cosy excuse for alfresco weekends. And yes, Piggy Food Co on the strip made parking remarkably unglamorous and child-friendly, which in our book is almost a public service.

Petty local history: Mojo’s Bar on Queen Victoria Street has been the sticky-floored launchpad for gigs and gossip for decades — the story even ran about locals pivoting to a record store, a hi‑fi system and beer on tap just to keep it alive. Propeller? That one’s a proper footnote now — Concrete Playground even marked it CLOSED. Meanwhile Pearla & Co arrived as the seafood bombshell (Urban List and Sitchu flagged its December opening) and Palette carved out that art-meets-beach vibe down near Leighton. Mrs Brown — yes, the quirky wine spot at 241 Queen Victoria St — has long been our eccentric aunt: a bit loud, a bit thrift-store, and oddly reassuring.

Who would hate it here: the kind of person who needs a mega-mall and a rooftop gym that’s open at 2am. The kind who thinks riverside beer gardens are overrated (to them we say: The Left Bank will disagree).

Who would love it: families who pile up towels at Leighton Beach and drop into The Waiting Room playgroup at 1 Johannah Street on Fridays; music lovers who remember Mojo’s; food lovers who followed Pearla & Co’s coastal roll‑out; anyone who likes their suburbs with a gallery on the corner, a pub by the water and a stubborn local character.

We’re small, a bit salty and insufferably proud. North Freo: come for the views, stay for the stories.
