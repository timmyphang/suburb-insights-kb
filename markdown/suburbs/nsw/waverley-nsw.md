# Waverley (NSW)

Slug: `waverley-nsw`

Back in the day we’d stroll down Bronte Road and argue about whose queue at Passeggiata was the nobler sin. We’re the lot who know that the bright yellow doors that Time Out raved about opened up a neighbourhood trattoria that made us feel like we’d accidentally wandered into someone’s Italian holiday photos — hand‑rolled pappardelle, house limoncello and all. We used to tell visitors about Frank’s Deli on the main street the way others tout a national monument: “Go for the Reuben,” we’d say, solemnly.

Our weekend routine used to look a bit like this: drop the kids at Waverley Park Playground to inspect the rocket ship, drag them away long enough to squeeze into Vinnies Waverley for a mythical second‑hand designer find (Pedestrian says it’s worth the rummage), then argue about whether Bronte Road Fish ever really replaced Bronte Road Bistro — the first‑look pieces noted tuna smash burgers and soft serve, so judge accordingly.

Of course, we’re not all hand‑rolled pasta and carefully curated op‑shop hauls. The council gives us material. As Pedestrian reported, Waverley Council once stripped the weights from the Bondi Park Fitness Station and we made our displeasure public with the kind of passion usually reserved for premiership finals. There was also that mural kerfuffle that Junkee covered — the anti‑Border Force artwork was vandalised and, as Waverley’s mayor John Wakefield said, it had only been meant to be temporary. Drama: passed around like takeaway coffee cups.

Who would hate it here? If you despise coastal chaos — closed sections of the Bondi‑Bronte walk from Notts Avenue to Marks Park and near Trafalgar and Boundary Streets (we remember that storm) — or you can’t handle families owning the afternoon (Eastern Suburbs Mums has our calendar of Bronte House open days and FIFA free viewings), move along. If you love beaches, an oddly specific village vibe, a piazza‑adjacent espresso‑for‑ten‑minutes‑too‑long, and restaurants that made Concrete Playground and Broadsheet give them a nod, you’ll fit right in.

We’re Waverley: proud, a little salty, and perfectly happy to remind you that yes, we’ve got a rocket ship in a playground and a trattoria with a personality.
