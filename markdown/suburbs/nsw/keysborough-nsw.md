# Keysborough (NSW)

Slug: `keysborough-nsw`

Back in the day our Keysborough weekend started like a low-key circus: the kids in the backseat still smelling of trampolines, we’d drive past 1 Fiveways Boulevard and watch Xtreme Air’s foam pits like it was a religious pilgrimage. We know every roofline of that place — sixty‑odd interconnected trampolines, six foam‑pit lanes, dodgeball courts and slam dunk lanes — because we’ve climbed out of them.

Mornings were a rota of practical things: dropoffs at the Keysborough Community Children's Centre on 364 Cheltenham Road (open 7am–6pm in the old listing, staffed by people who actually cared), a quick detour to Springers Leisure Centre at 400 Cheltenham Rd for netball or roller‑derby that doubled as cardio and neighbourly gossip, then maybe a quiet hour pretending to be cultured at Gaelic Park on 324 Perry Road when there was a performance or event.

We don’t do pretence about our priorities. If dumplings were the urgent problem of the week, Shark Fin Inn — the CBD/Keysborough name that cropped up in the city food roundups — used to be the reliable fix for yum cha plates and dim sims. If charity made you feel better about the world, Santa’s Beach House was even running pet photo sessions that donated $5 to the Australian Animal Protection Society at 26 Aegean Court. Small kindnesses, big schnitt.

There isn’t much scandal in the clippings — no council coups, no soap-opera mayors — just a very suburban archive: playgroups at Keysborough Primary and Keysborough Gardens Primary, Bounce Inc on the local play list, the local CFA brigade on Chapel Road keeping calm since 1956, and mums’ directories full of State of Soul Babies and Kids Yoga, Soccajoeys Springvale, Glittery Tapping Wonderland and True Colours Face Painting doing the real logistical work of childhood.

Who would hate living here? Nightlife hunters, one‑pub‑two‑DJ types and anyone whose hobby is criticising playground layouts. Who would love it? Families, amateur sports teams, anyone who likes the sound of children screaming happily and volunteers turning up at the fire brigade on Chapel Road. We’ll keep the trampolines, the dumplings and the charity photos — Keysborough has always been gloriously, usefully ordinary.
