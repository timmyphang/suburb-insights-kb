# Williamstown (NSW)

Slug: `williamstown-nsw`

We start our mornings like everyone else who lives here: pretending the bay view is the reason we pay the rent. Back in the day Broadsheet’s guide reminded us of our working‑class roots — the train that rattles past the Newport rail yards, the industrial approaches — and casually dropped Bathtub Gin Co. and Hobson’s Bay Hotel into the list of places to know. We like that: gritty history with a rooftop to look smug from.

A weekend routine, as the old guides put it, is embarrassingly simple. Ferrying over on the Southbank to Williamstown run with Melbourne River Cruises was a famously cheap day‑trip, Broadsheet and Urban List said, and we’d pair that with fish and chips from Off The Pier at 1 Syme Street (yes, the guide named it), then sprawl on the grassy foreshore near Williamstown Beach while the kids/cheeky adults pretend the waves are surfable. Time Out used to remind you the Williamstown Surf Life Saving Club patrols in summer — reassuring for the same people who bring folding chairs and a complex about sunscreen.

Our streets are earnest about retail: Concrete Playground pointed out Fancy on Ferguson and Avery & Arlo as the spots you walk past twice because you forgot your bank card and then buy something anyway. For the evening, there’s the neon promise of Lower West Side Wine Bar — Concrete Playground’s review even warned us the pink signage is serious business. And for those who want to feel cultured between chips and bay views, Seaworks and the Timeball Tower give us the maritime cred — Sitchu even dates Gem Pier back to 1839, so yes, our pier is older than some relationships.

Who would hate living here? People who abhor heritage piers, tiny boutiques, or any place that expects you to queue for a seaside deck. Also anyone allergic to people loudly calling everything “family‑friendly” — Urban List names Sebastian at 26 Esplanade as a kid‑friendly perch, so bring the brood and the patience.

Who would love it? Families, weekend wanderers, and anyone who likes their postcodes with a side of history, bayside walks and a wine bar that glows like a suburban disco. We’ll keep the view; you bring the snacks.
