# Bentley (NSW)

Slug: `bentley-nsw`

Back in the day, when you said "Bentley" around here you either meant a swanky tasting menu or a bloke misreading his GPS. We know — our suburb’s own press inbox is mostly crickets, so the name has been doing all the heavy lifting for us in the food pages.

We like to remind visitors that Bentley Restaurant & Bar used to sit tucked into the ground floor of the Radisson Blu Plaza hotel on O’Connell Street, the sort of place that made oysters and blacklip abalone with hen‑of‑the‑woods look like a life choice. Nick Hildebrandt and Brent Savage ran the joint; Broadsheet noted they’d given the kitchen “the freedom” to do elegant menu magic, and Time Out ran out the red carpet for a 20‑year greatest‑hits send‑off. The Bentley group then went and multiplied — Eleven Barrack, Brasserie 1930 inside Capella Sydney, Watermans at Barangaroo, King Clarence, Ashe and a fleet that includes Cirrus, Monopole and Yellow — so if the name felt familiar, that’s because it was busy colonising Sydney’s dining map.

Petty local drama? Sure — the farewell tour. The group called time on the original Bentley dining room, giving themselves a few months to run a greatest‑hits menu before the lease folded in August. Concrete Playground marked the final service for Saturday, August 29, 2026. We cheered, we mourned, we booked a table for nostalgia’s sake — all very suburban and very done with sentiment.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t stand long lunches, polished hospitality or restaurants that unapologetically ask you to linger, then this place will feel like an endurance test. If “built for long lunching and lingering dinners” sounds like a sentence, you’re not our people.

Who would love it? Lovers of theatrical menus, obsessives about wine lists (Cirrus even won Australia’s Wine List of the Year), and anyone who enjoys the idea that our little suburb’s name has been moonlighting as a Michelin‑adjacent brag. We’ll keep the local playgroup running and let the food writers argue over which Bentley was the best — we know the truth, and it smells faintly of abalone and final‑season nostalgia.
