# Osborne Park (NSW)

Slug: `osborne-park-nsw`

Look, we’ll be honest: we live in Osborne Park, NSW, and we know every corner of our postcode — which is why it’s endlessly entertaining that every glossy write-up the internet spits out is actually about the other Osborne Park, the one on the west coast. So consider this our cheeky love-letter / roast of the Osborne Park our Google always mistakes us for.

Back in the day (and in most of the articles we found), that Osborne Park felt like the industrial cousin who discovered luxe self-care. You’ve got warehouse spas — Merse Wellness Spa (Unit 8/454 Scarborough Beach Rd) and Ember Bathhouse — which Time Out and Urban List treated like secret chill-out palaces disguised as factories. We can just picture the semi-industrial strips sighing with relief that someone's installed a Finnish sauna next to the loading dock.

And then there’s food and drink: Micrology — the roastery at 1/58 Collingwood St — was Time Out’s darling, lauded for microlots and cold-brew casks. Pala brought Filipino-influenced brunch (longganisa breakfast burritos and ube lattes, Broadsheet noted) and the European Foods Marketplace turned grocery shopping into a dairy pilgrimage with “Australia’s largest cheese room.” Heroes and Villains let you drink among the vats in a no-frills taproom, which sounds like a great place to contemplate life’s choices.

For parents, the press pages mentioned the Beach House indoor play centre off Hutton St and WA Ninja Academy as a party option — and the local hospital popped up as a maternity catchment. Bakeries and bagels weren’t forgotten either: Neighbourhood Bagel (34 Hasler Rd), Gather Bakehouse (50 Howe St) and Connells Family Bakery earned shoutouts for bagels, bakes and jam donuts.

There wasn’t any juicy barista feud or council scandal in the clippings — mainly spa openings, roastery accolades and bakery recommendations — so if you’re after drama, you’ll probably have to make your own.

Who would hate it? People who can’t handle a cold plunge next to a loading bay or a beer served in a room with vats. Who would love it? Coffee nerds, spa devotees, families juggling play-centre run-and-recovery rituals, and anyone who thinks cheese rooms are a lifestyle choice.

We’re proud of our own quieter Osborne Park, NSW — but honestly, reading about that other one makes us consider a weekend flight and a long soak.
