# Bondi Junction (NSW)

Slug: `bondi-junction-nsw`

Back in the day we used to joke Bondi Junction was less a suburb and more a very busy foyer for Westfield, trains and anyone trying to get to Bondi Beach without actually going to Bondi Beach. We’d drop off the kids at Play Studio Kids on Oxford Street (155 Oxford Street, if you wanted directions), duck into the Oxford Street Mall, and clock the same people loitering outside the Apple Store like it was a town square.

Our mornings used to be a circuit: station to mall to coffee spot (Broadsheet once insisted there were “best cafes” to discover) and, if we were feeling bougie, a wander past Zimmermann’s expanded flagship — yes, that marble table once owned by Iris Apfel made headlines. Weekends could include a cheeky shuffle through Event Cinema, or a solemn moment when Westfield closed and later announced its reopening after that awful April 2024 attack — a wound the community still carries and that prompted an inquest and plenty of tough questions.

Bondi Junction has always had the odd split personality. The Boot Factory was lovingly restored into a community hub with a leafy Norman Lee Place courtyard, while the Hideaway went all NYC basement jazz and martinis on Spring Street. For a minute there we had everything from Cross Section’s viral Chicken Caesar wrap to lyf’s hotel beer-garden vibes and novelty wellness spots — Soak Bathhouse in the Verdical building and Xtra Clubs Bondi promising self-serve sauna zen. Billy the Pig even took over the Eastern Hotel for a spell.

Who would hate living here? People who crave quiet cul-de-sac suburbia. If you hate queues, fashion flags like Zara and Hollister, or the constant reminder that you’re a five-minute train ride from both a beach and a shopping centre, this isn’t your scene.

Who would love it? Families who use Waverley Park’s rocket-ship playground and Bondi Beach Playgroups, wellness fiends who once queued for Soak or the sauna, and anyone who enjoys the weird little mash-up of boutique heritage (Boot Factory), nightlife (The Hideaway), and a retail megahub that can feel like a small city. We’ll always be the place you pass through — but for a lot of us, that’s the point.
