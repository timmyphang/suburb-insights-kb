# Moorebank (NSW)

Slug: `moorebank-nsw`

We park the car, turn off the engine and pretend we’re in some sort of suburban pilgrimage: six townhouses at 76–78 Nuwarra Road going full North Pole, bulbs everywhere. As Urban List noted, that West Moorebank display used to be the sort of thing you timed your December night for — neighbours competing to out-Christmass one another, and us judging quietly from the footpath.

Back in the day — and yes, these are older write-ups we’re leaning on — our Saturdays had a particular rhythm. We’d roll past Manheim Auctions on Moorebank Avenue, remember the smell of diesel and auctioneers on a good day (that listing used to sit on the local business pages). Barnes Products on Greenhills Avenue was the place we’d point out to anyone who made moulds or cast stuff; it was on the local listings too, proud and Australian since 1986, according to the page. If you needed loud karting bragging rights, Urban List once had a nod for Extreme Indoor Go Karting in Villawood — very nearby and very fast on paper.

Then there was the odd bit of council-park-level drama that made national headlines: Moorebank High School installed fingerprint scanners to stop what Junkee called “poo vandals.” Yes, biometric scanners and a privacy row — cybersecurity experts weren’t thrilled; we were just amused someone thought fingerprints would fix school toilets.

Who would hate living here? If you want your nights filled with world-class live music rooms and boutique inner‑city everything, the old write-ups don’t make Moorebank sound like Melbourne’s music map. If you’re allergic to postcodes with entry-level housing energy, Time Out once pointed out that postcode 2170 — which includes us — is a hotspot for first‑time buyers, so expect starter homes and earnest renovators.

Who’d love it? First‑time buyers (Time Out’s data loved 2170), people who enjoy a Christmas lights spectacle that doubles as a local tradition, weekend tinkerers who used to swing by Manheim Auctions or Barnes Products, and anyone who appreciates a suburb that’s quietly getting on with things while the rest of Sydney overthinks its latte order.

We know our corners, our seasonal spectacles and our weird controversies — roast us if you must, but we’ll keep the lights on.
