# Castle Hill (NSW)

Slug: `castle-hill-nsw`

Back in the day our Saturday mornings in Castle Hill started with a slow shuffle past Castle Towers — more stroller parade than runway — and a detour to the Heritage Park for kids to run wild where convicts once made history. As Broadsheet said years ago, we were the evolving suburb about 30 kilometres from the CBD: green fields, views and increasingly fancier dinner options that felt slightly bewildering next to the playground.

We remember when The Piazza at Castle Towers opened in 2018 and promised nightlife for the Hills — 14 new retailers, Gami Chicken, Betty’s Burgers, PappaRich — suddenly our chief claim to late-night glory was a food precinct. The Baron got a write-up too, before it quietly shut its doors; now it’s one of those listings you scroll past and sigh.

For families, Castle Hill reads like a brochure. The Pavilion Theatre still hosts the Castle Hill Players (they’ve been doing it for decades), Powerhouse Castle Hill is the unexpected museum day out, and Coolong Reserve keeps toddlers and scooters well entertained. The Hills UPMarket at The Terraces, Castle Hill Showground, gives us one Sunday a month where everyone wears linen and buys someone else’s handcrafted soy thing. We even had the original Wiggles pop up at Castle Hill RSL for a charity night once — for an entire generation that was peak culture.

There’s been drama too — not the glamorous kind. We had that worrying asbestos saga at Castle Hill High School that nobody wanted in the PTA minutes. And yes, people keep reminding newcomers that we’re convenient: Castle Towers, two train stations and the Metro access Time Out mentioned — handy for the commute, less handy for pretending you live in a sleepy country town.

Who hates it here? Anyone chasing grimy, relentless nightlife or an endless stream of edgy pop-ups. Who loves it? Families, bakery hunters (Luca Bakery used to be tucked away like a country kitchen), people who like a decent café meal (Sol Cafe got a good rap) and those who want a suburb that tries hard to be both classy and sensible.

We’re proud, a bit wholesome, and perfectly content to argue over who makes the best sausage roll at the UPMarket.
