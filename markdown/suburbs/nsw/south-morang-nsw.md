# South Morang (NSW)

Slug: `south-morang-nsw`

We’re the lot who’ll tell you South Morang smells faintly of tomato sauce and eucalyptus at the same time — and we mean that literally. Back in the day Farm Vigano hosted Passata Day-inspired celebrations and the Melbourne Tomato Festival on Bushman’s Way; Time Out still remembers the place as a 16‑acre, heritage‑listed ode to pizza and share plates. As locals, we used to pretend those weddings were for our friends.

Saturday mornings here look the same no‑matter‑how‑many‑new‑houses: we lace up for a stroll in Plenty Gorge Park (Urban List calls it a cracker of a walk), wave at the neighbours doing the same, and quietly congratulate ourselves on having a national‑park backdrop for backyard cricket. If you were feeling brave you could attempt the 330/340 McDonalds Road pilgrimage — Hoopla opened as Australia’s purportedly grown‑up kids’ play centre, all slides and a bar, which made parents smug and toddlers terrified in equal measure.

Our public‑transport flex is oddly real. As Broadsheet noted, Hawkstowe station — the one that sprang up with the Mernda line extension — was once named the city’s best station for cleanliness and lighting. We like to tell visitors that’s our shiny little trophy while pretending the rest of the timetable isn’t held together by hope.

Petty drama? We’ve had our fair share. Pedestrian TV once chronicled a cyclist wandering into a roo‑occupied patch of Hawkstone Park and somehow surviving the encounter — a story we still use to scare first‑time dog walkers. Junkee even used South Morang as shorthand for the commute: a counsellor was quoted spending three hours a day travelling to and from work, which is the kind of dedication our parents call “character building.”

Who’d hate living here: anyone who thinks “walkable nightlife” means a cocktail bar on the next corner, or people allergic to kids, kangaroos and the occasional tomato festival. Who’d love it: families, hikers, anyone who savours heritage venues like Farm Vigano and the smug glow of having Plenty Gorge at your doorstep. Our council is the City of Whittlesea (25 Ferres Boulevard) — they see us, and we see them, usually from the car on a Saturday.
