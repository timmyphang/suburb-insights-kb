# Marrickville (NSW)

Slug: `marrickville-nsw`

Back in the day we used to moan that Marrickville was a secret only we could enjoy. Now it’s the kind of place where converted warehouses elbow Federation cottages in a slow, stylish waltz and you can get lost between a brewery, a library and a bánh mì joint before lunch.

Our Saturday routine used to be sacred: wander the Marrickville Organic Food and Farmers Market at Addison Road Community Centre, grab something from a stall, then wander past the old Poor Toms warehouse to see what spectacle someone has installed this week. Doom Juice reopening a permanent cellar‑door there (yes, with a skull‑shaped disco ball and maximalist neon) felt like a homecoming for the venue‑hungry among us. Once upon a time Marrickville Traders moved into that sprawling heritage warehouse on Chapel Street and we pretended we weren’t excited about new shops that didn’t sell candles with artisanal guilt attached.

We’re proud of our craft‑beer history — Grifter, Mixtape and Batch were the sort of neighbourhood brag we’d whisper to newcomers — and the Marrickville Hotel and Gasoline Pony have manned the bar while the rest of us argued about which local spot serves the best pho or modern Mediterranean. Gelato Messina turning its Marrickville HQ into Erin in March 2025 was one of those moments we collectively nodded at, like, yes, this is our era now.

Local drama? Inner West Council approving the Sydney Portugal Community Club to host 15 events — five with up to 5,000 people — made us realign our weekend earplug strategy. No soap‑opera rants, just the practicalities of where to put your picnic rug on event day.

Who would hate living here? If you need quiet streets, zero queues and no indie record stores you can spend an afternoon falling into, Marrickville will test your patience. If concrete breweries, volunteer arts spaces and a library carved out of an old hospital at Patyegarang Place make you twitch, maybe try somewhere less lively.

Who would love it? Artists, music nerds (Resist Records), craft‑beer pilgrims, families who bring kids to Jarvie Park and Steel Park, and anyone who thinks multicultural food is life. We know every corner, and honestly — we wouldn’t have it any other way.
