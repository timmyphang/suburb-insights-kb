# Kareela (NSW)

Slug: `kareela-nsw`

Back in the day a Sunday in Kareela looked like this: we’d stagger out of Cocoon ELC’s carpark on Bates Drive, pick up something dangerously elegant from Moss Bros in the revamped Kareela Village — Jackie Park’s art‑deco dessert salon that used to serve fifteen different sweets — then decamp to Sir Joseph Banks Native Plant Reserve to pretend we were planting grevilleas for posterity. As Broadsheet reminded us, Jensen’s reappeared here too, Carl Jensen bringing his famous roast duck back to Shop 1/13 Freya Street and a dining room big enough for your entire extended family (they wrote 150 seats; we asked no questions).

Our routine was annoyingly wholesome: kids to Cocoon, quick detour to Baked Patisserie if the kids behaved, then a picnic among the banksias and grevilleas in the reserve that the Sutherland Shire Council set up in 1969. Mums of the Shire ran the local playbook — “bring a hat, bring a picnic rug, and bring the grandparents if you expect to eat Jensen’s roast duck.” Concrete Playground even waved the reserve under our noses in the wildflower guides, so we felt culturally validated for liking native shrubs.

Petty local drama? The papers didn’t give us any juicy feuds, so we’ll supply our own: the ongoing contest between who gets the last Moss Bros custard tart and who has to fetch the esky. But seriously, the available stories are more about families and services than scandal — twelve preschools listed on CareforKids, vacancies and the very adult figure of $140.42/day for childcare that makes you contemplate extreme life choices. There’s also Kareela Family Dental, which opened in 2022 and gave us a reason to floss with pride.

Who would hate living here: people who want nightlife louder than a set menu, or anyone allergic to kids, native plants, and desserts that look like small sculptures. Who would love it: local families, plant nerds, loyal diners who remember Jensen’s comeback, and anyone who thinks an art‑deco cafe and a tidy native reserve make for a perfectly civilised Sunday. We’ll keep minding the playgrounds and the pastry queue — someone’s got to.
