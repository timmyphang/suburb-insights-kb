# Claremont (NSW)

Slug: `claremont-nsw`

We live in Claremont, NSW — except most of the clippings we hoard are about other Claremonts, so consider this a town with identity issues and very good PR people. Back in the day the write-ups that land in our feeds were all very specific: Broadsheet and Sitchu fawning over Il Santo and Corvo, Urban List name‑checking Typika Artisan Roasters, Time Out marvelling at the Cadbury factory and its proposed $150 million Chocolate Experience. It’s like our suburb went to a cousins’ reunion and never came back.

Our morning routine, theoretically: we’d stroll past whatever passed for a main drag here while remembering Urban List’s round‑up of cafes and Broadsheet’s notes about handmade pasta and wine lists. In the archives Il Santo was the quiet new osteria; Avalon was the Mediterranean project inside The Grove Residences; Corvo opened in the Claremont Quarter. None of these are promises about today — more like postcards from the claremont family tree.

There’s no petty council kerfuffle in our feed, no local barista scandal to gossip over — just events that keep turning up in festival lineups and listings. Concrete Playground and Urban List have the Claremont Showgrounds on rotation: MOULD x Pinot Palooza, Spilt Milk, Wildlands — three ways to attract an army of people who will spill something on your shoes and then post about it. Time Out’s longreads remind us that Claremont once birthed a chocolate factory people still want to tour; they even got council approval for a giant Chocolate Experience. We can be smug about that for at least a decade.

Who would hate living here? People who loathe queues, festivals, and the existential confusion of being mistaken for other Claremonts. If you crave absolute anonymity, the prospect of showground crowds and a tourist‑magnet chocolate attraction will have you updating your LinkedIn to “remote island hermit.”

Who would love it? Food wanderers and wellness devotees — the archives point to pasta‑led osterias, Mediterranean menus, Typika in the cafe lists, and boutique wellness spaces like Merse and Element on Urban List’s radar. We’re small‑town calm with a global brochure — and we can laugh about it. After all, if you’re going to be mistaken for somewhere else, you might as well be the version that gets the good restaurants and the chocolate factory.
