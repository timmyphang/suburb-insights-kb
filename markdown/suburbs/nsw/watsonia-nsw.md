# Watsonia (NSW)

Slug: `watsonia-nsw`

We’re the sort who still get nostalgic about a second-Saturday morning: the Diamond Village Craft Market used to pack the shopping centre carpark, while we pretended we only popped into Lee’s IGA for milk and came out with a crate of impulse snacks. Back in the day the Diamond Village Shopping Centre had everything you needed — Hair Headquarters for the quick trim, Dr James Clarke’s Dentistry for the polite dental lectures — the kind of suburban one-stop that makes you feel like you’ve left the city but kept the essentials.

These days we like to stand on Grimshaw Street and point out the spot where Melbourne’s third-longest underground rail tunnel runs almost half a kilometre beneath Watsonia — the Hurstbridge Line extension between Grimshaw Street and Elder Street that wrapped up as part of the North East Link works. It’s comforting, really: if anything goes wrong above ground at least we have a tunnel taking the drama elsewhere.

We’ve had our excitements. Plenty Gorge once threw a grassfire our way and we all briefly remembered how loud community group chats can be. And for a hot minute Time Out put us on the “top 20 Victorian suburbs set to boom in 2024” list, which was equal parts flattering and suspiciously specific neighbourhood bragging material.

If you’re after chaos and non-stop novelty, Watsonia will test your patience. People who hate the idea of a measured, practical suburb — where the shopping centre does what it says on the tin and MumsPages lists multiple local services, classes and activities for kids — will find it maddeningly sensible. If you can’t bear a craft market on a Saturday that’s more community stall than artisanal theatre, this isn’t your stage.

But if you love a place that quietly does the job — a reliable IGA run, a hairdresser who knows your name, a dentist you actually trust, the occasional market where you can buy something knitted by a neighbour — you’ll love Watsonia. We’re not loud, we don’t try too hard, and we secretly enjoy that everyone who drives through ends up staying for longer than they planned. Welcome, or, as our craft-market stallholders would say, pull up a chair and buy a candle.
