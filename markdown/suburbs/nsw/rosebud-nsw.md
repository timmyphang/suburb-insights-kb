# Rosebud (NSW)

Slug: `rosebud-nsw`

Back in the day our weekend started when the sun hit the bathing boxes and someone suggested, with deadly seriousness, a detour to the Point Nepean Coastal Walk. We’re the kind of place where “a quick stroll” means counting the golf holes at Top Fun Shack, then arguing about whether the bayside kayaks really are beginner-friendly because Concrete Playground said so.

For years the local press loved a good makeover story. Hobson — the art‑deco number on Point Nepean Road (1489 Point Nepean Road, if you like a postcode) — used to be the place for remixed brunch classics and Inglewood coffee, all with a breezy foreshore swagger. Blue Mini famously took over the old Rosebud Ten Pin Bowling Centre and pivoted into an emporium during the 2020 lockdowns, which is exactly the sort of proud, slightly ridiculous reinvention we cherish. And if anyone scoffs at our cultural cred, we point to Reload Records and Craig “Breedlove” Gawthorne — a proper, long‑running record store whose stock reads like someone paused vinyl in 1989 and never hit stop.

We’ve also been listed as retirement‑friendly (Time Out’s seal of relaxed approval), which explains why half our conversations involve which cafe reopened first and whether the market still sells the best local produce. The Peninsula Film Festival — started by actor and local Steve Bastoni — put us on the map for outdoor cinema folks, and was slated to return in January 2025, if you enjoy watching movies with sand in your shoes.

Petty local drama? Mostly business reinventions and playlist gatekeeping. OH YA, launched in 2023 by Sarthak Bansal, earned a shout for doing Indian food here on the Peninsula — which gave us something to argue about other than whose childhood memories of the bay are more authentic. Junkee even reminded the world that “rosebud” is a cheat code in The Sims; we use that line whenever someone asks how to buy a holiday home here.

Who would hate living here: people who demand neon skylines and 24/7 espresso theatrics. Who would love it: families, retirees, day‑trippers who like mini golf and coastal walks, and anyone who thinks a bowling alley becoming an emporium is peak local drama. We’ll keep the bathing boxes; you keep the noise complaints.
