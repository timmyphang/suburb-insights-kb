# Winthrop (NSW)

Slug: `winthrop-nsw`

We know the moment in Winthrop that tells you everything — the car parks are full because someone's dragged half the city into Winthrop Hall for Perth Upmarket. Back in the day Broadsheet called it one of the city's biggest design markets, with more than 180 (then 200+) makers squeezing into the University of Western Australia’s Winthrop Hall, and we’ve been politely elbowing for the best ceramics ever since.

Our weekend routine used to be predictable: wander the Perth Upmarket stalls in the Winthrop Hall and grounds, sniff a bit of artisan cheese because, yes, The Cheese Maker was a regular, then head for some zen. Classic Flow Yoga was once a summer morning ritual in the Winthrop Gardens — more than 350 people reportedly rolled out mats beneath the Moreton Bay Fig trees. That was our version of church.

We’ll admit our local culinary brag used to be very specific: Tankk Gourmet Fish & Chips had a Winthrop outpost — the Urban List even listed it alongside Como and Melville for its lemon pepper seasoning and those $10 deals on Mondays and Tuesdays. Queta, the brow technician Urban List name‑checked, was the kind of person people quietly booked months ahead to avoid eyebrow regret. Those were the small comforts.

Petty drama? There isn’t much to fling here except the spreadsheets. Sitchu even mentioned Winthrop when whispering about property investment near Melville — which means our suburb quietly graduated from “nice place to live” to “dot on someone’s growth strategy.” Also: real estate listings like 75 Aitken Drive used to pop up on Sitchu, which made everyone mildly hopeful and compulsively curious.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to markets, community yoga, or artisanal cheese (and yes, people like that do exist). Who would love it: market-goers, bargain fish-and-chips hunters, weekend yogis under fig trees, and anyone who likes their suburb low-key but with a predictable dose of creative chaos.

We know what they say about Winthrop. Back in the day, we showed up anyway — bags in hand, mat under arm, ready to buy something handmade and pretend we’d planned it that way.
