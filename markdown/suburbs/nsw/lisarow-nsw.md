# Lisarow (NSW)

Slug: `lisarow-nsw`

We know Lisarow the way locals know a shortcut that confuses everyone else: by station, by fairground, and by the fact we’ll quietly roll our eyes at any newbie who thinks “Central Coast” means surf and non-stop nightlife. The Lisarow Train Station is our little landmark — the place where schoolbags get shuffled on and off, where we judge commuters by how many reusable bags they’re carrying, and where you can time your morning gossip to the timetable.

Back in the day CentralMums ran a proper “Meet My Suburb — Lisarow” piece that called out the family-friendly stuff, and they weren’t wrong. The one event nobody forgets is the Lisarow Public School Country Fair — listed as happening on Saturday 27th May (9am–3pm) — with rides, stalls, food, show bags and a large auction. We used to measure the year by who won the giant auction prize and how many show bags you smugly managed to wrestle into the car.

We’ll admit: we’re not pretending to be a cultural precinct. We’re more of a community precinct. If you’re into neon-lit laneways and Michelin itineraries, Lisarow will politely hand you a show bag and point you towards the train. If small-scale chaos involving baking competitions, raffle tickets, and precisely one dodgy novelty stall fills you with joy, you’ll fit right in.

There isn’t much scintillating scandal listed in the archives — CentralMums’ write-up and local event listings are the kind of content that makes national outlets yawn — and honestly, that’s part of our charm. We prefer trading juicy drama for extra slices of lamington and swapping gardening tips over the fence.

Who would hate it here? The person who wants every weekend booked with festivals and pop-ups. Who would love it? Families who like friendly streets, a school that runs a proper country fair, and commuters who appreciate a sensible station without the pretense.

We are Lisarow: modest, reliably suburban, and suspiciously good at making a Saturday morning feel like an event. Come for the auction, stay for the show bag — or at least take the train home with something to smile about.
