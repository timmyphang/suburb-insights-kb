# Camillo (NSW)

Slug: `camillo-nsw`

Back in the day we thought Camillo was just the street sign you’d glance past on the way to the shops. Turns out our little postcode is more of a name-drop than a nightlife hotspot — we share it with counts, chefs and a holiday house that likes to pose on dunes.

For a proper local morning you’d drift past Westfield Park Child and Parent Centre at 11 Hemingway Drive, because for years the PlayConnect+ group met there on Mondays during school term from 9:30–11:30. As PlaygroupWA noted, those sessions were free and supported kids 0–5 with delayed development or disability — practical, earnest stuff that actually grounds a community more than any glow-in-the-dark cocktail menu ever could.

If you had kids at Grovelands Primary, you knew the P&C uniform shop routine — polite chaos and a hard stop at 9am. The notices reminded families you could always place orders online if you missed the rush, the kind of small administrative mercy that makes local life tolerable.

Meanwhile, our name seems to have been busy abroad. There’s a Camillo House on the Mornington Peninsula — an “artful oasis” perched on Blairgowrie’s dunes — and a Don Camillo that’s been called Hobart’s longest-running Italian. Even the Negroni lore keeps popping up under the name Camillo: Count Camillo Negroni gets credit in several pieces for swapping soda for gin and inventing the thing. We get called glamorous by association; we’ll take it, but with a grain of sand from Blairgowrie.

Who’d hate it here? If you crave rooftop DJs, seven‑day late‑night dancing or a cocktail bar serving 30 Negroni permutations, you’ll be disappointed — those scenes live in stories about people named Camillo in Melbourne and Surry Hills, not our cul‑de‑sacs. We’re not a stage for hype; we’re a place with a pragmatic playgroup, a school P&C that runs on local goodwill, and a shared name that keeps turning up in fancier footnotes.

Who’d love it? Families who value support services, people who appreciate a quiet postcode and anyone who likes the idea that our humble suburb keeps popping up on postcards, restaurant histories and cocktail folklore. We’re modest, useful and oddly well‑travelled by name — and we’ll keep it that way.
