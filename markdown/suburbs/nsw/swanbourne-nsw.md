# Swanbourne (NSW)

Slug: `swanbourne-nsw`

Back in the day we’d wake to that ocean light and think: did we move to a postcard or just the back of Instagram’s feed? Swanbourne’s always been that annoying combo of salty air and suspiciously good pate — at least according to the old directories.

Our weekend starts — or used to, depending on how up-to-date your guide is — with a wander down Waratah Avenue. Broadsheet used to point us toward The Other Side for simple, wholesome breakfasts and Side Piece Deli (from the Daisies and The Other Side team) for pastries and stacked sangas that made you feel a bit too smug about leaving the city. For the people who like their pastry with a French passport, Choux Cafe and Patisserie was the mention in every list. And if you wanted something to take home, Time Out once praised Kirkwood Deli for its housemade jams and breads.

If you fancied leaning into the “coastal Hamptons” energy, The Shorehouse was the one Broadsheet and Time Out loved to show off — the wraparound views from Marine Parade, seafood, Josper-grilled things and the sort of deck that makes you consider midweek entitlement. Urban List even remembered Edition and Upperhand Burgers for when you needed serious lunching or a burger that tried harder.

Petty local drama? Not much in the clippings we’ve got — apart from the out-of-town headline where Pedestrian TV reported Ben Cousins was apprehended at Campell Barracks and taken to the Sir Charles Gairdner hospital. That’s the kind of news that doesn’t make our sunset strolls any less calm, but it did make for a weird Tuesday tabloid conversation.

Who would hate it here: anyone allergic to sea breeze, long lunches, and the possibility of strolling past North Swanbourne Beach at 282 Marine Parade — yes, Time Out archived the nudist history, and no, you can’t unread that detail. Also, if “quaint coastal suburb with good cafés” triggers your nerves, move on.

Who’d love it: families — Sitchu flagged Swanbourne as family-friendly — people who want Claremont Quarter nearby, and anyone who enjoys choosing between a patisserie, a deli, a wine bar or a Shorehouse sunset. We’ll keep the sand between our toes and the menus in rotation, thanks.
