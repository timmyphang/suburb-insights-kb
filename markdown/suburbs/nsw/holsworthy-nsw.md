# Holsworthy (NSW)

Slug: `holsworthy-nsw`

We’ll be blunt: Holsworthy doesn’t try very hard, and we like it that way. Back in the day the summer that nearly went off, fire crews were still trying to hold a blaze that blackened 2,430 hectares — we remember the backburning near Heathcote Road and the way the smoke hugged the Holsworthy military base. That kind of drama reminds you this place lives on the edge of proper bush, not curated parkland.

Our weekday rhythm used to mean Community Group mornings. Tuesdays meant Coffee & Chat, 10–12, and Wednesdays meant Playgroup/Mother’s Group, 09:30–11:30 in Building G229 — the sort of place where you’d swap biscuit recipes and barracks anecdotes. The Holsworthy Community Group billed itself as a tri-service crew looking after serving members and civilians alike; membership was a modest $15 a year for access to lists, discounts and a say on the committee. Britt Phillips was the go-to contact (0497 700 155, coordinator@holsworthycommunitygroup.com.au) — because in Holsworthy, things run on goodwill and a good contact list.

We also had the story you don’t see in brochures: a training exercise that went sideways. Pedestrian TV reported a NSW Police constable accidentally shot himself in the foot during training at Holsworthy; an investigation followed. Not our proudest headline, but it’s part of local life — we’re honest about the mishaps, then get on with it.

Who would hate living here? If your idea of home is a café strip and a late-night cinema, you’ll find Holsworthy a touch quiet and a little too close to the rumble of the base. If you need constant shininess, go elsewhere.

Who would love it here? People who value a fierce, practical community — service families, folk who turn up to Coffee & Chat, and anyone who likes the kind of place where the local group actually runs the playgroup and the committee minutes are a proper thing. For years the Community Group tried to keep everyone connected, and that says more about Holsworthy than any glossy suburb profile ever could.
