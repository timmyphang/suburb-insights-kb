# Broadmeadows (NSW)

Slug: `broadmeadows-nsw`

We know Broadmeadows — not as a punchline, but as the place where your weekend plans can pivot between a community hub playdate and a mall pop‑up. Back in the day the headlines loved us for being useful: Broadmeadows Central used to host all sorts of food theatrics, from Bonchon’s grand opening (remember the $1 wings celebration) to the Greatest of the Mall 2026 doughnut drops — Raph Rashid’s baklava‑cheesecake doughnut was allegedly handed out there. We saw lines for free pastries like it was a sport.

Our Saturday morning ritual used to be simple: drop the kids at the Broadmeadows Community Hub — the Playgroup Victoria pages made a point that Hume City Council ran that venue with parking — then wander the mall for whatever pop‑up civilisation was offering. If you were into bubble tea, Concrete Playground even pointed out there’s a Chatime listed in Broadmeadows, so yes, your sugar cravings were never ignored.

We’ve been on the maps, too. The Suburban Rail Loop proposals and earlier planning pieces had Broadmeadows pencilled in as a proposed stop — so we’ve always had that quiet civic confidence: we’re small on pretence, big on potential (or at least on proposal documents).

Who would hate living here? People who need artisan everything and zero centre‑court shopping. Folks who think a community hub and a good mall activation are incompatible. If you can’t do a weekend that involves playgroup, a cheeky wing deal or queuing for a novelty doughnut, this suburb will feel like a mismatch.

Who would love it here? Families who’ve used the Broadmeadows Community Hub and the Mary Magdalene Broadmeadows/Dallas Anglican Church playgroup. Food curious people who remember Bonchon’s opening and the mall pop‑ups. And anyone who likes the comforting certainty of a local centre that stages its own mini festivals — free doughnuts, $1 wings, a bubble tea stop — all in roughly the same postcode.

Sure, the press clips we have are from earlier rounds of coverage, but for us that’s the point: Broadmeadows keeps doing its thing, whether the stories are new or nostalgic. We like it that way.
