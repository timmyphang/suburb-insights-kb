# Busby (NSW)

Slug: `busby-nsw`

Back in the day we’d slip downstairs from Oxford House and pretend we weren’t lured by velvet. You know the sort: soft lamp‑lit ambience, velvety red carpets and a record player that made us feel cultured while we ordered something that tasted suspiciously like indulgence. That was Busby’s at 21 Oxford Street — a natural‑wine and record den that sat under the boutique hotel and liked to be taken seriously about its taste in both bottles and beats.

Our weekend routine used to be painfully specific: a slow daytime café browse (yes, it was a day café, too), then come sundown hide behind a wine list curated by Mike Bennie, try a Paolo Maffietti/Mabye Sammy cocktail if we were feeling brave, and share snacks from Clayton Wells that could sneak up on you (Wagyu flank tartare, anyone?). There were BYOR nights and vinyl residencies; sometimes they spun hip‑hop after the aperitivo hour and we’d debate whether that counted as immersive dining or a very fancy riot.

We’ll admit it — Busby’s leaned into vibes. Concrete Playground and Time Out wrote nice things about its mood and menu. Urban List even suggested popping next door for “great vino and hi‑fi tunes,” which felt like permission to be smug on a date. They ran things like $30 pasta‑and‑wine nights (we found the event listing from September 2025 particularly aspirational) and a weekly Sunday small‑plates thing while they were running it. Before that, Busby’s even tried our green thumbs with a pop‑up gin bar in the Royal Botanic Garden at Victoria Lodge (2017–18) — named after James Busby, because branding loves a bit of heritage.

Who would hate living here? People who can’t handle natural wines, people who think a table is a place to eat and not a listening bench, and anyone who believes carpets shouldn’t feel like velvet draped over their shoes. Also, if you detest the idea of a wine bar playing records and occasionally slipping into hip‑hop, this suburb will quietly ruin your evening.

Who would’ve loved it? Vinyl obsessive snobs, natural‑wine evangelists, date‑night strategists and anyone who wants their drinking to come with curated opinion and soft lighting. We’ll miss the theatrics — even if we’ll never admit it on the first drop of a record.
