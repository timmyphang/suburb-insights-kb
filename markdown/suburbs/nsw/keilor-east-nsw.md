# Keilor East (NSW)

Slug: `keilor-east-nsw`

Back in the day, after a lap of the lake we’d stroll over to the quarry and marvel that someone had finally turned Niddrie Quarry into something that didn’t involve trucks. As Broadsheet put it, roll up to the family-friendly spot in the Valley Lake estate and you’d find a jaunty blue-and-white building that locals nicknamed Perry’s — Peregrine Social Club — complete with a doggy drink station and puppacinos. For years we told visitors, “Yes, we were promised a cafe when we bought here,” and for years Broadsheet reminded everyone it took about 15 years to actually arrive.

Our Saturday routine used to look like this: a lakeside wander, a check-in at Peregrine Social Club (45 Valley Lake Boulevard gets a name-drop in a few roundups), then a quiet drive past Overnewton Castle if we’re feeling slightly Bridgerton and very proud of our own history. When we want to feel cosmopolitan without the circle rate, we remember the Parisian Patisserie Boulangerie and Little Sister cafe — Urban List called the latter “Keilor East’s little gem” — and we nod, because regional gems deserve credit even if the city mags moved on.

We’re proud of our contradictions: a Texas-style Houston’s Barbecue tucked in an industrial estate (Time Out loved recommending it to the carnivorous) and picnic-ready green space like Brimbank Park — Concrete Playground lists it as a Maribyrnong River Trail pit-stop. Also, for anyone who liked to mourn shuttered local heroes, Crumpet & Co gets a respectful “was” in closed listings.

Who would hate living here? If you crave neon nightlife and never-ending footpath chatter, Keilor East will bore you to tears. If you’ve never wanted to explain “No, it’s the quarry turned lake” at a dinner party, move along.

Who would love it? Families who appreciate parks and kind cafes, dog owners who remember the puppacinos, history buffs who’ll make a morning of Overnewton Castle, and anyone who likes their barbecue serious and their weekends low-key. For years the press covered us in fits and starts — we’ll take the quiet, the lake, and the smugness that comes with knowing our own backyard better than any headline.
