# Whittlesea (NSW)

Slug: `whittlesea-nsw`

Back in the day we’d time our weekends by the decibels: the exact moment the kids stopped screaming and the slides started doing the work for us. As Broadsheet gushed about the Supanova nine-storey waterslide and Concrete Playground catalogued the Lava Lagoon lazy river and Volcano Beach wave pool, we learned the local clock runs on Funfields opening hours — Funfields at 2365 Plenty Road, Whittlesea, in case you forgot where you parked the kombi.

Our Saturday morning routine used to be unglamorous and perfect: a quick detour past the queue for the big slides (Concrete Playground and Time Out both sent field reporters), then a pilgrimage to Whittlesea Bakehouse — the one that, as Time Out noted, snagged the title for Australia’s Best Sausage Roll at the 2025 South Australian Baking Show. We weren’t fancy; we just wanted a pie that could withstand a poolside towel and a small person leaning on our shin.

We should confess: the only real local scandal here is how consistently all the stories about us are about either rollercoasters or pastry. There’s also the wholesome municipal work — Whittlesea Community Connections has been doing the quiet heavy lifting since 1973, running the Food Collective and local programs (they list a contact on their site: 03 9401 6666 and an Epping office on High St). The archive of articles we’ve got is basically Funfields, Whittlesea Bakehouse, and community services — which is less scandal, more reliable playlist.

Who would hate living here? If your life’s aesthetic is “inner-city omakase and no sunscreen,” Whittlesea will test you. If you can’t cope with the idea of being known primarily as a theme-park-adjacent town with an award-winning bakehouse, then move along — we’ll keep your parking spot.

Who will love it? Families who want a day trip that actually wears the kids out, pastry pilgrims who worship flaky pastry, and anyone who appreciates a suburb that quietly outscores others on mid-range delights. Time Out even put us on a “most up-and-coming” list in 2026 — we’ll take that, tuck it into a paper bag with a sausage roll, and call it progress.
