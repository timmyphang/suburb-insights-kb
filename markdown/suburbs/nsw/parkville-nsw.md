# Parkville (NSW)

Slug: `parkville-nsw`

Back in the day we used to call Parkville Melbourne’s genteel brain: the bit of the city that wears a tweed jacket and brings a casserole to gallery openings. We wander Royal Park like it’s our backyard — the Australian Native Garden is the kind of place Time Out called “the jewel in the park’s crown,” and if you time it right the Jacarandas make the whole neighbourhood look like a slightly posher Instagram filter (cheers, Urban List).

Mornings here were a gentle relay race of campus foot traffic and prams. Broadsheet used to list the neighbourhood spots we’d brag about: Cafe Piccolina with its ghost sign for John Bull Oats, Standing Room Coffee tucked around the University of Melbourne, and Ho Ho's Canteen — the small places that made our weekdays tolerable. The Potter Museum’s big comeback — that “65,000 Years” exhibition — felt like the suburb flexing its cultural pecs, and House residency experiments like Cherrywood and the “Residence” project had us guessing which culinary identity crisis would arrive next (Concrete Playground chronicled the chef-in-residence hunt with all the suspense of a reality TV callback).

Petty local drama? We had it, but of the tasteful variety: restaurants flipping concepts annually, a museum that shuts for seven years and then reappears with a manifesto, and an underground station that became everyone’s favourite construction update. Time Out wagged a finger about the swanky new Parkville Station under Grattan Street and the promised public artworks for the Metro Tunnel — so much civic optimism you could almost smell the terrazzo.

Who would hate living here? If you need nightclubs at 2am, constant neon and 24/7 pointless excitement, Parkville will make you file for a personality transplant. If your idea of a weekend is terribly loud and identifiably non-leafy, you’ll find the Burke and Wills memorial and the Nature Playground dreadfully civilised.

Who would love it? Academics, museum-goers, families who treat Royal Park like an extension of their living room, and anyone who quietly enjoys being near the University, the Zoo and galleries like The Dax Centre. As Urban List and Broadsheet once reminded us: this is a suburb that reads quietly, parks loudly and occasionally stages a five-course gala at the Potter Museum just to show off. We wouldn’t have it any other way.
