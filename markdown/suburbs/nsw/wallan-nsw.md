# Wallan (NSW)

Slug: `wallan-nsw`

Back in the day the headlines about Wallan were gloriously uncomplicated: we were either where you booked a birthday party that ended in mud, or where someone pointed out a bit of railway that needed fixing. We know the narrative — and we own it.

On a Saturday morning we picture the lot of us piling into whatever gets us there to the paintball fields — Time Out once described Wallan Paintball as Victoria’s largest paintball venue, even listing the address at 315 S Mountain Road, Heathcote Junction. Nearby mums were organising playgroups; Playgroup Victoria used to list Blossom Buds Early Learning as “a warm, nurturing” spot in the heart of town. Not glamorous, but practical. And honest.

The petty local drama? It’s a pressy one: Pedestrian reported a past derailment near Wallan Station and carried the union’s line that the track was awaiting maintenance. No melodrama about villains or conspiracies — just a reminder that the things that affect us most are often the things the rest of the state would rather skim over.

Who would hate living here? If you only move for laneway espresso theatrics or an endless rotation of boutique this-or-that, Wallan will feel underdressed. If you’re offended by development corridors and new estates, you’ll tut every time Time Out points out our “up‑and‑coming” status (they even cited a tidy growth stat — 0.029 per cent — as evidence that we’re on the rise). You’ll shake your head at the retail hubs and improved transport links many others cheer.

Who would love it here? Families, for one — the playgroup and early learning listings show we’ve got the basics for kids and community. Adventurers and weekend warriors love the fields (paintball, anyone?), and bargain-seekers and movers who read Time Out’s notes about affordability and lifestyle will nod approvingly. We’re not trying to be the city; we’re the place you move to when you actually want room to breathe, a good kids’ centre, and a decent excuse to get muddy on a Saturday.

We’ll take the “up-and-coming” label and the paintball bruises with pride. It’s honest, it’s growing, and it’s ours.
