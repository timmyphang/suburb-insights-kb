# Baldivis (NSW)

Slug: `baldivis-nsw`

We know the best booth in town — the one with the chequered tiles and the vinyl seats where you can pretend you're in a 1950s diner. Back in the day Short Order rolled into Baldivis as its third outpost, Mata did the Grease‑movie thing with the fitout, and for a minute the idea of burgers, shakes and fries felt theatrically legitimate rather than just convenient. It was billed as a test bed for new menu ideas and was open seven days a week (that’s what the listings said).

Mornings used to look like this: we’d elbow past prams on our way to Brother of Mine, where the Humble brothers roasted coffee on site and the menu played nice with seasonal produce. Broadsheet even noted it could hold its own against cafés further north — which, coming from them, was basically a compliment wrapped in a latte art heart.

Petty local drama? Oh, we’ve had our moments. Once the cops stumbled upon a house‑sized hydro setup after someone left the door open — yes, that story made the rounds and reminded us that Baldivis will surprise you, for better or for more headline‑worthy reasons. For all our family‑friendly parks and walking trails, we’re not immune to tabloid fodder.

If you can’t handle a 40‑minute commute — Sitchu reckons we’re about that far south of Perth — or you believe every night must involve rooftop DJs and CBD taxis, this place will make you twitchy. Ditto anyone allergic to backyard barbies, corner blocks and neighbours who actually wave.

Who actually loves it here? Families, the people chasing new builds (we’ve seen the listings on Uluru Crescent, Campolina Avenue and Parker Way), anyone who likes Stockland Baldivis for shopping runs and mornings that start with a roast‑on‑site espresso rather than a tram timetable. And for those who like a pint with a side of local banter, the White Horse Tavern was slated to trot in at 124 Amazon Drive — pet‑friendly alfresco, big screens — the kind of place you’ll book for a birthday and then forget you booked because the snags were that good.

We’re a little bit suburban, a little bit theatrical, and very much ours. Come for the booths, stay for the parks — and mind the open doors.
