# Wollongong (NSW)

Slug: `wollongong-nsw`

Back in the day — or, more accurately, according to all the older guides we still love trawling — Wollongong was the place the mountains cheekily met the sea and everyone pretended that was totally intentional. As Broadsheet put it, we swapped steelworks for a small‑city coastal vibe and, yes, a surprisingly vigorous culinary scene (we see you, Baby Face Kitchen).

Our weekend starts with the eternal indecision: ocean pool laps or cliff strolls? Broadsheet and Concrete Playground used to map out the rock pools from Coalcliff to here, and Nuns Pool — the old Chain Baths below Flagstaff Hill — still gets name‑checked as one of those embarrassingly scenic, history‑soaked spots. If you’re feeling cultured, Time Out reminded us we have Nan Tien Temple — allegedly the Southern Hemisphere’s largest Buddhist temple — which makes for excellent photo diplomacy with visiting relatives.

Coffee used to be a mild flex: Opus Coffee Brewers was singled out for sleek windows, hanging plants and a very considered espresso setup. If beer is your brunch deity, Concrete Playground had a soft spot for Illawarra Brewing Company Brewhouse (yes, where you could once judge a Saturday by the burger truck outside).

We do have our dramatic moments — Junkee loved the tale of Elton John’s Gongside cameo, and the world cycling champs taught us to respect two things: speed and swooping magpies. The magpies, frankly, are the unofficial town cullers of ego. Also, Urban List promised shiny new Mariyung trains to make the commute less tragic, which is our favourite form of optimism.

Who would hate living here? People allergic to spectacular backdrops, swooping birds and live music: Concrete Playground and Junkee both make a point that we host festivals (Farmer & The Owl, Great Southern Nights), so if you crave silence and zero cultural events, maybe not.

Who would love it? Outdoor obsessives, weekend escape artists and anyone who likes choosing between beaches like Bombo, Easts, Surf and Kendalls, or a quick 90‑minute detour from Sydney. For years the guides have agreed: we’re best when you embrace the paradox — a bit gritty, a bit gourmet, wildly scenic and stubbornly ours.
