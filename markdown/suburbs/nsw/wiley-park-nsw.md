# Wiley Park (NSW)

Slug: `wiley-park-nsw`

We like to think Wiley Park announces itself the minute you walk past the park: a toddler chasing pigeons, a parent clutching a Playgroup NSW printout, and the lingering memory of last night’s dinner that tasted suspiciously of garlic and lemongrass. Back in the day Broadsheet sniffed us out for a very good reason — Rocs & Rolls, run by the Tran sisters inside the Wiley Park Hotel, turned wok-fried shellfish into a choose-your-own-adventure. Snails, razor clams, Balmain bugs, whelks, crawfish and sauces like creamy salted egg or lemongrass, garlic and chilli sate — it was the sort of place you’d whisper about to friends, then loudly text them the bill.

We used to be the kind of suburb where mornings were a sequence: drop-offs at Koala Kids Preschool (37 Defoe Street — yes, we still know the number by heart), the little centre that said it catered to just 15 children a day and ran Monday to Friday, 7am–6pm. It felt intimate, like someone had taken a loaf tin and only baked a handful of kids into it. And if you couldn’t be bothered with the official centre, Playgroup NSW’s postcode search always came up in conversation — a lifeline for anyone trying to meet other humans with mismatched nappies and shared patience.

We don’t pretend our press is always glamour. Once we even featured in a Pedestrian TV inquest report: investigators found no CCTV evidence of a certain man’s movements and no sightings of him using taxis from his Wiley Park home. Not the kind of publicity council PR teams pay for, but it’s part of our scrapbook — messy, real, and occasionally head-scratching.

Who would hate living here? If you require valet parking outside your artisanal bakery or nightly jazz at exactly 8pm, Wiley Park will make you restless. Who would love it? Families who prize community-level chaos, people who remember Rocs & Rolls with fondness, and anyone who prefers a suburb with a history you can chew on (literally, if you once ordered razor clams). We’ll keep minding the park, the preschool roll call, and our slightly overcooked sense of pride.
