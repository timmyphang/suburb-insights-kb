# Uraidla (NSW)

Slug: `uraidla-nsw`

We wake up here the way you do in small towns: with a plan that involves a hat, a thermos, and the comforting certainty that something public will be happening on a Sunday. We drag ourselves to the markets at the Uraidla Institute on Main Rd (they used to run on the 1st and 3rd Sunday, if you remember), then nod solemnly at the Uraidla Friendly Grocer because yes, they still hold the hall keys and therefore, in effect, run half the town.

Weekends are a low-key relay of good intentions. Families drop toddlers at the Uraidla & Hills Community Kindergarten Playgroup (last updated 11/04/2026 — so yes, it’s still a thing) while the Uraidla & Summertown H & F Society Inc quietly gets on with organising the annual Uraidla Show and the Sustainability Fair from 86 Swamp Rd — uraidlashow.com still tells you when to bring the preserves and the pride.

We’re the kind of place that once got creative with everything: the Uraidla Hotel used to be famous for its market-garden–inspired reno and rusty windmill lights (Julie Peter’s handiwork, as Broadsheet wrote), the neighbouring bakery and converted squash-centre cafe — the Uraidla Republic — once served coffee on vintage crockery, and there was even a little brewery in the bakery with a brewer called Oscar Matthews who used to brew beers you could watch through a glass wall. Lost in a Forest turned a church into a pizza-and-natural-wine hangout with stained-glass sunlight and a mural, which reads like the plot of an indie film we half-star in.

Who would hate living here? If you need 24/7 neon-lit nightlife, three takeaway chains within a five-minute walk and a tram every ten minutes, you’ll loathe the sleepy rhythm: markets, a show that smells of jam competitions and sawdust, and community-run everything. We like that the town can be bossed by the Friendly Grocer and an H & F committee. It keeps us humble.

Who would love it? Families who want a real playgroup, punters who plan their year around the Uraidla Show, lovers of tiny-home charm and hills that look over eucalypts and cherry orchards (as UrbanList once gushed). We’ll probably be here, politely arguing about which month the mulled-wine season actually begins.
