# North Beach (NSW)

Slug: `north-beach-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed, sand in our shoes and opinions about swell size, and head for pole position on the deck at Diggies Cafe — because, as Concrete Playground sagely observed, “pole position is the deck dining area.” We liked that view: the lighthouse, the low tide, the kind of horizon that makes you nod like the ocean’s your aunty who tells tall stories.

For years the local brochure could have been subtitled: “Things you can fall off (intentionally).” Skydive the Beach Wollongong used to point out you could gorge on adrenaline for six minutes, then land on our sand and pretend gravity was on your side. And if you preferred a bed with a sea view and someone else to blame for the morning coffee, Novotel Wollongong Northbeach used to look out over North Beach and “give easy access to local walking trails, swim spots and the area's nightlife,” which we appreciated when we were too tired to walk home.

There isn’t much hot municipal drama in the clippings — no council coups, no one stealing the surf lifesaver’s whistle — which is perhaps our charm. The archive’s whisper of us is more seaside postcard than tabloid scandal: cafes, a hotel, an airport-adjacent way to throw yourself out of a plane and land on soft sand. That’s the extent of our celebrity and we’ll take it.

Who would hate living here? If you need an endless parade of bespoke coffee houses with avant-garde menus, or you’re allergic to the constant possibility that a random tourist might have just done a tandem skydive and now needs directions, then yes, you’ll be miserable. Also, anyone who can’t wake up early enough to watch the lighthouse look dramatic.

Who’d love it here? People who want the sea as a neighbour, breakfast on a deck that knows all your secrets, and a hotel that’s a literal stone’s throw from the beach when you’ve had one too many local sunsets. We’re modest, salty and utterly proud of it — and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
