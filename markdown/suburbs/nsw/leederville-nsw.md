# Leederville (NSW)

Slug: `leederville-nsw`

Back in the day we’d saunter down our compact strip like we owned the place — Luna Leederville’s neon still warm from the night before, the Oxford Street mural by Sarah McCloskey waving us in with more culture than our entire uni degree. The paperwork said “village-like”; we called it a place where you could walk to dinner, a record store, and whatever existential crisis comes with choosing between Pixel Coffee Brewers and Foam Coffee Bar.

We used to spend Saturdays arguing about where to eat — Pinchos or Will Street? Vikka’s pizzas got Time Out praise and, frankly, the rest of us just accepted defeat. The late-night wander would end at Servo in Electric Lane or, if we were feeling historically sentimental, at The Oxford Hotel’s garden bar. Ten and Sen on Newcastle Street were the “new openings” people whispered about when they wanted to sound very now.

If you liked film, we had Luna Leederville for festival season: German Film Festival dates, Jewish International Film Festival screenings, NT Live beaming in West End hits — we weren’t just a strip of cafes; we were where cinema went to feel important. Concrete Playground even points to 741 Newcastle Street for when you needed to give your gelato a mission.

There’s a softer side, too: parents met at West Leederville Playgroup, Coaching Zone used the Loftus Recreation Centre for fitness (with creche facilities, apparently), and The Garden once offered kids-eat-free deals that made dinner feel like community service. Siena’s at 115 Oxford St used to be the local plot twist for kids who liked indoor play areas and parents who liked wine.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe walking, hate choice, or prefer your local strip to be a single bland supermarket aisle, Leedy’s chaos of cafes, tapas, izakaya, and boutique shops will ruin you. For everyone else — film buffs, pizza evangelists, parents who appreciate a play area with their pasta, and anyone who enjoys a village that thinks it’s a metropolis — Leederville was doing us proud. We’ll keep the mural. You keep the scooter.
