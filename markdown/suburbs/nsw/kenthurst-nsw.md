# Kenthurst (NSW)

Slug: `kenthurst-nsw`

You know you’re in Kenthurst when our weekend opener is a sausage sizzle and a photobooth queue longer than the council meeting. We’ve got opinions, we’ve got gardening gloves, and yes — we have feelings about New Line Road.

Mornings here are a carousel of tiny socks and playgroup singalongs. Back in the day our local playgroup met at the Kenthurst Literary Institute (131–135 Kenthurst Rd) on Thursdays, 9:30–11:30 — and if you’ve ever tried to herd small people after that, you’ll understand why the Kenthurst Fair is basically our national sport. The fair, part of the Hills Orange Blossom Festival for more than 40 years, still brings the street parade, bands, stalls, face painting and gardening workshops that make us proud and mildly exhausted.

Speaking of proud: Kenthurst Rotary marked a milestone — the club’s 50th birthday (was set for 6 November) and their 2023/24 Changeover at the Australian Brewery (30 June) are the sort of local achievements we brag about at the sausage sizzle. We’re civic-minded like that.

Now for the petty drama the rest of Sydney pretends not to notice: traffic. As galstoncommunity.com.au reported, thousands of Hills residents, commuters and tradespeople demanded action on New Line Road, Kenthurst and Annangrove Roads because, frankly, we’re tired of gridlock. The council even unanimously supported a notice of motion to seek reclassification and upgrades — including asking to make Annangrove Road an arterial state road and upgrading sections to four lanes. It’s our version of a revolution: tar, chip and bureaucracy.

Who would hate living here? If your life demands 24/7 inner‑city nightlife, artisanal everything, and zero sausage sizzles, Kenthurst will feel like a long country walk with intermittent jazz. Also, if you can’t handle community fetes where bubble acts and live jazz compete for attention, steer clear.

Who would love it? Families hunting for playgroups (hello, Castle Hill Kookaburras at Kenthurst Public School, 111 Kenthurst Road), gardeners, parade-lovers, and anyone who thinks Rotary milestones are worth celebrating. We’re small, slightly congested, and exhausting — and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
