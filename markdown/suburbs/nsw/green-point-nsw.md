# Green Point (NSW)

Slug: `green-point-nsw`

We’re the Green Point that nobody Googles first — the one that gets mistaken for a flea market in Cape Town, a Brighton picnic spot, or an entire Brooklyn neighbourhood with better pizza. We know every mailbox and every patch of curb, and yes, we secretly enjoy the confusion.

Back in the day the internet decided our name had better PR than we did. Time Out wrote about the Green Point Flea Market making a comeback in Cape Town — stalls, antiques, food and all the drama — and Sitchu kept chatting up Green Point Reserve in Brighton as though it were our weekend lawn. Meanwhile, a whole dossier of Greenpoint, Brooklyn guides keeps stealing our thunder: Time Out and Urban List rave about Paulie Gee’s, Frankel’s and Chrissy’s Pizza at 142 Nassau Ave, Broadsheet name‑checks queue‑worthy gelato and old favourites elsewhere in Sydney, and BrooklynBridgeParents was busy listing family amenities like Ferox Athletics and The Program NYC at 255 Java Street. Even MamansBK turned Greenpoint into a parenting hub. None of them are us. We know.

Our morning routine is predictably low-key: we watch delivery drivers puzzle over the GPS, we smile and point them toward “the real” Green Point they were trying to find (or not), and we go back to enjoying the fact that you can still park on our street without a two‑hour prayer. The only local scandal is whether to correct tourists when they ask for bagels, or just let them believe we invented Paulie Gee’s.

Who would hate living here? If your whole identity is built on queue‑worthy gelato, laminated Jamaican patties (we’ll let Broadsheet keep admiring those in Glebe) or a waterfront skyline view — and you expect it at house‑price levels — you’ll be miserable. You’ll resent the calm, the lack of a “scene,” and the way our postcode politely refuses to trend.

Who would love it here? People who enjoy being under‑celebrated, who prefer a genuine lawn to a curated piazza, and who get a kick out of watching strangers slowly realise they’re in the wrong Green Point. We’re low‑fuss, quietly smug, and impossible to hype — and that’s exactly why we like it.
