# Thirroul (NSW)

Slug: `thirroul-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed to the smell of sea and whatever smoky wood‑oven magic Ciro’s was pulling from 1 Raymond Road, then stroll down palm‑lined streets as every guide kept reminding visitors that Thirroul is a seaside pocket that time forgot. We know the routine: a meander past where Black Market Roasters set up a second place, a detour to where Frank’s Wild Years used to spin vinyl and pour cocktails just off Lawrence Hargrave Drive, and a peek at the sand where Flanagans Dining Room served seafood right on the beach in the holiday‑movie way Broadsheet loved to write about.

We’re not big on pretending we’re Sydney — Concrete Playground and Time Out both treated us like the romantic day‑trip you catch on the train or the Grand Pacific Drive — framed by the escarpment and ocean, with Sublime Point walking track and the Thirroul–Wollongong bike path for anyone who likes their cardio scenic and their ego intact. UrbanList even put our pizzas on a pedestal; Franco Pizza Bar and Ciro’s hauled the good stuff south when the city tired of its own trends.

There’s no petty council scandal to report here in the clips we have — no kerfuffle, just quiet reputation building: surf, vinyl, pizzas that make you forgive the traffic, and a community co‑op (Flame Tree Community Co‑Op) being smug about less packaging. Time Out and Concrete Playground both noted film crews liked our palm trees too — our Headlands Hotel and petrol station got some screen time for ITV dramas, which frankly made the place feel momentarily famous.

Who would hate it here? People who crave neon nightlife, vast retail monocultures, or a suburb without a single boutique record‑shop courtyard. Also anyone allergic to beaches, guitars and slightly smug food writing.

Who would love it here? The weekend pizza pilgrim, the person who judges a town by its vinyl racks and ocean pools, the hiker who thinks Sublime Point is a religion, and anyone who still believes a day‑trip can change your life. We’ll keep the palm trees. You keep arriving by train.
