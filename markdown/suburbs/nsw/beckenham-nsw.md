# Beckenham (NSW)

Slug: `beckenham-nsw`

We’ll be honest: we live in Beckenham, NSW, but most of our press clippings are from other Beckenhams — which is peak Beckenham energy and frankly quite on brand. Back in the day Broadsheet, Time Out and Urban List were obsessing over a café called Fika on Brix — the sort of place you’d find in Mills Park Recreation Centre at 86 Brixton Street — and we still reminisce like it was our local institution. Imelda Latief’s first café venture got name-checked for brioche French toast, weekend pancake stacks and cheeky things like bulgogi beef hash and chicken katsu bao. Beans were by Grand Central, the alfresco was pet-friendly and there was a playground, so parents could pretend the pancakes were for the kids.

We say “back in the day” because all of the pieces in our file are older — pre-2024 — so this is nostalgic roast, not a current guide. Time Out loved the signature brioche. Urban List praised layered drip cakes, garlic mushrooms on toasted rye and a lamington stack pancake that sounded like a dare. If that sounds like a menu dreamt up by a committee of sugar and Scandinavian influencers, that’s because it probably was.

There’s no petty council drama in our clippings (shocking for a suburb), so the local tiff section is thin. Instead, here’s a stately observation: our Beckenham identity is wonderfully porous. Other Beckenhams pop up in the press too — The Birdwood at 76 Malcolm Avenue in Beckenham, Christchurch gets a shout for woodfired pizza, and there’s a theatre costume vibe via Malthouse Costumes — which proves one thing: the name “Beckenham” comes with built‑in cultural versatility.

Who would hate living here? People who think brunch is a personality trait and anyone allergic to layered drip cakes or playgrounds with a view. Who would love it? Parents who need a pet-friendly alfresco and a playground of excuses, brunch hunters who worship creative pancake stacks, and those who enjoy being part of a suburb that’s happy to borrow its PR from Perth and Christchurch and still call it home.
