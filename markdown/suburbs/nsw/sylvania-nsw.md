# Sylvania (NSW)

Slug: `sylvania-nsw`

We roll over Tom Uglys Bridge like it’s a ritual — weekend sun, traffic light arias, and the eternal debate: do we queue for nostalgia or for lunch? Back in the day, we’d pull up to Paul’s Famous Hamburgers off the Princes Highway and accept that a constant line out the door was part of the flavour. Paul’s had been doing “The Works” since 1957 — beef, fried egg, bacon, cheese and pineapple — and, as Time Out and Broadsheet reminded us, it was a mandatory pilgrimage for anyone pretending to be on a Sydney road-trip.

If we weren’t licking milkshake froth from our chins (Time Out’s old-school vanilla milkshake, anyone?), we’d eyeball the baby-blue flash of Elate Gyros + Souvlaki Bar further down the Princes Highway — Urban List said their gyro came with a bit of “magic sauce,” which felt almost scandalous next to Paul’s wholesome anarchy.

We’ve always had an oddball claim to fame too. Junkee still remembers Sylvania Waters as Australia’s first proper reality-TV spat — we were on telly before telly knew what to do with suburbia. That legacy is the kind of bittersweet reputation that makes you grin and roll your eyes in equal measure.

Who would hate living here? Anyone allergic to queues, nostalgia or slightly giddy local fame. Parents who need a picnic with built-in conveniences might grizzle — Canberra Road Reserve’s 2022 playground upgrade gifted us rope bridges, big spiral slides, a scooter track and a basket swing, but no public toilets to complete the fantasy. Also, if you despise runners, dodging Concept Track Club Sylvania Waters sessions (Urban List praised it) will test your patience.

Who’d love it here? Families who actually use the Sylvania Community Centre on Canberra Rd, mums booking Santa at Southgate Sylvania, burger pilgrims who treasure a greasy, generous throwback, and anyone who enjoys a weekend that feels like a low-budget road movie. We’ll keep waving from the Princes Highway, clutching our chips — embarrassed, proud and utterly local.
