# Helensburgh (NSW)

Slug: `helensburgh-nsw`

All the write-ups we found are a bit nostalgic, so forgive us if this reads like a postcard someone left in a dusty tunnel. We know Helensburgh like the back of our gumboot: the abandoned 1880s rail tunnel that people whisper about on the train is the one with the glow worms — the Helensburgh Glow Worm Tunnel — and yes, the internet has been cheekily stealing our vibe ever since.

On a weekend we’d pile into the car and walk the Glow Worm Tunnel Walking Track until the light show started; the rule, according to the guides, was to switch off torches and wait for those bioluminescent pinpricks to do their thing. If you camped nearby at Newnes Campground you could poke around shale oil ruins by day and then creep into the tunnel at dusk like polite, well-behaved goblins. Dharawal National Park’s eucalypt and shale country is the sort of backdrop that makes city Instagram captions write themselves.

We used to brag — and still do, quietly — that even city cocktail-makers borrowed our aesthetic. Urban List said “Nocturnal Desire at Pleasure Club” took inspiration from our Glow Worm Tunnel; apparently we’re now an ingredient in someone else’s cocktail. Meanwhile, ghost-hunting types have explored the Old Helensburgh Railway Tunnels; Junkee listed them among Australia’s spooky spots, which sent half the armchair paranormalists down our lanes for a listen.

We’ll also remind you that the Sri Venkateswara Canteen was the place locals mentioned in Broadsheet: the Hindu temple’s weekend canteen served savoury Indian doughnuts, dosa, biryani and deep-fried flat breads — a bit of culinary history tied to a 1978 land purchase and a first consecration in 1985. And for hands-on animal energy, Concrete Playground wrote about Symbio Wildlife Park at 7–11 Lawrence Hargrave Drive, with behind-the-scenes encounters that used to let you meet meerkats and red pandas up close.

Who would hate living here? People who need constant latte options and avoid dark, slightly damp tunnels at all costs. Who would love it? Hikers, campers, families who want wildlife encounters, and anyone who prefers a local glow to city neon. We’ll keep the tunnels lit — metaphorically — and let Sydney keep mixing cocktails inspired by our backyard.
