# Narara (NSW)

Slug: `narara-nsw`

Back in the day we’d call Narara “the town that runs on steam and sausage sizzles” and honestly, we miss the romance of it. Our first-Saturday ritual used to be the Narara Mini Steam Trains (11am–4pm, weather permitting) — five bucks of joy? Nah, rides used to be $2.50, kids under three free, enclosed shoes required and the whole affair fuelled by a sausage sizzle. It was gloriously small-scale and stubbornly wholesome.

For the parents among us, the Narara Valley Baptist Church playgroup at 149 Narara Valley Drive used to be the weekly lifeline through school terms — craft, circle time and morning tea for about $20 per family per term. If you wanted rescued goats and budget-friendly animal interactions, Fur and Feathers Fun Park (out the back of Narara Nursery) used to babysit the tiny zoologists for around four bucks a head.

Our community safety flex used to be the Narara Rural Fire Service “Get Ready” weekend — fire trucks, face painting, a jumping castle and, yes, another free sausage sizzle so you could learn about bushfire prep while chewing.

We used to have help on hand at the Narara Community Centre (2–12 Pandala Rd, opposite Narara Valley High School) where Community SOS provided practical stuff: financial counselling, the No Interest Loan Scheme, EAPA energy bill assistance, tax help, counselling and room hire. In short: if life got messy, there were places that helped.

If you hate quiet parks and small-town logistics, you’ll loathe us: Sensory Park on Showground Road (opened December 2015) and the nearby bike track aren’t exactly Champagne nightlife. And if your vibe requires nightly cocktail-bars-in-every-postcode, remember we’re only moments from Wyoming Shopping Centre and Gosford CBD, with Narara and Gosford train stations and the M1 for when civilisation calls — but it’s a commute.

Who’ll love us? Families who like a proper playgroup, folks who appreciate a $2.50 nostalgia train ride, people who value hands-on community services and anyone after dog-friendly green space. We’re modest, a little earnest and very much our own thing — and yes, we’d happily trade one CBD cocktail bar for another sausage sizzle any day.
