# Hoxton Park (NSW)

Slug: `hoxton-park-nsw`

We’ll admit it: our Saturday starts with a mission, not a latte. Back in the day when Savers landed in Hoxton Park Central — that enormous 1,590‑sqm superstore that Time Out wrote up as Sydney’s first Savers outpost — we’d turn up like magpies. One hundred thousand pre‑loved items, 25,000 new drops a week; you could spend a full arvo digging for gems and still argue you’d done your civic duty by diverting textiles from landfill. Yes, we know it sounds like thrift-store gladiatorial combat, and yes, we cheerfully bring snacks.

After that, if you needed to defrost, there was the LCC Ice Rink on Hoxton Park Road — 424/458, according to Urban List — where after‑dark ice discos and family sessions made us feel marginally more cultured than our shopping trolley habits suggested. For nicer walks that don’t involve sifting through vintage T‑shirts, the Great West Walk threads through the Western Sydney Parklands to Hoxton Park; we’ve clocked Breakfast Creek, Nurragingy Reserve and the views toward Blacktown Olympic Park and felt oddly heroic for leaving the car.

Local life reads like a directory with personality. Mumspages reminds us we’re great for families: netball, karate, jiu‑jitsu classes, party providers and kids’ sports all get bandied about. The Hoxton Park Tigers Cricket Club at 34–36 First Avenue gives our small people somewhere to learn how to celebrate loudly and wear white awkwardly. Total Tools and the Bottlemart cellars make it clear we can fix things and then celebrate doing so.

There’s not much in the way of scandal in the clips we’ve got — just a suburb that’s quietly doing things: a huge thrift store, an ice rink, walking trails and the usual parish‑and‑club rhythm (even Copernicus Cafe and Delicatessen over in Prestons gets a nod for paczki in Broadsheet’s notes).

Who would hate it here? If you hunger for boutique laneway cafés and curated minimalism, Hoxton Park’s unapologetic practicality will sting. Who’d love it? The treasure hunters, the families, the people who like long walks turned into excuses for morning snacks, and anyone who believes a weekend should end with something useful bought for $5.

We live here, we own the queues at Savers, and we’ll defend our ice disco dance moves to the death.
