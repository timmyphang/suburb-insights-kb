# Adelaide (NSW)

Slug: `adelaide-nsw`

We wake up slow, because we can — Adelaide isn’t pretending to be everywhere at once. Back in the day the travel guides called it a “perfect weekend getaway”; these days most of our clout arrives in festival-sized bursts. We used to start Mad March by swinging past Adelaide Central Market (which, yes, was already earmarked for a $600 million Market Square facelift — Sitchu had the traders list: craft beer, matcha, crepes and South American pantry staples) and end the day at Glenelg Beach or up at Mount Lofty Botanic Gardens because Time Out once told tourists those are the moves.

Weekends read like a curated feed: Broadsheet taught visitors “How to spend 24 hours in Adelaide” and Urban List insisted the restaurant scene had “skyrocketed” — and we’ll take that, but only after our second coffee. Concrete Playground’s weekender guide swore Mad March is the thing, and PedestrianTV kept the receipts when our festivals went full-on soap opera: Adelaide Festival apologised, Writers’ Week was cancelled after board members quit, and the chair Tracey Whiting resigned. Drama, meet the place that supplies the picnic rug.

Who would hate living here? If you need perpetual, relentless hype and a skyline that never sleeps, Adelaide will bore you awake. If your life plan involves being on every “best new” list every fortnight, you’ll find our pace almost suspiciously civilised.

Who would love it here? Food obsessives — Broadsheet, Time Out and Urban List have all been making lists about our eateries and markets for years — and festival lovers who treat Mad March like a pilgrimage. Families get a shout too: the mums’ networks and Kids in Adelaide keep the calendar full of things to do for the little ones. Wine fans and day-trippers will point at Jauma Farm and the Clare Valley nods you can find in the guides.

We’re quietly proud of our 29th-place glow-up in Time Out’s 2026 world city list — and honestly, we’d rather be known for good markets, stubbornly excellent festivals, and a city that knows how to simmer rather than scorch. Come for the events, stay for the calm.
