# Vermont (NSW)

Slug: `vermont-nsw`

Saturday morning in Vermont, NSW? We’re standing at the intersection of “we live here” and “the internet thinks we’re someone else,” coffee-less but loud with local pride.

Back in the day the only press our name got was scattered across other Vermonts — Broadsheet once wrote about a Vermont General Store (Melbourne), and even flagged a Vermont-based brand, Bravo Sierra, in a national natural-deodorant roundup. Concrete Playground reminds us with weird humility that the musical Hadestown first premiered in Vermont in 2006. Urban List will happily send you to 955 S Vermont Avenue for Parks BBQ — if you were aiming for Los Angeles instead of our cul‑de‑sac.

Our weekend routine? We don’t have a media blitz to boast about, so we make our own. We scroll through vermontmoms.com for seasonal rituals (yes, there was an “Ultimate Guide to ALL the Vermont Fall Fun”) and trade tips the old-fashioned way: over fences, in school pickup lines and on community noticeboards that never made it into a national guide.

Nobody here is asking for fame. If you showed up expecting a glossy feature and a neon marquee, you’ll be disappointed: Broadsheet’s listing for that Vermont General Store notes it’s permanently closed — which is a sentence that doubles as our temperamental local economy’s theme music.

Who would hate living here? People who need constant validation from the internet, who get testy when a search for our postcode delivers a Broadway-origin anecdote about Hadestown or a deodorant round‑up instead of a real estate listing. Tourists hunting for Parks BBQ at 955 S Vermont Avenue will be even more annoyed.

Who would love it? Families who read vermontmoms.com and actually follow the “fall fun” advice; theatre nerds who casually drop “Hadestown premiered in Vermont” at dinner parties and watch jaws drop; locals who like a quiet suburb that owns its mix‑ups with a grin. We’ll keep our community noticeboards, our leaf‑peeping tips and our small‑town humour — and let the rest of the internet keep borrowing our name.
