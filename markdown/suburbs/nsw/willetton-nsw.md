# Willetton (NSW)

Slug: `willetton-nsw`

Back in the day we’d call Willetton “sleepy” and everyone would scoff — then someone opened a noodle shop that did nearly 400 bowls a day and we all had to admit we were being unfair.

We are the people who’d drop the kids at Willetton Playgroup on Kingfisher Loop for the 9–11am weekday sessions, then wander over to Burrendah Boulevard like it’s our own food crawl. For years our claims to culinary fame read like a very specific passport: Enak Kopitiam with its kaya toast and teh tarik queues, Wang Xiang Noodle (Albee Huong and KokHau Chee opened it in late 2024 and yes, it famously shifted mountains of sang nyuk mian), Asian Bistro by Fugu 8 bringing Katong laksa and mee pok to a 60-seat corner, even Ghee Brothers popping up in the conversation when 2025 declared itself the year of Asian breakfasts.

Our strip has always been gloriously indecisive: Ageto Ramen at 4, 41 Burrendah Blvd for matcha ramen one week, Silver Sushi on a middling Tuesday, Gusto Gelato setting up shop and ruining our diets with pistachio. Ginza Nana Alley at 43B Burrendah Boulevard used to be the alley that made us feel slightly cosmopolitan. There was even Kalahari A Taste Of Africa and Patti’s Kitchen serving idli and Chicken Kothu — proof we do variety with gusto.

Petty local drama? We argue about sport like it’s theology. The Willetton Tigers’ mixed start to the 2025 NBL1 season still gives us something to talk about in the supermarket queue, and we swell with smugness remembering Willetton Senior High School turning up third in the state. The Dragons at 58 Burrendah Boulevarde give the cricket club something to brag about on the weekend.

Who would hate living here? If you need constant novelty or want to be the only person in a café, you’ll be miserable — we have queues, we have choices, we have a HomeCo. at 45 Burrendah Boulevard with Pepper Lunch, Boost Juice, Muffin Break and Hoyts keeping things loud and very suburban.

Who loves it? Families, food nerds who like a good queue, and anyone who enjoys a suburb that quietly punches above its postcode. We’ll keep our hawker-style nostalgia and our slightly chaotic Burrendah strip — and yes, we’ll see you at the next Tigers game.
