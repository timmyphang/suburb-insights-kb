# Somers (NSW)

Slug: `somers-nsw`

We still wake up to the same thing: that long, slow ribbon of sand the papers keep pointing at. Somers Beach stretches for three kilometres along the Mornington Peninsula and, as Concrete Playground put it, it's our holiday family favourite — a little more low‑key than Portsea and Sorrento, with sleepier surrounds and a usually kid‑friendly swell. We like that they say it like it's a compliment.

Our weekend ritual is gloriously simple. We wander down to the beach, try not to get sand in the thermos, and watch the tide do whatever tides do while the kids from Somers Primary School learn to be exactly the kind of sticky, sand‑covered people only a peninsula childhood produces. Sitchu's suburb notes kindly remind anyone thinking of moving here that Somers is part of the peninsula circuit with nearby schools like Somerville Secondary College and Mornington Secondary College doing the heavy lifting for teenagers who need more than beach naps.

There isn't much juicy local scandal in our press clippings — and that's the point. The articles we have are nostalgic postcards, not tabloidy exposes. If you're scrolling for council meltdowns or nightlife breakdowns, you'll be disappointed. The narrative is: sleepy beach, family spot, walkable coast. We own the quiet. We own the calm. We also own the fact that being quieter than Portsea is, technically, a flex.

Who would hate living here? Night‑owls and anyone who thinks every suburb needs to be a shopping mall at 11pm. If you measure happiness in late‑night bars and neon, Somers will feel like a permanent Sunday afternoon — which is to say, boring as sin for some and bliss for others.

Who would love it? Families who want the beach in walking distance, people who like horizons that actually go somewhere, anyone who prefers a long stretch of sand to a short, expensive photogenic jetty. Back in the day the guides called Somers a place to unwind; for us local weirdos, that description still fits perfectly.
