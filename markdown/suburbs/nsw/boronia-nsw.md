# Boronia (NSW)

Slug: `boronia-nsw`

We wake up in Boronia and the grand plan for the day is modest: find shade, chase a park, and possibly judge a scone from afar.

Mornings here used to revolve around places that sound like they belonged to someone’s tasteful aunt: Concrete Playground once wrote up Boronia Kitchen at 152 Pittwater Road in Gladesville — led by ex‑ARIA chef Simon Sandall and famed for rotisserie‑style chicken — so we’d smugly tell visitors we used to have dinner options with provenance. Time Out, meanwhile, gave the Boronia Tea Room a thumbs up for its veranda and “light and fluffy” scones, so we imagine ourselves very civil while eating very crumbly pastry.

Weekends are simpler and wetter: Boronia Park — the one in Epping — is the spine of our social life. Sitchu’s Epping profile name‑checks Boronia Park, West Epping Park and Forest Park as playground-rich spots, and Time Out even said the Epping Night Market was set to take over Boronia Park on the last Friday of every month. So our idea of nightlife is a market lantern, a paper plate and someone’s artisan dumplings.

There’s not much of the soap opera the internet likes — no council coups or scandalous coffee wars in the clippings we’ve got. The media mostly treated us like a map pin: “nice parks, quaint tea rooms, and a respectable all‑day diner.” Which is fine. We’re low‑maintenance.

Who would hate living here? If you need a constant rotation of buzzy laneway bars, nightlife that ends after midnight or a streetscape that changes every fortnight, this place will humble you. We’re more veranda than vibey.

Who would love it? Families and anyone who enjoys parks with actual playgrounds, a monthly market that turns the oval into a community piazza, and people who appreciate a proper tea‑room moment. As Time Out and Sitchu quietly agreed, we’re pleasant, parky and just classy enough to make you feel superior about your picnic blanket.
