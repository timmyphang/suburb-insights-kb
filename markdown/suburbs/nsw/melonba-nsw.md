# Melonba (NSW)

Slug: `melonba-nsw`

We live in Melonba — you know, that quiet pocket of Marsden Park everyone’s Googling when they want a house with swagger and no history. If you scroll back far enough (and by far enough we mean the dusty corners of real-estate listings), Sitchu once crowned 32 Versace Crescent as “Architectural Elegance” on a 321sqm corner block. Back in the day that was our billboard: double-story, neat lines, the sort of place estate agents whisper about when they think nobody’s listening.

Our morning routine is gloriously low-key: we stand outside and admire the newness. There’s nothing to queue for, no heritage plaque to argue over, just fresh render and the faint smell of eucalyptus someone planted recently as part of the landscaping plan. The street is one of those Marsden Park corridors the listing called “fastest growing” — which is developer-speak for: we’ve had a lot of change in a short time and yes, the council will need to pick up the tab for new bins.

Petty local drama? Look, the drama here is existential. Are we a suburb or a curated collection of display homes? Sitchu’s wording gave us permission to be proud, and also to feel slightly nervous when we see another “double-story” pop up two doors down. It’s less about who’s at war with whom and more about competing for corner-block real estate and trying to keep the neighbourhood from becoming a brochure.

Who would hate it here: anyone who wants leafy lanes, a post office that’s been open since before smartphones, or the kind of local lore that involves more than a listing headline. If you want faded pub signage and a roundabout that everyone knows a story about, Melonba will cold-shoulder you.

Who would love it here: people who like their lifestyle neat, new and architecturally inclined — buyers attracted to a 321sqm corner block and a double-story that promises low-maintenance living. We’re proud of our fresh edges and our “quiet pocket” vibes. Just don’t ask us for a cafe recommendation; our cultural guide is a real-estate agent’s brochure and, honestly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
