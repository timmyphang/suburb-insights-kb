# Hawthorn East (NSW)

Slug: `hawthorn-east-nsw`

We’ll start with a confession: we still get nostalgic walking down Camberwell Road, as if the entire suburb is one long, tasteful Instagram carousel. Back in the day — and by that we mean the clippings we could find in the archives — we’d be queued outside Light Years, queuing for their soft-shell crab burger (yes, the guides name-checked it), then wander past East End’s converted 1930s auction house to admire a pizza named after Max Gawn. There haven’t been any recent write-ups in our packet of clippings, so indulge us in some heritage-guided nostalgia.

Weekend ritual, as the columns told it: Rumour Has It was the bright, airy spot on Monteath Ave where you could have breakfast all day; Lights In The Attic did its Asian-spun thing on Camberwell Road; and St. Cloud Eating House — the warehouse conversion on Burwood Road — was doing rooftop gardens and, in 2023, a menu refresh under chef Jerry Mai. For the bookish among us, Readings was still the place to escape the scroll. For the person who needs to justify their life choices with carbs, there was Hank’s Bagelry on Burwood Road and Pizza Religion for the pizza purists.

Local landmarks we whisper about with a wink: the Auburn Hotel, the historic pub that’s been around since 1888 and, per a Broadsheet first-look, once underwent a big expansion to add a 230-seat pavilion, beer garden, kids’ playground and a shiny woodfired pizza oven. Little Lon Distilling Co. sits in its 1877 red-brick cottage on Anderson Road, pouring gin with a side of Melbourne history — the sort of sentence a guidebook loves.

Who would hate living here? People allergic to character: anyone who can’t handle heritage auction houses becoming wine bars, pubs that double as community centres, or a genuine rooftop garden above your pho. Also, if you despise having options for pizza, wine, books and bagels within a few tram stops, walk on.

Who would love it? The people who like their suburbs with a rooftop, a wine list, a woodfired oven and a bookshop. We’ll keep repeating the old guidebook chorus: quiet, leafy, and annoyingly good at feeding you.
