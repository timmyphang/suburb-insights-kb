# Bellfield (NSW)

Slug: `bellfield-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean the only stories we could find in the archive — Bellfield’s claim to fame wasn’t a buzzy strip of brunch joints, it was more of the outdoorsy, community-plot variety. We know this because the internet keeps reminding us.

We start the weekend like proper locals: we pretend we’re heading for adventure. As Urban List once name-checked Lake Bellfield for kayaking, brown-trout fishing, horse-riding and glamping, we nod sagely and reach for our thermos (or whatever makes us feel outdoorsy). Concrete Playground has us on the map too — the Grampians’ Peaks Trail even takes in Bellfield Lake, and readers have voted the Lake Bellfield toilets “loo with a view.” It’s the sort of unsolicited compliment we savour.

Back when lifestyle sites cared, Farm Raiser and the Bellfield Community Garden in Banyule were turning urban plots into something wholesome — veggie patches, pizza ovens and pop-up workshops — part of Open Gardens Victoria’s celebrations. As Concrete Playground reported, we’re the sort of place that grows basil with pride and hosts a communal oven that knows more about fermentation than half our neighbours.

There used to be whispers of new housing glamour — Sitchu listed a “brand new 3 bedroom luxury design” at 5/281 Bell Street — proof that if you squint, suburbia can pose as boutique. MumsPages kept the practical side covered with listings for body-shaping and pre-school dance classes, and Playgroup Victoria’s PlayConnect+ PDFs have our playgroups pencilled in for anyone who appreciates coordinated chaos and colouring-in.

Who would hate living here? People who need a permanent city skyline in their Instagram feed, or anyone who suspects a pizza oven is an insufficient cultural touchstone. Who would love it? Gardeners, bushwalking types, families who value a decent playgroup and anyone who judges a suburb by its community garden rather than its cocktail list.

We’ll keep tending our plots and polishing the loo view. For now, Bellfield’s vibe is low-key, green and quietly proud — just the way we like it.
