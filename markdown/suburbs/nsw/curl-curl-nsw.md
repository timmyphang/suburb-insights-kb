# Curl Curl (NSW)

Slug: `curl-curl-nsw`

Back in the day (and by that we mean the only recent thing in the file was a couple of house tours), we used to show out-of-towners the same script: stagger down the Curl Curl Boardwalk, stop at McKillop Park lookout, then argue politely about whether North Curl Curl is “the perfect Sydney beach” like Broadsheet once said. It’s got the lagoon, the little dog beach, the sheltered corner and that reliable surf — and, crucially, a rock pool you can pretend is your private lap lane when the swell makes the ocean behave like a drama queen.

Our weekend rhythm used to be embarrassingly wholesome. A curl down the coastal walk to Freshwater, a loungey gawk at the South Curl Curl Rockpool (yes, the one with the century-old dividing wall and the 50-metre lane), and a detour past spots Broadsheet and Concrete Playground used to gush about. Staple’s north-ward bakery was previewed in Broadsheet for its stone-ground sourdoughs — we queued like it was a civic duty. Pilu at Freshwater got the long-lunch treatment in profiles; Muchacha at 3/142 Pitt Road used to get the nod when we wanted something a little Californian-kitsch-meets-Mex.

We still meet at the practical bits of town: playgroups at the Curl Curl Youth and Community Centre on 242 Abbott Road, the Running Stars group that used to gather at John Fisher Park at 7:30am on Sundays, and the monthly beach cleans organised by the Curl Curl Community and Surfrider Foundation — first Sundays, tide depending. There was even a headline-grabbing moment: Time Out reported packages washing up on our stretch of coast once, because apparently the sea has its own sense of mischief.

Who would hate living here? People who want silence at 6am, who prefer to never see sand in their shoes, or who think a community run is a suspicious cult. Who would love it? Families, swimmers, and anyone who likes to live where design writers from Sitchu can swoon over “resort-style” homes and where you can walk from rock pool to lagoon without feeling like you’ve left civilisation.

We’re a bit smug about it, but only because the sea does the actual hard work.
