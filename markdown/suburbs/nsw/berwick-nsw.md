# Berwick (NSW)

Slug: `berwick-nsw`

Berwick, NSW — we know every corner of this place, even if the internet keeps confusing us with its Victorian cousin. Back in the day our Sunday mornings were a very specific ritual: a stroll to Akoonah Park Market (Time Out says it’s held every Sunday), a dash past the Old Cheese Factory’s Springfield Homestead and a quick, guilty stop at To Be Frank on 14 Adams Lane for the sort of croissant people brag about (Sitchu had them in the best-croissants roundup).

We’ve watched the hometown dining scene swivel between proud locals and headline seekers. MPD Steak Kitchen opened here as a glossy, Zagame-affiliated entry in the suburbs (Concrete Playground noted the Victor and Robert Zagame link), and Two Fat Indians at 84 Bemersyde Drive was always the curry stalwart on the list (Concrete Playground again). Then there was Munchies — when its Clyde Road Berwick site closed in February 2023 it left a hole in our late-night plans that Broadsheet still mourns.

Clover Cottage Estate was that grand old thing that went quiet for a decade and, according to Sitchu, was preparing to wake up again — because obviously our suburb does nostalgia well. The Berwick Neighbourhood Centre has long been the practical side of us: kinder, kids’ programs and community classes across 112 High Street and Timbarra on Parkhill Drive (their own site lays it all out). And if you were a parent trying to survive a pub dinner, Urban List’s shout-out to the Berwick Spring Hotel’s warehouse-sized Kids Planet made us smugly grateful.

For a while we even borrowed a bit of spa-culture cred from the wider world — Broadsheet and Concrete Playground wrote plenty about Sense of Self (Freya Berwick and Mary Minas) and their bathhouse plans, even a Sydney outpost pencilled in for 2025, which made us feel momentarily cosmopolitan.

Who would hate living here? Nightlife chasers who need wings at 3am (Munchies’ Clyde Road closure hit them hard), and people allergic to playground-sized pubs. Who would love it? Families, history nerds who like 1860s cheese factories, and anyone who enjoys a suburb that reinvents itself one reopening at a time. We’ll be here, holding a market bag and pretending we meant to move back.
