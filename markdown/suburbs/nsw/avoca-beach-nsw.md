# Avoca Beach (NSW)

Slug: `avoca-beach-nsw`

We are that sight on Avoca Drive at 6:15am — one hand on an oat-milk flat white from the little beachfront kiosk, the other unclipping Dobby the Cavoodle from the Subaru Outback roof racks while a Volvo XC90 idly livestreams the surf for Instagram. We treat our Lululemon leggings like a uniform and our Alo pair like an Olympic medal. We wax our boards more faithfully than our cars. We call avocado toast “authentic coastal cuisine” with complete sincerity.

Our mornings follow a ritual: coffee from the kiosk, leash Dobby, scroll reels, park the Outback in whatever shade we can find. Weekends become a performance. Sunrise surf, farmers’ market with a reusable tote (no single-use in our vocabulary), brunch at the beachfront café for a single-origin flat white, afternoon dog-walk along the rock shelves, and a bonfire at night where the wine is in reusable glasses and the playlists are curated by someone who does “sound healing” on Sundays. We volunteer at the Surf Life Saving Club and post an ‘ocean therapy’ reel every Friday like clockwork.

The village keeps itself entertained with small wars. There is carnage at the beach carpark on sunny Saturdays: three Subaru Outbacks, a Hilux and an old Commodore all jockeying for the last shady spot while a surf-school parent holds up the queue with a string of wetsuits. There is the weekly-market turf war that has seen two factions claim the same patch of grass on Avoca Drive — acrimonious but with excellent baked goods. And then, of course, the pizza truck saga: two very serious letters to Central Coast Council — both penned in green ink — arguing whether a mobile pizza van is “street food” or “an affront to brunch culture.” We also argue about bins on school-drop-off morning and who owns the coveted corner of the cul-de-sac for weekend parking.

We are not above petty strata politics over who gets the storage cage in the complex. We take cafe loyalty personally. We will camp out a table at the beachfront kiosk and defend it with passive-aggressive eye contact.

Who would hate living here? Dan, 28, the investment-banking-in-the-city type who thinks “weekend warrior” means a rotary phone and silence. He hates sand, detests communal small talk, wears tailored suits to cafes and expects carpets that never see a granule of grit. Avoca’s salt-stained, chatty, communal chaos would wilt him faster than a boutique succulent.

Who would love it? Emma, 34: yoga teacher, surf volunteer, driving an Outback with roof racks, Cavoodle named Dobby, shopping at Lululemon, drinking oat flat whites like a life choice — she would adore every sun-bleached, latte-sipped minute. Welcome.
