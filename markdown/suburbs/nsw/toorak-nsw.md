# Toorak (NSW)

Slug: `toorak-nsw`

Back in the day we’d stroll down Toorak Road and pretend the rest of Melbourne couldn’t see us inhaling prestige. We know every terrace, every hedge trimmed to within a millimetre, and where the best second‑hand Hermès once surfaced at the Toorak Opportunity Shop—Time Out used to brag about that little conspiracy of luxe castoffs.

Mornings were for slow walks through Toorak Village: we used to duck into Fleur Espresso on 1A Grange Road when it was new, and pick up a ready-made rescue for dinner from Lafayette Fine Foods at 429 Toorak Road on the nights the mansion demanded formality. Broadsheet catalogued our niceties for years — Bistro Thierry and its boeuf bourguignon were the sort of reassuring continental promises that kept us in tweed.

There was always something deliciously performative about us. Broadsheet and Concrete Playground even watched as Saint Haven — Tim Gurner’s private wellness project — planned a 1600‑square‑metre outpost in Toorak Village at 505 Toorak Road. We nodded politely and pretended to be surprised. Token and other Toorak Road entrants kept the dining theatre alive, and Broadsheet remembered when Made in Casa moved into Toorak Village — house‑made pasta and all — for those evenings we wanted to be seen as having exquisite taste and a booked table.

Our petty dramas are mostly archival now: Junkee kept a highlight reel of viral moments (you remember the tyreless Range Rover footage and the headlines) and Pedestrian TV told us Daniel Radcliffe once flipped a $2M Toorak flat to his parents. It’s the kind of gossip we file under “Did they, or did they not?”

Who would hate it here? Anyone who likes surprises, cheap rent, or parking that isn’t a calculated negotiation. If you like graffiti, communal laundries, or being late to everything because the tram’s blocked, move along.

Who would love it? Families who covet The Pottery at Geelong Grammar’s Toorak campus, people who like wine bars with encyclopaedic lists (Toorak Cellars got its share of praise), and anyone who delights in a postcode that, for better or worse, reads like an invitation. We aren’t subtle. We don’t apologise. We’re Toorak: prim, polished, and quietly pleased with ourselves.
