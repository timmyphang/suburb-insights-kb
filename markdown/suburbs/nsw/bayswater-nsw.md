# Bayswater (NSW)

Slug: `bayswater-nsw`

Back in the day, Bayswater Road was the kind of strip that made you choose sides: were we here for a piano bar that doubled as a shrine to nautical kitsch, or for the neon relics that inevitably ended up at auction? We remember walking past World Bar (24 Bayswater Rd) on a Wednesday and thinking, yes, The Wall will live forever — until it didn’t.  

A typical Saturday used to look like this: we’d wobble down from The Bayswater Sydney after checking in for a cheeky laundry cycle, detour past Thirsty Bird where the Los Pollos Hermanos pop‑up once turned lunch into television fan service (Shop 3, 2–14 Bayswater Road), and watch the crowds thin into The Hook — Concrete Playground’s ode to Bayswater Road’s former glory, all oysters and maritime cheek. Bones even packed up its original Bayswater Road ramen counter and slithered to Darlinghurst, because of course it did.

The petty local drama? Oh, we’ve had a catalogue. Club Candys copped a 72‑hour shutdown after an NSW police raid — Keep Sydney Open called it a waste of taxpayer money — and yes, the one‑punch headlines on Bayswater Road made us roll our eyes and clutch our takeaway dumplings. Meanwhile, the Kings X Coca‑Cola sign sold for a cool $100K (all proceeds went to charity, if you’re keeping score), and property owners were quietly humming about deals that might see Darlinghurst Road and Bayswater Road redeveloped. Junkee had thoughts. So did we.

Who would hate living here: people who enjoy silence, predictable bedtimes, or not explaining to visitors that yes, the heritage hotel in the movies was the Royal Eagle. This place wears its history and its hangovers on its sleeve.  

Who would love it: anyone who misses a skyline of neon and sticky‑floor folklore, who adores the idea that a pop‑up can briefly turn a lunch service into a pilgrimage, or who appreciates a piano bar that remembers its own punchlines. For years the local pages on Broadsheet and Concrete Playground tried to catalogue it all — messy, theatrical, and unashamedly loud — and honestly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
