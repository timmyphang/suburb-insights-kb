# Windsor (NSW)

Slug: `windsor-nsw`

Back in the day, when Time Out was waving pom‑poms and naming Windsor the tenth coolest neighbourhood in the world, we were already rolling our eyes and polishing the lime‑green facade of the Windsor Castle Hotel. We know Chapel Street like the soles of our shoes — 60 Chapel Street where Windsor Wine Room launched with its eight‑page bottle list and a $45 set menu, Tokyo Tina at 66A Chapel Street that used to be an old bong shop turned long‑standing staple, and The Chapel at 113 Chapel Street doing Mediterranean share plates and DJs when the mood struck.

Our usual weekend shuffle — yes, we’re the ones who live about five kilometres from the CBD and will tell you so — would take us past places that tried to out‑flair each other: Coil with its vinyl sibling and strict no‑camera dancefloor, Agnii promising clay‑pot Sri Lankan theatrics (shout to Gayan Pieris of Many Little), and once‑upon‑a-time Barbados Slim bringing fried plantains and rum‑spiced BBQ to the sandwich game. Normandy Wine & Grill used to sit pretty on High Street with window‑bench seating and steak frites, and later Duke Lane opened at 2 Duke Street with Latin‑inspired morsels that made us talk about chimichurri like it was a personality trait.

We’ll admit it — Otakoi, the Ukrainian spot on Chapel Street, even snagged Time Out’s 2026 People’s Choice Favourite Restaurant. We clapped. We cried. We rolled our eyes at the hype, then queued like everyone else.

Who would hate living here? People who can’t handle a street that keeps trying new things: the camera‑shy clubbers at Coil, the natty wine crowd at Windsor Wine Room, the folks who miss a quieter postcode. If you like predictable cul‑de‑sac quiet and zero nightlife, this isn’t your cuppa.

Who would love it? Food obsessives, vinyl hoarders, anyone who thinks a neighbourhood should have both a notorious lime‑green pub and a window‑bench bistro. For now, these are stories we keep in our back pocket — the archive says it all — and we wear Windsor’s slightly chaotic, very tasty reputation with pride.
