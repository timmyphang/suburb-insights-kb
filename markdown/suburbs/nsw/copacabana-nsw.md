# Copacabana (NSW)

Slug: `copacabana-nsw`

Back in the day the papers were more interested in our coastline than our council meetings, which suits us fine — we prefer whale tails to headlines. We start the weekend like any self-respecting Central Coast crew: a slow drive out of the city (yes, it’s “just over an hour’s drive from the city” if Sitchu’s to be believed), a beach walk at Copacabana, and then the moral dilemma — do we queue for Burnt Honey Bakery or pretend we were never hungry?

Burnt Honey is the sort of place Broadsheet and Sitchu fought over in their features: cult favourite, queues, naturally leavened sourdough, dense seeded rye, kouign‑amanns and Pepe Saya butter croissants. It’s the kind of bakehouse that makes you feel slightly worse about all your last week’s life choices and very glad you made room in the boot for loaves.

From there it’s the predictable good stuff: Copacabana Beach (Time Out once waved it up the global ranking), Bouddi National Park strolls, and Concrete Playground’s tourist checklist — shipwrecks, snorkeling, fresh seafood on the sand and the occasional swamp wallaby looking at us like we’ve interrupted its holiday. And yes, for those planning romance, Sitchu remembers The Copacabana Beach House as a top adults‑only hideaway.

If you want drama, you won’t find it in our clippings. The stories that love us are transportable — travel guides, bakery love letters and whale‑watching roundups — not scandal columns. That’s fine. We like being the kind of place that journalists write about when they want serenity, pastry or a good whale breach photo.

Who would hate living here? People who can’t handle queues (Burnt Honey, remember), those who loathe sand in their shoes forever-more, and anyone who thinks “wildlife” should only be ornamental on a cushion. Who’ll love it? Bakery pilgrims, bushwalkers, whale‑watchers, and anyone after a coastline that still makes the city feel like a distant memory. We’ll keep the croissants warm and the headlands yours.
