# Keilor Downs (NSW)

Slug: `keilor-downs-nsw`

Back in the day our Saturday mornings started the same way: we’d wrestle the carseat, negotiate with a toddler who believed shoes were optional, and trundle off to the Keilor Downs playgroup. Playgroup Victoria even has us listed (May 3, 2023) — the sort of official imprimatur that makes sticky hands feel like civic participation.

We live in postcode 3038, which means we share a postcode with Keilor Lodge, Taylors Lakes and Watergardens, and we’ve got the receipts: Broadsheet put our lot on the map the weekend the Premier told us we were back in stage-three lockdown. Yes, Daniel Andrews personally announced we were in and out of lockdown like it was a new Netflix drop. It was our most consistent community event for a while — the only thing more predictable than the playgroup roster.

Our weekend routine used to be unglamorous and perfect: playgroup coffee (we won’t pretend it was artisan — it was coffee and sympathy), fingerpaint masterpieces that looked suspiciously like council maps, and a quick text chain to confirm whether “we’re still allowed to go outside” had been updated for the seventh time that week. For a minute there we thought we’d get through life without ever having to explain the difference between Keilor Downs and Taylors Lakes to a delivery driver. Joke’s on us.

Local drama? Other than lockdown press releases and the occasional debate about whose turn it is to bring snacks, we’re gloriously unremarkable. If you want public spats and headline-grabbing feuds, you’ll have to look elsewhere — we prefer our arguments to be resolved with a biscuit and a passive-aggressive text.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to toddler art, patience, or the slow rhythm of suburbia. Who would love it: families who find joy in the everyday, people who liked that time we were all in it together (even when it was stage-three togetherness), and anyone who appreciates that being listed on Playgroup Victoria and Broadsheet is basically suburb street cred.

We’re Keilor Downs — slightly damp from lockdown nostalgia, proud of our playgroup, and stubbornly down-to-earth. Come for the community, stay for the fingerpaint.
