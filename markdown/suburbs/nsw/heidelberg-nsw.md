# Heidelberg (NSW)

Slug: `heidelberg-nsw`

We start our weekends where most suburbs begin — pretending we’ll go for a run and actually ending up at a food-related detour. As Urban List used to point out, when we hit the 10km mark we’d swing past the Heidelberg Football Ground and then merge onto the Main Yarra Trail back to Clifton Hill Station — the kind of route that makes you feel athletic until someone mentions cannoli.

Back in the day we were spoiled for treats: Pieman’s Son in Heidelberg Heights was name-checked by Urban List for pies (Pat Cremean and a Bocastle Pastry family history got an approving nod), and Concrete Playground celebrated Cannoleria’s warehouse move to 69 Sheehan Road in Heidelberg West — factory-sized cannoli production, they wrote, with plans for an on-site eatery. Bounce Inc’s Heidelberg location and Bounce Heidelberg also got their shout-outs for keeping small humans airborne and furious for hours.

We’re proud of our cultural flex: Time Out reminded everyone the Heide Museum of Modern Art sits on about 6.5 hectares and that the whole suburb lends its name to an art movement — yes, the Heidelberg School — which explains why we’ll insist a walk through the gardens counts as culture and cardio. Sitchu used to rave about Little Black Pig & Sons — handmade pasta, rotating menus — the sort of suburban restaurant that confuses Vinnies browsers and foodies alike. Burgundy Street even earns a picnic mention. The Austrian Club’s long history in Heidelberg West (founded in 1944) gives us an excellent excuse for strudel.

Petty local drama? Pedestrian TV once reported that on a very strange night a tip-off from locals led police to find a saltwater crocodile in Heidelberg Heights at around 8:30pm. We still joke: it’s not a proper suburb until the wildlife gets involved. And Junkee’s old coverage of the proposed Suburban Rail Loop keeps us politely hopeful that one day the planners will give us the train attention we secretly crave.

Who would hate living here? People who need instant city-chic without a hint of suburbia’s eccentricities — and anyone who thinks a museum lawn is a poor substitute for rooftop bars. Who would love it? Families who want Bounce and bakery runs, art-lovers who worship the Heide and the Heidelberg School, and anyone who believes a good pie or cannoli justifies a ten-k click. We do, and we’ll keep pretending that’s exercise.
