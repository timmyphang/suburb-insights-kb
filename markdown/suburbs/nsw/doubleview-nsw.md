# Doubleview (NSW)

Slug: `doubleview-nsw`

Doubleview, NSW — back in the day we’d start the weekend like it was a slow, perfectly curated cameo in a lifestyle column. Broadsheet and Urban List kept writing love notes — mostly the old Perth ones, mind you — about our corner spots, so forgive us if we still talk like we’re on a coastal cafe crawl.

Mornings used to mean queuing (politely) for the takeaway window at The Corner Dairy — yes, the one name-checked at 260 Woodside Street — or slipping into Little Sisto on Sackville Terrace for that reliably simple brekky. Broadsheet once crowned Folk & Merchant at 243 Herbert Street as our sunshine spot; we remember the outdoor seating, the feeling of a place that replaced a beloved local haunt and tried very hard to be everyone’s living room.

Our bowls club wasn’t just for bowlers anymore. Jujube Dining popped up at the Doubleview Bowls Club and, in one of those excellent local plot-twists the internet eats up, a Rockpool alum even ran a Special Delivery residency there. Suddenly the bowlo was plating Taiwanese-influenced dishes beside the old chips-and-schnitty specials — high culture meets clubbies, and we loved the polite chaos of it.

There was also talk of a two-level small bar called St Brigid at 257 Scarborough Beach Road — split levels, courtyard, outdoor fireplace — the sort of thing that made suburban Saturday nights feel aspirationally cosy. Urban List and Sitchu also fussed over our Mexican offering (Jalisco Doubleview) and told visitors to end their evenings with share plates and natural wines at The Corner Dairy. Old clippings, old love affairs, but real ones.

Who would hate living here? If you need neon CBD energy and 24/7 hustle, Doubleview will feel like a slow drip of polite craft-beer chat and brunch decisions. Also, if you’re allergic to a bowls club doing dinner, well, you’ve got issues.

Who’d love it? Families who like a community playgroup (shoutout to Playgroup WA and the Gwelup Playgroup that includes Doubleview), foodies who enjoy discovering a chef where you’d expect a meat tray, and anyone who likes their small bars with a courtyard and a hint of suburb-aspirational charm.

All this comes from older guides and features — think “back in the day” rather than “book tonight” — but the vibe? That, happily, tends to stick.
