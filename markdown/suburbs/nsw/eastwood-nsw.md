# Eastwood (NSW)

Slug: `eastwood-nsw`

We know the moment: we step off at Eastwood Train Station, shoulders already full of grocery bags, and someone’s arguing into their phone about whether Yifang’s pearls are a crime against tea. This is our morning — loud, practical, delicious and unapologetically multicultural.

As Time Out once noted, Eastwood’s gone from Granny Smith country to “a dense hub for Asian dining” and honestly, we wear that like sauce on our sleeves. Broadsheet used to make us famous for Yeodongsik — the cult Korean spot that moved into a bigger space and had queues for kimchi pancakes, haejang‑guk and perilla noodles — and we fondly remember lining up for that rare style of bibimbap. Kowloon Cafe brought neon‑lit nostalgia with its overstuffed French toast and pineapple buns. 678 Korean BBQ had its in‑house butcher and soy‑marinated pork short rib. Western Legend BBQ began life as a 2017 food truck and never quite lost that swagger. Ming’s got a shout for being the busy live seafood market. Pu Ji Mi and Woo Wol’s were name‑checked in Korean restaurant roundups. Concrete Playground even remembered that Butchers Buffet once opened here. We’re a lot of things, but subtle isn’t one of them.

Urban List reckons we’re about to get louder — later trading, live music and a proper nightlife upgrade (news: 1 June 2026). That tracks. We’ve always been strong on late‑night dumplings, Korean fried chicken and Asian grocery hauls; now we might just stay up to argue about which noodle is superior until the moon clocks off.

Who hates Eastwood? People who crave quiet cul‑de‑sac afternoons and a single option for dinner. If you can’t handle crowded lanes, multi‑generational food courts or the existential choice between bulgogi and charcoal grill, this isn’t the place.

Who loves Eastwood? Families, commuters and young professionals who want value, variety and a community that’s loud in the best way — as Sitchu put it, a vibrant multicultural hub. If you grew up here like Hyun Lee, the Asian grocery aisles are your safe space. Come for the food, stay for the noise, and if you can’t decide what to eat, just follow the queue. We’ll save you a spot — as long as you’re not stealing our pineapple bun.
