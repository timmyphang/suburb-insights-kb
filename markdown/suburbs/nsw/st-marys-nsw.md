# St Marys (NSW)

Slug: `st-marys-nsw`

Back in the day we thought being “the start of something” sounded glamorous. We were the proud folks who used to tell visitors, with a straight face, that St Marys Station was the launchpad for the Western Sydney Airport metro — the line that would zip you to Western Sydney International (Nancy‑Bird Walton) Airport in about 15 minutes. Then the council brochures met reality: the metro plans got stuck in a $12‑billion limbo and negotiations threatened to turn our well‑timed optimism into a very expensive shrug. At least it gave us something to talk about at the pub.

Mornings used to look like this: a pilgrimage to 176 Queen Street for David’s Larder, where David McDonald — a third‑generation Scottish butcher — used to peddle haggis and black pudding from his shop and roving market stall. If the idea of heartiness makes you nostalgic, back in the day we queued for it like it was a rite of passage. And if you had a sweet tooth, Sydney Street Patisserie’s finger buns were the consolation prize — pink or white icing, regular or loaf‑sized, unbothered by the rest of the world.

The weekend routine always included scanning the timetable for WSI Link — the interim shuttle service — because until the metro arrives we’ve been promised smoother travel via a fleet of 43 new electric buses. Promised is the key word. For years the local narrative has been: rezoning for more than 9,000 new homes, life‑size train prototypes and futuristic station designs — the suburbs’ glow‑up playlist on repeat.

Petty drama? Oh yes. We’ve been told we’re about to be “supercharged” and then left to admire the artist’s impressions while excavators take a coffee break. The bus routes arrive, the prototypes look shiny, and our devotion to the future becomes a local sport.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to construction noise, existential uncertainty, or being loudly described as “set for huge growth” at family gatherings. Who would love it: people who like a good butcher story, finger buns with attitude, the kind of suburb that promises you a metro, a million apartment prospects and a respectable slice of waiting‑room patience. We’re not flashy. We’re the stubbornly hopeful kind of place that keeps coming back for the next render.
