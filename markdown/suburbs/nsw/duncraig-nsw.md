# Duncraig (NSW)

Slug: `duncraig-nsw`

Back in the day we’d brag about our coastal calm like it was a secret—then someone wrote about it and now we just roll our eyes. All the pieces we could find are older, so this is us reminiscing, not a guidebook.

Mornings used to look the same: we’d rattle past parks and beaches, the suburb praised for “an idyllic coastal lifestyle” and being a tidy 20 minutes from the CBD (Sitchu’s words, not ours). Breakfast was the subject of many a write-up — Little H was called “a little Duncraig gem” at 34 Marri Road, famous for red velvet pancakes and the brekkie burger — and the team from Little H even went on to open The Little Bay in Watermans Bay. For those pretending they eat healthy, Nourish and Feed opened its drive-through in 2015, promising seasonal bowls and locally sourced produce.

We also had our loud, slightly glamorous neighbours: Hoodburger’s 120‑seat outpost on Glengarry Road was described by Broadsheet as a nostalgic nod to ’80s/’90s Pizza Hut interiors, serving smashed patties and liquor‑spiked milkshakes. Bentley Polo in the Valley at Duncraig Stud — run by the Prendiville family — used to be our annual dose of philanthropy and polo horseshit, raising well over $780,000 for Telethon and Youth Focus across its decade.

Petty local drama? A few classics: the community playgroup that had been running for 27 years — Duncraig Playgroup & Playfun 3’s — announced the Playfun 3’s closure, which made everyone unexpectedly nostalgic. And who could forget the teen at Duncraig Senior High School refusing an Ian Goodenough‑named award — awkward, moral, and perfect fodder for the grapevine (Pedestrian.TV covered it).

Who would hate living here: people allergic to craft beer and sunshine. Rocky Ridge in Duncraig was a 250‑capacity, 40‑tap outpost adored by locals and dogs alike — if that bores you, move now.

Who’d love it: families who value parks, people who like a little suburban glamour (polo and charity lunches, anyone?), and anyone who enjoys a weekend that could end at a drive‑through salad or a beer hall with forty options. We’re politely smug, mildly nostalgic, and quietly good at fundraising. Welcome to our slightly showy, mostly pleasant corner of the map.
