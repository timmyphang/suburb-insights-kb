# Hinchinbrook (NSW)

Slug: `hinchinbrook-nsw`

Back in the day we used to joke that our suburb’s CV needed a map dot and a very firm shrug. Walk down Whitford Road on a Saturday and you could almost smell the open-fire asado from Club Uruguayo de Sydney — Broadsheet used to call it a cultural hub, with volunteer-run barbecues, salsa nights and the kind of live music that makes you suddenly remember you can dance. We still tell newcomers, “If you can’t handle someone stepping on your toes at a salsa night, this isn’t the postcode for you” — purely observational, of course.

Our weekend routine is a little humble and a little peculiar. For years the local mums found each other through The MomCo’s meetups, swapping tips about everything from school pickups to the best place to stand while pretending to check your phone. Meanwhile, if you thought our biggest claim to fame was local hospitality, Pedestrian.TV once reminded us otherwise when police were called after hundreds queued for a rental inspection here — yes, hundreds — which is the sort of chaos that gives suburban life a reality-TV edge without the sponsorship.

We also suffer from an identity crisis of the deluxe kind: every time someone googles “Hinchinbrook” they end up on Hinchinbrook Island’s hiking porn. For the record, Broadsheet and Time Out have written about that 32-kilometre Thorsborne Trail, the 3–4 night pilgrimage capped at 40 walkers, and Hinchinbrook Island Cruises — spectacular stuff, but located off Lucinda and closer to Ingham and Townsville than to our corner of Sydney. We love our namesake’s drama; we just wish the backpackers would stop turning up on our doorstep asking where to catch the boat.

Who would hate living here? People who despise queues, spontaneous barbecue smoke, or anyone allergic to community-run institutions that do things their own way. Who would love it? Folks who like their culture unpolished — the club that fires up an asado, parents who lean on The MomCo, and anyone happy to claim bragging rights to a name shared with one of Australia’s great wild islands.

We’re small, slightly confused, and quietly proud. Come for the salsa stories; stay because we’ll help you find the meetup.
