# Silvan (NSW)

Slug: `silvan-nsw`

We’re the sort of locals who, on a crisp spring morning, remember dusting tulip pollen out of our socks and apologising to strangers for stepping on someone’s picnic blanket. Back in the day the place that defined our weekends was The Tulip Farm — 357 Monbulk Rd, Silvan — where the Tesselaar Tulip Festival used to light up the Dandenongs with a month of tractor rides, live entertainment and themed weekends (Urban List and Time Out have the receipts).

Our routine used to be embarrassingly wholesome: a slow drive past the Silvan Reservoir Park for a cheeky picnic or hike (as Broadsheet recommended), then a wander to Blue Hills Berries & Cherries to pretend we were farmers for an hour, picking strawberries, raspberries, blueberries and the rest of the berry alphabet. For accommodation nostalgia, we’ll mention Silvan Acres — the quaint studio Airbnb that Urban List once cheekily tagged as a mountain-bike‑friendly hideaway next to the strawberry farm.

If you’re after local drama, we did have a headline or two. Pedestrian TV ran with the Tromp family saga — a national soap-opera level road trip that began with them leaving their Silvan farm back in 2016 and somehow kept everyone talking. It made our streets feel simultaneously famous and faintly exhausting.

Who would hate living here? People who hate mud on their shoes, people allergic to small-town queues during festival season (that month of KaBloom — 21 March–19 April, per Urban List — was a traffic-management exercise in patience), and anyone who finds tractor rides and markets intolerably wholesome.

Who would love it here? Folks who think a good weekend involves flowers underfoot, fruit you pinched yourself fresh from the vine, and a reservoir that insists you slow down. The Alfred Nicholas Memorial Garden and the long-running tulip celebrations used to lure people out of the city for a day that felt intentionally old-fashioned and very, very floral (Sitchu, Time Out).

We’ll admit it: we lap up the kitsch. If nostalgia had a postcode, ours would smell faintly of tulips and jam.
