# Maitland (NSW)

Slug: `maitland-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed, squint at the Hunter mist and think: where else can you get a heritage gaol, a giant boot and a seaside shipwreck in the same weekend? As Broadsheet once pointed out, Maitland had a riverside dining moment and a parade of recommended spots — Coquun, Lorn’s Icky Sticky Patisserie, The Cunning Culinarian and Seraphine Cafe at the Maitland Regional Art Gallery — so we weren’t exactly starving for culture. (Note: that was the Broadsheet take, not a live menu guarantee.)

Weekend routine, if you were us: a cautious civic stroll past Maitland Gaol — yes, the one Time Out lists with self-guided audio tours and torchlight nights that make even the bravest aunt reconsider her life choices — then an escape to the bushier bits. Concrete Playground and Sitchu both remind us the Maitland Bay track and the PS Maitland shipwreck make for excellent day tripping, and there’s apparently a two‑person glamping tent on a farm near Maitland if you fancied pretending you were roughing it.

We’ve had our tabloid chapter too. Pedestrian still remembers that paparazzi kerfuffle back in 2017 and the odd celebrity mention — because why shouldn’t a regional town get its fifteen minutes of scrolling fame? Groovin the Moo has also peppered our memories; the showground has hosted tours with names like Dean Lewis, GLADES, Jack Grace and Ocean Alley, so we can be loud when we need to be.

Who would hate living here? If you’re allergic to oversized roadside art (hi, Big Ugg Boot) or you find torchlit gaol tours distressing, we’re probably not for you. Also, if you need the CBD on tap every Tuesday, Time Out notes most Sydneysiders relocate here for a reason — we’re not pretending to be the city.

Who’ll love it? Families — we’ve got playgroups like PlayConnect+ at Maitland Presbyterian Church (3 Church Street), Maitland Nature and Nurture Art at 11 Steam Street and the Gillieston Heights Community Hub on Redwood Drive & Pines Street — and anyone who enjoys heritage, weird local landmarks and accessible coastal escapes. Come for the shipwreck walks and stay because you realised the boot makes a great selfie.
