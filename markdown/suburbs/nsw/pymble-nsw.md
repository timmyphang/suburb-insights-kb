# Pymble (NSW)

Slug: `pymble-nsw`

We know how this goes in Pymble: we’ll start our Saturday with a slow drive down the Pacific Highway, because of course the Pacific Highway is where you end up, and someone in the back will remind us that the Pymble Hotel on Pacific Highway used to be the place for cheap steaks on Tuesdays and trivia-and-schnitzel Wednesdays. Back in the day — and, let’s be honest, most of the articles about us are “back in the day” — we queued for pastries when Du Plessy Pralin & Otello’s Japonaise cake went viral and, yes, people flew from China to West Pymble for one. No shame: we’d do the same.

Our weekend loops tend to include Phillip Mall where Brick Lane Espresso used to be the hidden gem with an Italian-tinged menu and great coffee, and Prince of Wales Drive where Ku-ring-gai Fitness and Aquatic Centre was the spot for splash and chaos. If we were stocking the pantry, Tom’s Cellars in West Pymble would have been the boutique bottle-shop stop — handpicked wines, craft beer and the smugness that comes with it. For proper dining chatter, we remember The Sawmill — built on the site of the old timber mill, a love letter to our timber past and the owners’ Italian heritage.

Now for the local soap: Norman Griffiths Oval. We’ve watched a humble soccer field be dragged through asbestos finds, drainage dramas and a legal blowout that ballooned budgets from $3.3 million to headlines about $20 million. It’s the kind of council saga that turns a park into a legal thriller and gives our mums something to rant about on the school run.

Who would hate living here? If you crave inner-city chaos, 24-hour everything and a sense that no one waters their garden, Pymble will bore you stiff — we prefer our leafy peace, thanks. Also, if you miss an endless rotation of cafés being reviewed as “the next big thing,” sorry — our scene mostly lives in memory.

Who’d love it? Families who value good schools (Pymble Ladies’ College sits on sprawling grounds), folks who like a boutique bottle-shop run and a community that rallies at Robert Pymble Park (reopened May 2023) and playgroups coordinated through Playgroup NSW. We may be leafy, occasionally litigated and fond of a nostalgic queue, but that’s Pymble: quietly posh, slightly dramatic, and ours.
