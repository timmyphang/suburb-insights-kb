# Avalon Beach (NSW)

Slug: `avalon-beach-nsw`

We know Avalon by the sight of a Lululemon-clad Cavoodle owner reapplying sunscreen on the Barrenjoey Road footpath while their Prado takes up two parking spots and the oat piccolo steam fogs the air. We move at dawn — surf, breathwork, oat flat white — and we say “sorry” in a note before “bloody hell” in the group chat. We volunteer at the Avalon Surf Club on weekends, not because we have to, but because it looks good on our profiles next to a decorative surfboard that never quite leaves Bondi. 

Saturday routine: a 6:00am paddle with the surf club, a pilgrimage to Avalon Beach Café for the oat piccolo (small, of course), then the farmers’ market for kombucha, basil and a suspiciously artisanal loaf. Sunday routine: long beach walk with the Cavoodle, brunch where the menu insists it’s seasonal and we insist it’s worth it. We do morning meditation, an F45 or beach yoga class midweek, post a golden-hour snap, and drop a casserole to the new neighbours because that’s how we signal trustworthiness. Sunscreen gets re-applied with military precision.

Our dramas are deliciously petty. The council repaint of parking lines on Barrenjoey Road sparked a neighbourhood group chat civil war that lasted three days and produced three passive-aggressive petitions about “tourist hogging”. When Avalon Beach Café dared to change piccolo sizes, three scathing Instagram reviews arrived like a white-lightning storm. The mums’ group is split down the middle over turmeric lattes versus honest oat flat whites — alliances have been formed and broken over beans and turmeric. There is, inevitably, a Toyota Prado owner who insists they’re “ruggedly eco-conscious” and “carpool occasionally” while straddling two bays like it’s the era of the land baron.

Who would hate Avalon? The 25-year-old nightclub DJ who thinks “quiet coastal life” means “no rules” would last one week. They want cheap takeaway at 3am, car alarms as a soundtrack, and 90-minute street parking for pop-up parties; we prefer sunrise yoga, polite note-leaving, and a strict “no party vans at the headland” policy. They clash with our vibe of calm competitive politeness.

Who would love it? The 38-year-old Pilates instructor with a Cavoodle, a sensible Subaru Outback that fits surfboard and grocery equally well, a boutique studio commute, and a penchant for oat flat whites will feel right at home. Welcome — just remember to carry sunscreen and a spare parking etiquette.
