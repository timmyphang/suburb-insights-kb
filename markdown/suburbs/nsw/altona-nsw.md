# Altona (NSW)

Slug: `altona-nsw`

Back in the day we’d stand on the recycled-timber arms of the Y-shaped Altona Pier and pretend we were at some coastal European escape — except our seagulls had opinions. Time Out wrote about that $11.9 million makeover, and honestly, it made for a cracking sunset spot (in the era the papers were excited about it).

Our weekend routine used to look like this: shuffle down Pier Street, queue at Matteo’s Delicatessen for a sandwich so good Broadsheet raved about its jam-packed shelves of passata and tins of fancy fish, then wander toward the grass to eat whatever Alex’s Fish And Chips at 11 Ford Road had handed over — which Urban List once praised for proper, ’80s-style chips. If you were feeling extra, Yo‑Mama at 87 Pier Street was the place for fro‑yo decadence. BAM Street Food found a permanent home at 16 Harrington Square and for a while we loved that the smash-burger legends weren’t just a van anymore.

We know we live in the shadow of Williamstown’s swagger — Concrete Playground even noted Altona sat there for years — but we’ve got our own trinkets. The Hobsons Bay Coastal Trail snakes through jawbone reserves and the Altona Coastal Park; Pauline Fraser’s Seaborn sculptures keep watch over the pier like maritime sentries. For the kids (and the nostalgics), Altona Homestead has all the colourful history and Lantern Ghost Tours that Junkee liked to spook people with. There was also talk of the Australian Ballet production centre tours — yes, sets and costumes assembled out this way.

Who would hate it here? People who need buzzy laneway cocktails every night, or anyone allergic to wide skies, long sand, and the occasional seaside eccentric. Who would love it? Families — Sitchu actually listed Altona as great for young families thanks to beach access and green spaces — dog owners (Burn’s Reserve is a proper shy-pooch-friendly stretch), walkers who worship the coastal trail, and anyone who likes their city with a hint of seaside laundry and a good deli counter.

We’re proud: a little weathered, quietly ambitious, and very fond of our pier views. If that’s not you, there’s always Williamstown.
