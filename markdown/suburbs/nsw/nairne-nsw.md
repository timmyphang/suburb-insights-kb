# Nairne (NSW)

Slug: `nairne-nsw`

We wake up in Nairne thinking we’re in a country postcard, then someone reminds us we’ve still got bins to put out and a community newsletter to proofread. Weekends here start with dragging guests out of the cedar hot tub at Oliver’s Yard — yes, the Urban List one that sleeps eight and comes with a pizza oven and a games room. It’s the kind of place people book when they want vino and zero small talk with the city.

For a quieter rummage, we used to browse Pallet Home + Table’s salvaged timber and curated homewares in the old 1853 deli — Broadsheet had a nice write-up back in the day. And yes, we still tell the tale of Rise Artisan Bakery like it’s a local ghost story: Broadsheet noted the bakery set in a former schoolhouse and church, shelves of loaves and vintage jams — but the listing now says it’s permanently closed, so that nostalgia is fragrant but toasted.

Our civic life is embarrassingly organised. We read Nairne News (editor Kerri Oakley — that one’s current) to find out who’s running the sausage sizzle, and we rely on the Nairne Museum at 82 Old Princes Hwy if we want to remind ourselves why we’re all here — they open Sundays 2–4pm, in case you’re feeling historical. The Soldiers Memorial Hall on the Princes Highway still does the heavy lifting for community events — it’s got a stage, kitchen and room for about 175 people, which is more seating than some of our weekend dramas merit.

Kids’ activities used to include GKR Karate at 5 Saleyard Road — mums’ listings sang its praises for free trials, so if you’ve got a child who likes a gi, there was, at least once, somewhere to try it. The Nairne and Districts Residents Association has been a thing too; we’d point to its meeting notes and notepads if you wanted proof of civic enthusiasm.

Who would hate it here? If you want a 24-hour latte and a skyline that requires binoculars, you’ll be bored. Who would love it? Folks after a cosy Airbnb escape (Urban List’s Oliver’s Yard), heritage buffs who’ll make the Sunday Museum pilgrimage, and anyone who thinks community newsletters are peak entertainment. We’re small, organized and quietly stubborn about our charm — and yes, we’ll forgive you for mentioning that closed bakery like it’s still serving croissants.
