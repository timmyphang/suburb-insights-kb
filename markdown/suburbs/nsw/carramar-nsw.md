# Carramar (NSW)

Slug: `carramar-nsw`

Back in the day our weekend map had exactly two pins: the Karitane Fairfield Child and Family Hub for playgroup, then a straight line to Art of Gelato Michelangelo on 30 Horsley Drive. We’d shuffle the pram into Play Strong Carramar Supported — the free playgroup that used to run Fridays at 9:30am — and swap parenting survival tips while the kids dismantled a Lego set. Bookings were via christine.phillis@health.nsw.gov.au or afulloon@playgroupnsw.org.au, because nothing says suburbia like emailing about biscuits and finger paints.

After a morning of organised chaos we’d wobble over to Martino Piccolo’s gelateria. As Broadsheet noted, the man’s a Gelato World Cup‑class show‑off — in the best way — and Art of Gelato Michelangelo at 30 Horsley Drive used to be our unofficial reward station. We’d queue like it was a cultural rite: tiny spoons, capricious kids, big smiles. We won’t pretend the queue didn’t double as social currency.

If you want local trivia, here’s a weird flex: Jovan Curic, the founder behind Superior Burger, grew up around these parts. So yes, we produce future burger nostalgists and World Cup gelato champions. We’re quietly ambitious that way.

There’s not a lot of glossy hype about Carramar — which suits us fine. We’re not pretending to be a riverside hamlet or an Instagram grid of boutique everything. If you hate community routines, small triumphs, and the idea that a gelato shop can be the town’s cultural centre, you’d probably hate living here. If you loathe sensible Fridays full of toddlers and emails about craft glue, move on.

But if you love being the sort of place where the playgroup organiser knows your kid’s favourite block and where a world‑class gelatiere once made our humble strip feel slightly famous, you’ll fit right in. For years our favourite brag was simple: we have a gelato shop that makes people travel, and a community that shows up on Friday mornings. Come for the nostalgia, stay because someone always has spare fairy‑bread.
