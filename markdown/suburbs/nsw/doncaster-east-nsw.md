# Doncaster East (NSW)

Slug: `doncaster-east-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start Saturday by folding up the pram at Doncaster Primary School’s playgroup, wrestle a sticky yakka out of a toy box and parade the kids toward Surrey Gardens like we were in a pastoral brochure. South Surrey Park and those English‑style lawns are the suburb’s flex — grass you can roll on without judgement, and playgrounds that make the weekend feel like a school fair on repeat.

For our slightly more cultured impulses there used to be Three Monkeys Place tucked in Jackson Court, bringing a bit of inner‑city pizzazz with a South East Asian‑tinted menu. We’d pick up something ridiculous from Meno Zero on Doncaster Road — yes, they famously had 24 gelato flavours, including the pizza Caprese oddity — and stash a bottle from Nicks Wine Merchants (Jackson Court) if we were pretending the kids weren’t coming home with half the sandpit.

The suburb even earns a nod on the serious food lists — Time Out once flagged Kobe Teppanyaki in the area as the place you booked for wagyu and family milestones. Which is saying something: our idea of a big night out is cleaving a slab of beef at a communal table while someone else pretends to flip an omelette into a hat.

Petty local drama? Remember the cucumber episode — a 12‑year‑old allegedly finding a needle in a cucumber bought from Woolworths at Devon Plaza. It was one of those small-town headlines that made everyone check their salads and their group chat forwarding habits.

Who would hate living here? If your vibe is late trams and constant rooftop bars, this isn’t your postcode — Sitchu even notes life here is “more likely to revolve around a car boot full of groceries than a late tram home.” If you want anonymity and neon, move on.

Who’d love it? Families, obviously. Sitchu keeps calling it one of Melbourne’s most family‑friendly pockets for a reason: playgroups, parks, solid schools, the Doncaster East Baptist Church’s Sunday‑school room and garden that still smells faintly of glue and Goldfish crackers. We’re cosy, slightly aspirational, and proudly suburban — with gelato options that can only be described as committed.
