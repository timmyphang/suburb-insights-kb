# Earlwood (NSW)

Slug: `earlwood-nsw`

We know where the river smells sweetest, where the footy kicks out most Sundays, and which corner shop still does a proper slice of nostalgia — we’re Earlwood, and yes, we know every tree on Homer Street.

Back in the day we’d roll out for a weekend loop: hop down to the Cooks River for a riverside slog, wander the Homer Street strip and pretend the Earlwood Village Festival was the Met Gala, then reward ourselves with whatever the bros at Ol’ Mates were wrapping up — Broadsheet called their schnitty sangas “whopping” and that family-recipe tirokafteri wasn’t shy. Pep’s Italian Ice used to turn up with New York-style semi-frozen scoops for sunlit afternoons, and Barlow & Co. brought the Texan Snack Pack to town (we still tell tales of molten raclette and brisket like it’s folklore).

We’ve always been on a project map. For years the planners dreamed of the GreenWay — the trail that was supposed to stitch the Cooks River in Earlwood to Iron Cove and the Bay Run — and Time Out and Concrete Playground ran the renderings like teasers for a rom-com. The Canterbury Velodrome sat there daring us to get fast, while the Earlwood Hotel balanced jukebox nostalgia with kids’ arcade energy. Sitchu summed us up best: leafy, village-y, up-and-coming — which is council-speak for “we’ve got good bones and more cake shops than we probably need.”

Petty local drama? Oh, we had that. As Broadsheet reported, Butterball was literally fighting to save its Souvlaki Sundays — and if you thought a kebab could spark civic passion, you haven’t lived here. There’ve also been quieter, kinder wins: a plaque at Earlwood Oval was rededicated to recognise the original Indigenous owners — small-town civic pride with proper teeth.

Who’d hate living here: anyone who needs non-stop nightlife, CBD coffee theatre, or a skyline view. Who’d love it: people who want wide streets, the river at their doorstep, a sandwich that hits like Ol’ Mates’ schnitty, and Saturday markets that still remember their grandparents’ recipes.

We’re not fancy. We’re the suburb that likes its gyros (Gyradiko gets a namecheck), its finger buns (Clemton Park Cake Shop, legend), and its weird little food experiments. Come for the eats, stay for the trees, and don’t be surprised if you leave with a story about a snack pack and a bridge that never quite arrived.
