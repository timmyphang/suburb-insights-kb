# The Hill (NSW)

Slug: `the-hill-nsw`

We stand on Campbell Parade like we own the hill — which, technically, we do, at least in the tales we tell tourists. Perched high above North Bondi was The Hill Eatery, the little place the Porch and Parlour team grafted up to feel a touch more grown-up. As Broadsheet noted, it was the sort of spot that stretched its hours for weekend breakfasts, made its own sourdough and served King George whiting with a fried egg and beurre blanc like it wasn’t showing off.

Our weekend ritual used to be part reverent, part pragmatic: queue for a table, argue about whether the mixed seafood pie was actually worth the hype (Time Out liked it), and watch the sun think about rising. The Hill Eatery prided itself on a sustainability-focused kitchen and home-style cooking that felt like someone else’s very good family dinner. We loved that we could be both responsible and slightly indulgent in the same mouthful.

Then the plot twist arrived — and yes, we mean the one Concrete Playground documented. The Hill’s site didn’t stay a Hill: Darren Robertson of Three Blue Ducks moved into the spot with Rocker. Suddenly the corner of Campbell Parade that once whispered “sourdough and restraint” started humming surfboard metaphors and humble seafood pride. Change? Imitation? Natural cycle of Bondi culinary real estate? We embraced all three while sipping something with a name we couldn’t quite remember.

Who would hate living here? People who can’t tolerate a community that’s half sustainability seminar, half surf-chic seafood hangout. Also anyone who needs permanence — this hill is remodelled more often than a weather app.

Who would love it? Folks who enjoy properly made, slightly fussy food; people who enjoy a kitchen that cares about where things come from; anyone who likes their breakfast with provenance and their dinner served with a wink toward the ocean. Back in the day, The Hill Eatery gave us that cozy, upmarket warmth. Now Rocker’s in the spot, and we’re polishing our plates and our surf metaphors. We’ll keep holding court up here — manners optional, opinions mandatory.
