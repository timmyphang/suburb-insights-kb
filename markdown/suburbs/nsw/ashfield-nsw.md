# Ashfield (NSW)

Slug: `ashfield-nsw`

We know Ashfield by the sight of a Volvo XC60 inching into a Bland Street park at 8:12am, driver in Lululemon balancing a long mac with oat milk while a cavoodle tips its head like it's late for Pilates. We are the people who can taste the roastery on the corner from our living room and still argue, loudly and with emojis, on the school-gate Facebook group about which sourdough is actually worth schlepping home. We kiss the dog on the head and pretend the SUV is a “family people‑mover” while secretly celebrating that vacated space like it’s a lotto win.

Our mornings are choreography. 6am espresso on the bench, a school-run playlist queued—Amy Winehouse for the drop-offs, Fleetwood Mac for the last-minute permission slips—then a brisk cavoodle promenade through Ashfield Park where every other walker nods as if we all share the same secret: we love the routine. Then it’s a reformer Pilates session, followed by brunch at the little roastery on Bland Street — sourdough toast for us, a long black for the partner, an obligatory flat-lay of the dog for the ‘Gram. Saturday is market reconnaissance and a strategic swing past Ashfield Mall to nab the thing we forgot while convincing ourselves we were only “popping in”.

We are endlessly proud of our tidy front yards with their native pot-plants and our SUVs that pretend to be eco-conscious. We also, loudly, love a good neighbourhood drama. Bin placement is not a suggestion; it is an art. The weekly passive-aggressive sticker war on wheelie bins is an unofficial sport. And do not get us started on the 8:30am parking tension around Ashfield Mall and the station—there is a Sunday stoush that runs longer than the farmers’ market and the viral lament, “Someone took my spot on Bland Street,” is our town cri de coeur.

If you need absolute, hermit-level silence, a three‑acre paddock and a backyard with no footpath traffic, you will hate living here. You will be baffled by pop-up markets, rallying for bake-sale fundraising, and our ritual of treating a parking spot like a championship trophy. You will find suburbia too social, too opinionated, and far too fond of passive-aggression with gardens.

But if you love curated chaos, short walks to great coffee, community ballet at the school gate, and the comforting madness of a suburb that argues about bins like it’s national policy, welcome home. We’ll save you a long mac and a prized parking spot—if you’ve earned it.
