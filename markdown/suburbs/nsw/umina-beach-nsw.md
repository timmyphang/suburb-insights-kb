# Umina Beach (NSW)

Slug: `umina-beach-nsw`

We know the walk: the esplanade, the gentle swell lapping at our ankles, kids wobbling on foam boards and someone else’s soggy towel trying to claim the best patch of sand. Back in the day the guidebooks loved to drop us into itineraries — Time Out called us the Central Coast’s go-to for “baby’s-first-surf-lesson,” and Sitchu reminded everyone we sit just south of Ettalong, bordering Brisbane Waters National Park. We wear that family-friendly label like sunscreen — slightly greasy, but necessary.

Our weekend looks like a coastal rom-com you didn’t ask for: cyclists finishing the Ettalong to Umina route dismounting like they’ve conquered a Tour de France stage (thanks, Urban List), while parents shepherd toddlers between surf schools that used to be the local go-to for learning to catch a wave. Rick Stein even took a dip here during his NSW road trip — yes, the chef who turned our tiny patch of sand into a TV cameo. We’ll sign autographs later.

There’s not much local tabloid petty drama in the clippings — which is probably for the best. Instead the stories are annoyingly wholesome: community groups doing dunecare and bushcare, volunteers planting dunes and keeping the place tidy (the Umina Community Group has pages full of it), and someone from here — Felicity — packing up for LA and ending up running PREAM and Riot Grill. We’re small but exportable.

Who would hate it here? If you want a set of 24-hour options, a skyline of cranes and glass, or a nightlife that runs past 11pm, Umina will politely disappoint you. If you want sand with a side of peace and people who will actually show up for a dune-planting, you’ll fit right in.

For anyone who loves a quiet swell, an esplanade walk, community dunecare, a short ferry hop from Palm Beach/Ettalong, or the idea that our little Umina Beach (2257) once stocked passport stamps for a book crawl (yes, Umina Beach Book Nook was listed in that Global Book Crawl) — you’re home. We’ll be here, sand in our shoes and a resigned grin for anyone who still calls us “just a beach.”
