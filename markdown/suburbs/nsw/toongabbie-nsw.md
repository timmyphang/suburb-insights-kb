# Toongabbie (NSW)

Slug: `toongabbie-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed and follow our noses to X Dreams — or XDream depending on which review you read — for vada and hoppers before the 9–5 had even yawned. As Broadsheet put it, this humble 25-seat takeaway was where Umakanthan Yogalingam got the neighbourhood hooked on Sri Lankan breakfasts; Time Out cheekily called it “Sydney’s finest (and cheapest) hoppers.” We’d call that a morning well spent, even if you had to brave Portico Place plaza across from Toongabbie train station to get there.

Weekends for us meant a slow wander along the new bit of the Great West Walk by Toongabbie Creek — the patch that links Parramatta Park through Blacktown and the Western Sydney Parklands towards Penrith. The Hills Shire Council’s bushwalk maps even send you off the beaten track into our watery bits, which is fancy talk for “bring decent shoes.”

We’re not short on family infrastructure either. Parents tote toddlers to Toongabbie East Public School playgroup or set up fort at Reynolds Park’s castle playground; Binalong Park still gets a thumbs-up for being fenced and shaded (the kids designed the place, apparently). Time Out even noted Sue Savage Reserve as one of the spots that hosted family movie nights in 2026 — so yes, we get screens under the gumtrees now. Fancy.

If you’re after scandal, the local archives mostly offer hoppers, playground plans and walking maps — not a spicy council feud to be seen. Dish and Amma’s Modern Kitchen were the sort of names Broadsheet and Time Out could not stop praising; Fernando from Dish even parlayed his Toongabbie rep into Kottoo in the CBD. So our brag is quietly culinary, not necessarily tabloid-ready.

Who’d hate it here? If you’re allergic to good, cheap Sri Lankan food or find kettle-fed community courts too cosy, you’ll scowl. Who’d love it? Families, walkers, and anyone who thinks a Thursday night at the Toongabbie Hotel (yep, Time Out once noted their karaoke) is peak suburban sophistication. We’ll keep roasting ourselves — but only between hopper bites.
