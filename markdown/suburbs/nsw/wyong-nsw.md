# Wyong (NSW)

Slug: `wyong-nsw`

We start our mornings like everybody else in Wyong did back in the day: blurry, suspicious of the week, and vaguely hopeful there’s still a marshmallow big enough to fix it. The Marshmallow Co., tucked into the Chapman Building, used to be our unofficial dessert council — Concrete Playground noted its OTT s’mores and stacks of handmade marshmallows (25 flavours, Time Out even said about 40) that could make a grown adult consider a career change to confectioner.

Weekends were a choose-your-own-adventure: paddle a calming lap on Avoca Lake (Sitchu recommended stand-up paddle-boarding), or wander the town centre during Taste of Wyong and Toukley, where Concrete Playground promised $5 tasting boxes and a cheeky buy-one-get-one to keep the indecisive among us fed. For a proper local flex, we’d trot out the Old Wyong Milk Factory, where Little Creek Cheese used to offer tastings of lemon myrtle fetta, garlicky labne and, yes, gin-flavoured cheddar — enough to make any cheese board a conversation starter or an argument, depending on how much wine you’d had.

If nostalgia counts as nightlife, we had Love Lanes Festival — the ninth annual — turning the town into a lantern-lit maze with four love-themed lanes, five stages and karaoke for those who confuse romance with public humiliation (as Concrete Playground documented). Time Out even mentioned the shiny Mariyung inter-city trains stopping at Wyong once upon a press release, which was our official moment of pretending we commuted to the city like responsible adults.

Our small-town roster isn’t all festivals and fluff: families remember Wyong Public School on Cutler Dr as a hub for playgroups — Ganang Aboriginal Playgroup led by Aunty Sue, outdoor and sensory sessions — and Wyong Baptist Church at 100 Alison Rd ran playtime on weekday mornings (Little Locals had the listings). That’s the real backbone.

Who’d hate it here? If you need a perpetually curated cocktail lane in every strip mall, Wyong will test your patience. Who’d love it? Folks who like festivals that smell faintly of marshmallow, cheese experiments, paddleboards at sunrise and a community that still runs playgroups for the real-life small humans. We’ll keep the lanterns lit — just don’t ask us to share the gin cheddar.
