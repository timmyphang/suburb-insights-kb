# Bayswater North (NSW)

Slug: `bayswater-north-nsw`

Back in the day we used to brag that Bayswater North was the sort of place where your Saturday starts with a packing list: kids’ ballet at Arrabri Community House on Tuesday nights (yes, that one), karate at GKR Bayswater Prime up at Level 1, 5 Market Drive, and a detour past 1/82 Bayfield Road East to wave at the metalworkers at R & Dee Fabrications because someone needs to fix our bike rack.

We remember when Bakery Hill was our local claim to craft-producer fame — Broadsheet even notes it opened here in 1999 and then finally packed up after nearly 25 years and decamped to Kensington. We like to tell visitors about that like it’s an origin myth: once we made whisky; now we just make plans about making whisky again.

For a brief, lockdown-era renaissance there was Dröm Bakery — the Urban List piece that said they started during lockdown and officially opened on Wednesday 8 March. We queued like civilisation depended on moon-shaped croissants and rotated goods; it felt suitably dramatic for a postcode where the biggest thrill used to be a new bakery opening.

We’ve had our moments of civic glamour and our practical ones: Premier Homes & Granny Flats at 110 Canterbury Road doing the whole generational-building thing, Nido Early School quietly promising tiny humans the Reggio Emilia dream, and the All Stars Choir meeting at 265 Canterbury Road on Wednesday mornings (they meet; we sing; someone always forgets the notes).

There was also that thrilling time Broadsheet listed us among 88 suburbs advised to boil water as a precaution. Glamorous, really. And once a Sitchu roundup had Bayswater North rumoured as a contender for a permanent Melbourne drive-through — the kind of speculation that keeps the neighbourhood newsletter alive for weeks.

Who would hate living here? If you crave inner‑city buzzy bars or think “community calendar” is a personality flaw, you’ll find us a little boring. Who would love it? Families, small-business folks, builders, people who rate a reliable local bakery, karate classes for the kids, and anyone who secretly enjoys a postcode that can moonlight as a whisky origin story. We’re proud, a touch rusty, and perfectly fine with that.
