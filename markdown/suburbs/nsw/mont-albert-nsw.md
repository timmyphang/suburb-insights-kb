# Mont Albert (NSW)

Slug: `mont-albert-nsw`

We know the bit — sun on the verandah, hydrangeas that look like they’ve been groomed by committee, and a bloke on Hamilton Street who still waves at the 109 tram like it’s his cousin. Back in the day the guides loved to tell the story of Mont Albert: Time Out even flagged our ornate Victorians and Edwardians and trumpeted a 16.5% growth in 2025 with a median house price that made neighbours check their hedges twice.

A weekend in our patch used to mean a gentle circuit: browse the Mont Albert Village windows, peer at listings like the one on Beresford Street, maybe queue (politely) for a table at Via Porta — as Broadsheet noted, the siblings behind it brought a sun‑bleached delicatessen vibe and a basement bakery that smelled like continental promises. The old milk bar got a French‑inspired rebirth as Matilda, and Concrete Playground once sent us vinyl pilgrims to Discrepancy Records in Mont Albert North — more than 15,000 LPs, they said, if you wanted to look like you knew who the B‑sides belonged to.

We’ll admit it: our past was curated. Mister and Miss at 711–713 Whitehorse Rd made brunch into an event, Churchill on Hamilton Street was the sort of relaxed spot that people recommended for Sunday lunches, and Cantina Centrale — well, Concrete Playground even noted its classy wood‑fired pizzaiolo ways before it shut up shop.

If you hate Mont Albert, you probably want everything loud, fast and open past 10pm. You’ll find our pace infuriatingly civilised, our gardens too tidy, our discussions more likely to be about paint colours than protests. If you crave a skyline, you’ll be bored. If you need a 24/7 nightlife rotation, we’ll apologise in advance.

But if you love good coffee rituals (the kind that arrive with a pastry history), vinyl rabbit holes, heritage houses you could lose a toddler in, and a playgroup — St Hilary’s — that actually remembers your kid’s name, we’re the suburbs’ very gentle hug. As the old guides used to say: quaint, considered, and just proud enough to refuse to shout about it. Come for the charm, stay for the hedges.
