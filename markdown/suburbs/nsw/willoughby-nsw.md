# Willoughby (NSW)

Slug: `willoughby-nsw`

Back in the day we used to stagger out of our Federation homes, blink at the jacarandas and make a beeline for whatever Willoughby was doing that weekend. As Broadsheet noted, the Incinerator Cafe was literally a thing — the Walter Burley Griffin‑designed incinerator converted into a café overlooking Hallstrom Park, with a wood‑fired oven to make you feel cultured before brunch. Time Out even filed it under "a building with strong bones" at 2 Small Street.

Our Saturday ritual used to involve a quick detour to Willoughby Seafoods at 285 Penshurst Street if you were pretending to be on top of dinner plans, or buying bulk nuts and tea from The Source Bulk Foods when we were being virtuous (Broadsheet had that on file). For a rare night out we’d remember Braci Osteria + Pizzeria — Time Out called it the buzzy centrepiece after it opened in 2021 — or the old‑school comfort of La Botte, which Time Out praised for four decades of quiet excellence. Concrete Playground’s guides used to map the Willoughby Road hustle — Gelato Messina, Bar Li’l Darlin, Annata, The Stoned Crow — the usual suspects that made Crows Nest feel like the North Shore’s answer to inner‑city dining.

Look, we don’t have a breaking scandal to sell you — the data’s a stack of lovely profiles and reviews rather than council rage. So no petty local drama this week; we’ll let the heritage listings and food reviews carry the personality. Sitchu once sent a photographer into a Federation makeover on Bedford Street (Bone Made’s work got a feature), and they also noted Hallstrom Park and Willoughby Leisure Centre as family staples.

Who would hate it here? If you crave Surry Hills‑style chaos, you’ll be bored — Concrete Playground said Willoughby Road competes with the inner‑city scene, but it’s a gentler rival. If your whole vibe is neon, pulsing clubs and zero heritage, move on.

Who would love it? Families who want parklands, people who like a neighbourhood that remembers its architecture (Laurelbank Cottage, anyone?) and anyone who enjoys the idea of calm streets that once hosted a converted incinerator with a wood‑fired oven. For years the local guides have told that story — and frankly, we’re proud of it.
