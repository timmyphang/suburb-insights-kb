# Carrum (NSW)

Slug: `carrum-nsw`

Carrum — we’re the lot who still know where the best sunset happens and won’t shut up about it.

Mornings here start with a train trip past the foreshore playground at the corner of Johnson Ave and Old Post Office Ln — the lighthouse and play boat are the kind of thing you point at and say, “That’s where we taught the kids to be dangerously patriotic about swings.” Back in the day the playground sat so close to Carrum train station (on the Frankston line) that you could time a school run to a single platform announcement.

Weekends used to look predictable in the best way: families at the foreshore, the kids bouncing off Funtopia Carrum Downs & Cranbourne North (as Urban List once name‑checked), and the slightly more committed among us heading to Bayside Rock in Carrum Downs to pretend we’re national climbing champions for an hour. When the sun dipped properly, the Carrum Bowling Club was the place to be — Time Out’s writeup still has that timber clubhouse and the new synthetic green wafting ocean breeze vibes.

We’re not above a bit of local lore. Broadsheet loved the Dainton Family Brewery out in Carrum Downs — the plain warehouse beside the freeway — and we always bragged about the beers they brewed: Red Eye Rye, Samurye lager, Insane Uncle IPA. They even had table tennis and live music on Fridays and Sundays, which, yes, made our Friday night nanna‑rock fantasies oddly plausible.

Petty local drama? We’ve had it. Junkee immortalised the night a 17‑year‑old toured Frankston in a motorised esky; you couldn’t invent that and expect us to believe you. And for years Domain and Time Out kept whispering about growth — Carrum was even listed with 11.5 per cent growth in 2025 — so yes, there’s an air of “don’t blink” around the property pages.

Who would hate living here? If you despise beaches, second‑hand brewery charm, or people who can’t help pointing out a sunset, this isn’t your circus. Who would love it? Families, climbers, and anyone who appreciates a suburb that quietly has the lot — playgrounds, climbing, craft beer lore and a bowling club that genuinely makes evenings feel like a coastal postcard.
