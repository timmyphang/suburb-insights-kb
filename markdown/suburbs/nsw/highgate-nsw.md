# Highgate (NSW)

Slug: `highgate-nsw`

We wake up with the city in our bones and the smell of pastry dreams — back when we could still saunter to Chu Bakery opposite Hyde Park for a matcha‑yuzu ‘Chu’ puff and a Dukes Coffee Roasters shot before the park filled with weekend strollers. Hyde Park’s two lakes, islands and those ridiculously photogenic shady trees have always made it feel like a secret getaway five minutes from the CBD; Time Out even remembers the aqua play space installed in 2012, for reasons of municipal boasting.

Our weekend loop used to look like this: pick up something flaky from Chu, wander past the playground, duck into Beaufort Street to pretend we’re only popping in for one thing. Broadsheet’s old directory read like a who’s‑who of temptation — Lo’ with its southern‑inspired nonsense like hummingbird pecan pie and a curated bourbon list, Si Paradiso doing puffy pizza and courtyard DJs until the neighbours cursed the sunrise, Gelato Messina churning panna cotta with fig jam, and the daftly beloved Highgate Continental and Bar Rogue doing their rounds. Tom’s came back bigger; Must Winebar earned a tearful exit after twenty years. It was all very charmingly excessive.

Local drama? It was mostly of the delicious sort: we mourned Must Winebar’s farewell like it was a local celebrity, we argued about whether Si Paradiso’s courtyard was a cultural hub or a noise nuisance (both could be true), and we proudly pretended Beaufort Street was our tiny, taste‑saturated kingdom. As Sitchu pointed out, we were the inner‑city miracle of Art Deco homes, accessible transport and enough cafés to make people from the CBD queue for a croissant.

Who would hate living here? Someone who despises puffy pizza, live music courtyards, and the faint, ongoing hum of people deciding between gelato flavours. Who would love it? Families who used to drop their toddlers at Highgate Playgroup in Forrest Park, food obsessives who recall Lo’, Gelato Messina and Chu, and anyone who likes their city life served with a generous side of leafy Hyde Park. We’ll keep the corner for you — as long as you bring pecan pie.
