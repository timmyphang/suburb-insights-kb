# Kincumber (NSW)

Slug: `kincumber-nsw`

Back in the day we’d swagger out like we owned the coast — with prams, picnic rugs and an opinion on pesto — because Kincumber’s grand cultural contributions, according to our filing cabinet of internet clippings, are very specific and very wholesome.

Morning routine: we trawl the listings on What’s On 4 Kids for the week — art, craft & pottery, baby and toddler classes, dance, gymnastics, music, playgroups, special‑needs classes, sports and family days out. If you’ve got children, we’re organised; if you don’t, we’ll borrow yours for the school holiday craft. The site even used Wish Upon A Party Co as an illustrative local provider, which tells you everything about our weekend priorities.

Back in the day Concrete Playground swooned over Bombini — Italian fine dining, apparently served from a cottage‑veranda, with a three‑course menu and produce from a two‑acre vegetable garden and orchard. It’s listed as CLOSED now, so we like to whisper about it at farmers’ markets like it’s a mythical nonna who left town after one too many compliments.

For outings, Sitchu reckoned Block ’n Tackle was well‑loved in Kincumber, and they suggested not missing Hawkesbury Brewing Co over in Lisarow. Translation: we’re low‑key but we know how to enjoy responsibly — or at least responsibly until the kids’ bedtime.

Local drama? Our archive doesn’t give us much to work with. No council wars, no barista rebellions, just a lot of mums’ groups, kids’ listings and one gorgeous restaurant that folded. Honestly, we’ll take it. It means the biggest scandal here is whether the toddler’s glitter glue was organic.

Who would hate living here: nightlife nomads craving neon, people who measure suburb quality by boutique density, anyone allergic to bake sales and craft glue. Who’d love it: families who want classes by the dozen, food nostalgics who still reminisce about Bombini, and people who enjoy a quiet beer run to Lisarow without a queue for a nightclub entrance.

We’ll own our placid, crafty, slightly nostalgic vibe — and yes, we’re fine with being the place where someone once grew tomatoes on two acres and called it haute cuisine.
