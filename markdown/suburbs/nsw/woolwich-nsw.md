# Woolwich (NSW)

Slug: `woolwich-nsw`

We like to think Woolwich is the kind of place where time slows to the rhythm of a ferry horn and a deckchair being unfolded — and not just because everyone’s on peninsula time. Since there aren’t any recent write-ups, we’re riffing off the archives: the papers used to sing about our wraparound balcony, our jacarandas and our uncanny ability to make a Sunday feel like a private club luncheon.

Back in the day the Woolwich Pier Hotel — a local that’s been here since 1885 and got a shiny revamp in 2018 — was the pièce de résistance. Reviews loved the colonial‑clubhouse fit‑out, the chicken pot pie and the balcony that lets you watch the harbour and skyline have their little meet‑cute. Urban List used to list its Friday happy hour, pulled pork belly sliders and meat‑raffles; Broadsheet noted the menu flirtations with miso salmon and duck confit l’orange. Concrete Playground and Time Out treated the place like a lower‑north‑shore institution with private rooms and long memories.

Our weekend brew used to be less about queues and more about views: guides recommended starting near The Old School House, meandering past jacarandas on the hour‑and‑a‑half loop, then flirting with the idea of a dip at Woolwich Baths before the obligatory post‑walk nap. Deckhouse — that glass‑fronted café at Clarke Point Reserve — earned mentions as the place you booked for wedding receptions and brunches with a harbour backdrop. Cucinetta got name‑checked as the old Italian with views of Lane Cove River and the Harbour Bridge. Ironwood Coffee Company cropped up in listings as the family‑run corner that does Greek‑leaning all‑day fare.

Petty local drama? Mostly the kind you get when a sleepy peninsula gets praised to kingdom come: are we exclusive, luxurious, ridiculous? Time Out put Woolwich among Sydney’s wealthiest suburbs and Sitchu called us an old‑world enclave of waterfront mansions and parkland. We own that. We also own the jacaranda photo you’ve already taken.

Who would hate living here: anyone who needs late‑night club chaos, discount footpath markets or even a proper high‑rise skyline view from their balcony. Who would love it: people who want picnic sunsets at Clifton Gardens, quiet harbour swims at Woolwich Baths and a pub that remembers its history while serving slightly fancier pub food. We’re small, scenic and smug — and we quite like it that way.
