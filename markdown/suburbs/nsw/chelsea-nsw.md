# Chelsea (NSW)

Slug: `chelsea-nsw`

We wake up, roll our windows down and inhale the kind of sea air you’d expect from a place people keep confusing with somewhere else. We’re Chelsea — the one on the South Coast of NSW — and yes, we know our name shows up in more glossy places than we do.

Back in the day Concrete Playground pointed out our little local crown jewel: Chelsea Haircutters. Peter Thomsen — the leading stylist and Global Dyson Styling Ambassador — has been holding court there for over 25 years, so if you remember getting a proper blow‑out before a wedding, that probably happened under his lights (as Concrete Playground noted). That’s our version of glamour. No neon galleries, no nine‑metre waratah sculptures — just a salon that knows how to make the rest of the coast look presentable.

Meanwhile, every fashion list under the sun keeps flirting with the word “Chelsea” — Broadsheet name‑checking “Chelsea styles” among boot silhouettes — and suddenly everyone thinks we’re a shoe. We’re flattered, honestly, but no: Broadsheet was talking boots, not beachfront patios.

Tourists? Bless them. They arrive expecting the High Line and galleries (Urban List) or a chic French‑American bistro on W 23rd (that’s Cafe Chelsea in Manhattan at 218 W 23rd Street, by the way) — and we spend a delightful five minutes explaining that “Chelsea” is a popular name. We also get mistaken for the Chelsea Australian Garden in Olinda (Time Out), with its enormous waratah and winding billabong. We admire that garden from afar. We are not that garden.

Who would hate living here? People who need an every‑weekend artisan market or permanent gallery crawl. Or anyone who refers to us only because they want a photo next to a nine‑metre sculpture.

Who would love it? Folks who enjoy a long‑running salon where the hair people know your wedding date, those who appreciate being conveniently misidentified by travel writers, and anyone who prefers local, lived‑in character over curated tourist clichés.

For years the press has put “Chelsea” on other maps — and honestly, that suits us just fine.
