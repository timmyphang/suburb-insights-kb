# Blaxland (NSW)

Slug: `blaxland-nsw`

Back in the day our weekend kicked off like a well-rehearsed community theatre: we’d stumble out of bed, throw the kids in whatever was clean, and head for the parks because that’s what Blaxland does — parks and low-key spectacle. Full disclosure: our clippings are older, so think of this as a nostalgia tour rather than a current events briefing.

We start at St John’s Park, where the playground isn’t pretending to be minimal — it’s proper inclusive, with scaling walls, nest swings, a sensory corner and a shiny new accessible toilet block. It’s the sort of place where birthday parties look like a small UN summit of scooters and cupcakes. If that’s taken, Lennox Park is our quiet rival: bike and scooter path, tennis courts and enough open grass to toss a ball until someone asks for a break.

For the person who still believes trains are the peak of civilisation, Wascoe Siding Miniature Railway has been doing its thing since 1966. The Blue Mountains Railway Society ran those tiny locomotives on the first Sunday of the month (Feb–Dec, 10–3), and yes, they advised enclosed shoes — because nothing says safety like tiny platform etiquette.

We’re embarrassingly proud that Sitchu slid our name into its Blue Mountains restaurant roundup — “From Katoomba to Leura, Blackheath to Blaxland” — which is the online equivalent of being invited to the grown-up table. And if you like houses that come with actual character (and a backyard that backs onto bushland), real estate listings used to brag about 28 Centre Crescent’s 691sqm block, 15 Hersey Street’s stone benches and flow, and 40 Old Bathurst Road’s tall ceilings, double carport and a detached studio. Not glamorous — honest.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe children’s laughter, miniature steam engines that are inexplicably charming, or yards that promise a possum as a next-door neighbour, Blaxland will feel like a long weekend you can’t escape.

Who’ll love it? Families, small-town romantics, train nerds and anyone after a house that backs onto actual bush. We’ll keep the barbie warm and the miniature railway on standby — just don’t arrive without enclosed shoes.
