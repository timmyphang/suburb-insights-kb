# Moreland (NSW)

Slug: `moreland-nsw`

Moreland, NSW — we’ll admit it: our morning ritual used to be dramatic in the most charming way. Back in the day we’d wander past the Moreland Hotel on the corner of Sydney Road and Moreland Road, admire that unassuming north façade and wonder who thought a Roman courtyard belonged behind a regular pub door. Then we’d keep walking to the strip at the Moreland Road intersection and breathe in the bakery steam from Der Partisan — those European loaves were practically a local miracle.

Weekends were a choose-your-own-adventure: plant people could get lost in the wonderland inside Leaf & Bear on Colebrook St, while the adrenaline crowd booked a night at Ukiyo on 54 Moreland Road for escape-room theatrics. If you were into music and art, Mycelium Studios on Moreland Road used to host free exhibitions during the Brunswick Music Festival and the whole place felt like a community that rehearsed loudly and proudly.

We’ve also seen the suburb be theatrically imperfect. There was that hectic video of a PSO incident at Moreland Station — proof that even our commute can become cinema. And Howler’s amps have been through a few planning storms; the venue’s stubborn survival is practically a local hobby. The Merri Creek Trail strolls, stopping at De Chene Reserve, provided peaceful dog-park interludes between Sydney Road’s sensory overload and a few too many development proposals.

Who would hate living here? If you crave suburban silence, zero tram noise and no strange late-night gigs, this place will test your patience. If you hate queuing for good bread, love a beige shopping strip, or think a Melbourne lane is subtle — we’re probably not compatible.

Who would love it? Artists, musicians, bakers, plant hoarders and anyone who enjoys a neighbourhood that makes a racket for the right reasons. If you once queued at Der Partisan, got lost in Leaf & Bear, or cheered at a Mycelium Studios show, you know what we mean: Moreland has always been gloriously, unapologetically itself — a little rough, a little loud, and endlessly sentimental. We’d argue it’s worth the occasional drama.
