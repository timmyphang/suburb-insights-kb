# Oran Park (NSW)

Slug: `oran-park-nsw`

We still chuckle that one complex tried to be the whole postcode. As Time Out noted, the Oran Park Hotel at 81 Central Avenue used to arrive like a $50-million answer to “what if a pub had seven bars, two cinemas and a rooftop?” We’d nip down on weekends and pretend we were in a tiny, very loud city — three levels, a retro pizzeria, an open‑air atrium and enough function rooms to host every christening and corporate awards night simultaneously.

Our weekday shuffle was less glamorous: drop-offs at Great Beginnings on 1 Laura Street, a swing past Grand Prix Park on Leffler Street for the small people to exhaust themselves, then the grown-ups would compare playgroup notes (MyTime Currans Hill ran on Tuesdays at the Currans Hill Community Hall, 2 Lando Way). Camden Kids and Camden Mums Hub kept us organised; they were the unsung event managers of suburban life.

There was a gentle arms race in the background. We weren’t the only ones building multi‑venue hubs — Parc Pavilion got name‑checked alongside the Oran Park Hotel, and the precinct scene suddenly felt like a competition to see who could fit the most bars under one roof. As Sitchu cheerfully reported, the Hotel used to be marketed as a 7,500 sqm hospitality colossus; we liked to say it doubled as a landmark and a GPS fail-safe.

If you hated spectacle, Oran Park might have felt like living inside a brochure. If you could not abide pistachio for breakfast, Banksia Cafe’s pistachio‑drizzled waffles and pancakes (yes, Urban List wrote about those) would have been your personal torment. If you found the idea of lighting displays twee, South Circuit’s inclusion on Time Out’s Christmas lights list was not going to win you over.

Who would loathe living here? People who crave anonymous streets and zero events — this place was designed to be noticed. Who would love it? Families who wanted everything within a short drive: cinemas and a kids’ play area at the big precinct, community playgroups, Jan’s Wonderland on 21 Sargent Street, Hoopsters at Oran Park High School (1 Podium Way), and even Fussy Grape down at Oran Park Drive and Camden Valley Way for a weekend detour.

We’re proud of our slightly overachieving little corner — ostentatious, child‑friendly and unashamed about being loud.
