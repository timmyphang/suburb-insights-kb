# Sefton (NSW)

Slug: `sefton-nsw`

Back in the day we’d wander down 66 Clapham Road and the whole strip felt like someone’d installed a permanent night market in the middle of suburbia. Spicetown used to take over that spot — Concrete Playground remembers it as a neon-powered hawker hub with more than 25 stalls, running Thursday to Sunday. We’d stand under the glow, eating whatever smelled the loudest and pretending not to be dazzled by the aesthetics borrowed from Spicetown Leichhardt.

Our weekend ritual used to be gloriously simple: pick up whatever Shepherds Artisan Bakehouse had just made — Urban List even listed its Sefton outpost alongside Glebe and Castle Hill when they were writing about the best sausage rolls — then meander past the market stalls while asking ourselves if we were food critics or just very committed grazers. Concrete Playground’s events page once crowed about the small-scale cultural takeover our suburb could pull off; for a while, Sefton was quietly doing festival energy on a local budget.

We know what people say about us: “quaint,” “a bit random,” “where did that much neon come from?” We own it. If you like your nights curated by a string of paper lanterns and your weekends ruled by the scent of frying and flaky pastry, you’ll get along with us famously. If you hate eclectic night markets, artisanal sausage rolls, or the idea of a good old-fashioned crowd turning Clapham Road into a global food alley, then this isn’t your vibe — and frankly, that’s fine by us.

There isn’t a lot of current fuss in the files we dug up — most write-ups we found are from earlier rounds — so think of this as a fond scrapbook: Spicetown’s neon nights and Shepherds’ sausage-roll acclaim used to be the kind of thing that made us grin with civic pride. If you like places that turn up the colour, the smell and the enthusiasm without asking permission, Sefton used to be our perfectly messy little anthem. We miss it, and we’re glad someone noticed.
