# Beaumaris (NSW)

Slug: `beaumaris-nsw`

Back in the day we’d amble down Beaumaris Concourse like it was our local runway — only with less drama and better pinot. We used to start weekends pretending we’d only pop in for a coffee and inevitably end up at Beau Kitchen & Cellar (the Beaumaris Hotel redevelopment spot with that very grown-up Bertha oven) because who can resist the smell of pastry and civic pride?

We used to buy a bottle next door at Rack and Ruin, where they quietly rotated four to six new drops a week and made pinot noir sound like a personality trait. Or we’d get ambitious and queue for the drinkable-Dubai-chocolate spectacle at Beau’s Frozen Yoghurt & Smoothie Bar on 48 East Concourse — yes, it was a thing, and yes, we filmed it for posterity.

There was a time Commonfolk was the morning sun in cafe-form — light-filled, donating 20 cents from each coffee to The Cup That Counts, making us feel magnanimous for the price of caffeine. Madoljai on N Concourse used to remind us that Thai cooking can be nostalgic and mysterious at once. At night Le Bar Supper Club whispered 1920s excess and made us feel faintly ashamed of our Netflix habits.

We didn’t always agree. During COVID a few of our local haunts made odd calls — Saska Cafe even temporarily refused reusable cups — and we loudly debated sustainability over take-away napkins. A certain eatery dangled $2 Blackman Bay oysters on Wednesdays and we debated morality versus value while loosening belts.

Who would hate Beaumaris? If you worship inner-city chaos, crave neon lights, or think coastal art trails are a waste of a walk, this place will bore you. If you can’t stand artists named in plaques — Arthur Boyd, Frederick McCubbin — or the polite ritual of dog-owners at the Beaumaris dog beach starting at the southern wooden ramp toward the yacht club, move along.

But if you love a tidy Concourse, seaside walks to the Ricketts Point Marine Sanctuary, community craft nights (shoutout to Rae Easton and Bayside Paint and Sip), mild indulgence, and the small-town assumption that everyone sort-of-knows everyone else’s business — you’ll adore it here. We do, awkwardly and proudly.
