# Davidson (NSW)

Slug: `davidson-nsw`

It’s a Tuesday evening and Borgnis Street is already practicing its annual glow-up — think Christmas Vegas but with stubbies replaced by fairy lights and a very earnest collection tin. We’ve watched the neighbours go full production every year, all in the name of raising money for Bear Cottage, and honestly, if community spirit had a postcode it’d have our street number.

Our weekend routine is low-key: a jog past the house at 121 Prahran Avenue (yes, the one the listing rhapsodised about — three good‑sized bedrooms with built‑ins, floorboards throughout and that “modern kitchen with dishwasher and electric cooking”), then a detour to admire which block has out‑swung the others in tinsel. Back in the day Broadsheet even used our surname-adjacent fruit in recipes — Analiese Gregory and Clayton Wells’ Davidson’s plum‑glazed lamb ribs made the plum feel like a national treasure, and Broadsheet’s guide to native food suggested the fruit was the sort of tart, jammy thing that turns sauces into conversation pieces. Time Out liked it frozen into sorbet once, too — apparently zingy and refreshing, if you’re into that.

There isn’t a scandal to unpack here — no council coups, no hipster takeover, no dramatic coffee‑shop closures — which is both incredibly boring and weirdly comforting. If you moved here hoping for salvoes of drama you’ll be sorely disappointed; the closest we get is debating who put up more lights on Borgnis and whether the soundtracks should lean Carols or Queen.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t stand a street that converts to a charity spectacle come December, or if the thought of native‑fruit lore being the town’s cultural export makes you twitch, Davidson isn’t your vibe. We also suspect anyone allergic to floorboards, built‑ins and a sensible kitchen layout will sob quietly in the city.

Who loves it here? Families who actually like neighbours, buyers who savour a practical three‑bed with a study, and anyone who appreciates that our name gets attached to a plum tasty enough to star in Broadsheet recipes and Time Out sorbets. We’ll keep the lights on — you bring the pavlova.
