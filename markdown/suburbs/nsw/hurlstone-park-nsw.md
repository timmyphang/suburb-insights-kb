# Hurlstone Park (NSW)

Slug: `hurlstone-park-nsw`

We stand on Canterbury Road like we own the footpath — which, frankly, given how long some joints take to open, feels about right. Walk down our high street and the past keeps showing up in all the best ways: Broadsheet once said Frankly Nick’s was the first place to serve dinner in the area in more than three decades, and yes, the slices were the sort of cheese-pull you used to boast about at Sunday arvos. A few doors down, Urban List once listed My Sweet Boutique at 342 Canterbury Road — Nutella-filled waffles that made the school run smell suspiciously like dessert.

Back in the day we celebrated the small victories: HP Bowlo arrived as a small bar homage to the old bowls club (the original was demolished in 2017), serving local craft beer, tinned cocktails and Friday arvo meat tray raffles supplied by an outstanding butcher around the corner. Time Out noted the Canterbury‑Hurlstone Park RSL had been around for decades too — the sort of place that anchors a suburb whether you’re into schnitzel or just into having somewhere to complain about the trains.

Speaking of trains, Time Out ran the drama: the T3 Bankstown line was set to close for works and the eventual metro maps put Hurlstone Park on the proposed route. Translation: we oscillate between smug about being “on the map” and waiting a very long time for it to actually arrive.

Nu Bambu used to be the weird restaurant-in-an-RSL story you told visitors — Concrete Playground later listed it as closed, which is our suburb’s unofficial tagline: charming, slightly mysterious, and excellent at providing history lessons through shuttered storefronts.

Who would hate living here? If you need a rotating carousel of three new cafes a week, constant late-night buzz, or think meat-tray raffles are beneath you, you’ll find Hurlstone Park maddeningly unrefined.

Who would love it? Families — CareforKids listings used to show plenty of childcare options (Hurlstone Park Children’s Centre on Smith Avenue, Noah’s Ark on Duntroon Street, plus several others) — people who like pockets of sweetness (literal and figurative), a good slice, a hidden op-shop detour into Dulwich Hill, and a weekly meat raffle to make you feel predictably local. We’re small, stubborn and unapologetically ours — and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
