# Brooklyn Park (NSW)

Slug: `brooklyn-park-nsw`

We’ll say this straight: back in the day Brooklyn Park felt like someone repurposed a council chamber into a mid‑century daydream and then wondered why the dogs never left. As Broadsheet noted, Yellow Matter set up shop inside the old council chambers — think pale timber, archways and a red‑and‑white pool table — and Karma & Crow eventually moved from Richmond to share the space, turning the whole thing into a cafe-by-day, brewery-by-night contraption that made our weekends feel like one long, tasteful repartee.

Our morning ritual used to be a mournful shuffle past the municipal building turned pizza-and-beer temple. Yellow Matter brewed on site and served veg‑forward pizzas while the pool table offered a taciturn commentary on our weekend competitiveness. Chicco Palms, which Broadsheet covered when it opened in 2017, brought its California-ish, Roman‑style pizzas to the neighbourhood — because why have two great ham-and-cheese options when you can have a perfectly blistered crust and indecision?

We aren’t blind to the irony: a former council chamber now hosting artisanal brews and hand-stretched dough is exactly the sort of thing that makes someone from a neighbouring postcode tut into their cappuccino. As Urban List pointed out, the fit‑out looked suspiciously boutique — which is a polite way of saying we’re all simultaneously proud and mildly embarrassed by how well the place dresses.

No petty council war to report here — the only real drama was a tasteful migration: Karma & Crow leaving Richmond after eight years to nestle into Yellow Matter’s orbit (as Broadsheet explained). That’s the kind of gentle soap opera we enjoy: relocations, ovens fired up, a pool cue slamming, and a dog approvingly circling your chair.

Who would hate living here? People allergic to mid‑century aesthetics, small‑batch brewing or any neighbourhood that thinks a pool table is a public service. Who would love it? Pizza obsessives, beer appreciators and anyone who enjoys a suburb that upgraded its civic architecture into somewhere you can spend an afternoon pretending you’re on holiday.

For years the local scene read like a tasteful postcard — slightly smug, undeniably cosy, and forever smelling faintly of wood‑fired crust. We wouldn’t have it any other way.
