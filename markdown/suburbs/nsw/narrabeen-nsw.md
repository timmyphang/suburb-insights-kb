# Narrabeen (NSW)

Slug: `narrabeen-nsw`

We are the ones who set an alarm for a sunrise at Narrabeen Tidal Pool and then pretend we didn’t. Back in the day the guidebooks gossiped that the wooden wharf makes for an "epic photo opportunity" — and, sure, it does. We stand there, salt in our hair, watch the sun peel over the ocean and feel superior to everyone who slept through it.

Our weekend looks the same: a lap of the 8.4km Narrabeen Lagoon circuit (Concrete Playground had the map), a wobble on a paddle-board in the lagoon because Broadsheet swore it's a top paddle spot, then a stroll up Ocean Street where Mexicano, Fat Pomelo and Barbuto used to buzz opposite the beach. Bonfire Bread used to be our excuse to buy morally righteous pastries. Oceans across from the sand used to play live music and pour Allpress Espresso while the crowd debated whether the surf was better than Collaroy. We’re nostalgic, not delusional.

Local drama? We have it in muted tones. The Narrabeen RSL was, historically, "in troubled waters" (Junkee), which gave us the kind of small-town theatre no one advertises. There’s also the Wakehurst Parkway ghost stories people tell on night drives (Pedestrian TV), and the occasional reminder that storms have done nasty things around here — landslides and flood warnings have featured in the news. It’s not a soap opera; it’s coastal reality.

Who would hate living here? If you despise sand-in-every-crevice, think an 8.4km boardwalk is excessive, or need an urgent train line yesterday — yes, there’s been talk of better rail and bridge works (Pedestrian TV), but it’s not exactly a quick fix — you’ll be miserable.

Who’d love it? Surfers who worship the famous break, paddleboarders who treat Narrabeen Lagoon like their cathedral, families who sign up for Tuesday Dance Baby at Lakeview Hall, Narrabeen Tramshed, and anyone who remembers Bonfire Bread and a ferry beer from 4 Pines with a fond smile. We’ll keep our sandy feet, our sunrise photos, and our slightly smug seaside calm — and if you can’t handle that, head inland. We’ll still wave from the tidal pool.
