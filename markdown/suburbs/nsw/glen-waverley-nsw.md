# Glen Waverley (NSW)

Slug: `glen-waverley-nsw`

Back in the day we used to stroll down Kingsway like we owned the weekend — kids on scooters, parents juggling shopping bags from The Glen Shopping Centre and takeaways that guaranteed you’d eat with chopsticks in one hand and a soft-serve cone in the other. The place had that deliciously specific identity: a shopping mall, a train line, and a dining strip that refused to be anonymous.

We remember lining up for Shira Nui’s omakase — Hiro Nishikura’s piece-by-piece approach made waiting lists feel like performance art. Andzero on High St Road used to tempt us with wagyu hamburg rolls, onigiri and pastries from Cobb Lane; Ramen Ramen at The Glen bragged about a 24‑hour tonkotsu broth and three-day-aged noodles; Uni Boom Boom turned sea urchin into a full-time job (sashimi, wagyu, laksa — you name it). Sushi Jiro’s 67‑metre sushi train and all-you-can-eat soft-serve were the sort of family spectacle that felt uniquely our own. Wagyu Ya’s teppanyaki, Marble Yakiniku, The Boiling Crab, Dumpling Empire, Steak Ministry at Ikon Centre and even Kariton’s Filipino outpost all chipped in to make Glen Waverley the suburb that ate better than you did.

We’ll admit the Glen Waverley line got its own review — Pedestrian TV ranked it #5 and called it “full of school children and normies,” which, frankly, we took as a compliment. Between Glen Waverley Secondary College, Central Reserve, Monash Aquatic Centre and childcare like Kids on Gallaghers at 254 Gallaghers Road, the suburb was built for carting small people around with dignity (or as much dignity as you can have when you’re diving into uni-topped wagyu).

Who would hate living here? People who can’t handle choice, or who recoil at the idea of a 67‑metre sushi train. Who would love it? Families, foodies, and the investor types Sitchu kept whispering about when it touted Glen Waverley as a smart buy — hey, those 35 Arlington Drive and 3/1 Frank Street listings didn’t appear out of nowhere.

So yes, we know what they say about us. They also missed the bit where our late‑morning chaos tastes astonishingly good.
