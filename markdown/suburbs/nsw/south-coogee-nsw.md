# South Coogee (NSW)

Slug: `south-coogee-nsw`

Back in the day we’d stumble out of bed, lace up for the Coogee Steps near Cairo Street and call that our cardio. The stairs made us feel coastal and key‑fit at once — or at least that we tried. Afterward, we’d nurse our aching calves while reminiscing about the small neighbourhood haunts that gave South Coogee its particular hum.

We used to queue for whatever Bakehouse South Coogee was baking that week, while the villagey Lion and the Buffalo on 203 Malabar Road kept rabbit porcelain and a Fintan Magee mural on display like a suburban art tribute. The South Coogee Bowling Club’s revamp — Bistro on the Greens — turned the greens into a place with banana‑leaf wallpaper, a kids play area and pizzas you could eat with pride. Coffeeroom Coogee cropped up on the strip and made paninis respectable again. Broadsheet’s old guide even pointed out that Coogee’s cafes stretched from the beachside up into the leafy backstreets — quaint, tidy, and full of people who remembered when the busiest thing was a Sunday bowlers’ lunch.

Council bureaucracy was our quiet soap opera: there were plans to stitch the coast from South Coogee to Lurline Bay, courtesy of Randwick Council’s $4.9 million buy on Cuzco Street, with concept designs and consultations pencilled in for 2025–26. Meanwhile the Ivo Rowe Rockpool got a new staircase — fibre‑reinforced polymer and stainless steel — and was slated to reopen on October 21, 2024. Progress, but not the sort of drama that needs reality TV.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe stairs, community bowls clubs, or the idea that your Sunday run ends with sea spray and a bakery queue, yeah — this isn’t your scene. If you need endless nightlife or a postcode that never stops being flashy, go elsewhere.

Who’d love it? Families who liked Bistro on the Greens’ kids’ corner, swimmers who missed the rockpool, runners who regard the Coogee Steps as character building, and anyone who appreciates a village strip where the Lion and the Buffalo used to treat local sourcing like a civic duty. We’re low‑key, salty and stubbornly proud — and we’ll keep pretending those stairs are optional.
