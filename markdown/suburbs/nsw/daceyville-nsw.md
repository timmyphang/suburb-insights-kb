# Daceyville (NSW)

Slug: `daceyville-nsw`

Back in the day we used to brag that Daceyville still ran on community timetables: school tours at Daceyville Public School on Joffre Crescent, PCYC Eastern Suburbs doing the school‑holiday shuffle, and Elizabeth Springs Christmas Trees turning the PCYC carpark into a festive forest on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays until they sold out. The internet still remembers all of it — Eastern Suburbs Mums had the listings, and MumsPages keeps the directories for The Parents Village at 24 Isaac Smith St and Smarty Pants Kidz.

We start our weekend like a true local memory lane: drop the kids off at the PCYC holiday program (they had that April session once upon a time), swing past the Parents Village if someone needs perinatal advice, and wave to whoever is running the multicultural stall from Sydney Multicultural Community Services on General Bridges Crescent. Later there’s the old ritual of checking notices for Daceyville Public School tours — because even our smallest cul‑de‑sacs have a bulletin board that knows more about the term dates than we do.

There’s no juicy council scandal in the files we’ve been given, which is somehow reassuring. The pages are full of useful, gentle things: NDIS provider lists, pamper‑party vendors, childcare listings like Hibiscus Children’s Centre, and even Southern Cross Care’s residential aged care on Gwea Avenue. If you were hoping for a catfight over parking, the archives instead give you playgroups, cricket coaching at Pro Performance Cricket and community service posts. Very civil. Very suburban. Very wholesome.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe baby groups, four‑week holiday programs, and an existence that includes seasonal tree buying at the PCYC, Daceyville will feel like slow‑motion suburbia — all the community and none of the hype. If your idea of fun is a surprise nightclub, we’re probably not your scene.

Who would love it? Young families, perinatal support seekers, grandparents after a steady neighbourhood, or anyone who wants a place where the local noticeboard could double as a social calendar. We’re the kind of suburb where you measure time by school tours, holiday programs and the Christmas tree run — and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
