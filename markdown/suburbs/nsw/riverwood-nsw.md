# Riverwood (NSW)

Slug: `riverwood-nsw`

Back in the day — and yes, our briefing only brought us nostalgia — we’d amble out for a Saturday that tasted like a very particular kind of local pride.

We knew the rhythm: queues snaking at Sunshine Asian Supermarket when the Feng family opened their yum cha at 10am, siu mai and har gow flying out the door at about three bucks a pop. We’d try not to elbow anyone out of the way while pretending we weren’t there for the barbeque pork buns.

We used to boast a banh mi that made other suburbs jealous. Bae Mi on Belmore Road got written up as one of Sydney’s best, the kind of place reviewers name-drop in lists and make you feel smug for living "out here". Around town you’d also hear about Kick Start Café — that container-turned-cafe on the corner of Washington Avenue and Kentucky Road — doing its quietly brilliant thing training local young people through the Riverwood Community Centre referral. The Centre itself has been the spine of the place since 1974, with programs, a community garden and a Spring Fair, and more than 2,000 people passing through each week when it was really humming.

On the weekends we’d drive north — well, a proper drive — to Riverwood Downs Mountain Valley Resort for a river-side campsite that welcomes your dog and, allegedly, your existential need for bass: Subsonic used to turn the valley into three days of music and camping. Back home, Rotary Park remained our modest patch of green, the small playground and that outdoor gym you could pretend was a workout.

If you hate community action, curious food queues, multi-day camping festivals or people who fix cars with a weird amount of pride (shout out to Cools Auto Trim on Leedham Place), you’d probably loathe living here. But if you love an actual community centre that helps people, dim sum so good a line forms, banh mi that gets written about, dog-friendly riverside camping and a hotel that hosted trivia and pool nights on Josephine Street, you’d fit right in.

We’re not trying to be the most polished postcode, just the one with the best stories — and, evidently, the best darned pork buns.
