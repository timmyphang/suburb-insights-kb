# Telopea (NSW)

Slug: `telopea-nsw`

We know the exact bench at Sturt Park that catches the sun at 9:15 on a Saturday, because we’ve sat there pretending to read while watching the skate park, the full-size basketball court and the cricket batting cage stage their weekly talent show. Sturt Park isn’t a brochure bullet point — it’s our BBQ-laden living room, complete with picnic tables, toilets and enough footy action to delay any sensible weekend plan.

Back in the day the listings sites liked to flaunt us: Sitchu once put 37 Robert Street on a pedestal — six bedrooms, a short stroll to Oatlands Golf Course and schools like Cumberland High and Carlingford West. We’ll accept the humble flex. Playgroup NSW also treated our parenting heroes right, listing the Telopea Wednesday U2s at 10am at Dundas Area Neighbourhood Centre (21 Sturt Street) — yes, that’s the place where kids rule the thermostat and the biscuits disappear suspiciously fast.

We’ve got pockets of bush too — Elizabeth Macarthur Park is that 2.4-hectare escape with a 300-m path, a cheeky creek under a bridge and swings that actually survive the chaos. Telopea Public School sits opposite Dundas Library so you can drop off the small people and pop in for something educational between snack time and the next playground negotiation. The Telopea Baby Playgroup holds court in the SaCC rooms on Chestnut Avenue, which is as wholesome as it sounds.

No, we don’t have rooftop bars or foodie markets on every corner (and the reviews that confuse Canberra’s Telopea Park with our suburb are nobody’s fault but the internet’s). If you need neon nightlife and artisanal everything, you’ll hate the gentle hum here: the soundscape is more swing set than DJ booth.

Who will love it? Families who actually value a skateboardable life, commuters happy for a train close by, golfers who appreciate Oatlands, and anyone who thinks a well-maintained cricket cage is the height of civic achievement. We’re not flashy. We’re practical, tree-lined and quietly smug about our parks. Pull up a bench — we’ll save you a seat and the last timeless biscuit.
