# Plympton (NSW)

Slug: `plympton-nsw`

Back in the day we used to joke Plympton was the sort of place where your weekend highlight was spotting a new pie at the bakery and nobody blinked when you said “Anzac Highway” like it was a personality trait. We still love it — even if our nightlife is basically a polite nod at The Highway (290 Anzac Highway), which, as listings have reminded tourists, has long been the local pub with a decent Roast of the Day and a garden screen for when sport matters more than small talk.

Our Saturday routine? We’d drift past the Plympton Community Centre on 34 Long St and then argue, very gently, about whether to detour to North Plympton for Banana Boogie. Yes, Banana Boogie — the Belair & North Plympton bakery that used to win national pastry gossip (Australia’s Best Sausage Roll in 2023 and 2024, an award-winning vanilla slice in 2025, and the machine that apparently produced up to 4,000 sausage rolls a day). We treated those wins like local premierships — proud, slightly smug, and ready to queue.

We also remember when British India reopened in 2019 under Arun Dachepalli, serving up a fresher, often-vegetarian take on Indian classics. Little Canton at 295 Anzac Highway cropped up on lists too, reliable for yum cha when family catch-ups weren’t being held in someone’s lounge room.

We’ve got community bones: Play to Grow Playgroup at St John the Baptist Catholic School (342 Anzac Highway) and the Plympton Sporting & Recreation Club at 4 Park Tce mean there’s always a game, a fundraiser, or someone selling raffle tickets for a good cause. Novita’s presence on Anzac Highway reminds us the suburb does the practical, helpful stuff too.

Who would hate it here? If you require a rooftop with more than one plant and a DJ, or you get irrationally offended by the idea of a pub being the cultural centre, this might not be your scene. Who would love it? Families, people who like their bakeries to have accolades, and anyone who enjoys a suburb that’s quietly good at being itself. We may not be flashy, but we’re delicious, practical and stubbornly community-minded — and we’ll fight you for the last sausage roll.
