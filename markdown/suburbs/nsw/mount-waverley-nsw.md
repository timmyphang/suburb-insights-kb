# Mount Waverley (NSW)

Slug: `mount-waverley-nsw`

You know the trick to a Mount Waverley morning: queue at the Pinewood Child and Family Hub for PlayConnect+ on a Wednesday, clutching a sippy cup while someone texts Eva Dimou to confirm the session time. As the local listings used to tell it, PlayConnect+ was Wednesdays 10:15–12:15 at 13 Pinewood Drive — proper suburban choreography, rehearsed.

Back in the day our weekend stopwatch started at Stanley on 63 Stanley Ave — that Melbourne café crew who turned a former milk bar into a daytime eatery. We used to line up for granola, acai bowls, avo on toast, chocolate waffles and the occasional schnitzel, with Inglewood Coffee Roasters doing the heavy lifting at the takeaway window. Son of Tucci at 5 Hamilton Walk quietly earned its stripes too; Concrete Playground even wrote that it opened softly and was warmly received. Piccolino on Blackburn Rd was the long-running local that reminded us lots of things in Mount Waverley last longer than fashion trends.

We’re not above a little municipal gossip: Proud Peacock’s history — the move from Glen Waverley/Pinewood and that fire it survived — is the kind of saga that keeps PTA meetings interesting. And yes, we remember the 28 August 2020 water warning — the one Broadsheet archived where 88 suburbs, Mount Waverley included, were told to boil the kettle. If you think our drama is glamorous, it’s actually about dumpling loyalty: Dumplings Prime in Hamilton Place earned local love, and arguments over whose dumpling is steamier are basically civic duty (Sitchu noted it as a local favourite).

Who would hate living here? People who need 24/7 inner‑city chaos, night-clubbery or endless novelty. If you want skyline noise, you won’t get it — Urban List and Concrete Playground consistently called us leafy and family-friendly, about a 30-minute drive from the CBD. This place isn’t for the restless.

Who would love it here? Families, people who prize parks and top school catchments (hello, Mount Waverley Secondary College), anyone who likes reliable local favourites and a community that knows where the best dumplings and long-running pizza joints are. We’ll be here, holding the pram and the takeaway, politely queuing for another chapter in the suburban saga.
