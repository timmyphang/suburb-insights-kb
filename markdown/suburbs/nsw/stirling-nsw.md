# Stirling (NSW)

Slug: `stirling-nsw`

It’s 8am in Stirling, NSW. We pull back the curtains, take a breath of whatever fresh air we have, and the internet has other plans: boom — Aptos, Justin James’s converted church, is trending in Stirling. Except it isn’t ours. Classic.

Back in the day Broadsheet’s Adelaide directory used to lavish attention on a different Stirling — Aptos, Marshi’s Kitchen, Patch Kitchen & Garden and Stirling Cellars & Patisserie — all with very tasteful lighting and very specific Instagram angles. Time Out and Urban List keep making the same mistake too, pointing cameras at a Stirling ten kilometres north of Perth with a $1.07m median house price, or applauding Bluff Knoll and the Stirling Range for their wildflower season. Meanwhile our Stirling nods politely from the corner and asks, “Did you mean one of the others?”

Our weekend routine is therefore half actual life and half brand management. We mow the lawn, we walk the dog, we check whether Percy’s Playground or Stirling Leisure have hijacked another hashtag (Urban List mentioned those). We are professionals at being patient while the internet confuses us for Adelaide’s Cellars & Patisserie or Perth’s Stirling Bridge. It’s flattering in a detached way.

Local drama? Honestly, the biggest scandal here is that we’re perpetually the understudy. Other Stirlings get degustations, converted churches, two-person steam rooms and repurposed boat bars. Concrete Playground and Sitchu keep trotting out Stirlings from Melbourne to the UK (yes, we see you, Mount Stirling and Cambusbarron). We take it on the chin and wave them through — we’re not needy.

Who would hate living here: people who crave being on every list and craving the prestige of Broadsheet features, Michelin-adjacent tasting menus and a steam room in Room Four. You’ll be frantically emailing editors and still getting links to a vineyard you didn’t even know existed.

Who would love it: people who prefer their suburb to be a little mysterious, who like knowing the best coffee without the crowds (and who don’t mind explaining, repeatedly, that no, we are not the one with the converted church). Come for the anonymity; stay because we’re quietly better at being ourselves.
