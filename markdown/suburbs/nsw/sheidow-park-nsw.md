# Sheidow Park (NSW)

Slug: `sheidow-park-nsw`

We stand by the sandpit next to the veggie patch at the rear of Sheidow Park School and nod like we own the place — because for years the Bush Playgroup did, every Tuesday during school terms, 9:00–10:30am. Back in the day the school at 21–43 Adams Rd felt like a small village: a public primary with a Steiner stream, an OSHC chalked up for Before School Care, After School Care, Early School Finish, Pupil Free Day and Vacation Care, and parents swapping car park gossip between handover shifts.

If you scroll the local mum-blogs you’ll see the same polite obsession: Goodstart Early Learning Sheidow Park on Young Street (1A Young St) used to be the six-thirty-to-six-thirty answer to early commutes, and MumsPages listed every baby class and service you could imagine under “what mums need.” Adelaide Mums Group kept us organised with everything from Kindergym trips to the odd free netball coaching session at Southbank Boulevard Reserve — yes, there was a City of Marion annoucement for a Tuesday afternoon class for 5–18 year-olds, the kind of “free community fun” that reads like a postcard.

There’s no hot take, no scandal in the printouts — mostly directories, class listings and market dates (remember Woodend Spring Market — Sheidow Park, Sun 18 Nov?). That’s the whole local flex: quiet, organised, and a bit agenda-driven. We don’t have recent glossy features; the internet knows us as a neat list of services and community events. So, for years the local online presence has been: mums, kids, programs, repeat.

Who would hate living here? Someone who needs 24/7 nightlife, a skyline of late-night bars or a constant stream of trendy pop-ups. If your idea of excitement is a boutique opening every other week, you’ll find our calendar a little too wholesome.

Who would love it? Families who want reliably scheduled activities, bush playgroup sandpit chats, a Steiner option at the primary, OSHC cover for the working week, and markets that still smell like doughnuts and second-hand books. We’re modest, practical, and proudly a postcode for people who pack snacks and a spare hat.
