# Huntingdale (NSW)

Slug: `huntingdale-nsw`

We live here, so we’ll say it: Huntingdale is the suburb that quietly refuses to choose a vibe. Back in the day a converted warehouse became Kaiju! Cantina — a full-on, retro ’70s beer hall with 16 taps, colourful murals and cheeseburger-style pizzas (and yes, a Kaiju Krush if you fancied a tropical pale ale). Broadsheet and Time Out loved telling the story; we loved the noise.

Our Saturdays look like an indie romcom montage: drop the small people at Sonas Huntingdale (they’ve had a mini remodel, bigger atelier and a nature-play zone where kids learn to get gloriously messy), then wander past the place that used to be the local eco-cake haunt Urban List praised for zero-waste bakes. If nostalgia’s your thing, remember Pixel Bar on North Road — 1279 North Rd — where console wars, jaffles and cocktails once collided into one gloriously nerdy night out.

There’s a practical side too. Broadsheet wrote about the Cranbourne–Pakenham sky rail plans that promised a 17-kilometre walking-and-cycling trail and more than 430 extra car spaces across Carnegie, Hughesdale, Huntingdale and Clayton — which is council-speak for “we’ll give you more places to park your weekend problems.”

The petty drama? It’s not a council feud, it’s economics. Junkee reminded us that students cram into four‑bed share houses round here, paying rent the rest of us pretend not to remember — $900 a month back then, and a 20-minute bus to campus that turns studying into a commute sport. We feel for them, but we also know that cheap eats survived it: Time Out kept pointing out Chef Wong as the small family-run yum cha spot worth queueing for.

Who would hate living here: people who need their suburbs sparkling like a lifestyle blog — if you recoil from murals, arcade beeps or the idea of a warehouse pizzeria, best keep driving.

Who would love Huntingdale: parents who value Sonas’ messy masterpiece zones, beer pilgrims who once sought out Kaiju’s taps, gamers who miss Pixel Bar’s projector nights, and anyone who appreciates a suburb that’s happily a bit of everything, all at once. We wouldn’t change it — even if we sometimes wish it’d pick a playlist.
