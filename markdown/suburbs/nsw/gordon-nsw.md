# Gordon (NSW)

Slug: `gordon-nsw`

Back in the day we liked to pretend Gordon was a secret—until half the North Shore rolled up every 2nd Sunday for the Gordon Markets and discovered our antiques, plants, opals and slightly alarming quantity of LED lighting. The markets have been under the shelter beside Gordon Station for over 30 years, run by the Rotary Clubs of Ku‑ring‑gai and Turramurra, so yes, our bric‑a‑brac comes with pedigree and a loyalty card.

Our weekend ritual used to involve a cautious circuit: a stroll down St Johns Avenue (remember when Ku‑ring‑gai Council turned it into an "Eat Street" with landscaped terraces, widened footpaths, street furniture and new paving?), a meander past the market stalls, and a sidelong glance at the kitchen theatre that was Bar Infinita on 10 Saint Johns Avenue — they even served a winter truffle special in 2025, because suburban moderation is for other suburbs.

We also have the things that make locals smug: a new playground at Gordon Recreation Ground with climbing nets and swings, tennis courts within earshot, and street parking that still feels like an amenity rather than a competitive sport. The Gordon Community Centre at 110 Lewis Luxton Avenue keeps the tiny tyrants entertained with playgroups, and the Gordon Preschool Playgroup at Gordon Primary School on 11 Knoke Avenue is at least trying to be theatrical about reopening plans for 2027.

There’s no messy celebrity scandal here, no fake‑outrage headlines—just tasteful terraces and monthly bargains on vintage watches. If you’re the sort of person who hates gentility, alfresco dining and rotary‑run markets, you will loathe living here. If you need an eternal rager and potholes to scream into, drive elsewhere.

But if you like leafy streets, a market that will sell you an opal and a succulent in the same transaction, tidy playgrounds where children practise being yuppies in training, and a council building at 818 Pacific Highway to remind you that somebody is still doing planning maps—well, we’ve got your postcode. We’ll keep the terraces polished and the markets running; you bring the curiosity and sensible shoes.
