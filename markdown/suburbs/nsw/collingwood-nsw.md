# Collingwood (NSW)

Slug: `collingwood-nsw`

Back in the day we’d wander down Smith Street with our pockets full of optimism and our ears full of whatever band was finishing up at the Gaso — which famously shut, then reopened under Scott Pickett as the Smith St Hotel, bandroom and all. That was the kind of petty local theatre we live for: landlords making decisions, restaurateurs making comebacks, and us pretending we weren’t taking notes.

Mornings used to mean a lap of the old favourites: a detour past Alimentari on Smith Street for the rotisserie smells, a peek into Collingwood Yards to see who’d claimed the next affordable studio, and the occasional pilgrimage to Proud Mary — the warehouse café that trained baristas and hardened our sense of inner‑north superiority. Meg Tanaka’s Cibi at 33–39 Keele Street was one of those anchors, and STREAT’s Cromwell Street flagship breathed that industrious, dough‑kneading energy into the neighbourhood for years.

Our nightlife pedigree was equally brag‑worthy and slightly chaotic: Easey Street’s roof‑top train carriages, the basement micro‑club the New Guernica crew launched, and the promise of the Eclipse single‑screen cinema at 32 Wellington Street — all the kinds of places you’d tell your out‑of‑town mate about to make them feel cultured and exhausted in the same sentence. Slowpoke Lounge & Lookout perched on Zero Gipps? Yep, we’d watch the city like it was our rooftop soap opera.

Who would hate living here? People who need neat hedges, quiet cul‑de‑sacs or chain‑store predictability. If you crave an immutable postcode where nothing ever changes, Collingwood will test your patience with its restored workers’ cottages, warehouse conversions and the occasional new taqueria (Santito on Smith Street sprang up in a formerly devout spot). We’re a bit noisy, a bit gritty, and emphatically not anonymous.

Who would love it? Creatives who want Collingwood Yards’ cheap(ish) studios, food obsessives who remember Zareh’s charcoal oven ambitions, parents who appreciate the Collingwood Children’s Farm, and anyone who thinks a neighbourhood should have a bandroom you can actually hear from your balcony. We’re loud, opinionated and endlessly renovating ourselves — and frankly, we love it that way.
