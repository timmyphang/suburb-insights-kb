# Tweed Heads (NSW)

Slug: `tweed-heads-nsw`

We start our weekend the way locals used to — by pretending we live on both sides of a state line. Note: our dossier has no recent pieces, so we’re speaking in past-tense souvenirs and Broadsheet itineraries, not current council press releases.

Back in the day we’d wander down Bay Street, which Urban List cheekily suggested felt like “the next James Street,” and queue for pastry glory at The Bread Social (12 Bay Street). If we were feeling virtuous, we’d hit Delightful afterwards for one of their loaded sandwiches and smoothies — the sort of mid-morning refuel that convinces you you’re on holiday even if it’s Tuesday.

By lunchtime we might have been on a Catch a Crab Tour or a Tweed Eco Cruises Aboriginal‑led outing (Concrete Playground loved the bamboo‑spear mud‑crab moment), coming back smelling of river salt and grilled crab we’d earned with sticky fingers. Artsy types among us would insist on the Tweed Regional Gallery and the Margaret Olley Art Centre for cultural capital — Broadsheet and Sitchu both made them non‑negotiable stops on any two‑day plan. We even used to brag about Hiromi Tango at Sullivan+Strumpf when visitors were impressed by our tiny-town-big-art flex.

The border thing gives us endless bragging rights: Duranbah, Fingal Head (Dreamtime Beach), and views of Wollumbin/Mount Warning make it feel like you’re holidaying on someone else’s postcode. Time Out once crowned Tweed Heads a trendspot — and Sitchu tracked the spike in interest — so yes, people notice us getting cooler.

Who would hate it here? If you loathe artisan bakeries, pelicans, or the idea of a pony club hiding behind the airport at Sussyer Street, you’ll be restless. If cinemas and family services are the only things that comfort you, remember HOYTS Tweed City used to sit on Minjungbal Drive and The Family Centre had offices on Frances Street and Amber Road — we’re full of practical anchors too.

Who would love it? Families who rely on MyTime sessions at Amber Road and playgroups at Bridge Club Park; road‑trippers from Brisbane who stop for crab tours and gallery afternoons; anyone who likes their surf, hinterland rainforests and art served with a side of humble local bragging. We’ll keep the secret for now — but not for long.
