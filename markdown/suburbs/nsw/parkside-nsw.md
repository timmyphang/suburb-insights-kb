# Parkside (NSW)

Slug: `parkside-nsw`

We know the feel of Parkside, NSW — the way the gum trees make a neat canopy and our phones keep trying to send us to other Parksides. Saturday morning, we’re pushing the pram around the park and the algorithm is like, “Did you mean Parkside Coffee at Stuart Street Reserve?” or “Fancy a Haigh’s Chocolate Factory tour in Parkside, Adelaide?” No, mate. We’re right here. Literally.

Back in the day the press loved to file every parkside joy under the same heading. Broadsheet once christened a hole-in-the-wall Parkside Coffee (Perth) that served Miller & Baker pastries and Daylight Coffee, while Urban List kept name-checking that same Northbridge window. Concrete Playground wrote a love letter to Outfield in Ashfield — “eat on the grass,” they said — which is gorgeous, but it’s not our corner of grass. Time Out and Sitchu also chimed in with Parkside paeans (Davaar House, Haigh’s, the Parkside Hotel, all very glamorous neighbours… in other cities).

Our petty local drama is therefore mainly clerical: we spend half our lives correcting strangers who send birthday invites to the wrong Postcode. Real estate listings promise “close to the city” and “leafy streets” — yes, thank you Sitchu, we got the vibe — but also a surprising international cast of names who apparently live here in spirit if not in reality.

Who would hate living here? People who demand exclusivity. If you want a suburb that can’t be googled without ending up in Perth, Adelaide or Ashfield, this will annoy you. Also, those who like their suburb to have only one identity. Sorry — we’re polyamorous with place names.

Who would love it? Anyone who likes a quiet, tree-lined option that lets you pretend you live next to a chocolate factory, a parkside cafe, and a boutique spa — even if those are applause from other states. We’ll keep walking the lawns, politely correcting the internet, and laughing when a distant Parkside takes the credit for our picnic spot. Come for the trees, stay because the confusion is excellent small‑talk.
