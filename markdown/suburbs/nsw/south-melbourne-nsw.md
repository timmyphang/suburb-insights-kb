# South Melbourne (NSW)

Slug: `south-melbourne-nsw`

Back in the day we could time our week by the South Melbourne Market: the frantic Saturday shuffle down Cecil and Coventry Street, the way Pizzateca Lupa — run by Lino and Gabriele Torre — made the place smell like a Roman Nonna’s pantry, and the line-ups at Cannoleria and Atypic Chocolate that proved our sweet tooth is, frankly, indecent. For years the market was even named one of the world’s top-rated — Time Out had us equal fourth — which made us tilt our heads like proud show dogs and pretend we always planned to shop ethically.

We live between Southbank and the grassy stretches of Albert Park, so mornings can be a salad of Clarendon Street bustle, Montague Street’s industrial sighs, and the terrace-house vs modern-apartment tango on Park Street. The hospitality pages used to preview new things like Aegli’s lamb shoulder promises and list Lucia at the foot of Eastern Road; the old Hotel South Melbourne’s Clarendon Street revival and pubs like O’Connell’s Hotel, The George Hotel and The Montague popped up in so many guides they felt like distant relatives we only saw at market festivals.

Petty local drama? Oh, we’ve got lore. One Junkee nugget about a bloke fined $1,600 for visiting a “semi-intimate partner” in South Melbourne still gets a chuckle at dinner parties — nothing says inner-city romance like municipal wording and a hefty fine. The Temperance Hall hosted The Tenth Festival of Surrealisms once, which summed up our capacity for high-minded whimsy followed by a quick trip to the pub.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to weekend crowds, boutique homewares, or the sound of enthusiastic parents corralling kids around the market’s Easter egg hunts. Also, if you crave anonymity, the tight-knit scene of Guardian Early Learning — South Melbourne and the playgroup chatter around the new South Melbourne Primary School will find you.

Who loves it: food obsessives, market pilgrims, parents who want community, and people who enjoy living where the city spills onto the streets. We’ll keep holding space for them — and for anyone who brings a cannoli to a poetry reading.
