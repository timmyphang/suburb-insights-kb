# Eastlakes (NSW)

Slug: `eastlakes-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean the stories we can actually find — Eastlakes felt like Sydney’s best-kept practical secret: close to the city, quietly affordable and just weird enough to keep the posers away.

We live where Time Out kindly put us among “the cheapest suburbs within 10km of Sydney’s CBD.” Translation: you can afford four walls and a lawn and still catch a late-night Uber home — until Uber fares crept up, which Time Out also flagged as hitting inner‑south routes, Eastlakes included. So yes, we’re close, but your Uber surge might make you rethink buying a city‑adjacent life unless you enjoy drama and economy-class fare-splitting.

As Broadsheet once noted, Crown Group rolled out plans for Eastlakes Live and an Eastlakes Town Centre — a $1 billion, two‑stage dream with more than 134 apartments, a restaurant promenade, landscaped gardens and even two big supermarkets in the plan (one an Aldi). That was the shiny future on the brochure. Whether it becomes our reality or one of those “rendering vs. reality” romances, we’ll enjoy watching the cranes like a community-wide soap opera.

We can be homey, too. Sitchu once listed 61 George Street as the kind of full‑brick, easy‑care home a young family or downsizer could snag — which says something about who’s moving here: people who want space without the mortgage-shaped aneurysm.

Our headline moments have been eclectic. The Lakes Golf Club made national news after police discovered a body in one of its ponds; it was a grim episode that reminded us being small-time local doesn’t mean you skip the big, sad stories. And on the cultural ledger, the old Eastlakes scout hut once gave a band the raw sound they needed — the Atlantics recorded a B-side there, proof we’ve had a bit of grit and creativity for a long time.

Who wouldn’t love it here? Families and anyone who wants a foot in the eastern suburbs without selling a kidney. Who’d hate it? If you require artisanal everything, instant tramlines and an unbroken supply of brunch flex, this isn’t your love story — yet. We’re affordable, oddly historic, and quietly waiting for whatever the next rendering promises.
