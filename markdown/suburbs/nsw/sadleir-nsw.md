# Sadleir (NSW)

Slug: `sadleir-nsw`

Back in the day our Saturday morning looked less like brunch and more like a group chat vigil for whoever finally updated the MumsPages entry for Sadleir (2168). We pour the tea, we scroll the "Businesses and Service Providers for Mums" listings, and we argue like it’s a family election — because apparently our civic life lives in a directory.

Our weekend ritual is one part practical — checking the MumsPages pages for "Pregnancy and Birth Products, Services and Activities", "Sports for Kids" and "School Holiday Camps and Activities" — and one part performance art. We know which kid needs which camp, which soccer class someone once reviewed, and who typed a single devastating one-star about a trial class three years ago. As MumsPages cheerfully catalogues, Sadleir has been mapped by pushchairs and playdates rather than by cafes with pretentious names, and honestly, that suits us.

There’s no council scandal in the data to drag into the roast, so our local drama is refreshingly low-stakes: the fiercest debate here is whether the MumsPages directory should list more sports options or more holiday camps. Riveting stuff. If you were after a tabloid brawl, you’ll be disappointed — our online community prefers comparative shopping lists to gladiatorial council meetings.

We also carry a curious bit of internet prestige: Lisa Sadleir pops up in the archives as a trilingual mum who writes for Multicultural Kid Blogs and runs Family Life in Spain — a reminder that the name "Sadleir" migrates well. She’s in Andalucía, publishing tips about moving to Spain and raising bilingual kids, which is our aspirational alter-ego while we chase sunscreen and permission slips at home.

Who would hate it here? If you despise children’s yoga schedules or find yourself allergic to directories, you’ll want to look elsewhere. Who would love it? Families, practical mums and dads, anyone who appreciates a suburb whose online life is a reliable resource for pregnancy services, kids’ sports and school holiday options — the sort of place where community is measured in activity listings and shared recommendations.

We’re not flashy. We’re organised, slightly obsessive about which camp has the best review, and quietly proud of it.
