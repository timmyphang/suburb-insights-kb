# Jannali (NSW)

Slug: `jannali-nsw`

Back in the day we were the kind of place people only passed through on the way to somewhere louder. The paperwork in our inbox is old — so this is a fond retro roast based on memories, listings and the occasional nostalgic write-up.

Mornings used to look like this: we trundle down from the platform at Jannali train station, clutching a paper, and queue at West End Espresso — as Broadsheet noted, it basically put us on the coffee map with Reuben Hills beans, Ovvio Organics tea and bread hauled in from Cronulla’s Thoroughbred Bakery. The cafe’s nook overlooking the main strip felt like our living room; even if the files are from an earlier era, we still brag about that corner like it’s heritage.

Weekends were small and excellent. The Union Place Hotel, a stone’s throw from the station, was Time Out’s idea of downtown Chicago in an Al Capone mood — which is peak theatrical for a place opposite a suburban platform. Box Road hosted the Jannali Moonrise Festival (remember November 1–2?), with market stalls, food vendors, a kids’ zone and live music and tribute shows that made us swap our slippers for something festival-appropriate.

There’s also the quieter side: families used The Family Co. centre at 90 Soldiers Road — a short walk from the station with free street parking — for playgroups and mums-and-bubs yoga (six classes for $110 a term, Monday mornings if the program listing was right). Soldiers Road Early Education Centre sat opposite Jannali Public School and had a big outdoor playground that we pretended we built ourselves.

Petty local drama? The archives are disappointingly wholesome. No major council melodramas in these clippings — mostly cafes, a hotel with a costume, and mums swapping snack tips. We like it that way.

Who would hate living here? If your idea of suburbia includes 24/7 bright lights and a rooftop cocktail bar on every corner, Jannali will bore you gently. Who would love it? Families who value a good playground (Charles Orwin Reserve), short bushwalks (the Oyster Gully walk via Box Road) and a main street that remembers your name without having to be reminded.

We’ll keep minding the station, the strip and our modest bragging rights — nostalgia suits Jannali.
