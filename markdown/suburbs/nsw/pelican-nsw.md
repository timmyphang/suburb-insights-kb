# Pelican (NSW)

Slug: `pelican-nsw`

Back in the day we used to joke that Pelican, NSW was a town named after what everyone else thought was our mascot: a bird that looks like it’s permanently mid-eye-roll. We know all the local rituals — the pelican feeding at Waterfront Plaza that draws the same small-circle of awe-struck tourists, and the eco-friendly pelican talks that Marine Wildlife Rescue Central used to run on weekends and daily at 3.30pm during school holidays, as Time Out helpfully noted.

A weekend with us is very simple. We’ll start by watching the Waterfront Plaza pelicans do their theatrical thing (The Urban List made a whole tourist suggestion out of it), wander past the Pelican Pavilion — Concrete Playground once described it as a cafe-style spot serving smoothies, juices, breakfasts and lunches right by the sand — and then argue for precisely 12 minutes about whether that counts as culture or just very wet wildlife content.

We’ll remind you we’re not precious about our namesakes. Broadsheet once used Marrickville’s Pelican at 522 Illawarra Road as shorthand for milk-bar nostalgia — “all-day dogs, classic milkshakes and freshly squeezed juices.” That Marrickville vibe? It’s the sort of retro charm we secretly admire from a respectful distance.

Who would hate living here? People who want an ever-changing, boutique, Can-You-Hear-Me-Now kind of scene. If your ambition is to be first in line for every trend, or you can’t bear the idea of pelicans stealing the photo-op at 3pm, this isn’t your postcode. Also, if you loathe the sound of gulls trying to make a deal with the seagull mafia, you’ll be miserable.

Who’d love it? Parents who want low-stress educational outings (Time Out flagged weekend pelican talks), day‑trippers who appreciate a good fish-and-chips-with-a-show setup, and anyone who likes a place that’s more about shoreline rituals than rooftop cocktails. We’re a bit weathered, mildly comic, and entirely unapologetic about our birds — and honestly, that’s more than enough.
