# Belrose (NSW)

Slug: `belrose-nsw`

We begin our Sunday on the rooftop, because where else do you gossip while buying corn? Back in the day the Belrose Super Centre (4–6 Niangala Close) used to host the Belrose Produce Market up on the carpark — Cambridge Markets carting out bread, veg and the kind of small goods that convince you you’re living your best semi-rural life. As locals, we’ve always known the best way to pretend we’re outdoorsy is to queue for tomatoes with a view of a shopping centre.

Weekends wobble between homemaker errands and culture: the HomeCo complex in Belrose once got the IKEA Plan and Order point treatment, promising kitchen and wardrobe solutions for those mid-reno crises. Concrete Playground even listed OneWorld Collection Belrose for Hamptons-inspired homewares, so if you wanted coastal chic for your new built-in cupboards, Belrose was quietly ready to style it for you. And for the theatrical types, Glen Street Theatre on Blackbutts Road staged things like Agatha Christie’s The Mousetrap — proper grown-up entertainment when we weren’t at the playground.

We haven’t forgotten the time Broadsheet said 4 Pines was slated to open a spot at Glen Street Theatre back in April 2017, promising house-made bread and brunch spilling onto the football oval. Whether that turned into the local institution we imagined or was a fond memory of an ambitious press release, it still ranks as local lore.

Petty local drama? Junkee once flagged a Belrose-born property wunderkind — Stephanie Brennan — for amassing six properties. As Junkee reported, yes, someone from around here was flexing a bit in the tabloids; we clapped, we rolled our eyes, then we went back to the produce market.

Who would hate it here: anyone allergic to kids, sensible preschools and indoor soft-play — Time Out’s old notes on the Belrose Hotel (5 Hews Parade) make it clear we are proud parents-on-a-suburban-safari territory. We’ve got Belrose Community & Children’s Centre, The Village Creative Education, Uniting Forest Preschool and Sustainable OSHC for the organised and the exhausted.

Who would love it: families, renovators, and anyone who wants Hamptons cushions after buying seasonal tomatoes on a supermarket rooftop. We are quietly useful, mildly aspirational and very good at pretending the shopping-centre skyline is a view.
