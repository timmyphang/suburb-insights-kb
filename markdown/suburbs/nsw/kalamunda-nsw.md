# Kalamunda (NSW)

Slug: `kalamunda-nsw`

We know this hill too well — the way the morning light hits Zig Zag Drive and the little parade of vans that used to queue for markets on the Central Mall. Back in the day Broadsheet and UrbanList were the ones whispering our secrets: Mason & Bird was in the Zig Zag Cultural Centre (7 Williams St) and the papers treated it like a hometown celebrity, and yes, Emmanuel Mollois once put a Paris Brest in Kalamunda that people still mention with reverence.

Our weekend ritual used to be embarrassingly wholesome: a circuit of the Kalamunda Farmers Markets in the Central Mall (the listings argued whether it was Saturdays or Sundays — UrbanList vs Sitchu, the only local disagreement more heated than which hill has the better view), a wander through the History Village for a quick lesson in why we like being stubbornly old-school, then a lazy sit in Kalamunda Village pretending we’d go on a Bibbulmun Track section that starts on our doorstep. Time Out loved telling visitors about the Rocky Pool Walk in Kalamunda National Park and that the Bibbulmun Track kicks off here; we’d smile, conceited and muddy.

We have family-friendly credentials too: Kalamunda Water Park at 36 Collins Rd made us look like a town that understood small people with big energy. For the slightly domestic among us, First Avenue Kitchen & Home in Barberry Square (Canning Road) was the lure for fancy candles we didn’t need. Kishi Sushi Bar on Haynes Street got a nod for being quietly competent when everyone else was talking about pies.

We even had culture that wasn’t the usual country-halls-and-bingo sort — the Perth Observatory’s Perth Sky Sculpture Park launched with Mayor Margaret Thomas front and centre, which made our outer-metro eccentricities feel official.

Who would hate it here? Someone allergic to hills, hikes and small-town weekend schedules; someone who needs an all-night kebab and a tram to feel alive. Who would love it? Hikers setting off on multi-day adventures, picnic connoisseurs (yes, even The Amazing Picnic Co once included Kalamunda), history fans, and anyone who prefers a village that brags quietly about its best bits.

We’re that place up the hill people drive to for a day and stay in for a lifetime — or at least for one very committed weekend.
