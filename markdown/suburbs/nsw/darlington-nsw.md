# Darlington (NSW)

Slug: `darlington-nsw`

We roll down Cleveland Street like it’s a rite of passage — lecture over, hangover forming, eyes already on whatever pasta shrine used to be our salvation. Back in the day Broadsheet called Kindred a homey 40‑seater run by an ex‑A Tavola chef, with housemade pasta, gelato and “natural” wines; we remember it as the place that made us feel fancy while still being four stops from uni.

Our weekend drift used to look like this: a slow detour to Terra Cotta Roasters in the backstreets (Concrete Playground name‑checked their bottomless batch and on‑site roasting by Cedric Kim), a debate about whether Jak’s Table’s yakitori — yes, the yakitori grilled over coals on the footpath Broadsheet mentioned — counts as dinner or performance art, then a slow stumble past 137 Cleveland Street, where Longshore got a name‑check in the roundups.

We live in the weird sweet spot Time Out nailed: theatre one minute (the Seymour Centre towers over our cultural calendar), student beers the next (Time Out still remembers the Royal doing cheap rump steaks and jugs for the Uni crowd), and Hermann’s Bar supplying improv or a painfully earnest open‑mic night when we need one. Concrete Playground even remembered the Sheffer Gallery showing local art — yes, we have culture; it just wears a hoodie.

Petty local drama? There’s no council saga in our file, so the only feud we can safely admit is aesthetic: plant‑based pasta purists vs the person who still thinks a rump steak is a personality trait. As Broadsheet and Time Out showed us, we’re equal parts trattoria and pub crawl.

Who would hate living here? If you need suburban silence, pristine lawns and zero student energy, Darlington will test your patience. Who would love it? Food obsessives, theatre goers, and anyone for whom “walkable” means a dozen very different excuses to stay out late.

Full disclosure: these bits are from older write‑ups — “back in the day” feels appropriate. We’re proud of the snapshot, even if the headlines are a little vintage. Darlington: rough edges, cultured palate, and always a good story on the way home.
