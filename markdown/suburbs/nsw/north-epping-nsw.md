# North Epping (NSW)

Slug: `north-epping-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll the stroller down to Epping Oval like it was our civic duty — because Ryde District Mums said so, and who are we to argue with a tag page? The playground there was that classic suburban win: fenced, off-street parking for the grandparents, BBQ and toilets for the weekend takeover, shaded picnic tables when the sun finally showed up, and two play areas so siblings could disagree about age-appropriateness in peace. There were swings, slides and a path circumnavigating the oval that meant scooters and prams could complete a lap without anyone having to moralise about helmets.

For years the local write-ups also rhapsodised about Ron Payne Park and its then-brand-new playground — bright, transport-themed equipment aimed at the 2–7 squad, a short scooter track, a covered picnic table and toilets so the outing didn’t end in an urgent text to Mum. The review notes were politely honest: lovely new gear, shame about the bark chip ground and the lack of shade. We still remember the bark chip crunch like it was a minor rite of passage.

If you liked a bit of dismount from suburbia, the mum blogs pointed us towards Whale Rock and the bush trails you could reach from the fire trail at the end of Boundary Rd near Braidwood Ave. There was even a neat little 1.8 km loop starting from Ron Payne Park for the people who wanted fresh air without signing up for a midweek marathon.

We should admit: all this is from older local posts and meetup notes — Ryde District Mums collecting park spotlights, and Mama Tribe listing mums-and-bubs meetups — so think nostalgia, not a live directory. Things may have moved on since.

Who would hate it here? Nightlife chasers and anyone who measures a suburb by cocktail options will sigh and leave. Who would love it? Families who value fenced play spaces, picnic logistics, scooter laps and a bush walk that doesn’t require a permit. We’ll keep the leftover sausage roll. You bring the sunscreen.
