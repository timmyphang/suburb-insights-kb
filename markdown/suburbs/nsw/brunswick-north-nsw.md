# Brunswick North (NSW)

Slug: `brunswick-north-nsw`

We wake to the soundtrack of Sydney Road — trams, a distant band soundcheck and the guy who thinks yelling into his phone is ambient percussion. We live here, so we know the good bits: the Merri Creek Trail is our weekend escape if we want a breezy spin without pretending to be elite; the Upfield line and bike lanes mean we can leave the car at home and still complain about parking.

Our Saturday looks like this: a slow roll along the Merri Creek, coffee grabbed courtesy of the many places Broadsheet recently pointed us to (their best-cafes guide, April 28, 2026, did not lie), a quick climb at North Walls on Hope Street if we’re feeling virtuous, then a browse through East Brunswick Village — Time Out’s 2025 profile nailed it: market-style supermarket, bakery, microbrewery and that boutique cinema that makes you feel cultured by association.

We used to brag about Brunswick’s live-music pedigree — the old Broadsheet directory name-checked Howler, the Brunswick Hotel, the Retreat and the Spotted Mallard — and, sure, those venues helped make this place noisy in the best possible way. The pubs have pedigree too: Concrete Playground loved Hotel Railway’s old-school pull, and the Edinburgh Castle Hotel at 681 Sydney Road crops up if you like your local history with a pint.

Petty local drama? Not much in the clips we read — no council wars or scrolling feuds — just a proud, loud, culturally messy neighbourhood that Time Out describes as having strong Greek, Italian and Middle Eastern influences. Broadsheet’s restaurants roundup (May 10, 2024) reminds us there’s cheap, cheerful and gloriously unpretentious food on every corner.

Who would hate it here: people who crave beige predictability, immaculate private lawns and silence between 7pm and 8pm. Who would love it: anyone who likes live music, falafel that won’t bankrupt them, cycling routes that feel like a mini-escape and a village that actually has a village square — we’re looking at you, East Brunswick Village.

Oh, and if you bring kids: the Brunswick North West Primary School’s Yakia Barring wetlands — “surprise track” in Wurundjeri‑Woi‑Wurung — is a reminder this place has roots as well as a soundtrack. We wouldn’t have it any other way.
