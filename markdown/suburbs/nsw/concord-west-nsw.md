# Concord West (NSW)

Slug: `concord-west-nsw`

Back in the day we’d announce the weekend like it was public holiday: who wants coffee, who wants a playground? We’d drift down Victoria Avenue to Three Spoons — the quiet main-street café the guides raved about — and pretend that a simple brunch there was our idea of urban sophistication (Time Out and Urban List both had thoughts on that one). 

From there the parade split like a small-town rom-com. The kids got cycled off to Bicentennial Park for the mini traffic town with its roundabouts and road markings (Ryde District Mums couldn’t shut up about it). The ones with shorter attention spans ended up at the fenced Warbrick Park on Yaralla Street or the Rhodes Park 5 Senses Garden on Killoola Street, which Inner West Mums described as a fully fenced, all-abilities miracle for small humans and anxious parents.

If you were less child-anchored, our options were very Concord West: Platform 82 used to sell breakfast platters big enough to make you reconsider life choices, Casa Bella on Concord Road was the generous pizza option, and Cycleworld at 317 Concord Road was where we secretly pretended to train for Gran Fondo but mostly bought fancy inner-tube patches (Concrete Playground had notes on that). For the weekend warriors, there was a go-karting venue that Urban List put on a list — Sodi RT8 karts, lap timing and all the faux-F1 drama — and Spitfire Paintball on George Street for when we needed to shout at friends while wearing camouflage (Time Out's listing held the details).

We know what they say: Concord West is the low-key sibling of Concord and Rhodes. Sitchu called it leafy, wealthy-ish and dominated by couples with kids; Inner West Mums pointed out the medical precinct and Concord Hospital looming nearby. Fine—own it we do.

Who would hate living here? People who need a 24/7 nightlife and a skyline view. Who would love it? Families who worship parks, cyclists who like boutique bike shops, and anyone who enjoys living somewhere that quietly prioritises playgrounds, pizza and sensible breakfasts. We loved it then — and we'd probably still turn up for brunch, if only to argue about whose kid actually ate the last sausage roll.
