# Malvern (NSW)

Slug: `malvern-nsw`

Back in the day — or at least in the clippings we've got — Malvern liked to be photographed from the right angle: avenue trees, Edwardian weatherboards and a pub that everyone agreed deserved a glow‑up. We know every corner of Glenferrie Road because we walked it when the newspapers were writing about the Angel of Malvern stepping back into life after a long sleep: a heritage corner since 1856 being reborn across three levels, with a ground‑floor public bar, Flores upstairs and a top‑level cocktail bar called Lately. The developers and the lifestyle pages loved that one; we loved the idea of somewhere new to complain about.

Our morning ritual used to look like this: a stroll through Central Park to check the cricket oval and the flowerbeds, a detour past Milton Wine (remember when Time Out wrote them up?) and a lazy eye on the Gardiner Hotel reports that promised rooftop terraces and pub classics. Breadcetera at 399 Wattletree Road in Malvern East was the sort of market‑bakery the features couldn't stop describing as "European‑village‑adjacent" — and Gelato Messina on Glenferrie Road even made the lifestyle roundups when it opened.

Petty local drama? Mostly the kind that lives in press releases: a three‑year closure here, a multi‑level relaunch there. Broadsheet and Concrete Playground did the heavy lifting — we supplied the opinionated nods and the raised eyebrows.

Who would hate living here? Anyone allergic to properly spaced parks, historic pubs, and being told by house tour blogs that their north‑facing courtyard is "a breath of fresh air." If you crave grit, late‑night chaos or a suburb that refuses to be put in a neat "best suburbs to live" roll call, Malvern will politely bore you.

Who would love it? Young families and people who read Sitchu and Time Out for neighbourhood validation — the ones who like proximity to schools (hello Ashwood High School zone and Malvern Valley Primary mentions), weekend markets, and a wine shop you can pop into without a scarf. We live here, we know it, and we’ll happily smug about our parks while sipping whatever the lifestyle pages tell us to sip next.
