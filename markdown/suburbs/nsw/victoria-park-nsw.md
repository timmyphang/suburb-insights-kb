# Victoria Park (NSW)

Slug: `victoria-park-nsw`

We’ll say it straight: Victoria Park used to be the little grassy island that behaved like it owned the intersection. As Time Out put it, a beacon squeezed between Parramatta Road and City Road — perfect for students, families and anyone trying to look casual on their way from the University of Sydney to Broadway.

Back in the day our weekend routine was gloriously predictable. Mornings meant laps at the Victoria Park Pool — an eight‑lane 50m outdoor pool, heated to about 25°C, which made us feel Olympic-adjacent without actually having to train. Afternoons were for pretending we were picnic connoisseurs beside Lake Northram, then wandering over to the historic sandstone Gardener’s Lodge Café for whatever it was people used to sip while pretending essays weren’t due.

We’ve had our loud nights. Junkee reminded us that NYE in the Park once turned the place into a proper festival field — The Presets, The Jungle Giants, Hayden James and the rest made sure the neighbours learned our playlists by heart. Flight Facilities even staged an Airfields event here; you could tell everyone was trying to be very cultured while still queueing for chips.

Petty local drama? Oh, we had it soft and suburban: The Lansdowne used to loom over the park and Broadway — a pub with more gig history than some inner‑city venues — and when it closed people acted as if the entire cultural economy had collapsed. We like our mild catastrophes.

Who would hate living here? People who crave absolute silence, punctual tram timetables and zero students on the lawn. Also anyone who wants a suburb that never changes its mind about being both a serene park and an events field.

Who’d love it? Students who want class between lectures, swimmers who appreciate a heated outdoor pool, music fans who miss the old Lansdowne nights or the NYE crowds, and anyone who enjoys a suburb that can be quiet and raucous in the same breath. We’re proud of our little island by the big roads — messy, musical and oddly lovable.
