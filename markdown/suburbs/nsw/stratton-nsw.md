# Stratton (NSW)

Slug: `stratton-nsw`

We live in Stratton, NSW — which, if you’re the internet, mostly means one of three things: a very stylish bar in Newstead, a distinguished film critic, or a playgroup in Bude. Yes, our name gets around more than we do.

Back in the day Broadsheet, Urban List and Concrete Playground loved the Stratton that isn’t ours: Stratton Bar & Kitchen — the Mrs Brown’s spin-off that took over two World War II–era hangars on Stratton Street in Newstead. As Broadsheet noted, it had a lofty arched glasshouse, some 35 cocktails and a cuisine-crossing menu (cheeseburger dumplings, cacio e pepe udon, chicken karaage milk buns — the sort of comforting identity crisis you can order at the bar). Concrete Playground remembered the 920-square-metre layout, a 150-seat dining room and even cheeseburger bloody marys. Urban List kindly listed it among Newstead’s best restaurants at 3–5 Stratton Street, next door to The Triffid. Those write-ups are older, though — so we’re careful to say “used to be” rather than “head to”.

Meanwhile, Time Out and Pedestrian have been busy chronicling David Stratton — the bloke who put the name in lights for film buffs. And if you dig deep enough the Stratton playgroup in Bude pops up (parenting newsletters and term-times aplenty), which is delightfully international for a name we mostly wear in silence.

Our morning routine here is simpler: we refresh Broadsheet to see whether anyone’s finally written about Stratton, NSW. Spoiler: they haven’t. We’re not offended. We like being the quiet cousin of hangar-chic cocktails and cinematic retrospectives. It suits us.

Who would hate living here? People who want their postcode to come with press clippings, neon signage and a sun-kissed atrium. Who would love it? Folks who appreciate a little mystery, who find it amusing when your suburb’s claim to fame is shared by a bar in Brisbane and a national film critic.

So yes, we know what the internet thinks “Stratton” looks like. We’ll keep making our own small, unglamorous memories and smiling when someone tags the wrong Stratton in an Instagram post. It’s fine. We’ll take the anonymity.
