# Carrington (NSW)

Slug: `carrington-nsw`

We know Carrington like the back of our hands — not the slick suburb on glossy postcards, but the patchwork of Carrington Streets, the old pub names and the waterfall postcards people pin to their travel boards.

Back in the day we’d start the weekend wandering Carrington Street, past places that made headlines: Soul Dining’s moody set menus at 50 Carrington Street, June’s Shoppe’s pint-sized treats at 60 Carrington Street, and Steel Bar & Grill’s wood-fire pitch (also listed at 60). As Concrete Playground put it, The Carrington on Bourke Street used to be our reliable pre-match pitstop — an easy 15-minute stroll from the SCG — until the Drink N Dine crew clinked their last glasses and closed its doors on that fateful Sunday (they even ran a closing piece). Pedestrian.TV even chronicled the Jamie Wirth reboot attempt, because of course we have rebrands and comebacks here like everyone else.

We’re sentimental about the old-school stuff: Sitchu still trills about the Katoomba Carrington hotel and Champagne Charlie’s cocktail bar, and if you liked waterfalls you’d remember Carrington Falls and the Budderoo National Park turn-off — the kind of nearby nature that lets us pretend we’re outdoorsy between lunches.

Petty drama? It’s mostly about openings, closings and whatever replaced the last beloved burger joint. We watched venues shuffle names and menus like someone dealing a deck of slightly soggy cards. Broadsheet reminded us of Charlie Carrington’s Atlas Dining era and his offshoots Little Havana and Lomah — prime chef gossip that felt oddly relevant because of the name, even if Melbourne was doing the heavy lifting.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who wants one bland, stable high street with a single bakery, three identical gyms and zero rumours. You’ll be tripping over yesterday’s rave review and tomorrow’s “closed for good” sign.

Who would love Carrington? People who like their espresso with a side of history, those who queue for plant-forward Turkish plates at spots like Maydanoz, lovers of art-deco hotel drama, and anyone who thinks a waterfall day trip is the perfect antidote to pub closures. We’ll keep loving it — in all its slightly chaotic, much-loved, ever-changing glory.
