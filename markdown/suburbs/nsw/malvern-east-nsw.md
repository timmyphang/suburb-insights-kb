# Malvern East (NSW)

Slug: `malvern-east-nsw`

Back in the day we’d tell you Malvern East was the suburb you didn’t know you needed until you actually needed a loaf of Blanc Bakery bread at 9.05am. We still wear our local loyalty like a comfy jumper: Central Park’s avenue trees, the bocce court and that Edwardian oval where someone very earnest always brings a cricket kit on a Sunday.

Our morning ritual used to involve a stoic pilgrimage down Wattletree Road — Broadsheet used to point us toward Cafe Fergus or Servery and Spoon when we wanted something that looked like brunch and tasted like content for Instagram. Later on, Breadcetera opened at 399 Wattletree Road (the team from Riserva and Lucia, if you’re keeping score) and, well, it became the place you’d bump into someone carrying an Austro pastry and a jar of something artisanal.

We’re proud of our small-town upgrades. The East Malvern RSL’s makeover into Banjo’s Social Club x Stomping Ground Beer Garden gave us a container bar, food trucks and, for a while, a Sunday petting zoo — because who doesn’t want to watch someone sip wine and interrogate a goat about suburbia? Parkside Local on Waverley Road made a first impression with Teo’s pizza and trivia nights, proving we can appreciate both good dough and useless facts.

If you hate trees, local festivals and the uncanny way everyone knows which footy team used to train where, you’ll despise us. If you require a non-stop nightclub on your doorstep or think a market-style grocer is a cooperative, this isn’t your vibe.

We’ll admit to being a bit smug about having Chadstone sitting smack-bang in the middle of suburbia — yes, the “fashion capital” is technically next door at 1341 Dandenong Road — and we love that Holism Retreat at M Gallery Chadstone gets named in lists about massages (self-care is a sport here). For years the East Malvern Food & Wine Festival and Glow Winter Arts Festival lit up Central Park and gave us reasons to queue for tastings and inflatables.

Who will love Malvern East? Families who want parks without the pretence, bread nerds who still reminisce about Blanc loaves, and anyone who thinks a good local wine bar (we’re looking at you, Riserva) is a civic amenity. We know our streets, our cafés and our bocce lanes — and we’ll happily show you where to stand for the best light.
