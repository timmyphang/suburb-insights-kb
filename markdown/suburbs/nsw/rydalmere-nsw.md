# Rydalmere (NSW)

Slug: `rydalmere-nsw`

Back in the day — and by “day” we mean all our sources are pre-2024, so this is more nostalgia than an events guide — Rydalmere felt like Sydney’s underrated sibling: a bit river‑side, a bit playground‑obsessed, and very proud of its parking.

We like to start our weekends the classic way: ferry plans for the kids at Eric Primrose Reserve (perfect for a picnic and, honestly, a good spot to pretend you’re commuting), then a meander along the Subiaco Creek Bushwalk tucked behind Bunnings for that “nature but still close to a hardware store” vibe. Families used to swarm Rydalmere Park on Park Road — yes, the one across from Aldi — for soccer, cricket, and that very specific post-game chat about nets and sunscreen. Upjohn Park and WinJoy Reserve were the other go‑to spots if you liked your playgrounds with a side of low key.

Our cultural flex? Parramatta Artists’ Studios quietly taking a second home here — six warehouse studios in Rydalmere — so we get to pretend we’re artsy without actually having to move to the inner west. For a Thursday family night out there was FC Social Food Truck Hub at 6 Park Road, which used to run dinner Thurs–Sat and Sunday lunch, because nothing says suburbia like eating wood‑fired pizza on a big green.

Coffee and carbs were never neglected. Big Tree House on 88 Calder Road served Campos coffee and proper café classics (Cajun chicken burgers, corn fritters) and the Imperial Gluten Free Bakery at 215–221 Victoria Road was a revelation for anyone who needed a pie, banana bread or even a pizza without the gluten drama.

We’ll admit we’re missing the trains — the old T6 Carlingford line was closed to make way for the Parramatta Light Rail (remember the temporary 535 bus?), and there was chatter about Metro West maybe poking a station our way by 2030. Plans, promises, and optimism: Rydalmere’s official mantra.

Who would hate it here? Someone who needs rooftop bars, two‑am kebabs and a skyline view. Who would love it? Parents, bushwalkers, artists, and gluten‑free bakers — basically anyone who enjoys wide parks, practical ferry stops and a suburb that quietly gets on with being useful. We’ll keep the esky ready.
