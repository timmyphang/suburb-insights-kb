# Glendale (NSW)

Slug: `glendale-nsw`

We start our weekends the way every Glendale local secretly does: by remembering that once upon a time a national food site sent people north to our postcode like pilgrims. As Concrete Playground cheerfully reported, if you wanted Banjo’s Bakery Cafe’s spaghetti bolognese pie, lasagne pie or the double cheeseburger pie in NSW, “you’ll need to head north to Glendale” — and suddenly our sleepy strip felt like the only place in the state serving pastry-encased pasta wonders.

Back in the day that line read like a badge of honour. We’d brag that national outlets knew where to find us, then watch bewildered out-of-towners arrive clutching map screenshots and asking where the Americana at Brand was. (No, that’s the Glendale that Time Out wrote about on the eastern edge of the San Fernando Valley. We know. The internet is very confused.)

There isn’t a lot of local tabloid drama in the clips we’ve got — mostly lists, mom-groups and big-city guidebooks that keep insisting there are three Glendales and none of them checks their GPS. So our petty scandal is simple and gentle: we are the NSW answer to someone else’s city guide, and we wear that like a T-shirt from a gig you only halfway remember.

Who would hate living here? People who crave being the subject of constant online thinkpieces. Also anyone who likes their suburb to only ever be mentioned in one timezone. If you require global recognition for your latte art, Glendale’s humble claim to fame as the place Concrete Playground sent people for limited-edition pies will feel a little underwhelming.

Who would love it? Food pilgrims with a taste for the gloriously bonkers — the sort of person who will happily drive for a pastry mashup because a national site once joked we were the only NSW stop. If you like being delightfully obscure, occasionally conflated with Glendale, CA or AZ, and proud of our very specific crumbs of fame, you’ll fit right in. As far as we’re concerned, that’s enough glory for the week.
