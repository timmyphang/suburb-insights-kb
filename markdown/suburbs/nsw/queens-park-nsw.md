# Queens Park (NSW)

Slug: `queens-park-nsw`

Full disclosure before we roast: all our intel here comes from older profiles and reviews — think “back in the day” rather than “this weekend”. So consider this more of a fond postcard than a live events calendar.

We start our Saturday like proper locals: we’ll wander the leafy streets Sitchu once gushed about, cut through Centennial Park and somehow end up at the eastern end where the old women’s change shed used to sit. You remember it — Concrete Playground wrote about how the disused sports storage was handed over to Trippas White Group and turned into the modest little eatery known as the Queens Park Shed. The striped deck chairs outside? Iconic napping infrastructure. The Shed was adjacent to the children’s playground and sporting field, so it was always half picnic, half minor parenting triage centre.

Back in the day Sitchu tagged Queens Park as one of Sydney’s sought-after leafy suburbs — close enough to Bondi, Paddington and the CBD to make you feel cosmopolitan, far enough to keep the tourists on their way to the beach. They even pointed out the convenience of Westfield and Eastgate for when you need something beige and functional, immediately.

We could invent a feud about the trees, or a boundary spat over heritage facades, but the real story is quieter: heritage homes getting light-filled makeovers (yes, Carla Middleton Architecture got a Sitchu home tour), parks being used the way parks should be — for toddlers to last-minute rugby drills and dogs to judge our life choices. That’s the local drama, and it’s perfectly civilised.

Who would hate living here? Anyone allergic to trees, playgrounds or neighbours who actually use their lawns. If your vibe is 24/7 nightlife, Queens Park will politely suggest you try the CBD instead.

Who would love it? Families who prize off-leash chaos, people who want a leafy commute that ends with a decent walk in Centennial Park, and anyone who likes the idea of a neighbourhood that’s more living room than billboard. We’ll be on the deck chairs, watching the world amble by — and quietly pleased that someone once turned a shed into the kind of place that makes you stay.
