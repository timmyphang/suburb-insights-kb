# Bicton (NSW)

Slug: `bicton-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start our Bicton mornings with the same civilised argument: Point Walter’s sandbar for a stroll, or the wider Swan River paths that Melville Mums calls “pram‑friendly”? We usually compromise — a circuit along the river, past Bicton Baths and the Bicton Pool (yes, the 33m outdoor pool with hydrotherapy that Melville Mums remembers by name), and then the real decision: who’s doing brunch today.

We’re lucky the internet already did the homework for us. As Urban List swooned, Little Stove was the cute, garden‑inspired spot with “some of the best pancakes in town” and a high tea that makes your nan approve. If you wanted your pancakes with drama, Kava Cafe’s allegedly epic red velvet pancakes were on hand. Puck Espresso’s bright orange machine is the sort of thing people mention when they have feelings about caffeine. And for nights when we’re feeling spicy, Broadsheet once celebrated Rym Tarng — a poky 16‑seat BYO in Bicton Central, known for prawn‑and‑pork fritters and a proper massaman curry.

We’re also the sort of suburb that treats a former Brumby’s like a national treasure: Hunter Bread turned that space into a tiny “community bread shop” doing long‑fermented sourdough, which Broadsheet wrote up when it opened. For seafood nights, Sitchu pointed out Fins Seafood’s Bicton retail outlet and bar. Even the booze buffs had a moment — Dan Murphy listed “Bicton” among participating stores in a national tasting roundup.

There isn’t much petty newspaper drama in the files we dug up — mostly glowing directories and helpful mum‑blog tips — so the local soap opera is: which bakery do you queue for, and who has the better pancakes? That’s it. Small stakes, big carbs.

Who would hate living here? People allergic to small, well‑loved places: tiny 16‑seat restaurants, narrow queues for sourdough, and other humans with prams on the river path. Who would love it? Families (Attadale Playgroup even lists a Carroll Drive postal address), swimmers, bread obsessives, Thai food fans and anyone who enjoys a proper local that’s content to be talked about by Broadsheet and Urban List rather than reinventing itself every week.
