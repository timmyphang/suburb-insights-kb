# Ultimo (NSW)

Slug: `ultimo-nsw`

We start our Saturdays the way Ultimo taught us to: meandering down The Goods Line, pretending the Powerhouse’s Boiler House and Turbine Hall are just vintage industrial backdrops we paid extra for. Back in the day there was talk — and renderings — about turning that whole site into four exhibition halls and a 2,000‑square‑metre courtyard; we watched the drawings like they were a new season of drama.

Mid‑morning used to be a religious experience of queues and aromas. We queued for Age 3’s viral fried sandos, remembered Otogo’s tech‑forward ordering and onigiri, and slipped into Kiki Dessert near Central Station for delicate cakes and a Studio‑Ghibli‑adjacent interior that made us feel like dessert was a cultural act. Kafe Kooks’ roti‑forward menu and a “slept‑on” Ultimo ramen spot have all been the kind of local food lore we trade like rare stickers. Design By Them on Wattle Street used to be the place we blamed for our suddenly tasteful couch.

Ultimo has never pretended to be sleepy. It’s where the ABC’s studios mean we could see Andrew W.K. turn the foyer into a mosh pit one week and read about an alleged triple j intruder the next. We also endured an awful op‑ed aimed at the ABC that made the whole suburb bristle — proof our cultural cachet has always been loud, messy and occasionally combustible. Festival people will remember SXSW and Vivid Food popping up across Darling Harbour and Ultimo, and proposals to extend Special Entertainment Precinct rules down Harris Street reminded us the council and the nightlife have a complicated tango.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who wants absolute calm, zero queues, and no museums — we’re the kind of place where the Powerhouse revamp gets debated over dinner and the Lord Wolseley Hotel is a predictable stop on any night out. Parents and swimmers will recall having the Ian Thorpe Aquatic Centre on their side; creative types will miss pottery nights at CeeCee Studios.

Who would love it here? Festival junkies, museum nerds, ramen devotees, and anyone who enjoys living where the ABC, creative start‑ups and design shops rub shoulders with late‑night dessert bars. Ultimo’s not trying to be perfect. It’s trying to be interesting — loud, slightly pretentious, and entirely our kind of chaos.
