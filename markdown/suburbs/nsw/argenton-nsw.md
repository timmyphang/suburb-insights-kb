# Argenton (NSW)

Slug: `argenton-nsw`

We know you’re home in Argenton when a Mazda CX‑5 pulls up at Speers Point Reserve, the driver pats the Yeti cooler and calls a boutique kombucha “fresh air fuel.” We wave to Jess at Speers Point Espresso like she’s family and then argue, politely, about whether the oat‑milk flat white is better than the one at Argenton Bakehouse. We wear Lululemon and Athleta like it’s a uniform, walk our Cavoodles on the same loop every sunrise, and still tell people we’re “getting away from it all” while ordering Coles pickup for the week. We can name three tradies — Stu the plumber, Damo the sparky, Mick the chippy — and the barista who remembers our kid’s soccer jersey. Our Saturday starts at 6:30am with parkrun or F45, flows through that flat white pilgrimage, detours to Bunnings for “one paint sample,” and ends with a Balter can from the Yeti cooler at the backyard BBQ. Sundays are lawn, coffee, a tinge of guilt about not visiting the grandparents, and a quick sprint to the oval for minis’ soccer. We check the neighbourhood Facebook group before breakfast like it’s the news. We rehearse polite outrage about “traffic to Newcastle” and we truly believe Bunnings is basically church.

Our local soap opera is epic in its mundanity. A new café near the shops set off a slow‑burn war over parking bay etiquette — furious posts about “people who double‑park for avo on toast,” comments threaded like a Greek tragedy, and a passive‑aggressive sign that reads “Please don’t block my nature strip, Dave.” The council’s proposal to shuffle verge‑parking and recycling pickup times produced an emergency thread, a flurry of “Keep off my nature strip” placards, and an accusation that shifting bin collection was “an attack on weekend barbecues.” The Argenton shopping strip parking wars are now performance art. We know exactly where to leave the Mazda, the HiLux, and the occasional e‑bike to avoid social death.

Who would hate living here? A 25‑year‑old nightlife‑dependent renter craving rooftop cocktails at 2am, a tram to the CBD, and a shoebox studio will spend every weekend on a train regretting their decision. We run on school‑term timetables, reserve gossip, and weekend barbecues; late‑night kebabs don’t compute.

Who would love it? Mid‑30s parents who own a CX‑5, wear Lululemon with pride, walk a Cavoodle to Speers Point at sunrise, volunteer at Argenton Public School P&C, and think Bunnings is a romantic destination — you’ll fit right in. Welcome.
