# Ceres (NSW)

Slug: `ceres-nsw`

We get asked twice a week: “Ceres? Like the thing with the market?” We say yes, but we’re the other one — the tiny, proudly obscure Ceres in NSW — and for reasons beyond our control the internet keeps sending visitors to CERES Community Environment Park at Cnr Roberts & Stewart St, Brunswick East. Back in the day that place was the headline grabber: Broadsheet wrote about CERES facing a donation crunch and even quoted CEO Cinnamon Evans about the money troubles, and Time Out was running features on the park’s grocery, bakery and its Fair Food delivery arm.

Our morning here is quieter. While Melbourne’s CERES was being praised as a solar‑powered hub with Merri Cafe Ceres and a permaculture nursery (Urban List and Sitchu), our sunrise still smells like gumleaf and the one bloke who insists on mowing at dawn. If you were the Googler, you’d find CERES Market and CERES Makers And Flea Market, the Harvest Festival (Time Out noted it returned in 2025 on March 29), and earnest write‑ups about frogs singing in a little pond (Urban List — yes, really). Meanwhile we’re mostly on tea and the council’s noticeboard.

There was real drama over there, not just artisanal soap drama: Broadsheet also reported Joe’s Market Garden — the two‑acre plot run by the Brunswick non‑profit — had to shut its farmgate to the public because it was financially unsustainable. So when people say “Ceres is closing!” we can sigh sympathetically from afar and point them to Fair Food, the grocery delivery project Time Out chronicled, which used to help local farmers reach the city.

Would you hate living here? If you need your suburb to trend, to have a monthly market with exactly 20 stalls and a grocery that labels everything “conscience‑led”, then yeah — you’ll be frustrated. Also, if frogs are your dealbreaker, this might be awkward.

Who’d love it? Plant nerds, weekend market wanderers and anyone who appreciates a place that once hosted a permaculture nursery and an earnest harvest festival, even if those were the headlines of another state. We’re small, we’re stubborn and we’re not on page one of Google — which, frankly, suits us just fine.
