# Castlereagh (NSW)

Slug: `castlereagh-nsw`

Back in the day our feed about Castlereagh was a bit of a patchwork — mostly older write-ups rather than anything fresh — so forgive us if we sound like nostalgics leafing through a very specific scrapbook. But we know this place: the river where a candlelight vigil once saw two trees planted and yellow belts displayed; the stretch of Castlereagh Street that kept turning up in every food and property story. As Pedestrian noted, some of the quieter, heavier moments have happened by the Castlereagh River. As Junkee remembered, we even doubled as screen-stomp territory when Pacific Rim sent kaiju down Castlereagh Street.

Morning routine (back when the reviews were new): we used to dodge the long queues for Pablo & Rusty's on Castlereagh Street — Concrete Playground and Time Out both gave it the nod — then wander past the gleaming Porter House precinct, where Lee Ho Fook was spun out as Victor Liong’s Sydney project and Colin Fassnidge put pub classics on The Castlereagh Club menu. If we were splashing out, Alpha and Beta offered their Greek thing; if we were hungry and reckless, Concrete Playground told us El Jannah’s Castlereagh store once did $5 chicken deals.

Petty local drama? Oh yes: our skyline hosted low-ceilinged, beige-on-beige ICAC rooms on the seventh floor, which Junkee cheerfully described — lawyers, rubberneckers and all. We’re not pretending it’s glamorous; it’s gloriously municipal in its own way.

Who would hate living here: anyone who can’t tolerate queues (Lune’s legendary lines were plastered in the Urban List chatter) or someone allergic to heritage restorations — Porter House’s multi-million-dollar facelift wasn’t for minimalists. And if you loathe being featured in a blockbuster accidentally, Pacific Rim has you covered.

Who’d love it: lovers of crust and command centres — pastry obsessives salivating at the thought of Lune’s Castlereagh plan, diners who miss the old-school Castlereagh Club roast by Fassnidge, fans of new precincts like The Porter House and the small army of cafés and bars that used to make Castlereagh Street feel like a tiny, overconfident culinary boulevard. We’ll keep minding the river and the street signs — even if the latest headlines have gone quiet, we know where our favourites used to live.
