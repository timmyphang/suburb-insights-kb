# Port Melbourne (NSW)

Slug: `port-melbourne-nsw`

We know the tide times here like other suburbs know their best brunch slot. On a Bay Street morning we used to wobble between Heart Bakes, Cafe Station Street Trading Co. and Cafe Noisette, clutching takeaway that was somehow both seaside and smug. For years Bay Street felt like the spot that remembered its industrial past but decided leather banquettes and good pastry were a suitable retirement plan.

Weekends meant wandering the Fennell Street precinct — Coe & Coe’s little empire, the reclaimed chocolate‑factory charm, Colonial Brewing Co. elbowing into the old Bertie Beetle footprint on Bertie Street. Colonial even went and won a national burger prize once; we still tell it like a medal, if a burger can be a medal. Starward Whisky’s distillery used to run tours and tastings, and after a glam refresh they added a kitchen where Drew Traynor (ex‑Eau De Vie and Cumulus) presided — proof we’ll turn anything into a hospitality experience if given exposed brick and a decent bar stool.

We’ve watched Station Pier get spruced up and open back to pedestrians after years of lockdown silence, and we remember the day Melbourne’s tantrum of weather nearly sent the Spirit of Tasmania on an unscheduled cruise, tearing up the boarding ramp and giving holidaymakers a dramatic story to tell.

If you liked oddball cultural detours, Metartworld’s scrap‑metal zoo and mirror‑maze was our favourite bit of glorified recycling — hundreds of statues you could actually touch, so you could say you came for art and left with a dented selfie.

Who would hate it here? People who need everything to be exclusively new, quiet and car‑free — Port Melbourne used to be industrial, then slightly smug, then proudly bayside. If you can’t cope with pubs that do simple things well (we’re thinking Prince Alfred, Railway Club Hotel, The Cricketers) or a takeaway chicken place trying to be healthy, you’ll be agitated.

Who would love it? Lovers of waterways that remember hard yards, folks who like their whiskey with a story, burger obsessives who appreciate colonial‑era factory conversions, and any parent who’s ever been grateful for Bubup Womindjeka Family and Children’s Centre or a German playgroup on a Monday. Back in the day we were industrious, then hospitable — and yes, a bit proud of both.
