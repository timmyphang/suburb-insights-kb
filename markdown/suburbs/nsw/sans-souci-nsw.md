# Sans Souci (NSW)

Slug: `sans-souci-nsw`

Back in the day we’d line up on the Grand Parade, sand in our shoes and opinions about pastry, because that’s what Sans Souci taught us: pick a side and eat accordingly. As Broadsheet noted when Son of a Baker opened in 2018, that cafe on the Grand Parade used to pull off burek next to lavender pancakes and even the occasional grilled scallop with lobster tail — posh beachfront fare with a side of sunscreen.

We know every angle of the river. The St George Sailing Club at 2 Riverside Drive was given the full glow-up — a $15 million rebuild that Broadsheet and Urban List described as a 1,600 sqm modern thing that seems to float over the Georges River. It used to bring seaside classics and a rather serious raw bar to our lunchtime lineup; Urban List later pointed out it’s a handy spot for family-friendly long lunches. Nearby, Blackwater (Shop 1, 8 Water Street) put rustic southern Italian on the Sans Souci map — Time Out even flagged a life-size horse at the door and some very pleasant gnocchi.

If you’re hunting for small-town essentials, Sans Souci had them in spades. The Party People on Rocky Point Road has been the costume-and-balloon oracle since the 1980s; concrete guides used to point us there when we needed fake moustaches or an inflatable unicorn for school fetes. Playgroup parents have long met at Sans Souci Baptist Church on 2 Hillview Street — Wednesday mornings are basically a local ritual, if the Playgroup NSW notes are anything to go by.

There’s no juicy scandal in our files — just renovations, menus, and a parade of family-friendly offerings. That’s fine by us. People who would hate it here: anyone who needs a 24/7 neon nightlife or can’t tolerate a main strip where your cafe options sometimes come with seabreeze and a kid’s birthday chorus.

Who would love it: families who need playgroups and parks, diners who appreciate riverside views, and anyone who secretly enjoys queuing for decent pastry while pretending they’re on holiday. For years the local listings have shown Sans Souci as quietly doing its seaside thing — and honestly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
