# Olinda (NSW)

Slug: `olinda-nsw`

We’ll admit it — our Tuesday mornings look like a deliberate act of chaos. We herd the littlies to Olinda Community Hall at 33 Coolcalwin Street for the 10:30am playgroup, swap emergency snacks, then nod politely at the person who still insists on cloth nappies. By lunchtime someone’s suggesting a dip: remember when the local pool hosted that “Come As You Are: Body Positive” session for mums? We do. It was exactly as wholesome and infuriatingly soothing as it sounds.

Here’s the thing about Olinda, NSW: for years the internet had other ideas. Travel pages and reviews kept falling in love — loudly — with Olinda in the Dandenongs. Broadsheet and Time Out used to gush about Cloudehill Gardens, the National Rhododendron Garden on Georgian Rd, and Only Mine chocolaterie in the Dandenongs. Urban List and Concrete Playground gave Billabong Falls and Olinda Falls rave notices, and even recommended scones and candles as a full cultural experience. So if you’ve ever Googled us and expected a mini-European hedge maze plus a 24-hour-brewed hot chocolate, bless you — you’ve been catfished by a prettier postcode.

We don’t have a hothouse of tourists snapping cherry blossoms (well, not en masse), but we do have a tight little civic life: playgroup meets, local workshops at the community house and the odd guided Mother Hike that trots back via Olinda Falls for light refreshments. The data says so; we’re not making this up.

Who would hate living here? People who demand boutique gardens at the end of every lane, or anyone whose idea of a weekend is a curated feed of artisanal chocolates and perfectly symmetrical hedgerows. You’ll be disappointed if you came for Cloudehill and Only Mine and instead found toddlers, community notices, and earnest mums trading walking routes.

Who would love it here? Families who value a proper playgroup, people who enjoy a local pool that once doubled as a safe, body-positive hangout, and anyone who likes their suburb communal rather than hyper-styled. We’re small, a bit muddled in Google’s mind, and unapologetically ordinary — and honestly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
