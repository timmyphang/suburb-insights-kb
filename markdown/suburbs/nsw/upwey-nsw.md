# Upwey (NSW)

Slug: `upwey-nsw`

Saturday mornings in Upwey we shuffle down Main Street like it's our Sunday sermon — past the two trees that, yes, got the solar fairy lights (cheers, Bendigo Bank grant), past the murals on the side of Pearl Garden that now say “Moving Forward Together,” and pretend we’re on someone else’s quaint film set.

Back in the day Maria used to be the place we pointed to when visitors asked where to get something that wasn’t quite brunch nor quite tacos — Concrete Playground wrote about Josh O’Brien and Omar Viramontes running Maria on Main Street and how it sat cheek-by-jowl with the foothills and the 1000 Steps. We mention it in the past because most of our printed plaudits live in archives, but the memory serves: cosy, slightly smug, very Dandenongs.

Civic life here is earnest and slightly theatrical. The council asked for community input on a draft Upwey Town Centre Masterplan (we dutifully filled in a form), and Ranges Trader has been keeping score: raised pedestrian crossings at the Morris Road and Mahony Street roundabout and zebra crossings were on the to-do list as part of streetscape and drainage works. It’s the kind of local politics that involves reflective vests and family-friendliness rather than outrage mobs — although the working bees do produce surprising energy.

If you hate craft stalls, live music and people who actually show up to meetings, you would loathe Upwey. If you can’t stand murals, markets or a community group that runs the Upwey Community Market, Upwey Archies and Upwey Sounds, this isn’t your postcode. We’re the kind of place where the Burrinja Gallery’s Indigenous and contemporary programs feel essential, not adjunct; where the Upwey Community Hall still hosts your cousin’s pottery class and the village green hosts a market on a good weekend (the UCG lists the dates if you want proof).

Who would love it here? Walkers who finish at the 1000 Steps and want a village to collapse into. People who like their public art literal and their nights lit by solar fairy lights. We’ll keep planting murals, holding markets and pretending our roundabout is the town square — and quietly loving every bit of it.
