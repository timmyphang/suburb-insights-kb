# Jordan Springs (NSW)

Slug: `jordan-springs-nsw`

We know the ritual: school run, a detour past the Jordan Springs Shopping Centre (you remember the one the directory called “the heart of the community”), and a quick sigh at Cullen Avenue before the kids tumble out. Back in the day the community hub at 2–6 Cullen Avenue was where our fortnightly playgroup rhythms lived — NurtureGroup Penrith meeting parents who’d been through the wringer with premature or sick newborns, and the MyTime and Cranebrook Kids pages pointing newbies in the same direction. It felt like everyone had a timetable printed on their fridge.

Weekends were predictably suburban: the shopping centre listing used to boast a Woolworths, a BWS and “about 18 specialty shops, restaurants, cafes and a gym” — which is council-speak for “everything you need within a short drive and enough foot-traffic to keep the local notices board interesting.” Montague Drive had Livvi’s Place for the small humans to test gravity; playgrounds were where we judged each other’s sunscreen application and shared parenting hacks.

Our petty local drama? It came in the form of community posts. The Jordan Springs Joeys Football Club listing was basically the local soap: sponsorship shout-outs (Borrowly Finance gets an honourable mention), volunteer-of-the-month nominations, and earnest grassroots-football updates. It’s the kind of civic theatre that makes your neighbour who coaches under‑10s feel like a minor celebrity.

Who would hate it here: lovers of anonymous high-rise living or people allergic to noticeboards and committee emails. If you crave late-night laneway bars with cryptic menus, Jordan Springs has a particular suburban cadence that won’t indulge you — but that’s the point.

Who would love it: families, especially those who once relied on support groups like NurtureGroup Penrith or MyTime Penrith; volunteers who live for small wins (we see you, Joeys Club nomads); and anyone who rates a shopping centre that actually lists Woolworths, BWS and a gym as comfort. For years this place ran like a community calendar stitched together by playgroups, local sports and the odd sponsorship post — not flashy, but perfectly ours.
