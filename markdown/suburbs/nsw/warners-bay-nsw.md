# Warners Bay (NSW)

Slug: `warners-bay-nsw`

We know Warners Bay like the back of our sunburnt hands. You can catch us mid-stroll on the Warners Bay Foreshore — that 8.9km water-hugging loop that, as Concrete Playground pointed out, links Eleebana to Booragul — or pretending we’re art critics while inspecting the Creative LAKE Sculpture Trail. Back in the day the sculptures made for excellent posing and baffling commentary.

Weekends used to look the same: a lap of the foreshore, a detour over the Redbluff elevated boardwalk at Eleebana, then collapsing at Hippo Espresso on The Esplanade (yes, Aaryn’s cafe that Concrete Playground celebrated as a 2024 Local Legend). If we were feeling fancy we’d remember the Mediterranean spread at Sephardim afterwards — that’s the kind of foodie flex the foreshore encourages. The Esplanade also hosted the Warners Bay June Market (it was moved once because of the Float Your Boat event), so there’s always been a calendar of small-town theatrics.

We’re the suburb that’s simultaneously kid-friendly and aesthetically smug: Warner Park’s fully-fenced playground with the popular group swing and even a ping pong table means parents can breathe; Chartley Street Reserve — tucked between Chartley and Whitby streets — is for the toddlers who enjoy bright colours and the adults who enjoy nothing more than being slightly smug about proximity to public toilets (or, er, the lack of them).

Petty local drama? We’ve had headline moments. Pedestrian TV ran the day the cops uncovered millions of dollars’ worth of cocaine on a yacht in Lake Macquarie — and yes, Warners Bay cropped up in the reports. We laughed, we tutted, we checked our jetties for suspicious packages.

Who would hate living here? People who loathe school holiday chaos, outdoor music, and literally being near water. If you can’t deal with families, sculptures, markets or the occasional tabloid moment, Warners Bay will feel like a relentless community festival.

Who would love it? Families, walkers, weekend roady types, and anyone who thinks a lakeside espresso followed by a sculpture-stroll is a valid life plan. For years the local guides have called it a little slice of paradise — and we’ve seen worse places to call home.
