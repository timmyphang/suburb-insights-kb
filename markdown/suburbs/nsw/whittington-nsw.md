# Whittington (NSW)

Slug: `whittington-nsw`

We start our mornings the way Whittington people do best: with an inbox full of nostalgia and another tab open to Whittington Mum. Back in the day the blog began when one of us was pregnant and decided enough was enough — she set up camp online to oppose the proposed closure of the Whittington A&E and the maternity unit. If that doesn’t scream small‑place solidarity, what does?

Weekends look suspiciously like campaigning: as the blog pleaded in pieces like WRITE TO SAVE THE WHITTINGTON, our Residents Association got serious about getting commitments and planned a proper letter‑writing push. We still remember the flurry of emails and the odd sense of civic heroism — mostly performed in pyjamas with a cuppa within reach. Mumsnet threads kept the stories buzzing; there were whole conversations about giving birth at the Whittington and memories of the then‑new birth centre that made us swill together over shared anxieties and triumphs.

The petty local drama? Oh, the theatre of it all. A high‑profile NHS panel once struggled to make a convincing case for "change" and "efficiency" — lots of waffly words, very little substance. We watched that unravel with the smug patience of people who’ve sat through too many public meetings. We didn’t invent banners; we wrote letters, we shared birth‑centre tips on forums, we made the kind of noise that sticks in the memory of anyone who thought closures were a quiet, bureaucratic thing.

Who would hate living here? Folks who prefer their suburbs quiet, apolitical and free of earnest mums who will, politely but tenaciously, turn up and ask awkward questions. If you loathe discussion threads about natural childbirth or find letter campaigns mildly aggressive, this isn’t your vibe.

Who would love it here? New parents, community organisers, the kind of people who believe the local hospital is a proper local concern and will make a blog, a forum post, or a flyer to prove it. We’re small but vocal; our glory is in our stubbornness. Come for the solidarity, stay because someone will always have a story about the birth centre.
