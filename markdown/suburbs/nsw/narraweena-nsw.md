# Narraweena (NSW)

Slug: `narraweena-nsw`

We know the walk from our driveway to Narraweena Community Centre so well we could do it with our eyes closed — Thursdays at 10:00 used to mean Narraweena Waku Waku Kids, a Japanese playgroup at 74 Oceana St where the kids learned nursery rhymes, did seasonal crafts and tore up morning tea (nut-free, BYO) while the parents pretended they weren’t learning the choruses. First two sessions were free, then the maths kicked in: $6 a session or $48 a term. We kept the email for enquiries tucked in our phones like a talisman: wakuwakuplaygroup@gmail.com.

Weekends used to taste different depending on which Beverley Job Park event we’d collapse at. The World Food Markets — remember those free gigs of multicultural food trucks and live entertainment? — made the park smell like half the planet and felt like a proper community fair every time they rolled through. Love Narraweena, our early Valentine’s community thing, brought face-painting and children’s art workshops and an excuse to buy something from a local business rather than chain up the road.

We watched new things land on our little map: Smokey Mary’s BBQ opened at 60 May Road (that March 2024 piece remembered owner Leo Nakamura and the Texas-style menu — brisket, pulled pork, sausages — which, yes, briefly made us think we’d been transported to a smokier postcode). Rose’s Continental Deli even got a nod in a deli roundup, which made our nonna very proud. Karuna Montessori sits tucked in a quiet pocket, offering those sweeping views over Dee Why and Long Reef Beach — the kind of place that makes the school run feel like a meditation.

Who would hate it here? Nightlife hunters and people who believe a suburb’s worth is measured by late-night neon. If your idea of fun is bars that never close, Narraweena will politely put you on a bus to somewhere louder.

Who would love it? Young families, people who like leafy streets, decent local schools and parks, and anyone who wants a suburb that still throws a market in the park and cheers when a new deli or smokehouse opens. We’ll take our calm, our community days, and our occasional smoky brisket, thank you very much.
