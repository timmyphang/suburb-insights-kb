# Kingsbury (NSW)

Slug: `kingsbury-nsw`

We know Kingsbury by the smell of frying batter and the one shopfront that refused to be only a chipper. Back in the day our local Serendib fish’n’chipper — run by Dilip Kithulguruge — started sliding Sri Lankan short eats in between the chips and the paper cones, and the rest of Melbourne quietly had to take notes. As Broadsheet noted, that little experiment grew so popular it sprouted a Northcote outpost with an all-you-can-eat buffet. Who said suburbia can’t be ambitious?

Our weekend routine used to be gloriously simple: someone queues at Serendib, someone else pretends to be surprised that “that curry” goes with fries, and someone scrolls through the old kingsburymom blog for domestic poetry about birthdays and life lessons. There’s a comforting, slightly bemused pride in that: we’re the place that accidentally launched a city food trend without bothering to rename the main drag.

There’s not much local tabloid theatre to gossip about — that’s partly the point. We’re quietly productive. We give Melbourne a recipe, not a scandal. Even Urban List later tipped its hat, tracing one of the city’s favourite Sri Lankan spots back to our chipper genesis. It’s the sort of origin story that reads like a civic achievement certificate: small shop, stubborn owner, surprising legacy.

Who would hate living here? If you need boutique markets on every corner, curated Instagram backdrops at 9am, or a revolving door of theatre companies staging interpretive performances about gentrification, Kingsbury will politely underwhelm you. We prefer businesses that sustain rather than trend-cycle.

Who would love it here? People who like their local history served with a side of chips. People who enjoy a suburb whose biggest brag is a clever menu change that made other people write think-pieces. For years the local Serendib taught us that innovation doesn’t need an arts grant — sometimes it only needs good food, a bit of nerve and a bloke called Dilip doing something different. We’ll keep eating, shrugging, and telling the story.
