# Mindarie (NSW)

Slug: `mindarie-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed and pretend we were on holiday — because that’s what living near The Marina Mindarie felt like. We’d saunter the boardwalk, point out the same sunset like it was a new thing, and, if we were feeling decadent, collapse onto a daybed at Cabana Pool Bar & BBQ at The Marina Mindarie. Urban List called it a “poolside oasis” and honestly, for a while, it was our excuse to wear linen unironically.

Weekends used to be a rotating program: Union Kitchen at Mindarie Marina for waterfront breakfasts with the dog (yes, it was dog‑friendly), then a cheeky pilgrimage to Indian Ocean Brewing Company at 33 Ocean Falls Boulevard for something that smelled suspiciously like care and quality ingredients. The Garden once sweetened Father’s Day by handing out complimentary pints “thanks to Indian Ocean Brewing Co.” — classy sponsorships, minimal assembly required.

We’ve been a festival town in the good way too. Time Out used to list the Mindarie Festival, which meant one thing: the place filled with people who knew how to have community, chips, and an oddly specific enthusiasm for local stalls. If your plan involved staying the night, Concrete Playground had a listing for Harbour Views at Mindarie Marina — because if there’s a marina, of course there are views and someone selling the idea of sleeping near them.

If you’re raising children here, don’t worry — we’ve always had options. MumsPages rounded up kids’ activities like Grasshopper Soccer and the North Coastal Children’s Community Choir at the Quinns Mindarie Community Centre, so there’s structured chaos for every age. And if the chaos gets to you, Connecting Dots Counselling was listed as a local support option — practical, grounding, and unflashy.

Who would hate living here? People allergic to slow coastal glamour, anyone who can’t deal with dogs on alfresco terraces, or those who think festivals are just an excuse for burning sausages.

Who would love it? Families who want choir practice and soccer on the same postcode, anyone who treats a brewery and a marina as cultural institutions, and us — the ones who still call a poolside daybed “self-care” and mean it.
