# Freshwater (NSW)

Slug: `freshwater-nsw`

Back in the day we used to tell visitors “welcome to Freshie” and point them at the swell. These days, our story in the papers reads like a tourist RSVP: Freshwater Beach — the spot Time Out reminds you is where Duke Kahanamoku’s 1914 demo basically invented our weekend plans — and a clutch of beloved venues that put our little cove on the map.

We start Saturdays with sand between the toes and a reconnaissance lap past the weatherboard house that Broadsheet and Time Out wrote about for years — Pilu at Freshwater — the Sardinian place Giovanni Pilu put on the culinary map in 2004. There was the Pilu Kiosk, famous for its suckling pig panini, and the Pilu team would later pop up over on the Harbour with Flaminia. If we’re feeling less formal, we wander past St Alma’s Mexican-inspired offerings and remember Concrete Playground’s shout-outs to Ribelle and Pilu Baretto. If the tide’s high and the mood is nostalgic, we spy the Harbord Hotel’s rooftop — Bombies — the ’70s-inspired nook Urban List liked to brag about, and the Harbord’s Balsa seafood downstairs that Concrete Playground once reviewed.

Our weekday rhythm used to include the Surfing Mums listing (Freshwater, Monday 9AM), Joey Jig Music Tuesday mornings, and a quick stop at Freshwater Massage on Harbord Rd when the sand had lodged itself in a tendon. Sitchu once called out Shaka @ Freshwater for capturing that easy “Freshie” energy — which is our diplomatic way of saying we’ll always smell like the ocean and complain about parking.

Who would hate living here? People allergic to community noticeboards, playground literacy (Major Fun Freshwater is the Harbord Diggers’ answer to “let kids burn energy”), or any suburb that unapologetically serves surf culture with a side of fine dining. If you crave anonymity, you’ll be introduced to everyone in line for the park.

Who loves it? Families who want a tiny beachfront village, surfers who treat history like a hobby, and food people who still tell Pilu stories like they’re gospel. We’re small, salty and a touch spare on scandal — and frankly, we like it that way.
