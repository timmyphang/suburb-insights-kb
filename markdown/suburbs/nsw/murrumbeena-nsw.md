# Murrumbeena (NSW)

Slug: `murrumbeena-nsw`

Back in the day we liked to tell people Murrumbeena was quietly making moves — not with a siren, but with a tray of hot doughnuts. We remember Oasis Bakery handing out 10,000 free doughnuts for International Doughnut Day and the Pistachio Papi pop‑ups that made the suburb briefly smell like ricotta and ambition. Oasis, the family‑run Middle Eastern bakery‑cum‑grocery, has long been our excuse to queue for a Lebanese‑style sausage roll, a piece of baklava or a trolley‑full of pantry stuff we didn’t know we needed.

Our weekend routine used to look very local: drop the kids at Murrumbeena Playgroup (it’s been running since 1974, so yes, our grandparents probably did parachute time), detour to Nature Boy or Iron Henry if you’ve got small people in tow, then loop past Levi on Railway Parade — the ex‑denim factory turned sprawling cafe near the station where the roasted‑chicken roll and chilli eggs made us feel industrious. If we were feeling civilised, we’d pop into Citizen, the former milk bar where Jansen Domantay used to be the friendliest face for Padre coffee and reimagined smashed avo.

We’ll admit our local culture is the polite kind of chaos: the Murrumbeena Wine Bar — celebrated in guidebooks as the neighbourhood’s cosy first bar — used to run live music, a weekly book club and a dog‑friendly courtyard; there was even a Wine Wednesday or Tequila Thursday on the calendar somewhere between the live sets. Meanwhile, Dance, Be In It Function Centre was keeping a different kind of rhythm with decades of classes, and Competition Tyres & More has been quietly under the same roof on North Road for more than 50 years.

Who would hate living here? If you need a neon-lit, 24/7 CBD pulse, Murrumbeena’s suburban hum will drive you mad. If your idea of a wild night is a razzle at a rooftop club, you’ll be disappointed by our book clubs and doughnut giveaways.

Who would love it? Families, food obsessives and anyone who prefers a neighbourhood that knows its bakers, its cafe owners and where to get your tyres fixed by someone who’s been there for half a century. For years the local press called us a suburb “to watch” — we’ll take that, with a jam‑filled doughnut in hand.
