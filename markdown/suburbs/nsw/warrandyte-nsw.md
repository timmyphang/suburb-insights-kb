# Warrandyte (NSW)

Slug: `warrandyte-nsw`

We know the footpaths here like the bottoms of our hiking boots — mud, gumleaf confetti and the occasional artisan candle stub. On a Sunday morning back in the day we'd amble down Yarra Street, grab something flaky from Field Day and pretend the riverside strip was our very own Scandi escape. As Broadsheet wrote, Field Day was quietly reshaping the riverfront — and honestly, who are we to argue with house‑made pastries and curated homewares when the river’s doing the heavy lifting for atmosphere?

If you want to properly lose a few hours, we used to head for Pound Bend at Warrandyte State Park — Concrete Playground name‑checked it as one of the best nearby swimming holes — and then wander the Black Flat via Blue Tongue Bend Loop because Time Out called it “the most peaceful hike in Australia” and we like to rub that in to city friends. For a gentler afternoon, the five‑acre Warran Glen Garden Centre and Cafe used to be the kind of place that made us feel faintly smug about our succulents.

We’re a spoilt little patch: Pietro Gallus’s olive groves and grappa tasting flights give the place a slightly Tuscan vibe (Urban List did a whole feature), Rob Dolan Wines had a porch you could get lost on with charcuterie and Stone & Crow cheese, and Altair and La Sala were the restaurants the city types mentioned when they wanted their bush dinner with a side of panoramic remorse. Homing Instincts was where you upcycled your aesthetic guilt into something beautiful for the house.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe kangaroos, rivers, and the existential decision between a creek swim at Pound Bend and yet another picnic hut at Pietro Gallus, this is not for you. If the idea of boutique jams from Warrandyte Berry Farm, mid‑century Casa Warrandyte Airbnb hideaways, and walking trails that make your phone battery feel inadequate sounds like torment, move along.

Who would love it? People who want a backyard that’s essentially a national park, who consider a grappa tasting room a valid weekend plan, or who like collecting locally made bread and stories. Back in the day we turned up for the river — and we’re still turning up for the peace, the pastries, and the long excuses to leave the city behind.
