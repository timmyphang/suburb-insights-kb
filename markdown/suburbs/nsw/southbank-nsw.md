# Southbank (NSW)

Slug: `southbank-nsw`

We know the moment: you step off at Flinders Street, cross over, and suddenly the city loosens its tie — NGV on one side, Crown glittering the other, and us, trotting along the Southbank Promenade pretending we don’t know which way to look first. Back in the day we’d start with a river cruise from the promenade for the skyline gossip, then wander past the Pepsi Max Vanilla Love Wall (James Goldcrown — iconic), spotting which restaurant Broadsheet had hailed that month.

Weekends used to be a choreographed blur of eating guides and hotel openings. Broadsheet and Time Out ran the rota — Nobu, Rockpool Bar & Grill, Bistro Guillaume, DOC/D.O.C., Marmont, Kolkata Cricket Club — and we’d vote with our feet. The Hannah St Hotel and its Coupette got a bit of column inches in 2026; before that we argued about the Red Stairs redesign and tried to work out if Aura and Melbourne Square were new neighbours or just very shiny scaffolding. Concrete Playground’s master plan talk — expanded lagoon, rainforest treetop walk, and “no more piazza” — gave us something to grumble about while sipping and pretending we weren’t.

Petty local drama? We had festival season: On the Banks turned the Cultural Forecourt into a proper gig site (The Streets, De La Soul, Marlon Williams rocked March 2026), and Southwave filled Rainforest Green with DJs like Maribou State and Folamour. For a week we were music critics, then back to being tourists.

Who would hate living here: anyone who prefers shade — the Southbank Boulevard Play Space (Mike Hewson’s ‘risky play’ boulders) is brilliant for adventurous kids but the limited shade gives us all premature tans. Also, people who loathe queues, cameras, and restaurants that make you feel both cultured and slightly out of pocket will not be our people.

Who would love it: culture addicts and food nerds who keep a Broadsheet tab open, families who clock a River Gardens cruise or book Oakwood Premier Melbourne for convention-friendly stays, and anyone who enjoys the slow civic experiment of a precinct in mid-revamp — Laak Boorndap, new hotels, and all. We’ll keep strolling, snapping at the Love Wall, and arguing about which riverside listicle got it right. Southbank: loud, shiny, and very much ours.
