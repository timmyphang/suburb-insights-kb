# Kingscliff (NSW)

Slug: `kingscliff-nsw`

Back in the day — and by “day” we mean everything in the files your aunt forwarded us from the internet — Kingscliff was the kind of place you’d mention in the same breath as Casuarina and Cabarita and expect everyone to nod like they know the coastline’s gossip.

We know the rhythm here: morning walks from Kingscliff Waters to Cudgen Creek, the sea on one side and a sense that everyone’s at least three degrees from someone who once played in a band. Remember the Sunnyboys? Yep — they hailed from Kingscliff, which gives us vintage cred when we nod solemnly at rafters and say “local legend.” Back then you could pop into a pocket-sized Salt Mill outpost and order Allpress coffee while someone loaded flowers into the boot at Farm & Co, and feel very pleased with your life choices.

There was a time the town leaned into being charmingly small-town—Taverna Kingscliff sold eGift cards for dinners that felt like family occasions, and Spice Den carried the Northern Rivers’ flavours down the coast enough that Broadsheet took note. The Blue Water Motel was newly revamped and dog-friendly, Jason Grant-spruced and saltwater-pool-glam in the brochures. Concrete Playground ran a “Seas the Day” women’s surf event at Kingscliff Beach — 300 competitors and workshops — which, honestly, made the whole place look like a surf-culture brochure come to life.

Our civic pride? Practical. The Family Centre once ran a Kingscliff Beach forum at the Kingscliff Beach Bowls Club (131 Marine Parade), and the Commonwealth Bank famously nominated The Family Centre for a $10,000 grant to run respectful-relationships programs in our schools. We’ve got Kingscliff High School and Kingscliff Public School, playgroups listed on Playgroup NSW, and GKR Karate for when someone needs to learn how to behave on Marine Parade.

Who’d hate it here: anyone whose weekend plan is “drive to somewhere with neon and three-level carparks.” We’re quiet, we’re coastal, and we get smug about a good coastal walk. Who’d love it: nature people, surfers (especially the kind who turn up for a women’s surf event), families who value community programs, and anyone who enjoys a motel with a saltwater pool and a retro vibe.

We’re not Byron. We’re Kingscliff — smaller, a little stubborn, and quietly excellent when you take the time to walk to the water.
