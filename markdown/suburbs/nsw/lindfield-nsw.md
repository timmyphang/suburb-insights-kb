# Lindfield (NSW)

Slug: `lindfield-nsw`

We shuffle off the 7:02 into Lindfield like we own a parking spot we don’t — except we don’t need to boast. Back in the day this is where we queued opposite Lindfield train station for Goodfields Eatery, the light‑filled place run by father and son George and Anthony Karnasiotis, trading lobster Benedicts and butter‑roasted lobster tails for brief, delicious smugness. The same era had Five Senses coffee on the list and a bagel with lobster nobody outside the North Shore could quite believe.

Our weekend ritual used to be embarrassingly predictable: a quick browse at Lindfield Bookshop on the Pacific Highway (yes, they had a kids’ space and would order that obscure picture book), then a detour to Gelatiamo for supposedly artisanal gelato, before apologising to the green streets because we were late for a park picnic. Café Lyon was the continental whisper in our midst — the cosy, French option if we were feeling cultured and fiscally conservative.

There was a brief, thrilling flirtation with nightlife when the Blackout Cafe, named after the London Blitz, offered dim rooms, cocktails and live music three nights a week — a fact we still mention at dinner parties when we want to prove Lindfield had edge. The listing now notes it’s permanently closed, which is somehow very Lindfield: polite, tasteful, then quietly gone.

If you’re the person who needs a rave on a Tuesday or someone to judge you for walking past a period home, Lindfield will bore you senseless. If you think “aspirational high street” and “large common” are reasons to live somewhere, you’ll fit right in. Estate listings used to tout brand‑new pads on Tryon Road “opposite Lindfield Oval” for people who like their modern interiors with a side of school pickup convenience.

Who loves us? Families who want excellent schools, leafy streets and a playgroup at Two Turners Reserve (we remember the Thursday sessions at 9:00 and 11:30). Architects and tourists once came to Lindfield Learning Village for the Kevin McCloud and Tim Ross “Live in Interesting Places” tour — because yes, even our quiet has interesting bones.

We’re not trying to be cool. We’re quietly pleasant, slightly smug about our brunch past, and very happy to wave from behind our hedges.
