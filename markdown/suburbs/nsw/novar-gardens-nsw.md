# Novar Gardens (NSW)

Slug: `novar-gardens-nsw`

Back in the day we’d brag that Novar Gardens was the kind of place where your weekend plan could be written on the back of a discount footy ticket and still fit. We live where Anzac Hwy points you straight to 489 Anzac Hwy — the Novar Gardens Bowling Club/Sports Club — the venue that used to host everything from Night Owls barefoot bowls to pétanque and venue hire. If you wanted local social life, that courtyard was our unofficial town square.

Our lazy Saturdays often looked the same: drop the kids at one of the tiny but stubbornly useful playgrounds, then wander past Cummins House. The Cummins Society kept the old place ticking with guided tours and those proper afternoon Devonshire teas (twice monthly, Feb–Nov), and there were even weekly garden and plant sales for anyone who enjoys pretending their postage-stamp yard is a cottage estate. Immanuel College at 32 Morphett Rd quietly reminds you this suburb has a serious school backbone — co-ed, Years 7–12, and even boarding once you count the futures it sends away.

If you have small people, we used to rely on Montreal Avenue Reserve — it was recently upgraded with toddler-friendly bits (dual slippery dip, nest swing, the lot). Lindfield Avenue Reserve was our big-park flex: shade, paths and room to actually cycle without giving busy parents a panic attack. St Andrew’s Reserve though — as Kids in Adelaide pointed out — has heaps of grass and parking but NO shade, NO toilets and it is NOT fenced, so it’s the place where we all practice our yelling technique.

Petty local drama? None of the snippets gave us juicy council feuds, so instead we’ll accuse the suburb of being quietly efficient and unbothered. MumsPages used to list all the local services — learning support, fitness, kitchen renovators — like a polite little black book for practical people.

Who would hate living here? If you need 24/7 cafés, dramatic nightlife or playgrounds with attendants, this isn’t your scene. Who’d love it? Families who like history with their picnic, folks who enjoy community bowls nights, and anyone who appreciates a suburb that quietly gets on with being useful. We’ll keep the Devonshire tea warm.
