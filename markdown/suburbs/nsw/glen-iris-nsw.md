# Glen Iris (NSW)

Slug: `glen-iris-nsw`

Back in the day we used to treat Burke Road like our own tiny cultural parade — sleepy school-runs, then a low-key culinary glow-up. We’d wander past Pantry Glen Iris and watch that rotating sign flip: bright brunch daylight, then Too Good Talker’s moody night energy. As Broadsheet noted, the place really knew how to do two faces.

Weekends were a very particular kind of local ritual. We’d pick up Grazia’s hard-to-find Ligurian focaccia or one of those pistachio bomboloni near Central Park, then debate whether to decamp to the vast beer garden that used to be Deeds Brewing. Huzzah — the big, open-air venture in the old Deeds site — felt like our communal backyard: bocce, pool, a retractable roof and about 15 taps. Concrete Playground and Urban List made a big song and dance about it being more of a social club than a pub. Then came the awkward chapter: Deeds Tap Room closed (it shut its doors on 22 March 2025), and Steve Jeffares later picked up the place with plans for the Paran Place Project. We watched that saga like it was our soap opera.

There’s zero pretence here — Sitchu’s right: leafy streets, white picket ambitions, pools and tennis courts galore. If you crave late-night neon and gritty alleyways, Glen Iris will bore you politely to death. If you loathe bocce, good coffee choices with theatrical sign-swapping, and a wine bar with a Hellenic vibe on Burke Road (Bar Sophia used to be a thing), then move on — this suburb will make you avidly miss the city.

But who thrives here? Families, obviously — with the Harold Holt Swim Centre, Gardiner Park, Bounce at 2 Weir St for the hyperactive small people, and the Save-A-Dog Scheme doing quietly heroic work for adopters. For us locals, Glen Iris has always been the kind of place that quietly does community: big patios, small rituals, and the occasional pizza that convinces you you’ve stumbled into Europe without needing a plane. We’ll keep minding the bocce balls and watching the sign flip — nostalgia suits us.
