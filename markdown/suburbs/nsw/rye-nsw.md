# Rye (NSW)

Slug: `rye-nsw`

Rye, NSW — back in the day our media folder looked like the lovechild of a geography quiz and a copy-paste error. We’re proud locals who know every corner of our postcode, but confession time: the only write-ups handed to us were older features about other Ryes — and we’ve learned to lean into the confusion.

Mornings here, in our imagination, begin on a pier with a signposted “Octopus Garden” — that’s Concrete Playground’s take on Rye Pier — and drift past rock pools and snorkel trails. The town in those clippings has a sprawling seaside pub called Haba (as Broadsheet noted), dreamed up by Jerome Borazio, squeezing live music, DJ sets and seafood into one 220‑person room. We’re not claiming it’s ours, but we’ll happily borrow their band schedule when explaining to visitors why we sound so cultured.

Back in the day Broadsheet also introduced Store 360 at 360 Dundas Street — pies, toasties and Dromana’s Kelter Coffee — and Angela Strickland’s Independent Wine Store, which opened in 2013 and stocked more than 300 bottles. Urban List liked Peninsula Pantry’s tucked-away vanilla slice so much they penciled it into bakery pilgrimages. Time Out, meanwhile, assembled a guide that insists “Rye is packed with great things to do” — presumably the sort of things you do in the Rye that already has four different tourism websites.

If you hate being constantly mistaken for a Mornington Peninsula day‑trip, you’ll grumble here. If you’re allergic to vanilla slices, boutique wine or the concept of an “Octopus Garden” sign, then frankly, move along.

But if you love beaches, village‑style shopping, a family‑friendly foreshore and the vague promise of a carnival (Sitchu reminds us of the annual Rye Summer Carnival and nearby hot springs), then come on over. We’ll point you to the nearest sand patch, tell you which old article to show your out-of-towner, and roast you for ordering the wrong Rye. After all, identity crises are just coastal charm with a past.
