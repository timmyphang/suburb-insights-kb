# Grays Point (NSW)

Slug: `grays-point-nsw`

Back in the day we’d launch our weekend with a walk down to Swallow Rock Reserve at the end of Swallow Rock Drive, peer at the Hacking River and argue about whether the water looks swimable or like a Netflix documentary about soggy socks. Then we’d mosey up to 110 Grays Point Road — Jack Gray — which used to be our neighbourhood cafe-by-day and, on select nights, a wine bar with live-music nights that felt like a slightly classier school reunion. Concrete Playground’s guide even had us pencilled in as the suburb with one cafe result; humble, but proud.

We don’t have a bustling main strip, and that’s the point. Our local CV reads: one notable cafe-turned-wine-bar, a lovely riverside reserve, and the sort of quiet where you can hear your neighbour leafleting their own lawn. For years that slim dossier defined us; Concrete Playground listed Jack Gray and even included it in a 2024 roundup of Sydney cafes — nostalgia, not a tourist brochure.

We’ll be blunt: if you need a 24/7 nightclub, neon sushi joints or six different coffee chains to feel alive, you’ll hate our satisfied quiet. But if you love river swims, low-key live music and a place that remembers the faces at its counter, you’ll adore this. We also quietly remember the sad day emergency services were called to Swallow Rock after a boat crash — we don’t make light of that; it’s part of our history too.

Who loves Grays Point? Us. Small, rooted and a little bit smug about it.
