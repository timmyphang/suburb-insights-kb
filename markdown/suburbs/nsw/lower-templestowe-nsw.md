# Lower Templestowe (NSW)

Slug: `lower-templestowe-nsw`

We start Saturdays like proper locals used to: a slow roll down to Macedon Square to see who’d nab a corner table at Riddik — the gutsy all‑day eatery that moved into an old ANZ branch at 1–3 The Mall, if you remember (as Broadsheet and Concrete Playground dutifully chronicled). It felt like someone repurposed a bank vault into brunch: decor with deposit-book energy, menu with full‑time ambition. Concrete Playground even called it “gutsy” back in the day — correctly, because we ate like it was our last deposit.

If Riddik was too on the nose, there was always Down The Rabbit Hole for the whimsical crowd — Urban List described the Alice‑in‑Wonderland motif that made even a sleepy Wednesday feel like a themed party. And when grandparents were in town we dragged them to the Golden Dragon Palace next to the McDonald’s for weekend yum cha (Time Out’s picks reminded us why the place filled up).

We’ve got parks that make you feel like you live inside a postcard. Sitchu name‑checks the Yarra River loops and Westerfolds Park, and Fitzsimons Lane turns up whenever someone suggests a picnic that will, predictably, last three hours and include a competitive game of “who left the esky at home.” Playgroup Victoria kept the littlies entertained — the Templestowe Baptist Church playgroup and regional PlayConnect+ listings were the kind of organised chaos we secretly love.

Petty drama? Back in 2020 we had the Yarra Valley Water boil‑water warning — Broadsheet remembers that one — which turned our suburban serenity into a low‑budget apocalyptic comedy: bottled water queues and conspiracy theories about tea. Otherwise our scandals tend to be the gentle kind: who took whose parking spot at The Templestowe Hotel, which Time Out notes has an indoor playground and arcade so the kids can be louder than the jukebox.

Who would hate living here? People who crave 24/7 nightlife, footpaths crowded with boutique shops, or anywhere that isn’t content with bigger blocks and riverbank peace. Who’d love it? Families, picnic obsessives, dumpling devotees and anyone who appreciates a repurposed bank turned all‑day diner — and a community noticeboard that never runs out of things to do (FindItLocally, we see you). We may not be flashy, but we’re comfortably loud, leafier than you expect, and forever proud of our modest suburban eccentricities.
