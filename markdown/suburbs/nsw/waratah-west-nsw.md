# Waratah West (NSW)

Slug: `waratah-west-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll the pram like it was a suburban ritual — a pilgrimage to whichever patch of bark-chip and swing set was doing the rounds. We’re Waratah West, so our weekend looks like a gently competitive circuit: Morpeth Road Reserve (yes, the one the City of Newcastle had on the list for replacement), then a detour if someone’s remembering Waratah Park behind Callaghan College.

Waratah Park used to be our pride: balancing equipment, that very small in-ground trampoline that makes grown-ups question physics, a climbing tower and swings. There’s actually parking, some shade and toilets — the sort of practical luxuries that feel like victory when you have a toddler and a long list of errands that include “nap” and “somewhere to sit down.”

For years the council’s playground audit (as Newy with Kids noted) put Morpeth Road Reserve on the upgrade roster alongside Johnson Park at Lambton and even the little setup at Stockton Pool — thrilling municipal bureaucracy, we know. But these lists are from the era of “watch this space,” so treat our nostalgia like a museum label rather than a hard promise.

If you like exhaustive directories (and let’s be honest, we do), MumsPages once had pages and pages listing businesses and service providers for mums in 2298 — interior designers, babies-and-kids supplies, health and wellbeing offerings. Back in the day we used it like a secret map: where to find someone who could wallpaper a nursery without judging your colour choices.

Who would hate living here? People who need a glittering nightlife guide or a postcode full of trend-chasing boutiques. If your vibe is “constant supply of artisanal whatever,” you’ll probably clock out faster than a kid off the trampoline.

Who would love it here? Families who measure success in functional playgrounds, nearby parking and a directory that actually lists where to get a highchair for hire. We’re not pretending to be the city’s centre of culinary innovation — we’re a neighbourhood that remembers what matters when your calendar is full of kinder pickups and park meetups. Come for the swings, stay for the sensible toilets.
