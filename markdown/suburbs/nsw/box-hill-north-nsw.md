# Box Hill North (NSW)

Slug: `box-hill-north-nsw`

We’ll be honest: all the clippings about our patch are “back in the day” notes — the lot of them are pre‑2024 or undated — so think old guidebooks, not new openings. Still, we know every corner of Box Hill North like the back of a well‑worn shopping tote.

A Saturday for us used to mean wandering down towards Box Hill Central, where the Fresh Food Market was doing its best impression of a small continent and Concrete Playground told everyone where to find the vinyl, the ramen and the plant that won’t die. If we were feeling theatrical we’d swing past Karen Margaret Studio of Performing Arts and imagine our kids as triple threats; if we wanted cardio with rhythm it was Dance Dynamics on Station Street making ballroom look deceptively sweaty.

We remember restaurants being the bragging rights — Urban List’s old guide called out a whole pan‑Asian renaissance, and yes, The Penny Drop at 913 Whitehorse Road used to be the place we pretended was casual but was really a modern Australian flex with cheeky Asian riffs. Zero Mode in Whitehorse Towers had the sort of viral dessert energy that made our phones heavier. Ichi Ramen? The broth that convinced us instant noodles were a sin we’d gladly confess to.

There’s a charm here that’s oddly middlebrow — vinyl next to ramen next to a garden centre — and that’s the point. If you hate choice, get out now. If you need your suburb to look like a single Instagram aesthetic, you’ll loathe the delicious chaos. If you flinch at markets, long lunch menus, kids in leotards or people who buy plants as emotional commitments, Box Hill North will make you very twitchy.

But who would love it? Food obsessives, bargain hunters at the Fresh Food Market, parents looking at Kerrimuir Primary School, Box Hill High School or Blackburn High School and thinking “that’s a plan,” and anyone who enjoys a neighbourhood that can serve ramen, records and a weekend dance class within a few tram stops. We’re proudly unfussy, and yes, slightly overcaffeinated by history — come for the food, stay because you accidentally bought a ficus.
