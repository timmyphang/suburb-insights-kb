# Plumpton (NSW)

Slug: `plumpton-nsw`

We start our Saturday at 63 Bottles Road, juggling backpacks and permission slips while Plumpton Long Day Care waves us off like a slightly overwhelmed cruise director. We’ve got good reason to be smug: on April 10, 2024 Children First’s Plumpton centre scored a NSW Government grant to kickstart an Integrated Early Intervention Preschool Program and add ten licensed places — yes, the kind of small victory that gets everyone toasts at the school gate.

By mid-morning we’re doing the pilgrimage most locals know: bulk essentials at BIG W on the corner of Hyatts & Jersey Rd, then a gentle detour to the new Farm Shop at Eden Yard. It’s actually charming in a suburb-that-doesn’t-pretend-to-be-Melbourne way — open Tue–Sat 9:00–16:30, Sun 10:00–15:00, selling market garden produce and, improbably, kiln-dried logs and Cumbrian barbecue charcoal. Fancy, but in a sensible, we-won’t-make-you-feel-guilty-about-keeping-the-oven-on kind of way.

Our petty local drama is mostly administrative: Nature Corner, the free playgroup run by Children First, is listed as temporarily paused — a reliable source of small anxieties for mums and dads who like routines. If you want structure with your chaos, there’s always Plumpton TurkuOz meeting Mondays at 9:30AM in the Neighbourhood Centre on 337 Rooty Hill Road North — the little weekly anchor in our calendar everyone appreciates.

Who’d hate living here? If your weekend mood is boutique laneway brunching and day-spa cosplay — the Hannah St. Hotel and 25‑metre lap pool crowd — you’ll be frustrated. We’re not Southbank. If you need a photogenic 60-seat daytime diner on every corner, go back to reading Concrete Playground’s Melbourne wishlist.

Who’d love it here? Families, absolutely: childcare options (shoutout to Children First and the Neighbourhood Centre), practical shopping at BIG W, local produce at Eden Yard’s Farm Shop, and an actually renovated local watering hole — the Plumpton Hotel on Richmond Road in Glendenning — that runs live music, big-screen events and a kids’ playground when you need it. We’re not pretending to be a city suburb, and we like it that way. Come for the sensible wins, stay for the community stuff that actually works.
