# Edensor Park (NSW)

Slug: `edensor-park-nsw`

Back in the day we’d mark Friday nights by the scent of beef and fries wafting through the car park — Holy Heffa, the American diner–inspired food truck that used to pull crowds in the Edensor Park/Bonnyrigg car park. Broadsheet wrote about it in 2016: long queues, milkshakes, specials and the famous Homeboy Heffa burger. We weren’t above queuing either — you know what a good burger does to a suburb’s morale.

For years the local narrative, at least in the pieces we can find, has been refreshingly simple: big burgers and a lot of childcare centres. Kindikids has been more or less a local institution — Centre 1 at 300 Edensor Road has been around since 1997, Centre 2 at 227 Edensor Road covers babies through pre-k, and Centre 3 on 33 Boomerang Road arrived in 2004. The centres’ descriptions remind us this place is a proper family precinct: shaded play spaces, playground equipment and a rotation of tiny humans who will one day run the place (after nap time).

If you came here hoping for a spicy local feud or a council kerfuffle to gossip about, you’ll be disappointed — the archive is mercifully low-drama. The most heated debates, historically, have been about whether the milkshake special should be vanilla or chocolate. Riveting.

Who would hate living here? If your happiness is measured by open-plan rooftop bars with curated playlists and a rotating cast of barista influencers, Edensor Park will feel a bit quiet. If you need endless late-night eateries or an indie record store on every corner, this isn’t your postcode.

Who’d love it? Parents who appreciate childcare options with real tenure (we can point to those Kindikids dates like a badge), people who enjoy a solid, no-nonsense feed in a car park when the food truck rolled through, and anyone who prefers suburbs that get on with it rather than staging themselves. We don’t pretend to be anything we’re not — just a place where kids learn, burgers once drew a crowd, and life is comfortably unflashy.
