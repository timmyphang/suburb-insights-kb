# Hampton East (NSW)

Slug: `hampton-east-nsw`

Saturday mornings in Hampton East start like this: we convoy down Hampton Street, arguing about whether 427 or 444 is the better place to blame for our pastry shortage. As Broadsheet once catalogued for us, The Hamptons Bakery sat at 427 Hampton Street and Hampton Wine Co. held court at 444 Hampton Street — relics of our bayside civility, filed under “places we fondly talk about in passive voice.”

Our weekend routine used to be simple: someone would swing by The Hamptons Bakery (Broadsheet and Urban List both took note of it) while another pretended to understand the Hampton Wine Co. wine list. Then we’d drop the small people at community play — because, yes, Playgroup Victoria and the Hampton Children’s Playhouse actually keep this corner human. Playgroup Victoria lists the Playhouse and, per the Playhouse’s own notes, the place is mid-renovation in 2026 and temporarily at 665 Hampton St, Brighton (Cnr Were & Hampton Sts). We applaud the scaffolding as progress and mutter about where the puppets have gone.

Petty local drama? We’ve got it in mini-episodes. La Svolta — the woodfire pizzeria Broadsheet covered at 450 Hampton Street — was updated as “Permanently Closed” in March 2025, which prompted the sort of civic grief usually reserved for beloved footy players and old roundabouts. Meanwhile, Sitchu’s Home Tour reminded everyone that The Block’s Kristy and Brett once made Charming Street look like a mood board come to life, proving you can renovate a five-bedroom house and still leave the neighbours suspiciously proud.

Who would hate Hampton East? People who crave 24/7 neon and indecipherable menus will find our calm coastal stub intolerable. If you measure a suburb in after-dark options and immediate culinary exotica, we’re politely not for you.

Who would love it? Families and anyone who appreciates leafier streets that have a storybook of local mentions — Broadsheet listings, Urban List shout-outs, Playgroup Victoria credentials, and the Playhouse’s yearly refurbishments — will feel at home. We’re the sort of place that keeps its bakery in memory, its wine bar in gossip, and its playgroups loud enough to prove the future is still happening here.
