# Wayville (NSW)

Slug: `wayville-nsw`

Back in the day, we judged weekends by what was happening on the patch of tarmac that is the Adelaide Showground. One minute it was a nest of ceramics and hand-thrown optimism at the Wayville Pavilion — Bowerbird Design Market bringing in something like 140–160 stallholders, Clay by Khoa and Ashlee Hopkins making debuts, $5 entry and kids for free — the next it was a river of flannelette and band tees for Groovin the Moo. Only in Wayville do you get artisan pottery elbowing festival wristbands on the same lawn.

Our morning routine used to be embarrassingly wholesome: queue for a pastry (The Village Baker, 113 Goodwood Road, got the nod in round-ups), dodge the Adelaide Farmers’ Market crowd where Abbots and Kinney used to show up, and then wander past the Goodwood Road taproom that Time Out called The Suburban Brew. By lunch the Goodwood Park Hotel — “The Goody” to those who remember the listing — had cheap beer and a live-music hum that smelled faintly of meal-deals. Sitchu even listed the Ridley Centre and Showground as Illuminate Adelaide spots for 2026, so we’ve always been equal parts craft market and light installation.

There’s no juicy council scandal in our files — just pleasant contradictions. The same concrete that hosted Royal Adelaide Show exhibitions and Groovin the Moo line-ups (Concrete Playground and Pedestrian TV have our festival history in neat lists) also hosted ceramic workshops and indie pop stalls. We prefer it that way: polite chaos beats boring suburbia.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t cope with the idea of stepping over espresso-laden shoppers, festival crowds, or the occasional craft fair queue for a handmade bowl, Wayville will drive you mad. Also, if you’re allergic to events happening on your doorstep — well, tough luck.

Who would love it? Makers, parents trawling MumsPages and Playgroup SA listings for local services, festival people, and anyone who likes their neighbourhood to have both a ceramics stall and a stage billboard. We’re a little bit muddy, a little bit musical, and embarrassingly proud of every pop-up and pint.
