# Redhead (NSW)

Slug: `redhead-nsw`

We greet the day from the high cliff that marks the end of a very particular kind of coast: Redhead Beach, where the long strip of sand finally runs into rocky outcrops and a little shark tower keeps watch while the sun does its thing. Back in the day travel guides told us to "soak up the first rays with a sunrise walk at Redhead Beach," and honestly, we still do — partly for the light, partly to prove to every weekend pilgrim that yes, this is the coast they scrolled for.

Our neighbourhood reads like a guidebook mash-up. Concrete Playground called the 14‑kilometre strip scenic and noted those dramatic rock formations that make our morning strolls look calibrated for postcards. Junkee reminded outsiders that Redhead anchors a long stretch of coastline up to Blacksmiths — 15km, depending which guide you ask — so anyone expecting instant city grid will have to drive for their avocado-on-toast level urbanity elsewhere.

If you fancy a bit more wild, Urban List points at Belmont Wetlands State Park — on Awakabal Country — as the nearby patch of dunes, wetlands and 4WD-friendly scrub around Lake Macquarie. Translation: if your idea of a weekend is suspiciously dusty camping rigs and sand in every crevice, you’ll fit right in. If your idea of a weekend involves clipped lawns and silent courtyards, you might not.

We don’t have much in the way of flashy nightlife listed in these clippings — apart from an obscure Concrete Playground note about a band called "That Redhead" showing up on free‑music roundups — so yes, we’re quiet by intent. Time Out’s odd little detour into national redhead pride antics (RANGA and pals) is a reminder that the country loves a ginger joke more than it probably should; we own that with a grin.

Who would hate living here? People allergic to sand, cliffs, morning light, or guns‑a‑blazing natural beauty. Who would love it? Sunrise walkers, coastal stargazers, anyone who thinks a shark tower is a charming municipal quirk rather than an alarm system.

We’re small, scenic and stubbornly honest about it — come for the beach, respect the wetlands on Awakabal Country, and don’t be surprised if you leave with sand in your shoes and a new appreciation for the word "Redhead."
