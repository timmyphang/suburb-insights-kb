# Willagee (NSW)

Slug: `willagee-nsw`

We start our mornings like it’s a low-key pilgrimage: back in the day we’d roll down Leach Highway to 73 Leach Highway and line up at Everyday Bread for a loaf that made the rest of the suburbs feel carb-lazy. As Broadsheet said, they brought sourdough and community spirit — and, whisper it, were planning to add coffee and takeaway sandwiches before the internet told us to get up earlier.

If you’re imagining artisanal chaos, Willagee’s got that in all the right corners. Pelican Croissanterie used to be our best-kept secret for croissants that vanished by midday (pistachio and ham‑and‑cheese devotees, you know who you are). Then there was Top Dup — famous for doughnuts they delivered all over town — which planted a flag at 82 Archibald Street and spawned Slice Dup for late-night Detroit‑style pan pizza that made us all slightly more mysterious on a Saturday.

We also used to wile away afternoons at Little Olive Leaf Cafe’s garden and judge each other’s muffin choices, and order pad thai, satay or pineapple fried rice from Tida Thai when we wanted to pretend we were at the beach without actually getting sand in our shoes. Full Spectrum Hair Concept was the place we waved at on Instagram and then nervously booked because our fringe had declared independence.

Facts, not rumours: Sitchu even put Willagee on an investment list, saying prices lifted more than 15% in a year and a typical house clocked in at roughly $640,000 — which means our humble suburb has the sort of glow-up that makes estate agents very polite and slightly smug.

Who would hate living here? People who need 24/7 haute cuisine, endless anonymity, or a quiet suburb that never once smells faintly of freshly baked temptation. This isn’t for the forever-clubbing crowd or anyone allergic to a community centre that actually runs things that matter — Willagee Community Centre at the corner of Winnacott and Archibald Sts has been a real place for real gatherings.

Who would love it? Young families, pastry hoarders, investors with a spreadsheet, retirees at Weeronga Village on Worley Street, and anyone who thinks being a short drive from the beaches and Fremantle is a life hack. We’re small, a bit proud, and deliciously underrated — and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
