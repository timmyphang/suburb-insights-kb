# Meadowbank (NSW)

Slug: `meadowbank-nsw`

It’s a second‑Sunday morning and we’re negotiating our way past a sea of reusable bags and artisanal everything at the Ryde Wharf Market — the one that, if you read the papers back in the day, turned up on the Parramatta River foreshore at Anderson Park with about 70 stalls every second and fourth Sunday. Parking was always premium, and the ferry terminal was, as Time Out put it, a “pleasant stroll” away — which is council‑speak for “good luck finding a park”.

Our usual weekend loop used to be simple: a quick hop off the train at Meadowbank station, stroll the Parramatta Riverwalk, sidle past Memorial Park for a look at the picnic groupies, then into Anderson Park for market browsing. If we weren’t watching toddlers tackle the Meadowbank Park Regional Playground (the one on Constitution Road with entry points at Ross Smith Avenue) we’d be pretending the $3.2m skate park — finished in 2019 — was there purely for cultural enrichment, not aggressive ollies.

Local property listings used to sell the dream with the exact words “perfect for astute investors or couples,” which is peak suburb‑marketing and frankly adorable. Sitchu’s blurbs loved reminding people how close we were to the station, the wharf and even the TAFE — all the ingredients for commuter chic or very sensible life choices, depending on how you feel about trains at 7:30am.

We don’t get into much drama — the facts are our spice: market days, premium parking, a playground that will win any toddler’s heart, and a skate park that took the bragging rights in 2019. It keeps things wholesome and slightly chaotic.

Who would hate it here? If you loathe weekend crowds, can’t abide bikes and skateboards, or think a ferry terminal is an affront to serenity, Meadowbank is not your jam. Who would love it? Families who measure Sundays by playground time, market detectives who judge people by tote‑bag choices, and commuters who like the convenience of train, wharf and river on their doorstep.

We know every corner from Meadowbank station to Anderson Park — and we’ll see you at the next market, fighting for a park space like it’s an Olympic sport.
