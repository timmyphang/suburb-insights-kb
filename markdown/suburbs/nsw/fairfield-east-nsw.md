# Fairfield East (NSW)

Slug: `fairfield-east-nsw`

We start our Saturday the way locals do: shepherding small people to the Fairfield supported playgroups, coffee grudgingly in hand, and plotting which slide at Deerbush Park’s all‑abilities playground will be our kids’ next obsession. Back in the day the internet wrote most of our headlines — so yes, this is nostalgia with a pram.

We know where the community stuff actually happens. Western Sydney Mums reminds us about the Fairfield Easter Show and the bargain‑priced Fairfield Easter Fair (two dollars entry! — we still can’t get over that), and the inclusive features at Deerbush Park that mean everyone gets to join the carousel. MumsPages keeps the directory tidy: learning support, family support, even stylists and pest control services listed under Fairfield East (2165) — the local version of “we’ve got a specialist for that.”

We won’t pretend we’re headline material every week. The press did turn up for a few bigger moments: Pedestrian TV ran the pandemic dramas — mandatory masks in Fairfield hotspot LGAs and the six‑hour COVID‑test queues that made whole Saturdays evaporate. There was even a Pedestrian story about Paradiso Receptions getting fined after, allegedly, a 700‑person wedding — the sort of thing that makes our version of “small town scandal” look like a Netflix subplot. All old stories now, but they do remind us our neighbourhood has always had more life than it gets credit for.

Who wouldn’t love living here? Families who want real community — playgroups that connect people, parks built for every kid, cheap local fairs that let you eat and shove the kids on rides without bankrupting your weekend. Who would hate it? If your ideal Saturday is boutique cafe-hopping while scrolling five separate influencers, our humble directories and community events will feel like a personality clash.

We’re proud of the low‑key chaos: the parks, the playgroups, the big Easter show nights, and the fact that when something actually happens the internet notices. We don’t need a glossy feature to tell us this postcode is proper community work in action.
