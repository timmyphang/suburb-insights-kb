# Brunswick West (NSW)

Slug: `brunswick-west-nsw`

Back in the day we’d step out the door in Brunswick West and feel like we’d accidentally wandered onto someone’s lovingly curated front lawn — but in a comfortable way, not a catalogue way. We know where the tram hums, where Gilpin Park gives you a patch of green that’s not trying too hard, and which corner used to be a post office before it quietly became Postmistress — the post office-turned-bistro on 71 Melville Road that brought wood-fired pizza and beetroot gnocchi to our street.

Our morning routine used to be painfully wholesome: pick up a no-nonsense sanga from Heaps Good Deli (true story — the name came from a taste-test reaction), pop into John Gorilla for a bit of neighbourly art and a courtyard sniff from whatever dog was visiting that day, or queue for Mister Truong’s banh mi if we were feeling optimistic. For a quieter, old-school breakfast we’d brag about Lankan Tucker on 486 Albion Street — the egg hoppers that made winter mornings worth it.

We’ve had our small-town calamities too. Remember that blackout that Pedestrian dubbed #Hipstergeddon? Six hours of darkness that revealed we’ll fiercely mourn our artisanal toast as if it were a public holiday. And yes, there were stories of students scraping rent together — Junkee’s piece on the cost-of-living crisis even mentioned a shared Brunswick West room for $950 a month — so we’ve got that genuine inner-north hustle under the veneer.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t bear tidy tramlines, tree-lined avenues, or a bookstore-café like The Forbidden Chapter on Victoria Street that flirts with romance novels and fantasy, you’ll feel restless. If you need a permanent downtown roar, you’ll find our quieter pockets — and our family-friendly bits — almost infuriatingly restful.

Who would love it here? Families and people who like being near parks, schools like Brunswick North West Primary (and its Yakia Barring wetlands), neighbourhood cafes and that rare combo of decent banh mi, a bakery with gluten-free cred, and hair places like Saka Hair Studio that actually know how to care for curly hair. We’re Brunswick West: not trying to be everything, just being exactly what the inner north needed — a little rough round the edges, lots of heart, and, historically speaking, very good at sandwiches.
