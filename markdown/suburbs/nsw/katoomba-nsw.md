# Katoomba (NSW)

Slug: `katoomba-nsw`

We get up before the fog hangs low in the Jamison Valley and pretend we discovered the view at Echo Point ourselves. By dawn you’ll find us halfway down the Furber Steps or on the Federal Pass chasing that sunrise over the Three Sisters — Urban List even recommends the early-sunrise ticket for the full drama, and who are we to argue with a listicle?

Back in the day our weekends were a procession of clifftop walks and bakery crumbs. Concrete Playground wrote up the Grand Cliff Top Walk and the way it stitches together Katoomba Cascades, Wentworth Falls and lookouts that make you look humble. The Black Cockatoo Bakery (165 Katoomba Street) was on Urban List’s best-bakeries roster, and yes, we used to queue for it like it was a religious experience.

We’ve always had a flair for the theatrics. Scenic World has been our amusement park for grown-ups — Scenic Skyway, Scenic Railway, cable-car shows — Time Out and Concrete Playground have been reminding Sydneysiders that you can get a 360-degree ego-check from 270 metres up. Broadsheet once wrote about after-dark stunts like Nocturnal at Scenic World and even the cheeky Beyond Skyway rooftop idea; we wore the light shows like a badge.

Our local food scene has some delightful oddities. As Broadsheet noted, a Unique Patisserie in Katoomba paired pastries with rare Malaysian dishes — bak kut teh and assam laksa, no less — proving our tastebuds are more curious than people assume. Mountain Culture brewery used to run cult hazy releases and tasting flights that made weekend visitors suspiciously proud of their beer knowledge.

The petty local drama? We’ve got that too. Junkee loved the viral sulphur-crested cockatoo shredding anti-bird spikes — honestly, the wildlife has better opinions than a lot of our renovation committees. Pedestrian.TV catalogued the Blue Mountains’ weather mood swings — snow one week, bushfires the next — so if you like your scenery with a side of unpredictability, welcome home.

Who would hate it here? People who want absolute silence and zero weekend tourists on the Skyway. Who’d love it? Hikers, romantics, anyone who thinks a clifftop hotel and a bakery with character beats the city any morning of the week. We know every lookout; we’ll point out the best one and then pretend we weren’t trying to show off.
