# Petersham (NSW)

Slug: `petersham-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start Sunday with a pilgrimage that looked suspiciously like indecision: queue at Sweet Belem for pastéis de nata, detour past Fanny Durack Aquatic Centre where Splash kept the chip butty dream alive, then wander the strip arguing whether Lunas’ chouriço-laced eggs or Perama 2.0’s Mykonos vibes were more morally obliging on a hangover. Our local guide shelf is full of clippings — all pre-2024, so forgive us if we sound like vinyl hipsters reminiscing about a catalogue that hasn’t had a refresh.

We know the smells here like a postcode-appropriate cologne: charcoal chicken wafts from Frangos (and as Junkee noted, the Silvas-Frangos axis of chicken fame), sea-salty optimism from Fich’s “stingray” schnitzel and coconut ceviche, and the unmistakable perfume of a pub revamp — Public House Petersham used to boast a wood-fired oven, Czech pork platter and wine on tap. The West Village (once the White Cockatoo) had that vine-covered, sunny-courtyard energy Time Out loved. Meanwhile Petersham Bowling Club staged trivia, line-dancing and the occasional Medieval Day like it was hosting a very specific, very enthusiastic pantomime.

We won’t pretend there’s been no change; Inner West Mums has been waving the gentrification flag for years — families, professionals, long-term locals all squashed into the same weekend market selfie. The Bairro Português festival used to turn the streets into a whole-heritage singalong of sardines and licensed bars, and for good reason: our Portuguese backbone wasn’t just a nickname, it was a lifestyle.

Who would hate living here? If you require absolute silence, zero charcoal smoke, or can’t cope with spontaneous barefoot-bowls invitations at 77 Brighton Street, Petersham might file you under “misfit.” Also, if you think a beer garden is an endangered species, this place will confuse you.

Who’ll love it? If you’ve ever cheered a cheap custard tart, booked a sunny courtyard table, queued for canteen chips at Splash, or secretly wanted to live where the pubs once won Sitchu’s future-best lists — we were built for you. We’re a little noisy, a little crumbed, and entirely ours.
