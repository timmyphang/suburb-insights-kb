# Mount Kembla (NSW)

Slug: `mount-kembla-nsw`

We know the view before your GPS does — the escarpment looming like a smug relative who’s always been here. We walk out the front and there’s Mount Kembla, and beyond it Mount Keira, Brokers Nose and Mount Ousley keeping an eye on the coastline. As Broadsheet said, Wollongong’s small-city coastal thing? Yep, we’re the dramatic backdrop to that postcard ten-minute drive from the Wollongong CBD.

On weekends we lob a daypack in the car and head to the Mt Kembla Ring Track — Sitchu has it on their “best hikes” list for good reason: dry eucalypt forest, coastal views, and the kind of wildlife spotting that makes city folk gasp and us roll our eyes. Back in the day Sitchu also pointed out the Pepper Tree Passive House, sitting against the mountain and famously built around a 50-year-old pepper tree. It’s the sort of treehouse that makes design blogs swoon while we’re just trying not to trip on the roots.

Once upon a local guide, Sitchu called the Mt Kembla Pub a “best kept secret,” tucked up in the escarpment and known for its food offering, craft beers and live entertainment — that was the vibe when magazines noticed us. Time Out even reported the opening of a 20‑kilometre Kembla Mountain Bike Trails through the Illawarra Escarpment; sounds epic on paper and we’ve watched riders cartwheel down the stories since.

Who would hate living here? People who measure suburb quality by how many malls or buzzy inner-city bars they can walk to. If your idea of paradise is a strip mall with infinite espresso variations and neon lights, Mount Kembla will feel like punishment. Also, anyone allergic to a good view — there are at least three peaks giving you judgmental panoramas at all hours.

Who would love it? Hikers who like a proper ascent, design nerds who want a treehouse that’s actually earned its trophy, and anyone who likes their coastal life with a side of escarpment drama. We’ll keep the mountain, the pubs-with-history, and the trails — and let the travel sites keep writing love letters. We’re fine being the secret the maps don’t quite understand.
