# Shelly Beach (NSW)

Slug: `shelly-beach-nsw`

Back in the day — or at least according to every travel guide that gossiped about us — we were the sparkling little bay you can reach from Manly by walking south along Marine Parade. As Broadsheet noted, we’re the low‑key cousin to the big, sandy drama of Manly, which is probably why so much marine life prefers our shallows: hello, Cabbage Tree Bay Aquatic Reserve.

Our morning ritual used to look like this: the Manly “Bold and Beautiful” swim squad would meet at 7am and run a 1.5km course from the front of Manly Life Saving Club to our cove and back, while the rest of us pretended that the turquoise, protected water was our private plunge. The Boathouse Shelly Beach — the waterside spot on 1 Marina Pde — was the sit‑down prize for anyone who could be bothered drying off. Time Out called us the “chill little sister” of Manly and, frankly, we leaned into it.

We’re the place you start a snorkel and bump into Port Jacksons, wobbegongs and the odd young dusky whaler in season, which Concrete Playground kindly listed as one of the best snorkelling kick‑offs in Sydney. Queenscliff to Shelly Beach is the short walk that makes you feel outdoorsy without having to commit to a whole hike; the Eco‑Sculpture Walk and Fairy Bower Ocean Pool are the kinds of local flourishes that let us say we’re cultured and marine‑minded at the same time.

Who would hate living here? People who worship big, aggressive surf. We’re sheltered, calm and — as Time Out enthused — perfect for sunbakers who like their waves minimal and their mornings quiet. If you need to be seen on a twelve‑kilometre strip of sand selling CBD‑adjacent lifestyle, you’ll get bored. We’re not for showboats.

Who would love it here? Snorkelers, early‑morning swimmers, families who want a safe, chill swim and anyone who thinks a public BBQ and a gentle walk counts as a weekend well spent. For years the local guides recommended kicking back here; we’ll keep supplying the clarity, the reef and the smugness. Come for the calm, stay because you forgot your keys in the sand.
