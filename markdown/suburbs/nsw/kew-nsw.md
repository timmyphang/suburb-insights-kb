# Kew (NSW)

Slug: `kew-nsw`

We know Kew like the back of our hand — the quiet shuffle of prams past High Street, the polite nod when someone mentions the schools, and the tiny, deadly serious debate over which Cotham Road trattoria used to do the best Sunday sauce. Back in the day Broadsheet called us “leafy old‑school charm”; we keep the charm, you keep the ink.

Our Saturday ritual used to be embarrassingly predictable: a slow stroll along High Street and Cotham Road, stopping for a browse at Toscano’s and Leo’s Fine Food & Wine when we were pretending to shop for a fancy dinner, then pretending some more while queuing for Gilbert’s or Frank & Harri. The Studley Park Boathouse was the outdoor escape — kids playing, couples pretending they don’t notice the ducks — and Time Out’s guide, bless it, reminded the rest of Melbourne we sit prettily by the Yarra in the City of Boroondara.

We were never a rave‑town. Our evenings used to hum instead: intimate wine lists at Cru and Bar Alba, Mister Bianco’s Bianchetto with its Fellini energy, and the skinny, beloved Skinny Dog Hotel on High Street when we wanted rooftop views without shouting. Sushi On’s tiny omakase room felt like a secret handshake for seafood nerds, and Chef David’s art‑filled relaunch added a touch of gallery drama to our otherwise very sensible dining map.

Yes, headlines have come through — a tram crash near High Street & Childers Street and other alarming stories that remind us even the polite suburbs have their shocks. We don’t make fun of that; we just add an extra lock on the shed and keep walking.

Who would hate it here? If you despise breakfast queues, low‑key wine bars, stately homes and parents who speak in school‑admission acronyms, run. Who will love it? Food obsessives, families after good parks and good schools, and anyone who enjoys a neighbourhood that feels like it’s preserved its manners rather than its nightlife.

We’ll keep the jacarandas, the long lunches and the polite arguments about whether Centonove ever did better ravioli. You’re welcome to visit — but don’t tell everyone.
