# Fremantle (NSW)

Slug: `fremantle-nsw`

Back in the day — or at least in every guide we've lovingly dog-eared — Fremantle was the little port town that refused to grow up. We know this place: the Fremantle Markets on South Terrace that travel sites swear have been selling weird jewellery and hot chips for over a century, the WA Maritime Museum sprawled on the foreshore, and the Cappuccino Strip where the breeze the locals call the Fremantle Doctor shows up like clockwork.

We used to point tourists at the same hits Broadsheet and Time Out did: Bread in Common, La Lune and Manuka Woodfire Kitchen getting a name-check for dinner; Bib & Tucker, Little Lefroys and Wild Bakery for morning missions (note: those were guidebook mentions, not our current endorsements). Urban List once mapped our perfect day — Leighton Beach, Monument Hill sunsets, then a boat to Rottnest Island — and people ate it up. Even the festivals treated us well: the Fremantle International Street Arts Festival has been running its four-day circus since 1999, Falls Festival has had a Freo leg, and Concrete Playground catalogued a string of touring acts landing here from Sex Pistols at the prison to G Flip at the Arts Centre.

Petty local drama? We had plans. Urban List warned us the Fremantle Traffic Bridge was scheduled to close in February 2026 for up to 12 months — fun, considering roughly 24,000 vehicles use it a day. So if you liked predictable commutes, that was blown to smithereens. Otherwise, our biggest fights are over cake stalls and whether Little Creatures is still the height of sophistication.

Who would hate it here: anyone who needs a silent suburb, a speedy commute, or who recoils at buskers, markets and the occasional quokka headline from Rottnest. Also, if you panic at 24,000-car detours, plan your exit now.

Who would love it: food obsessives, festival junkies, museum nerds and anyone who secretly likes a town that’s equal parts convict history (Fremantle Prison), foreshore charm and stubborn local character. For years the guides have loved us — and frankly, so do we.
