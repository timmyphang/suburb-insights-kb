# Spotswood (NSW)

Slug: `spotswood-nsw`

We live in Spotswood — the westside one Broadsheet keeps gushing about (yes, the Melbourne Spotswood — we’ll take the compliments). Back in the day we’d roll out of bed and wander down Hudsons Road to pick our sticky consolation prize: Candied Bakery had already outgrown its first nook and grown into the kind of American-inspired bakehouse Broadsheet wrote up for being unapologetically sweet. Nearby there was talk of bakeries doing knafeh and red-velvet croissants at Crème de la Crème — pastry riffs that made us feel very cosmopolitan for a postcode that used to be all factories and RSLs.

Weekends used to mean Grazeland — the permanent food precinct by the Yarra that Time Out and Concrete Playground treated like a never-ending festival. It was shipping-container stalls, three bars, a deck with West Gate views, and enough gelato bubble waffles and lobster rolls to keep a city fed. We watched Grazeland become seasonal theatre — winter wonderlands with falling snow, firepits and mulled wine, then BBQ Beer Festivals that made the whole place smell like someone had lit a very enthusiastic campfire.

If you were an inquisitive parent, Scienceworks at 2 Booker Street was our escape hatch: planetarium nights, Pink Floyd on full dome and reclining seats so you could feel cultured while horizontal. For the people who wanted to leave town without actually leaving, Hudsons Road Wine & Beer used to promise a deli, cheese and an encyclopaedia’s worth of tipples.

Local plot twist: the El Taco crew graduated from Grazeland’s shipping containers to running kitchens at Hotel Mexico — Concrete Playground covered that little upward mobility arc and we clapped politely while still ordering more dumplings from the festival stalls.

Who’d hate it here? If you can’t cope with permanent food precincts, riverside decks, or the occasional pile-up of gourmet trucks, this isn’t your vibe. But who’d love it? Families dragging kids to Scienceworks, pastry obsessives chasing the croquembouche croissant, and anyone who thinks weekend plans should include a roof, a river view and a row of containers selling very serious fries.

We’re proud of our slightly messy, eternally hungry little patch. It’s not polished, but it knows how to feed you.
