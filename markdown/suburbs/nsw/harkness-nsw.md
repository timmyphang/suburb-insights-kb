# Harkness (NSW)

Slug: `harkness-nsw`

We live in Harkness, NSW, which is either destiny or the sort of bureaucratic joke you only notice on your driver’s licence. Back in the day the only time our name made headlines it wasn’t about our cul‑de‑sacs — it was about other Harknesses doing actual things: Broadsheet wrote about Alium Dining opening in 2020 in Bendigo’s former Royal Bank and noted owner‑chef Jay Harkness as a prodigy; fashion press remembered Deiji Studios being founded in Byron Bay by Juliette Harkness and Emma Nelson; and the internet went full spellbook over Agatha/Agnes Harkness in WandaVision (Pedestrian TV and Junkee ran with the wink, the memes, and Kathryn Hahn getting a spinoff).

So our morning routine here is small and proud: we brew what we have, we dodge the potholes, and we explain for the thousandth time that no, we’re not a pop‑cult hotspot — unless you count the family name cameo. We’re the kind of place where the most glamorous thing anyone can claim is a shared surname with a Marvel witch or a Bendigo chef, and we lean into it because what else are we going to do?

There’s no local council drama in the clippings you can quote, no late‑night op‑eds, no lifestyle writers in linen telling you to brunch here. If you wanted scandal, you’d have to travel to Bendigo for Jay Harkness’s restaurant piece or to Byron Bay for Deiji Studios’ founders. The press mentions we have are older snippets — quaint, celebrity‑related postcards rather than a living gossip column — so our petty local drama mainly involves who took the last park bench.

Who would hate living here? If you want a postcode with its own influencer narrative or a weekly feature in the city mags, you’ll be gutted. If your personality requires a constant stream of new openings, celebrity chefs and boutique scandals, sorry — we’re quiet and name‑based.

Who would love it here? People who like a low‑key postcode with a cheeky surname to drop at parties. If you’re into subtle pride — “Harkness” on a mailbox and a smirk about Jay or Agatha in the footnotes — you’ll fit right in.
