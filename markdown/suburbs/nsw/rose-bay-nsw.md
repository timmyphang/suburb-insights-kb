# Rose Bay (NSW)

Slug: `rose-bay-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed, walk the foreshore and pretend it was our private harbour. As Time Out once noted, Rose Bay Foreshore on Caledonian Rd used to be the place for a calm swim or dog run, and the ferry-wharf-to-Watsons-Bay amble — passing Strickland House, Nielsen Park and Hornby Lighthouse — gave us a smug sense of civic fitness. We miss that smugness.

Our weekend orbit used to look very specific: drop the kids at Holdsworth Rose Bay Cottage playgroup on O’Sullivan Rd, buy pastries from La Bretagne in Plumer Village, then linger awkwardly by the Rose Bay Marina while deciding whether to book a scenic flight with Sydney Seaplanes. Lunch plans were a toss-up between the Boathouse (the old Regatta site, as Broadsheet remembered), Rasā House on New South Head Rd (Time Out’s “borderless Asian” waterfront wine bar), or Aambra in the converted Rose Bay Uniting Church — yes, a 120-year-old church serving Moreton Bay bug borek, as Broadsheet reported. For old-world options we’d nod at Catalina in Lyne Park, which Urban List still called an icon.

There was local colour, too. Club Rose Bay got a Merivale-style revamp with an Airstream bar and a sprawling dog-friendly courtyard (Concrete Playground raved), and in the less glamorous headlines Sydney Swans’ Taylor Adams was allegedly punched outside that very pub (Pedestrian). And let’s not forget Danny Lim’s notorious protest sign fine — Junkee archived that kerfuffle and our collective rear-guard outrage.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe tasteful mid-century furniture, waterside dining and people who can schedule a scenic flight between Pilates and a Levantine long lunch, then move along. If you can’t stand laneway murals and council beautification — Collins Lane, Percival Lane and that Wilberforce Avenue carpark makeover — you’ll be miserable.

Who would love it here? We would. Families who want a proper cottage playgroup, food obsessives who remember Fei Jai, Martina and Rasā House, boat nerds from Woollahra Sailing Club or Cranbrook’s boathouse, and anyone who likes their harbour views with a side of culinary bravado. For years the local press kept telling the same story — and frankly, we’re not tired of being right about it.
