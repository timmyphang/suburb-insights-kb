# The Ponds (NSW)

Slug: `the-ponds-nsw`

We know the rhythm here: 9am, a battalion of prams lines Riverbank Drive like it’s the 1pm sales at a department store. Back in the day we’d wave at each other outside The Ponds Community Hub (45 Riverbank Drive) while trying to remember which playgroup met on Thursday and which one was the monthly Sunday thing at The Lake Neighbourhood Centre on the corner of Spearmint & Camomile Streets. Those were our social calendars — playgroups like The Ponds Little Ducklings and The Ponds Little Joeys kept us functional and mildly smug.

Weekends meant a slow march to The Ponds Shopping Centre for whatever workshop was on — we once did a Parent & Kid Journaling Workshop there — then an obliging stroll along the Second Ponds Creek track where the kids could run free and the pond looked picturesque enough to make our social feeds jealous. For a place that’s only about 40km from the CBD, we cultivated a small-town hustle: community hub events, baby-feeding talks (yes, “Baby Talk at the Ponds — Feeding” was a thing), and the sort of local noticeboard chatter that doubled as matchmaking for new mums.

Petty local drama? It’s mostly logistical. We’ve had the “should we commute to the city?” thread — remember the Stanhope Gardens voice warning it’s better to find work in Parramatta or Penrith if you don’t love bumper-to-bumper romance? Classic. Otherwise our quarrels are over parking at the shopping centre and whose kid stole whose sandwich at playgroup. Deadly stuff.

Who would hate it here: anyone chasing late-night cocktails, unscheduled public transport, or a buzzing nightlife. If you need city convenience more than a community bulletin, you’ll grumble about the 40km and the family-first vibe.

Who’d love it: young families, organisers of sticker-swapping mornings, and anyone who measures a good weekend by how many kids you can tire out at Second Ponds Creek. Back in the day we chose The Ponds for the playgrounds, the shopping centre meet-ups, and that comforting scorecard of local groups that made moving here feel like an upgrade from “new town” to “our town.” We own that.
