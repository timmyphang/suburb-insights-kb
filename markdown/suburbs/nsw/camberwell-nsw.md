# Camberwell (NSW)

Slug: `camberwell-nsw`

Camberwell, NSW — we know the ritual. For years we’d shuffle out at 7am, half-awake, and be swallowed by the Camberwell Sunday Market’s nearly 400 stalls while the Rivoli Cinema’s Art Deco face looked on like a slightly smug aunt. Back in the day the market and the Camberwell Fresh Food Market were how you proved you were a person who *cared* about pre-loved wares and artisanal chutneys.

Our weekend runs used to involve queuing at bakeries that treated pastry like performance art. Toast House was the place Irene Lin and William Chen made pillowy house-shaped loaves and cream-filled pretzels feel like a civic duty. Penny for Pound (yes, the outpost on 387 Camberwell Road) turned croissants into headline news — Golden Gaytime specials and all — often in a standing-room-only kind of way. We’d nurse coffee sourced from Axil Coffee Roasters and pretend we’d discovered the suburb before everyone else wrote about it.

We’re not all dainty bakeries and boutique bottles, though — we had our own local saga. The Grill’d Camberwell staff petition and the fallout that followed reminded us that even here the glossy strip has real people doing the heavy lifting. It was a blunt note under the pretty awnings: grass roots don’t always make nice Instagram content.

Who would hate living here? If you despise small talk on Burke Road, can’t handle a 70-seater wine bar flexing a 180-strong wine wall (Gloria’s Wine, we see you), or recoil at pan‑Asian small plates paired with sleek Australian drops (Greenhart), then this isn’t your suburb. If you’re allergic to vintage hunts, don’t bother with Market 755 — or the boutique edit at Oscar & Wild.

Who’d love it? Families who remember the Camberwell Structured Playgroup’s early-1980s beginnings and its 40-year Fun Day run; people who like a Rivoli double-bill followed by a Franco-Belge butter wash; those who see charm in old garages turned cafes, like the Old Garage Cafe, or get a thrill finding a rare bottle at Camberwell South Cellars.

Camberwell is quietly proud, slightly theatrical and up for a market bargain. We’ll keep our pastries and our opinions — you bring the curiosity.
