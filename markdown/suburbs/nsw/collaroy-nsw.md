# Collaroy (NSW)

Slug: `collaroy-nsw`

We know Collaroy like the back of our sand-encrusted hand. Some mornings we’re the ones trudging down to the Collaroy Rock Pool before the sun melts into the Pacific — two odd-shaped ocean baths, chains and all — then wobbling over to the stretch of Collaroy Beach that’s somehow still the go-to for beginner surfers. Other days we’re pretending not to hear the cocktail orders drifting up from The Surf Deck while ogling the ocean.

We’ve watched The Collaroy change outfits — Merivale moved in back in May 2018 and turned the old Surf Rock into a multi-storey seaside venue with oysters, woodfired pizza and an upstairs Harriet’s-for-adults vibe. It’s the sort of place you’ll recognise by the uninterrupted beach views and the people who think every meal requires a view and a shot of lime. Across the road there’s Upper Crust — been serving pies since 1948 — and yes, we’ve definitely queued for one after a cold swim.

There’s been drama we can’t just laugh off. The #SydneyStorm did a number on our foreshore and, as Pedestrian reported, some waterfront owners even eyed up legal action against council and real estate over the damage. We’re not into lawsuits as entertainment, but we will admit the town hummed with serious conversations about sand, sea and insurance for a minute there.

Who would hate it here? People who need their suburb to be a perfectly curated, traffic-free fantasy. If you loathe surf chatter, pub patios, and occasional sand in places sand should never be — you’ll file Collaroy under “too much coastline.”

Who’d love it? Families who want playgrounds, camp groups who’ve used the Collaroy Centre on Homestead Avenue, walkers doing the Bicentennial Coastal Walk past the Collaroy War Memorial and Long Reef, or anyone who believes a messy, breezy beach town beats a glossy suburb any day. We’re proud of the beach, the pie shop that's been feeding us for generations, and the quirks that make Collaroy unmistakably ours — sand, surf, and all.
