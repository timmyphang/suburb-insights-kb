# Charlestown (NSW)

Slug: `charlestown-nsw`

Back in the day we thought suburb living meant one of two things: a backyard with a barbie or a shopping centre with a cinema. Charlestown cheerfully gave us both — and a bao truck to soothe the existential dread.

We remember Saturdays like a ritual: we’d wander through Charlestown Square (yes, the multi‑storey one the guides kept name‑checking), try to find the free spots that Hoyts Charlestown famously made a selling point of, and promise ourselves "just one thing" from the food court. In the glory days Urban List called Bao Brothers Charlestown "an absolute institution" for churning out what they dubbed "kickass buns of joy" — and New Shanghai, tucked in Shop L01, 1035 Charlestown Square on Pearson Street, got the dumpling nerds suitably excited.

For the family folk, life looked very practical. Charlestown Public School and Charlestown Pool (you know, the ones near Frederick Street) fed the weekly routine, and Charlies Angels Playgroup ran Wednesday mornings in the school hall so someone could actually finish a coffee — or at least pretend to. Concrete Playground gave props to Mama P, the family-run cafe started by James and Kylie Pheils, as the kind of place that made healthy food feel less like a punishment.

We weren’t exactly a hipster backwater — Sitchu kept reminding buyers that Charlestown Square was a proper amenity, even dangling Charlestown Square gift cards in Newcastle Food Month promos. Revo Fitness even used to list Charlestown among its clubs, which meant the “show up and win cash” promotions briefly made the treadmill feel like a lottery ticket. And for a spell 4 Pines in Charlestown ran a nurses’ discount week in May 2025, because medicine deserves beer.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe malls, free cinema parking, or the idea of dumplings in a shopping centre, you’ll likely bristle. If you think every suburb must have an artisanal lane weep for you.

Who would love it? Families, shoppers, dumpling obsessives, bargain‑chasing cinephiles and anyone who appreciates a suburb that happily wears its amenities on its sleeve. We’re a place of practical pleasures — and, yes, excellent baos — even if most of this was written about “back in the day.”
