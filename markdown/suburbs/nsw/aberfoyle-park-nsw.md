# Aberfoyle Park (NSW)

Slug: `aberfoyle-park-nsw`

We wake up knowing exactly where the kettle will be arguing with the toaster: in a suburb whose civic resume reads like a very earnest community noticeboard. Back in the day our mornings started with a quick detour past the Aberfoyle Community Centre at 1 Jessica St — you could smell the craft glue from the arts & crafts room and hear the echo from the hall hire queue. That place used to be the unofficial town square for stitches, scones and very polite fundraising.

On weekends we used to saunter by Resthaven Aberfoyle Park — the profile lists it as a state-of-the-art residential and respite place with its own café, salon and communal rooms, cheekily positioned near Aberfoyle Village and the Hub shopping centres. It’s the kind of neighbour who brings fruitcake to street parties and then organises the bingo night.

Our kids were ferried to the cluster at Aberfoyle Park Primary School Campus on 2 Campus Dr — remember when three schools cohabited like slightly bemused flatmates: Pilgrim School, School of the Nativity and Thiele Primary School. There was a preschool, a community garden and the Out of School Hours Care that operated from Geoff Simpson Hall, which meant our timetables were dictated more by school concerts than by Netflix.

The high-school kids had scope for actual schemes: Aberfoyle Park High School’s listings spoke of music ensembles, VET/SAFTS courses, the Ignite program for gifted students, STEM offerings and a healthy smattering of sports. It was the place parents quietly hoped would save their teenagers from doing very little.

The petty local drama? Not much tabloid material — more a bureaucratic sigh: Playgroup SA’s notice from 19 March 2020 that InterGen Playgroups were no longer running sticks in the memory. We still grumble about missed intergenerational chaos on wet Tuesdays.

Who would hate it here: people who crave late-night bars, buzzy inner-city noveltyism or zero-responsibility living; we’re not the suburb for anyone allergic to school fetes, community meetings or gardening bees. Who would love it: families, volunteers, retirees who appreciate a good communal lunch, and anyone who secretly prefers a suburb where the loudest thing is a school ensemble practising Vivaldi. We wear that small-town badge with pride — it’s unapologetically community-run, and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
