# St Kilda (NSW)

Slug: `st-kilda-nsw`

Back in the day we’d drag ourselves down to the foreshore with sand in our shoes and an opinion about the pier. We used to watch the sun hit the St Kilda Pier and pretend we weren’t eyeing everyone else pretending their life was a postcard. The Esplanade Market used to be our Saturday excuse to buy handmade nonsense, then wander past the Palais Theatre and the Espy like we hadn’t been there every weekend since ’98.

Mornings involved a half-serious stroll to the St Kilda Pier Kiosk or a cheeky pastry from Sebby’s Scrolls or Ms Felix — culinary pilgrimage routes more ritual than transport. Lunchtimes were reserved for whatever the old beachfront guides raved about: Stokehouse and Café Di Stasio used to be the names you dropped to sound like you’d read a Broadsheet feature. The Walrus on Inkerman Street, Saint George’s Italo relaunch and LOTI were the sort of places reviewers pretended they discovered by accident. The Prince rooftop once turned a former carpark into a beer garden and we all applauded, because who doesn’t want panoramic judgement with their lager?

Petty local drama? Our biggest recurring scandal was the St Kilda Festival lineup — whether it was Mallrat, Mo’Ju, Bag Raiders or Budjerah headlining, we argued on the foreshore about which act “got too big” while queueing for free music. The pier even went on to win an Australian Urban Design Award in 2026; we clapped and then immediately argued if it meant more tourists.

Who would HATE living here: people who crave predictable quiet, eight-hour sleeps, or a beach that doesn’t come with a soundtrack of street performers and rollerbladers. If you loathe late‑night revelry, Luna Park’s glow, and a boardwalk that’s permanently busy, move somewhere with fewer palm trees and more silence.

Who would LOVE it: food obsessives, festival folk, sunset chasers, and parents who know the St Kilda Playgroup at 67 Argyle Street is the kind of grassroots thing that makes a postcode feel like a community. We may be a little loud, a bit sandy and forever arguing about the best seaside bite — and frankly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
