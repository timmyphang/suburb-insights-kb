# Bacchus Marsh (NSW)

Slug: `bacchus-marsh-nsw`

We wake up, rub the sleep from our eyes and drive past the Avenue of Honour like we’ve done a thousand times — not because we’re nostalgic, but because it’s the scenic route to the fruit stand. Back in the day we’d load the car at Naturipe Fruits and argue about who picked the best peaches, then squeeze in a quick scramble up the 1000+ Steps at Telford Park Reserve before the kids demanded snacks. As Broadsheet once pointed out, people pass through us on their way to Lerderderg State Park; we’re the polite detour that somehow became a destination.

For years the town has been quietly doing culture and outdoors in parallel: Boyd Baker House hosted Concrete Playground’s A New Normal during Melbourne Design Week 2025, while Time Out earmarked the Lerderderg Gorge Circuit as a 13‑kilometre rite of passage for anyone who likes cliffs, river crossings and the smug satisfaction of spring wildflowers. There were plans for an all‑abilities sculpture trail at the 1000+ Steps lookout, upgrades to toilets and seating — proof we can be practical and arty at the same time.

No local soap‑opera to report — we don’t need one. The data is mostly brochures and event listings: a tidy catalogue of what we’ve got. That suits us. We’re small enough that the Bacchus Marsh Theatre Company on Grant Street and the Bacchus Marsh Golf Clubhouse on Links Road feel like community treasures, and there’s even been a Local Taproom on Grant Street and a FoodWorks on Bennett Street for when the fruit picking wears you out.

Who would hate living here? If you need a tram every five minutes or think “limited public transport into the gorge” (Time Out’s exact words) is a dealbreaker, this isn’t your postcard. If your hobby is rooftop bars and permanent city noise, you’ll file us under “quaint” and move on.

Who would love it? Hikers who want Lerderderg’s dramatic gorge, families who have been part of St Andrews Playgroup, weekend fruit pickers, golfers who appreciate a valley view, and anyone happy to trade a little quiet for big sky and actual space to breathe. We’re Bacchus Marsh — polite, practical, and quietly useful.
