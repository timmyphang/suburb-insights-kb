# Kariong (NSW)

Slug: `kariong-nsw`

Back in the day we’d tell the Sydney crowd Kariong was our secret — not because we’re snobby, but because the best weekend flex here was a bushwalk that genuinely makes you feel smug. Sitchu once described the trail to Kariong Brook Falls as “tucked away in Brisbane Water National Park,” and they weren’t lying: the 4km return track drops you into a sandstone amphitheatre with an emerald‑green pool that looks like someone Photoshopped the countryside. It’s an easy 1.5–2 hours if you move like a human, longer if you’re stopping to take dramatically wistful photos for the 'gram.

We still joke that our cultural exports include the falls and a name coincidence: Broadsheet once profiled chef John Rivera and mentioned his gelato brand Kariton Sorbetes — yes, the name made us blink and feel oddly represented. That article was about Melbourne food scenes, sure, but we’ll claim the name as long as it sounds exotic.

For parents, the internet once led them here too: keeping‑mum.com.au listed local mothers groups and even a “Group 208” and waitlist options. So if you’ve ever needed to be part of a scheduled catch‑up with prams and polite complaints about sleep, the paperwork was ready to welcome you. Those listings read like evidence we are quietly practical — not a place of flash, but of organised routines and sensible shoe choices.

Petty local drama? There’s none in our clippings to weaponise, so we’ll admit it: we’re more about quiet endurance than scandal. That suits us. People who would hate living here are those who need eight cocktail bars within five blocks, constant retail theatre, or someone else to mediate between them and the wild. If you require choice like that, you’ll be perpetually frustrated.

Who would love it here? Hikers, families who appreciate a reliable mothers group sign‑up, and anyone who likes that “tucked away” feeling without having to fly interstate. We don’t pretend to be flashy. We’ve got a proper falls, a suspiciously on‑brand sorbet name in longform journalism, and a community that shows up. We’ll keep it that way — you can keep the crowded laneways.
