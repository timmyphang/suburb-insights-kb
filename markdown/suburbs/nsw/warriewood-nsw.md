# Warriewood (NSW)

Slug: `warriewood-nsw`

You know the one-way squeeze that feels less like a road and more like a dare? That’s us. As Time Out noted, the descent down to Warriewood Beach is steep, the lane at times one car wide and “snarly” on weekends — and yes, you can practically park on the sand if you time it right. We love that chaotic, cliff-top-to-surf entrance; it weeds out indecision and people who don’t like stairs.

Weekend routine, for the record: back in the day we’d haul the esky down the steps, wander the grassy Turimetta Headland for the views (Concrete Playground reminded everyone Turimetta’s the quieter side between Narrabeen and Warriewood), then mosey to The Beaches Market — which used to be a Friday fixture in Warriewood with over 100 stalls and a heavy lean toward organic, home-grown produce. If you liked kids’ chaos, Urban List even listed Vitalands Kids Cafe as having a Warriewood location in its play-centre roundup, so parents could still pretend they were on holiday.

We’re a weird little mash-up: hang-gliders of nature (blow holes and headland walks, as Sitchu pointed out), small-scale industry (Hang 10 Distillery — Concrete Playground wrote about their bread-to-spirits crusade), and civic pride. Pittwater Park (1472 Pittwater Road) sits in our midst as the sporting hub — locals still call it ‘Rat Park’ if you want to sound like you’ve lived here a minute. For a subtle flex, Time Out once reported Warriewood as topping a list for most expensive rooms to rent — $600 median weekly — which makes for excellent dinner-party smugness.

If you hate stairs, narrow lanes, premium rent or markets that start before your hangover ends, you’ll loathe us. If you live for secluded beach walks, cliff-top calm, weekly stalls, craft spirits with an eco-twist or an occasional film festival satellite (Alliance Française added Warriewood to its 2025 lineup, as Concrete Playground noted), you’ll adore us.

We’re not trying to be Bondi; we’re the expensive, slightly awkward cousin who actually knows how to surf, can make a mean picnic from a market stall and will happily tell you where the blow hole is. Come for the views, stay because your car won’t fit through the town.
