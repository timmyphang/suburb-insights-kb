# Beaconsfield Upper (NSW)

Slug: `beaconsfield-upper-nsw`

We live in Beaconsfield Upper, NSW — or at least our post boxes say we do. Online, though, everything about “Beaconsfield” is either in Perth, Sydney or over the border in Victoria, so half the time we’re left wondering which Beaconsfield will RSVP to our barbecue.

Mornings here, for the record, have historically been playgroup mornings: Playgroup Victoria used to list sessions at the Upper Beaconsfield Community Centre, with access to the Upper Beaconsfield Toy Library and the local playground. The Beaconsfield Neighbourhood Centre also pops up in the archival playgroup listings, which is the kind of thrilling local press we treasure. For years the local “news” has been quietly proud of being useful for toddlers, not influencers — and we’re fine with that.

If you’re looking for local gossip, the internet keeps trying to distract us. Urban List once pointed us to Nu 56 at 56 Victoria Street in Beaconsfield NSW 2015 (fancy chefs, ethically sourced coffee) and even to Hunter & Fox Tattoo’s Beaconsfield studio in a Sydney roundup. Sitchu, meanwhile, remembered that Beaconsfield Upper, Victoria is a darling for nature-led Airbnbs and dog-friendly stays — “Serenity Abode Tiny House,” for example, got a name-check. The upshot: our suburb is either a playground hub, an Airbnb muse over the border, or the tattoo studio around the corner in someone else’s story.

Who would hate living here? People who need a weekly ‘hot new precinct’ listicle or who measure suburb quality by how many Michelin-trained chefs can be summoned within walking distance. Who would love it? Families who value volunteer-run playgroups, dogs that enjoy a tiny-house welcome, and anyone who prefers local community centres and toy libraries over trend-chasing.

We’ll own the reputation: we’re quietly practical, slightly dispersed across other people’s headlines, and perfectly content that our best publicity has been for playgroups and peaceful stays. Bring a thermos, a kid’s hat and a nice leash — and if Urban List calls you by the wrong state, just smile. We do.
