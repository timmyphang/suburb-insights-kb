# Norton Summit (NSW)

Slug: `norton-summit-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start the weekend by crawling up Old Norton Summit Road and pretending we weren’t just going for the view. From the hilltop beer garden at The Scenic Hotel you could watch the city get smaller while the festoon lights came on — we used to say the skyline looked better with a string of bulbs and a pot roast lamb on the table.

When Jordan Latour took over as head chef after the Scenic’s post-2020 revamp, we reassured ourselves that kangaroo schnitzels and flank steak tartare were just our countryside versions of haute cuisine. The Scenic also used to run that earnest Topsoil Garden Project — a therapy garden and greenhouse across the road — which, true to form, made half of us glow and the other half feel vaguely guilty about not volunteering.

For years the local calendar read like a very polite festival: Passata Party afternoons (we used to chop, bottle and pretend we’d made sauce for winter), Harvest Parties with wine tastings and DJs, and Sinclair’s Gully turning into a spot for summer picnics and Fringe fringe-ery at 288 Colonial Drive. Time Out even pointed walkers at the loop linking Norton Summit to Glenthorne National Park via the River Torrens. We liked that we could be outdoors without actually having to choose a hike with too many Lycra-clad strangers.

The village had its anchors — Norton Summit Primary School on 15 Crescent Dr (Bill Lewis was listed as principal), the Summit Community Centre at 4 Crescent Dr, the little Baptist church on Church Rd, and the medical centre on Crescent Dve for when the hangovers lasted longer than the passata jars. They were the sort of things that meant you’d borrow a ladder from a neighbour and leave it on their lawn two months later.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t stand small-town festivals, wineries rolling in on Harvest Day, or being roped into community crafts at the Centre, you’ll find it suffocatingly wholesome. Who would love it? People who want a place that still stages communal sauce-making, organises summer picnics at Sinclair’s Gully and treats the pub as a civic square — for years at least, that was us.
