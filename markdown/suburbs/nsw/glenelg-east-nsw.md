# Glenelg East (NSW)

Slug: `glenelg-east-nsw`

Back in the day we judged a good weekend by two things: how long the line was on Colley Terrace for Shadow Baking’s pandan brioche, and whether the Skyline Ferris Wheel was doing that slow, smug spin over the foreshore. We still remember the smell of fresh focaccia drifting down from Colley Terrace and the distant squeal of kids at The Beachouse — this place wears its seaside clichés proudly.

Our morning ritual used to be embarrassingly specific: a cautious stroll along the Glenelg Foreshore to check the tide, a detour past Moseley Square to see if Dante’s Deli had a spare bench in their dog-friendly courtyard, and a strategic glance at Jetty Road to pretend we weren’t tempted by Oliveti’s duck ragu rigatoni. As Broadsheet wrote, Threefold Distilling set up a tasting bar just five minutes from the Esplanade, so gin flights were always an acceptable reward for surviving the queues. Core Plus announced its first Adelaide outpost at 4–6 Brighton Road, which made our Pilates-envy very public.

There isn’t much local political theatre in the files we dug up — no ongoing soap operas, no council kerfuffles saving the headlines — just a reliably busy beach town that Time Out crowned one of Adelaide’s most famous beaches. Sitchu’s profile calls us a coastal pocket for families, professionals and retirees, and their property listings back that up: entry-level two-bed units on Augusta Street and ground-floor opportunities on Keen Avenue pitched to first-home buyers, downsizers and investors. Even the Christmas tree guides remember us — Adelaide Blue Lake listed Glenelg East among holiday pick-up spots. quaint, yes. Predictable, absolutely.

Who would hate it here? If you loathe family-friendly attractions like The Beachouse, can’t stand ferris wheels, or have an allergy to queues for picture-perfect pastries, this isn’t your slice of sand.

Who would love it? Families, first-home buyers, weekend food pilgrims, gin tasters and anyone happy to swap inner-city drama for seaside charm and the comforting certainty that somewhere on Colley Terrace, a pastry will be worth the wait. We’ll bring the beach towel; you bring the patience.
