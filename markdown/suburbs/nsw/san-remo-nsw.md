# San Remo (NSW)

Slug: `san-remo-nsw`

We stand on the bridge connecting Newhaven and San Remo like it's our own tiny harbour-view runway show: windblown, a touch dramatic, and always smelling faintly of fryer oil. Back in the day we’d stroll the George Bass Coastal Walk out past Kilcunda and through to the Bass Highway, pretend we were explorers and then reward ourselves with whatever the Fisherman’s Co‑Op was selling that week at 170 Marine Parade.

We used to tell visitors that San Remo was the gateway to Phillip Island — Time Out said it, so it must be true — and you could watch boats leave the jetty with Captain Brett hustling guests out for whales, dolphins and seal cruises. Kelp cropped up near the bridge as that charming beach-shack everyone mentioned, and Silverwater Resort sat up in the hills above us like the posh cousin who visits for Christmas.

Breakfast used to be a modest ritual: a detour to Kilcunda General Store where dishes were even served on “San Remo Bakehouse sourdough,” or admiring the gleam of a “handsome San Remo coffee machine finished in bronze with leather sides” (Salt Coffee Roasters, bless its aesthetic). Road-trippers would hit the fishing co‑op — Sitchu called it a compulsory pause — and pack the car with whatever the sea had been generous enough to cough up.

We won’t pretend we’re all sunsets and serenity. For those who can’t cope with coastal walks, boat cruises or a seven-kilometre wander that traces George Bass’s route, we’re probably not your vibe. And after the Easter long weekend in 2025 when emergency services were called to a beach near San Remo, we became painfully, usefully more aware of the water — respect the swell, respect the flags.

Who’d love it here? Road-trippers, coastal walkers, seafood lovers and anyone who thinks bridges are for lingering rather than just crossing. Who’d hate it? People allergic to wind, fryer oil or a town that’s content to be a proper little gateway rather than pretending to be a metropolis.

We’re modest, salty and stubbornly scenic. Pull up, take the walk, buy the chips from 170 Marine Parade, and remember: we like to pretend we’re a destination, but really we’re just the polite usher to Phillip Island.
