# Wantirna South (NSW)

Slug: `wantirna-south-nsw`

You know you’re in Wantirna South when your weekend itinerary reads like a shopping-centre loyalty card. We roll out of bed, drive down Burwood Hwy and end up at Westfield Knox — yes, that vast beast at 425 Burwood Hwy that Time Out once helpfully mapped for us — because where else do you go when your idea of culture is split between a cinema screen and a sushi train?

Morning routine: a quick game of pickleball at the Westfield Knox courts (Urban List slapped us on the 2025 map for that), then a lap of Knox O-Zone to see who’s queueing for the caravan that used to be La Petite Crêperie’s Juliette — nostalgic crepes were once part of our suburban cardio, according to Time Out. If we’re feeling very Melbourne, we’ll reminisce about the arcade chaos Broadsheet once cheekily recommended: Mario Kart at Timezone and nostalgic games that make adults slightly weep with both joy and shame.

We’re not short on spectacle. Concrete Playground ran DinoFest at Nortons Park — real-life, walking dinosaurs taking over Nortons Lane for sessions between June 28 and July 20, 2025. It’s the sort of family-friendly, slightly surreal energy you can only get here: big, loud, and perfectly suited to the wide open parks that suburbs like ours do well.

And yes, we watched Urban Alley roll into the Knox O-Zone scene — Concrete Playground covered the 2022 $5 million brewpub-and-distillery launch. Good on them for bringing fermentation theatre; we’ll applaud a fermentation tank like it’s a public sculpture.

Who would hate it here? If you crave hidden laneway boutiques, two-block commutes to a gallery opening, or a postcode defined by artisan minimalism, you’ll grumble. Wantirna South is unabashedly big-venue, family-centre, one-stop convenience.

Who would love it? Families, weekend warriors, anyone who rates a mega-shopping centre as a full-day entertainment precinct, and people who’ll happily swap a tiny espresso bar for a cinema, a brewery, pickleball and a dinosaur festival in one afternoon. We call it convenient, unapologetic, and earnestly suburban — and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
