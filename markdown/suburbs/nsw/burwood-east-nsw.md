# Burwood East (NSW)

Slug: `burwood-east-nsw`

We should say up front: these clippings are older — think nostalgia tour, not a live guide. Back in the day we used to climb up to the Acre rooftop at Burwood Brickworks and feel very smug about being both eco-conscious and mildly peckish. Acre crowned the centre with a 2,500‑square‑metre rooftop farm, Glasshouse cafe and a Farmhouse restaurant — proper working‑bee hives, worm farms and a quail coop, which made us feel healthier than we probably were.

A typical weekend started with brekky at one of those rooftop spots (Rombe used to be the sun‑drenched option at 70 Middleborough Road), then a wander through the Reading Cinemas if someone in our crew insisted on a matinee. By lunchtime the choices read like an ambitious homework list: Mansae Korean BBQ had expanded out here with its flame‑centric tables, Gold Leaf was the go‑to for yum cha, and China Bar Signature flexed as one of those legendary buffet beasts on Burwood Hwy. There was even talk of a Burwood East Eat Street promotion running through March 28 — local government energy, if not local drama.

Speaking of drama: you won’t find much scandal in these snippets. The headlines are mostly rooftop farms, Korean BBQ expansions and lists of where to eat. That’s fine. We’ll take a rooftop quail coop over a council snipe any Tuesday.

Who wouldn’t love it here? If you’re allergic to rooftop gardens, communal workshops and Wagyu sizzling on a built‑in grill, then this is not your scene. If you despise dumplings, buffets or a choice pharmacy opening at Burwood Brickworks (we remember the Choice Pharmacy grand opening), you’ll chafe.

Who would love it here? Families who appreciate a cinema and a farm‑to‑table vibe, mums who need meetup spots around the eastern suburbs, foodies chasing everything from Korean BBQ to Lanzhou beef noodles and the occasional rooftop cocktail (or whatever passed for one up there). For years the local story was less about controversy and more about trying to fit all the restaurants, rooftop farms and cinemas into one very ambitious postcode — and honestly, we quite liked it that way.
