# Harris Park (NSW)

Slug: `harris-park-nsw`

We know the moment: you step off the Harris Park train, the air smells like something delicious and very committed, and we all instinctively start walking towards the same three streets. Back in the day the City of Parramatta even gave Marion Street, Wigram Street and Station Street East a six‑month sticker as “Little India” — which, honestly, just confirmed what our stomachs already knew.

Weekends used to look the same: we’d queue for the chaos that was Chatkazz — Broadsheet called it “one of the most unique eating experiences in Sydney” — always full of families, all somehow affordable, and everyone gone within 40 minutes as if a dinner bell rang. Billu’s used to be the place for tandoori chicken that’s been marinating for 24 hours; Time Out and Concrete Playground treated the Wigram Street strip like a pilgrimage. Dosa Hut even got its turn in the spotlight when Time Out noted its absurdly high Google Maps review count — the kind of place where you learn to accept elbowing for a table as a form of cardio.

Our kids had their own local rituals too. Rosella Park, hidden among the apartments, used to be where the Playgroup NSW play van rolled up on Wednesdays during school term — sweaty kids, coffee from nowhere and someone saying “there’s that one swing again.” And if you ever wanted to know what apartment living felt like, Sitchu’s 6–7 Alice Street listing reminded everyone it’s a short walk to the station and the smell of curry at all hours.

The petty council headline about “Little India” reads like a love letter and a press release rolled into one — we mouth “officially branded” and then keep eating. Who would hate living here? Anyone who wants their suburb to smell like nothing, or who needs their dinner to involve linen napkins and long conversation. Who would love it? People who treat spice as a hobby, parents who value a rope-climbing tower and a BBQ in Rosella Park, and anyone who measures time in pani puri rounds.

We’re proud of our strip‑map chaos, our eateries-that-were-famous-in-every-list, and our tiny park that knows all our children’s names. Harris Park: loud, delicious and unapologetically on the menu.
