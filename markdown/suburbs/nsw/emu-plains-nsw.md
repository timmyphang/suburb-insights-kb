# Emu Plains (NSW)

Slug: `emu-plains-nsw`

Full disclosure: we’re riffing off older write-ups — Broadsheet, Time Out, Concrete Playground and the like — so think of this as a fondly annotated tourist brochure from the past. Emu Plains, as they used to tell it, is the place where the river slows down and we pretend that’s culture.

Morning in our part of town meant a slow walk along the Nepean at Regatta Park — remember when the Old Police Cottage got a second life and the riverside picnic seating made the whole precinct feel like someone finally read the brief? Bluemountainsmums wrote up the Regatta Park upgrade with its pavilion and foreshore paths; we’ll take those promenades, thanks very much. If it’s weekend vibes you crave, Concrete Playground used to sing the praises of Laughin’ Boy Cafe — the restored police cottage serving brekkie and live tunes while the river judges you quietly.

By arvo we’d mosey over to Mountain Culture’s Emu Plains spot. Broadsheet and Concrete Playground noted the brewery trade: a western‑Sydney production site that doubles as a brewpub (the Factory at 35 David Road gets a specific shout). They’d pour hazy things and spin a pizza out of a woodfire oven — and yes, Broadsheet followed Mountain Culture as it grew (they even bought Fox Friday, if you like corporate subplot in your hops).

Chocolate nerds: Time Out flagged Zokoko on Old Bathurst Road — bean‑to‑bar, medals and an actual café. If you can’t be persuaded to move here by riverside playgrounds and brewery beer gardens, possibly bribery via a decent slab of chocolate will do the trick.

Our market memory is a bit showy: Sitchu mentioned an Emu Plains Market that supposedly balloons into a 240‑stall summer extravaganza. Whether it’s that big every year or whether we’re remembering it through festival goggles — who knows — but the idea of artisan chaos by the stringybarks is part of the charm.

Who’d hate it here? People who need neon nightlife, uninterrupted CBD convenience, or a suburb that only serves one kind of Sunday routine. Who’d love it? Families (hello playgroups at Emu Plains Cottage, 15 Troy St), beer and chocolate appreciators, river walkers and anyone who likes a town that still remembers the Nepean is the point of view.

Back in the day, that’s Emu Plains. We still come for the river; we stay for the pizza, the beer and the chocolate.
