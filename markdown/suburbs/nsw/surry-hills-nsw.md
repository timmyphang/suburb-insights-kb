# Surry Hills (NSW)

Slug: `surry-hills-nsw`

We remember Surry Hills like a well-worn apron: stained with espresso, singed by woodfired pizza, and smelling faintly of someone’s excellent dinner plans. Back in the day the suburb read like a foodie’s Tinder profile — Broadsheet used to catalogue us as a “square-kilometre crawl” of restaurants and cocktail dens, and yes, there were profiles for Firedoor, Poly, Kiln, Bar Copains and Nomad if you wanted to play cultured roulette.

A weekend for us used to start with a circuit: a wander down Crown Street to check the market at Shannon Reserve, a nostalgic peek at Foster Street (Time Out named it one of the world’s Coolest Streets in 2024) and, if we were feeling indulgent, reminiscing about the old haunts named in every guide — Reuben Hills, The Dolphin, the Crix — that once stitched our evenings together. Urban List’s 2026 round-up read like our alumni list: Arthur, The Rover, Pellegrino 2000 — venues people wrote home about.

There’s petty local theatre, too, but it’s mostly planning-permit chic: developments like Surry Hills Village on Cleveland Street and Wunderlich Lane at Cleveland and Baptist promised luxury apartments, a Harris Farm Market and a boutique hotel. Concrete Playground even noted 40Res opening as a “relaxed” wine bar in 2025 — proof we can be fancy without trying too hard.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who treasures silence, owns a lawn the size of a small country, or thinks thirty brunch options is a sign of societal collapse. Also, if you don’t enjoy overhearing three different happy hours on your way home, Surry Hills is not for you.

Who would love it? Food obsessive types, theatre-goers who already have a Belvoir St Theatre membership in their wallet, families who use the Surry Hills Library and the playgroups at Level 1, 405 Crown Street, and anyone who thinks a monthly trip to Shannon Reserve markets is a civic ritual.

We’re nothing if not a little messy, a lot delicious, and forever being listed in somebody’s “best of.” We’ll keep the corner tables warm while the rest of the world argues about the next big precinct.
