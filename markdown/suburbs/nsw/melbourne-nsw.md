# Melbourne (NSW)

Slug: `melbourne-nsw`

Melbourne, NSW — we like to tell visitors the city is a mood, then watch them try to Instagram it while we roll our eyes.

Mornings for us used to start with the market chaos: wandering the stalls at Queen Victoria Market (remember when we could elbow our way past the garlic sellers?), then dragging our aching cultural egos to the NGV because, well, you can’t complain about art if you haven’t queued for it. If we’re feeling virtuous we’ll loop the Royal Botanic Gardens or the Capital City Trail and pretend that counts as exercise — Concrete Playground and Sitchu gave us the routes, so we follow them like good little tourists.

Weekends are a mash-up of curated lists: Broadsheet and Urban List telling us which restaurant is the new essential, Time Out crowning us “best city in the world” (they did, once — we still bring it up at dinner), and Concrete Playground reminding us there’s always another “50 best” list to argue about. Theatre season? Broadsheet teased a violent tennis‑themed rom‑com and a Macbeth reimagined as a teen idol — we’ll clap politely and pretend we understood the subtext.

Local drama? Oh yes. The papers have tracked our more performative moments: multiple protests sweeping the CBD, a “She Matters” mural getting defaced, and that mysterious late‑night bang that had half the north asking “WTF was that?” (Pedestrian TV) — nothing like a bit of civic chaos to make the espresso queue feel intimate. Mums in our hood still swap tips like state secrets — Equippers Melbourne’s back‑carpark trick is apparently a thing (Playgroup Victoria), and Glenferrie Festival turns Hawthorn into our annual big day out (Melbourne Mums Group).

Who would hate living here: people who crave silence, parking spots they can call their own, or suburbs without a festival, pop‑up or 100‑item “Hot List” to roll their eyes at. Who would love it: anyone who loves markets, museums, theatre, murals (the good and the controversial), and the comforting smugness of being able to say “Time Out put us on top once” while buying another ticket.

Back in the day the guides called us indispensable; these days we’re still here — slightly damp, highly opinionated and absolutely not sorry.
