# Mount Pleasant (NSW)

Slug: `mount-pleasant-nsw`

Back in the day we used to boast that our idea of small-town drama was whether the cellar door opened at 10 or 10:30. For the record: we’re still that boring, and proud of it.

We wake up to the kind of view that travel writers swoon over — the Brokenback Range folding into vines, lavender doing its best to photobomb every couple’s engagement photos, and the odd wedding party who’ve driven up because Time Out once called Mount Pleasant Wines “home to some of the Hunter Valley’s oldest vines.” As Time Out noted, our own Adrian Sparks has been turning those old vines into award-winning shiraz (and a dignified semillon), so yes, you can pretend you know about tannins when you’re here.

Weekend routine: a sleepy amble to the cellar door, a sniff, a swirl, and a conversation we immediately regret overhearing about terroir. Then we pretend we’re not eyeing the picnic blanket of strangers for inspiration. Sitchu also tipped Mount Pleasant Wines as a Hunter Valley must, which basically means we’re the polite sort of famous — respected, photographed, and mercifully not on any ‘too loud’ lists.

Local drama? There’s no scandal for us to weaponise — the papers only ever use us as romantic wallpaper. So instead we gripe about the tourists who treat our vines like a theme park and the couples who insist the lavender will make their Insta more authentic. We will whisper that the view is nicer when you know which plot belongs to the old vines and which one is constantly experimenting with “fun” clones, but it’s not juicy enough for a headline.

Who would hate living here? People who crave sirens at 3am, neon lights and 24/7 delivery options. If your ideal weekend involves a nightclub and a kebab at 4am, Mount Pleasant will feel like a very patient retirement village.

Who would love it? Lovers, wine nerds, and anyone who appreciates a shiraz with a backstory and a vineyard that’s been sulking in the same spot for generations. Back in the day people called it romantic — and frankly, we’ve been milking that compliment ever since.
