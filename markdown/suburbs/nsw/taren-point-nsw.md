# Taren Point (NSW)

Slug: `taren-point-nsw`

We’ll be honest: for years Taren Point felt like the kind of place that only revealed itself in small, dependable ways — and the odd escaped horse. We still tell the story of that day Pedestrian TV ran the photos of three horses galloping through Brighton Le Sands and Taren Point like it was the suburb’s welcome parade.

Mornings used to have a rhythm. On Fridays the Taren Point French playgroup met at 10:45am at Taren Point Public School, 93 Woodlands Road — a polite invasion of berets and storybooks. The same stretch of Woodlands Road hosted La Belle Vie Fresh Market events (listed at 93 Woodlands Rd), so there was always a whiff of croissants and community markets in the air. And if you wanted to stretch after morning chaos, Aamari Wellness at 38 Alexander Ave quietly offered Pilates, yoga and the like, if memory serves.

By arvo the locals still remembered what the pubs were for. Time Out described the Taren Point Hotel at 105 Parraweena Rd as that “massive watering hole” — the place that could switch from Anzac Day two-up and rowdy concerts to ping-pong in the family lounge before you’d even finished your pie. Urban List used to recommend the Taren Point Bowling Club for barefoot bowls — $20 a head, live music, trivia, meat raffles, roast dinner specials and the baffling inclusion of yum cha on the events board. Only here would that sentence make sense.

Petty local drama? Apart from equine mayhem, our scandals were smaller and more comforting: which family squeezed into the beer garden, who claimed the best seat by the ping-pong table, whether Holt Road’s Christmas lights would out-glitter the rest of the Shire — Sitchu did note Holt Road went full Father Christmas each December.

Who would hate living here? If you need an Instagrammable laneway of boutique cafés on every corner, you’ll think we’re a bit rough around the edges. Who would love it? Families who appreciate a pub that doubles as a playground, market-goers, and anyone nostalgic for a suburb that does community and chaos with equal enthusiasm. We’ll take the meat raffles and the market stalls — and yes, we still keep half an eye on the paddocks, just in case.
