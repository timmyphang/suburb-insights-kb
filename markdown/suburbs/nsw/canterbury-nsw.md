# Canterbury (NSW)

Slug: `canterbury-nsw`

We’ll be honest: back in the day Canterbury felt like a town that couldn’t decide if it was a racetrack, a food court or a suburb for baby playdates — and the old stories still make us smile.

Weekends used to look like this: we’d finish up at Kia Friday Night Racing at Canterbury Park, wander the Canterbury Park Summer Night Markets and argue over whose falafel from the stalls was the best (Concrete Playground had the listings). If you were feeling fancy — or just hungry — you might remember Pazar Food Collective on Canterbury Road, that Turkish‑Mexican mashup everyone wrote about (Concrete Playground / Time Out noted the makeover of 325 Canterbury Road). For cravings that required charcoal and communal tables, Jang Tur on Canterbury Road was the sort of place Time Out said packed out with big family groups.

Even our local leisure brag made the papers — Time Out ran the long-awaited Canterbury Leisure and Aquatic Centre piece about the outdoor pool filling up, which had us daydreaming about summer lanes and a sauna that might actually make us use our togs.

We’re not above citing our own virtue: the Bulldogs earned a gold star in Pedestrian TV’s archives when they handed out club memberships to refugee families — yes, we’re the kind of place whose footy team does social conscience. And for the parents among us, directories like MyBump2Baby have long catalogued the abundance of baby and toddler groups and antenatal classes that keep our little humans entertained (and us caffeinated — though the caf names live in memory, not this dataset).

Petty local drama? Mostly we bicker about whose kid got into which class and whether the night markets will ever reopen in the exact same configuration. We’re small‑scale, domestic theatre.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe the sound of cheers from the racetrack, can’t abide a multicultural food mashup, or are allergic to infant playgroups, this place will test you.

Who would love it? Families, food chasers with a soft spot for communal dining, anyone nostalgic for summer night markets, and people who appreciate a footy club that does more than kick goals. Just remember: these are older press clippings — think of this as our wistful postcard, not a live events feed.
