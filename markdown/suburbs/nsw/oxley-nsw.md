# Oxley (NSW)

Slug: `oxley-nsw`

We wake before sunrise because here your alarm is a gorge. We’re the kind of place that measures weekends in waterfalls, not brunch bookings — standing at the rim of Wollomombi Falls feels less like a photo op and more like a reminder that civilisation is optional.

Back in the day, Time Out billed the Green Gully Track in Oxley Wild Rivers National Park as the most challenging of the NSW Great Walks — a 65‑kilometre loop that eats 4–5 days for breakfast. For years the only traffic report we cared about was whether Cedar Creek Cottage was still the sensible access point to start that mess of ridges and river valleys. If Waterfall Way shows up on your road-trip map, congratulations: you’re driving the theatrical route that links Coffs Harbour to Armidale and sweeps you past national-park scenery that tries very hard to humble you.

We don’t have a heated dispute about roundabouts or a celebrity chef opening a new venue — the local gossip is whether the next bend in the track delivers another plunging drop or another patch of scrub that insists on making our boots redundant. Sitchu reminds us Dangar Falls and the gorge are the sort of things people write postcards about; we nod and pretend we weren’t already taking photos on the way to the bakery (which, to be honest, might be miles away).

Who would hate living here? People who need a 24/7 mall within walking distance, folks who mistake life for a curated Instagram grid, and anyone who expects the weekend to be a two-hour espresso break rather than a four-day humiliation at the hands of the bush.

Who would love it here? Anyone who reads maps like bedtime stories, people allergic to canned playlists and keen on natural cacophony, and those who think a serious hike is the best way to reset. As Time Out and the old regional guides cheerfully suggested, if you want drama it’s seasonal and geological — not manufactured. We’ll take our humbling waterfalls, thanks.
