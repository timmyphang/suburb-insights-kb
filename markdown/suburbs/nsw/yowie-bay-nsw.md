# Yowie Bay (NSW)

Slug: `yowie-bay-nsw`

Back in the day we used to do Sundays like the brochure: a slow stroll from the bright new-ish Kalang Lane Reserve down to the Yowie Bay pontoon, socks still damp from the bubbler at the playground. Kalang Lane Reserve was upgraded toward the end of 2021 — bike and scooter path, picnic areas, a full toilet block and a water fill station — and yes, it made us briefly believe we lived in a council-funded utopia for under-sixes.

We’re the kind of people who’ll point out the EG Waterhouse Camellia Gardens like it’s our civic duty. Sitchu and Urban List used to gush about those camellias — first planted in 1969, apparently forever in bloom — and the view over Yowie Bay that makes you forget you’re only an hour-ish from the city, depending on traffic and your tolerance for roundabouts. Time Out put the area in a picnic round-up too, which made everyone feel slightly fancy for a week.

Our favourite local theatre of small dramas is the pontoon. Mums of the Shire called Yowie Bay a “natural aquarium” and told the true story of the local fisherman who shared bait so the kids could watch fish and ducks feed. It’s wholesome and tiny and, honestly, the height of low-stakes excitement. The Yowie Bay Twilight Markets used to light up Yowie Bay Public School on 15th November — stallholders, last-minute Christmas shopping, and the sort of mince-pie optimism you only get at community markets.

Who would hate living here? If you need neon nightlife, endless artisanal everything or someone to loudly announce the latest CBD opening — this place will quietly frustrate you. If you loathe ducks, small fish, and the sound of scooters on shared paths, Yowie Bay will test your patience. Also, people who are allergic to preschools will struggle — Yowie Bay Preschool was a boutique fixture for decades, run by the same owner for 28 years and offering school readiness, yoga, meditation and the ELLA language program. We wore that stability like a badge.

Who’d love it? Families who like playground upgrades, picnic views, a polite fisherman and markets that feel like a community hug. For years the place hasn’t been flashy, just stubbornly lovable — a little slice of bay life that knows it’s not trying to be Bondi, and that’s the point.
