# Attadale (NSW)

Slug: `attadale-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start the weekend by pretending the Swan River was our backyard and then actually believing it. The green, tranquil streets and the foreshore make for a perfect lie: yes, we live by the water; no, we don’t all own a boat — but Point Walter Reserve used to supply a very convincing boat jetty vibe for the paddlers and kite surfers among us.

Our Saturday ritual involved an acoustic circuit of favourites that, full disclosure, the guidebooks called out long before we bragged about them. The Neighbourhood at 88 Moreing Road used to dish up acai bowls piled with mango, strawberries, kiwi, banana, coconut, goji berries and granola — the kind of bowl that makes you feel virtuous while you scroll. If you were being naughty instead of virtuous, Urban List used to point everyone toward The Cove Indian Restaurant, that colonial-house institution that amassed many happy regulars with a Saag Lamb Curry and a weekend buffet (Friday to Sunday). For those who took curry very seriously, Urban List also named the Cove and its Navratan korma as local classics.

We were fond of our small pleasures: Guru Spices at 88C Moreing Rd got name-checked as a neighbourhood favourite, and Peko Peko’s Attadale outpost used to remind us that sushi can come with house-seasoned rice and things like Balinese sambal chicken or Korean beef if you were feeling exploratory. For the hair-needy among us, The Nest Hair Boutique (yes, ladies colour specialists using Olaplex) used to be our tidy little salon confession booth.

Who would hate living here? Someone who thinks “laidback” is code for “boring”; night-owl types who measure neighbourhoods in closing times rather than park benches. And who would love it? Families who want room to roam, anyone who prizes a riverside walk over a rooftop bar, and curry devotees who take their Navratan korma seriously.

We’ll admit it: we’re smug about our quiet streets and our curry credentials. As the old guides said, Attadale used to be all the small pleasures lined up in green — and we loved it for that.
