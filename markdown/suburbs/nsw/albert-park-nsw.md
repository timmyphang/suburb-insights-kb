# Albert Park (NSW)

Slug: `albert-park-nsw`

We like to think Albert Park has the sort of calm that makes people whisper “prestige” while secretly checking their watch for the next lap of traffic. For years the papers treated us like a lakeside postcard: Broadsheet had its directory, Sitchu kept telling everyone about the 5km Albert Park Lake circuit we clock religiously, and Time Out reminded the world this is where the Grand Prix lands when it rolls in (yes, the circuit uses Aughtie Drive and Lakeside Drive — we know the corners better than the council does).

Back in the day the village felt like its own little ecosystem. We’d mooch past Bridport Street and Victoria Avenue, past the MSAC, and pop into the long-running Albert Park Deli — running since 1974 and apparently open from 5:30am — where the locals could get dinner and a story at any hour. The Garden of Eden Nursery used to be our go-to for anything green and responsibly moralising about compost. Pipis Kiosk was the seafood spot that made us feel like we were still on holidays; we still chuckle that its team took Pipis North to Fitzroy for a bit of summertime mischief.

The headline-grabbing local: the Albert Park Hotel. The 137-year-old pub got the full makeover treatment and a modern-Chinese turn — an atrium, dim sum, peking duck pancakes — Concrete Playground and Broadsheet wrote love letters about it. Wally’s, at 67 Cardigan Place, cropped up as the Euro-ish wine bar our in-laws pretended not to notice.

Who would hate living here? If you despise promenades, joggers on the 5km loop, an annual influx of race-day spectacle, or the smug satisfaction of a neighbourhood that’s quietly expensive (Pedestrian once pointed out we’re in the same breath as “melbourne’s most expensive shoebox” comparatives), then yeah — this might not be for you.

But who loves it? Families fishing stories by the lake, people who like their weekends to smell faintly of tyre rubber and beach prawns, and anyone who appreciates a deli that’s been doing its thing since 1974. We’ll keep circling the lake, knowing every corner of our little patch — and rolling our eyes affectionately when the city turns up for the weekend.
