# Walkerville (NSW)

Slug: `walkerville-nsw`

We live in Walkerville, NSW — and sometimes it feels like the internet thinks we’re shy, because when you look us up it keeps introducing you to three other, far more photogenic Walkervilles. Back in the day the Broadsheet entries gushed about a Coffee Institute at 96 Walkerville Terrace, Local Grind by the River Torrens and The Watson Art Series Hotel — all lovely, but not exactly our postcode. Even Sitchu has memories: they once crowned Yolk Cafe Best Coffee Spot in a Walkerville that isn’t ours.

Our morning routine here is deliberately low-key because our online identity is busy elsewhere. While Broadsheet once detailed Persian‑influenced gelato and Rio-roasted house blends at a Walkerville cafe, and Urban List fawned over Jacky Winter Waters and Liptrap Loft down in Walkerville North on the Gippsland coast, we make do with whatever the shore and the sky hand us. Concrete Playground used to recommend Walkerville as a tiny seaside village with views across Waratah Bay to Wilsons Promontory — again, a lovely read while we sip whatever we can scrounge and argue about whether “which Walkerville” counts as small‑town anonymity or global obscurity.

There’s no juicy petty council drama in the files we found — mostly travel pieces, hotel listings and award mentions — so we’ll roast what we can: if you hate being constantly mistaken for an Art Series hotel, a design Airbnb with a sunken bath, or the backdrop for a children’s‑book movie shoot (Concrete Playground noted Walkerville South hosted filming for Magic Beach), then you will loathe living here. You’ll spend every supper explaining that no, we’re not the Walkerville with the lime kilns or the Walkerville Terrace address, and yes, people still confuse the Gippsland beach for our backyard.

Who would love it? The kind of people who enjoy being underrated in peace — those who like the idea of coastal villages splashed across travel roundups and don’t mind sharing their name with a few award‑winning cafés and architecturally famous weekenders. We’ll keep our anonymity like an island: simultaneously frustrating, endearing and very, very ours.
