# Croydon (NSW)

Slug: `croydon-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean: the only pieces anyone could dig up were pre-2024 — we were the suburb that journalists popped into between lockdowns and local council meetings and then forgot existed. So yes: a little disclaimer for the pedants — there aren’t any recent features. We embrace being low‑maintenance.

Morning in our corner of the Inner West? We used to swear by the lockdown-era bakery culture of nearby Croydon Park. By George (remember when it opened in a former appliance store?) was the kind of place that pretended our sourdough crumpets from Scrumpets with fairy‑bread on top were peak sophistication. Small Talk bagels showed up Reuben‑style, Tuga’s pastéis de nata turned up like they’d never heard of moderation, and Ono/ Ōna coffee kept the queue civil. It felt like culture, until it felt like peak suburbs-against-the-world.

We’re not all artisanal carbs and mild smugness. The patch has events — Gluttony in Croydon Park once brought circus, cabaret and the kind of family-friendly chaos Time Out loves to catalogue — and Concrete Playground used to list our local goings-on, too. Families will tell you our libraries (Central Children’s Library, Shirley Library) and Local Mums Online playgroups are the real civic centres; there’s Little Stars Preschool Time and Dance Fitness With Saz if you want to be judged while trying jazz hands.

We’ve had our moments of proper worry too — Pedestrian TV covered a lockdown after a shocking incident in Croydon Park. We don’t make light of that; we just say: we know the difference between jokes and genuine concern, and we look after each other.

Who would hate living here? If you need endless, shiny new press coverage and a city-centre ego boost, you’ll find our media footprint disappointing. Who’d love it? Families who actually use the libraries and playgroups, people who like a skate park, ping‑pong tables and the odd parkour circuit in Croydon Park, and anyone who appreciates a neighbourhood that quietly does things — sometimes with fairy‑bread on top.

We’re modest, we’re opinionated, and we’ll always be better remembered for our crumpets than for our PR team.
