# Eleebana (NSW)

Slug: `eleebana-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed to birdsong and the comforting hum of suburbia — because, yes, Eleebana has always been the kind of place where the loudest thing is the cicadas and the odd council mower. Most of what people have written about us comes from those friendly pre-2024 travel and family blogs, so consider this a fond, slightly smug walk down memory lane.

A weekend used to start with a stroll along the Red Bluff elevated boardwalk, past the Creative LAKE Sculpture Trail that Urban List and Junkee liked to rave about — the Sculpture Walk linking us to Warners Bay and Speers Point gave the whole place its arty postcard vibe. Newy with Kids called the sealed, pram-friendly path ideal for families; we call it our outdoorsy flex.

Our centre of civic life? Bunya Park Playground and Community Garden — the one with an undercover electric BBQ, bubbler, toilet block, balance obstacles and a community plot that local hands tended. The mum-and-kid blogs loved to file that under “very wholesome.” There was also Eleebana Children’s Centre, where Merrin had been the sole owner for over 25 years, and Kindy Patch at 80 Cherry Road; CareforKids listed childcare options and the local OSHC ran out of vacancies so often we joked they were more exclusive than the cinema.

Real estate listings painted us as cul‑de‑sac serenity — Sitchu’s pages promised lake views to the front and bush to the back. Local services were small and unflashy: Kim Isenhood Hairdressing cropped hair by the lake, Finspire Advisers billed themselves as independent planners, and Valentine Eleebana Physio (Annette Chase) handled the sore bits after weekend walks.

Who would hate living here? If you crave neon nightlife, 24/7 cafés, and relentless urban buzz, you’d sob into your ride-share app. But who should move in? Families, walkers, anyone who likes public sculpture and a tidy community garden, and those who secretly enjoy knowing Warners Bay is just a convivial saunter away.

We may be understated, but we have art on our foreshore, a boardwalk that gets the travel press excited, and a community that prefers an electric BBQ to a rooftop bar. Not glamorous — just reliably lovely.
