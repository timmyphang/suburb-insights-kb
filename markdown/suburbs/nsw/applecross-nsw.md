# Applecross (NSW)

Slug: `applecross-nsw`

We start our Saturday with a limp stroll down Ardross Street like we own the pavements — past the spot where Nic & Kolo opened in May 2017 at 45 Ardross Street (they took over the old JACS site, if you’re still crying over that renovation). Back in the day the café strip here felt like a curated low-key flex: Typeface Books tucked into Applecross Village, Botanical Lane taking over the old garden‑centre (the site that used to be home to Cioccolato Espresso), and small things doing big work.

There’s delicious contradiction in our suburb: you can duck into a 14-seat tempura counter — Dining Akashi on Canning Highway — for something reverent and quiet, then drive (or pretend you’ll walk) to a riverside behemoth that was billed as The Heart, a 350-seat gastropub with 22 beers on tap and a 60-seat alfresco looking over the Swan River. Or you could stand on the Raffles Hotel veranda — that art‑deco riverside icon that’s been watering workers since 1896 — and feel like you’ve time-travelled to a postcard.

We also remember the little wins: Ipoh Garden’s original Applecross outpost kicked off its story here in 2001 (that Applecross location is now closed), BWG Steakhouse rising from the Bluewater Grill within Heathcote Reserve at 56 Duncraig Road, and Bar Olé planting a 240-seat tapas flag down at the base of the Riviere Apartments. The Heathcote cultural precinct — Goolugatup Heathcote Museum and Gallery — gives the whole place a slightly solemn, very local centrepiece, while the pirate-ship playground at Heathcote Reserve guarantees weekend chaos for a radius of at least three streets.

Who would hate it here? People allergic to river views, independent bookshops, and matcha fashion — yes, Daily Practice from the Nic & Kolo team was even described as a matcha‑focused cafe‑retail hybrid (we saw the headlines). Who would love it? Families who picnic under gums at Heathcote, diners who can swing from tempura to tapas, and anyone who thinks a village with both an indie bookstore and a 350‑seat riverside pub is peak suburban mood.

We’re biased, obviously. Applecross used to wear its contradictions like a good blazer — slightly too loud, quietly excellent.
