# West Ryde (NSW)

Slug: `west-ryde-nsw`

Full disclosure before we roast: our sources about West Ryde are a bit nostalgic — everything we’ve got is from the old files, so consider this more a love note to how it used to be than a live guide.

Picture this: it’s a balmy evening, and we’re sitting under the vine‑covered trellis at the West Ryde Hotel (1021 Victoria Rd), glass sweating, pretending suburban pubs invented ambience. Weekends used to start with a sneaky run to Soonhi Banchan for kimbap and a trolley of kimchi varieties — Broadsheet even said Sydney chefs loved it — then a strategic walk past Anzac Park’s upgraded playground (Wattle St / ANZAC Ave) to tire the kids before you lose your cool.

We’ve always had options. Time Out wrote about Mapo Galbi’s long communal tables and famous ribs — cheap, loud and unapologetic — and Gyeong Bok Gung got a namecheck for its banchan. Hong Kong Seafood Stall got local love in a city roundup. You could fuel the family, the fry‑up crew, and the late‑night sashimi aficionados in a single Victoria Rd shuffle.

Petty drama? Oh, we’re not above it. As Pedestrian TV noted, there was that awkward Tatsuya West Ryde apology after a rat was found in a side salad — yes, we remember the headlines and the mortified PR emails. Also, Tian Ci was listed as closed in Time Out’s notes, so the vegetarian yum cha crowd had to rework their weekend plans.

Who would hate living here? People who measure suburb quality by waterside brunch views — as Concrete Playground reminds us, that’s a different postcode. Also anyone allergic to enthusiastic junior netballers: Bennelong Sports Centre’s monstrous courts (29 outdoor netball, a 5,000sqm stadium — Ryde District Mums told us) meant weekend parking felt like a spectator sport.

Who’d love it? Families: the West Ryde Library’s kid‑friendly learning pod and microscopes are exactly our vibe. Food obsessives who like authenticity over pretense — from Soonhi Banchan to the many Korean BBQ spots — will smirk contentedly. And tiny train romantics: the West Ryde Mini Trains used to chug behind the shopping centre on the 3rd Saturday, and honestly, that’s our kind of civic joy.

We’re proud of our low‑key chaos. West Ryde might not be shiny, but it’s got character, carbs and community — sometimes all at once.
