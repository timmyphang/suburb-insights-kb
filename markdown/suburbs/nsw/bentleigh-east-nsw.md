# Bentleigh East (NSW)

Slug: `bentleigh-east-nsw`

Back in the day we’d wobble out onto Centre Road like it was our local runway — merchants, mums, and anyone with a reusable cup in hand. We used to crowd the communal table at The Merchants Guild (you remember the wooden checkerboard thing?) and pretend that Prana Chai was a personality trait. Broadsheet even wrote up our little Friday-only hummus and falafel vendor as if it were a civic monument. Glamorous, no?

Weekend routine used to be gloriously simple: a slow amble past 680 Centre Road to inspect the doughnut French toast at Merchant’s Guild (Urban List had the photos), nostalgic detours to Time & Place at 929 Centre Road for whatever the Mediterranean-leaning all-day menu was up to that month — falafel waffles was a headline none of us expected — then a sleepy swing by Good Times Milk Bar on Tucker Road when we felt like pastel-coloured optimism. Saul’s Diner on Centre Road used to fill the sandwich void for those who liked deli-style certainty, and Boosa’s panko za’atar eggs were the sort of thing you told people about until they ordered them too.

If you liked small local makers, Time Out once pointed out something literally “Made in Bentleigh East” — cotton and beeswax and that wholesome-hipster energy. And for the suddenly sporty among us, Urban List mentioned PicklePlay at 1 South Drive: infrared saunas, a cold plunge, a cinema room and an on-hand massage therapist. We’ll admit it sounded delightfully excessive for a place famous for weekend coffees.

We don’t have the tabloid melodrama of a high street scandal in these clippings — just a comforting pile of cafe brochures and lifestyle guides. That’s fine. It means our biggest quarrels were whether to queue for doughnut French toast or confit salmon on rye.

Who would hate living here? People who need a 24/7 nightclub, who fear communal tables, or who think a boutique pre/postnatal studio at 9 Scanlan St is suspiciously sensible. Who would love it? Families, sandwich obsessives (Saul’s was on the map), the Pilates-and-prams crowd served by Moving Through Motherhood, home chefs hunting artisan bits, and anyone who enjoys a suburban main street doing quietly ambitious things. We’ll take Bentleigh East’s modest ambitions any day.
