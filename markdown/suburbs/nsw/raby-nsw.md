# Raby (NSW)

Slug: `raby-nsw`

Back in the day, a Monday at Raby felt like half a village fete, half a gentle logistical exercise. We’d herd our tiny humans into the Raby Sports Complex on Raby Road (yes, the one with the postcode 2566) and gather for Play Strong’s PlayVan sessions — the supported playgroup that used to roll up at 9:30am, funded through the Department of Education’s Start Strong Pathways grant. If you’d asked, we could tell you who brought the boring raisins and who forgot the spare socks. For enquiries, Playgroup NSW had the email ps.campbelltown@playgroupnsw.org.au — proof that someone somewhere was organised.

We’re not pretending Raby is a lifestyle shoot. There’s no pretentious laneway full of brunch plates here; our claim to fame is communal practicality. The Play Strong program’s whole schtick was exactly that: early learning, building parent capacity and actually creating a pathway to preschool for the small humans who otherwise spend more time negotiating playground politics than spelling their names.

Petty local drama? The internet’s silence on Raby is the drama. There aren’t a swag of glossy thinkpieces to argue about, so our scandals are small: a disputed parking spot at the Sports Complex; the eternal mystery of who’s nominated to bring the laminator for craft morning. If you crave headline-making feuds, you’ll need to look further afield — we keep our melodrama cheap and local.

Who would hate living here? If you need a buzzing nightlife, celebrity chefs on speed-dial, or a cafe leaderboard to argue about, Raby will feel like a cul-de-sac of disappointment. We don’t have a million curated experiences — we have a sports complex, a playvan that used to show up like clockwork, and neighbours who know your kids’ shoe size.

Who would love it? Families who want practical support, parents after a live, low-key community and anyone who values a place that quietly does the work of raising the next lot. We might not make national headlines, but for years the Play Strong sessions showed exactly what matters here: neighbours turning up, kids learning through play, and a local cadence that’s steady and strangely comforting.
