# Floreat (NSW)

Slug: `floreat-nsw`

Back in the day the first thing we’d smell was salt on the boardwalk and someone else’s souffle pancakes. We know the walk at Floreat Beach like the back of our thongs — the Nature Walk and boardwalk give proper views to Fremantle and Rottnest Island, and that’s always been our opening scene.

Weekends used to look like this: we’d stroll the sand, pause by The Kiosk (it was the breezy, beachy spot for breakfast, lunch and the odd summer dinner), then wander inland toward 445 Cambridge Street where Someday Coffee Co used to sling granola, Buddha bowls and burgers on its outdoor seating. Broadsheet and Urban List both wrote about the place — Mary Street Bakery doughnuts and Kommunity Brew Kombucha even made cameos in the menu mentions. If we were feeling fancy, we’d reminisce about that era when Broadsheet kept listing new Floreat openings — Cafe Yoka cropped up in their 2025/2026 roundups, promising Asian-accented breakfasts: pulled beef and kimchi toasties, brisket Benedict on shokupan, mushroom congee and yes, soufflé pancakes. Those were the headlines for a while.

We won’t pretend Floreat’s always been headline-grabbing — some of it reads like the suburban dream on a block of 890sqm (remember that 1953 brick on 10 Dumfries Road?). The Boulevard Hotel was the old stalwart for a solid steak and a very serious wine list — 80 bottled wines and more than 30 by the glass, if you were the sort who liked choices and corkage conversations. For dumpling devotees, BamBamBoo’s modern dim sum setup even made the list, with iPad ordering and all the jingable options.

Who would hate it here? If you live for late-night neon and lanes where you can’t park a ute, Floreat is quietly smug about its dunes and semi-homey cafes. We’ll admit: we’re not for everyone.

Who would love it? Beach people, wine nerds, parents eyeing those roomy blocks and anyone who remembers when a seaside stroll could still end in a souffle. We’re small, salty and a touch twee — and we like it that way.
