# Greensborough (NSW)

Slug: `greensborough-nsw`

We spill out of the Hurstbridge Line at Greensborough Station like a small, polite invasion, lace up for the Plenty River Trail and pretend we're training for that Urban List 21km route to Clifton Hill. By the time we hit the parklands and football fields we're in full weekend local mode: joggers pretending it’s CrossFit, kids who actually know how to ride BMX lines, and us wondering where the espresso went (nostalgia, not inventing).

Back in the day Broadsheet even had us sneaking into the top suburbs list — #6, if you like modest surprise. Our weekends used to look like this: a long run along the Plenty River Trail, a detour to Dulce Gelateria at 36 Main Street (they opened with a surprisingly comprehensive vegan selection), and pizza nights at Prefisso 3088 on Hopkins Street, which Concrete Playground once called a relaxed, welcoming local pizzeria. Bargain hunters could make an afternoon of it at the Savers Recycle Superstore — Time Out said it was worth the long hike once you step inside.

We have a weird local CV: Kalparrin Gardens with its lakeside playground, BBQs, tennis courts and skatepark; Okami Greensborough that didn’t do the usual all-you-can-eat thing and went for made-to-order Japanese instead; Wing Chun Greensborough teaching wooden-dummy practice since 1995; Bagels & Co at 44 Main Street for when you need carb therapy. The Greenhills Neighbourhood House hosts playgroups, if your offspring are into socialising before they can spell “suburb.”

Petty drama? We’re low-maintenance — Concrete Playground’s events page for Greensborough is currently showing zero results, which is both a flex and an invitation: if you crave nightly gigs, you’ll be cross-eyed bored here. Our claim to headline news includes a KFC drive-thru TikTok that made the rounds and, yes, the whole town briefly rallied around Garry the gorilla statue when police returned him to the retirement village.

Who would hate it: nightclub refugees and anyone who judges a suburb by its 2am offerings. Who would love it: runners, families who rate a skatepark over a rooftop bar, bargain hunters, pizza people, sushi appreciators, and anyone who likes a place that feels more neighbourhood than brand launch. We’ll keep the duck pond, you keep bringing the gelato.
