# Derrimut (NSW)

Slug: `derrimut-nsw`

We’re at the Derrimut Community Centre at 9am, shoes still dusty from the weekend, watching the kids scatter like confetti across the hall. For years the playgroups ran out of this place — right next to Derrimut Primary School — and you could ring reception (9307 4700) or email derrimutcommunity@ymca.org.au and, if they weren’t busy, someone would point you to the timetable. That’s our proper morning: coffee in a thermos, a piece of paper with session times, and a small, suspicious glue-smudge trophy for “best finger-painter.”

Back in the day, if you had a more adventurous palate you might have caught Noah Sellami’s French tacos truck parked around Braybrook and Derrimut before he found his Fitzroy digs — Broadsheet tells that particular migration tale, and yes, we remember when the food truck was a local cameo rather than a city headline. For longer outings, we used to tip our hats toward the Werribee circuit — Sitchu still lists Werribee Park, the Victoria State Rose Garden and even AquaPulse at 80–82 Derrimut Road, Hoppers Crossing — because if you’re raising small people, a splash and a statue do wonders for morale.

LocalFitness treated Derrimut like it mattered too, compiling lists of gyms and 24/7 options so anyone who wanted to pretend they’d be up at dawn could sign up and then never show. That’s us: ambition on the form, reality on the couch.

Who would hate living here? If you need neon cocktails at midnight and streets named after boutique espresso blends, you’ll be bored — we used to have a rolling food-truck scene, but we aren’t Collingwood or Fitzroy. If your hobby is debating the best of Melbourne’s bars on a Tuesday night, Derrimut’s quieter, suburban rhythm will offend your timetable.

Who loves it here? Families who need real playgroups and a community centre that remembers your kid’s name; people who like a cheap gym option and weekend drives to Werribee’s roses and pools. We’re not trying to be everywhere else — we’re stubbornly Derrimut, with a community centre, three playgroups, and the memory of a French tacos truck that once parked nearby. Come for the practicality; stay for the glue-smudge trophy.
