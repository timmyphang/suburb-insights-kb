# Asquith (NSW)

Slug: `asquith-nsw`

We like to think of a summer in Asquith as one long neighbourhood performance — the kind where everyone turns up, someone brings fairy lights and someone else brings a very particular opinion on pool maintenance. Back in the day, Urban List even sent us the tourists: remember the house at 28 Lockwood Street with the LEDs that “dance to the tunes”? We do. We queue, we gasp, we pretend it’s our idea.

Our weekend loop is embarrassingly practical. Sitchu listings have long been our local gospel: 2/23B Bouvardia Street is about 400m to Asquith station and roughly 450m to Coles Asquith, and if you fancy something slightly more ambitious it’s 2.7km to Westfield Hornsby. We’ve seen character family homes on Heath Street with pools and separate studios — yes, they boasted a Smeg‑appointed kitchen — and the 17 Baldwin Avenue spot that tried very hard to be Zen with timber cladding and colourbond steel. We nod approvingly at ambition.

If you want petty suburban theatre, the internet provides. Mumsnet threads dug into “Asquith” nurseries — parents sharing mixed experiences, tales of high staff turnover and the usual caveat that quality depends on the manager and room leader. It’s the kind of local debate that gets hot enough to power the Christmas lights.

We also like the fact our name turns up in other places. Broadsheet once wrote about an artist called Steven Asquith and his Storm Concepts at Utopian Slumps; Time Out reminded us of Asquith Xavier’s story overseas. We mention them not to be confusing, more to prove we punch above our postcode in interesting ways.

Who would hate living here? If your idea of fun is permanent late‑night neon and never having to learn your neighbours’ names, you’ll be mildly inconvenienced. If you can’t handle slightly earnest Zen façades or the occasional thread about childcare, maybe pass.

Who’d love it? Families who value being in the Asquith Public School and Asquith Boys and Girls High Schools catchment, homeowners after a pool or a Smeg kitchen, and anyone who thinks a brilliantly over‑lit Lockwood Street makes December worth waiting for. We’re a little ordinary, a little proud, and absolutely ours.
