# Doonside (NSW)

Slug: `doonside-nsw`

We wake up in Doonside like everyone else — slightly surprised the wildlife park is literally in our postcode. Back in the day, Time Out reminded everyone that Featherdale Sydney Wildlife Park at 217 Kildare Road was the big local flex, with about 2,000 animals spread across the Doonside property. So yes, if your weekend vibe involves kangaroos who’ve seen more tourists than you have, we’ve got you covered.

Our proper weekend looks like this: a stroll to the Bungarribee Trail — Concrete Playground called it an easy 4km sealed pathway and even said it’s only a 20-minute walk from both Doonside and Rooty Hill stations — which is handy when you want to pretend you’re exercising but mostly admire the nearby Sydney Zoo and Blacktown International Sportspark from afar. For a post-walk celebratory beer, Time Out once noted the Doonside Hotel was built in 1962 "on a swamp marsh" and came with the comforting practicality of a bottle shop. Vintage, damp history — very local.

Food-wise, Urban List once announced "the birria taco wave has officially hit Doonside" and name‑checked Señor Toro as leading the charge, so yes, we used to have authentic taco clout. Findglocal kept the local rolodex: Johnnie's on Doonside Crescent, The Colebee at Nurragingy Reserve on Knox Road pitched as Western Sydney's premium function venue, and even Doonside Auto Parts on Hillend Road (open seven days, apparently) — all the essentials.

Petty drama? Our sources are older and unapologetically nostalgic, so there’s no fresh feud to drag across suburbia this week. Instead we’ll keep it simple: if you hate seeing rosellas on your way to the supermarket, or the idea of a 1960s hotel with a bottle shop makes you nostalgic for something you never lived through, Doonside might grate.

Who would hate it here: those who need a non-stop CBD soundtrack and boutique everything. Who would love it: families who actually use Featherdale, bushwalkers who appreciate a sealed 4km path, and anyone planning a function at The Colebee (as Findglocal promoted). We’re proud, a bit muddy, and historically excellent at hosting animals and tacos — take that, suburb snobbery.
