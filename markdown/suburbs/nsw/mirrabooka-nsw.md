# Mirrabooka (NSW)

Slug: `mirrabooka-nsw`

We live in Mirrabooka, NSW — but if you Google us you’d think we’re a long-lost sibling living in Perth. Back in the day the internet loved Mirrabooka 6061: Broadsheet once walked through “Mirrabooka Village” and praised places like Ben Thanh, Hu Tieu Thanh Liem and Afghani Kebab House; Urban List kept name‑checking MCQ Vietnamese Rolls, West Brothers and even the Xtreme Ice Arena. Meanwhile, our own patch (Mirrabooka 2264) mostly turns up as helpful directory entries on MumsPages and a few community mentions. File under: identity crisis with good food recommendations.

Our morning routine — as far as the written record goes — is less espresso‑cartel, more playgroup roster. Playgroup WA’s reports show a fair bit of behind‑the‑scenes hustle: from the ISHAR centre pilot that started in July 2022 to partnerships funded by The Smith Family and handovers to the Westminster Child and Parent Centre. In other words, we’re busy making sure kids and carers have somewhere to actually turn up, not just somewhere to post about.

There’s not much petty council drama in the clippings — the real civic flex is quietly practical. Urban List’s note about the Mirrabooka Friday Markets at Mirrabooka Regional Open Space, 38 Ashbury Cres, tells you everything: volunteer‑run, 47 years, profits ploughed back into sports, schools and community groups. That’s the sort of small stubborn kindness that doesn’t trend, but it keeps the suburb working.

Who would hate living here? If your vibe is “I need a single, consistent Google narrative,” you’ll be exasperated — the internet can’t decide which Mirrabooka you are. If you need round‑the‑clock nightlife, you’ll be bored. But who would love it? Families and organisers — MumsPages lists, Playgroup WA projects, ISHAR’s pilot program — people who value community infrastructure and a postcode that actually does stuff for its locals.

So yes, we know what people say: the web’s confused. We’re fine with that. We’ll keep running the playgroups and the markets, and let Perth keep the banh mi clout.
