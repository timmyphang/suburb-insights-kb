# Mulgrave (NSW)

Slug: `mulgrave-nsw`

Back in the day we were a low-key flex: not flashy, just quietly collecting oddball bragging rights. The press mentions we’re mostly in the archives — Broadsheet once put us at number 11 in PwC’s City Pulse list, which we’ll happily frame as “top-tier humble suburb” status.

Morning routine: we used to amble down Mackie Road to Mackie, that sun‑filled cafe just off Mulgrave’s Mackie Road — you knew the menu by heart and the banoffles were a communal sin. If you liked your brunch with territorial pride, that was our thing. For bigger nights — in the era when articles were being written about us — Village Green Hotel on the corner of Springvale and Ferntree Gully Rd was the one-stop entertainment venue where somebody’s idea of fun always outlasted somebody else’s liver.

Petty local drama (as recorded in black-and-white snippets): once we made headline news when, during an RBT in Mulgrave, a man allegedly handed cocaine to police — yes, our suburb has had its “what did we just watch?” moments, according to Pedestrian.TV. On the cultural front, Broadsheet name‑checked “Chae Chopstick Delight” (Urban List called it Chopstick Delight at 79 Mackie Road) as a chef-approved spot — whole-roast duck you had to order 48 hours in advance. We bragged about that like it was a medal.

We’re also the kind of place where communal child-rearing was a proper pastime — the old Mum blogs still talk about “the tribe”, kindergarten coffees and new-mums events. If you liked the idea of neighbours who actually show up to birthday parties, you’d get on well here.

Who would hate it here? Someone after constant novelty, nightlife on demand or living-room-sized artisan markets. If you require your suburb to be in every “best of” column every other week, Mulgrave’s low‑key, slightly off‑the-radar charm will feel like watching paint dry — but in a good way.

Who would love it? Families who want the tribe, dog owners who remember Divine Dog Grooming Salon at 9 Fulwood Pl, and anyone nostalgic for a roast duck you had to plan for two days ahead. We’re not a headline every morning — we’re the reliable subplot you keep coming back to.
