# Penshurst (NSW)

Slug: `penshurst-nsw`

We remember lining up under that retro red awning next to Penshurst Station like it was our civic duty. Back in the day Broadsheet wrote about Bsp’eria — the tiny pizzeria at 129 Laycock Road — and suddenly our sleepy strip felt illegitimately cosmopolitan. As Broadsheet pointed out, their Uncle Charlie (a 48‑hour fermented sourdough crowned with spicy ’nduja, mozzarella, ricotta and honey) made us feel briefly like we’d imported Brooklyn without the rent hike. Urban List even nodded them into a “best pizza” list and we still cling to that like it’s a school award.

Our weekend looks used to be gloriously low-effort: grab a burger from Short Black Espresso Bar (run by the Adamou brothers, Time Out reminded everyone), lumber past the shops and, if you were feeling fancy, duck into Ayam Bakar 7 on 34 Penshurst St for something that wasn’t pretending to be fusion. Time Out called Ayam Bakar 7 “camouflaged” and frankly that’s the vibe — good food doing its quietly excellent thing without much fuss.

We don’t have the never‑ending carousel of openings that the inner city brags about, nor do we need it. Our brag is that two national glossies once bothered to write two sentences about us and we treated it like a parade. We’ve also survived the kind of ridiculous heat warnings Pedestrian trotted out — “43–44°C in suburbs including Penshurst” — so if you can handle that, congratulations, you’re ours.

Who would hate living here? If you need a daily artisan relay of openings and PR launches, you’ll be restless. If you want neon nightlife and a 24/7 menu of “experiences,” move on.

Who would love it? People who like a solid slice, a proper burger from a local café, and an Indonesian joint that’s more tucked-away treasure than tourist trap. We’re content, occasionally famous, and stubbornly local — and we wear it like a well-loved, slightly singed hat.
