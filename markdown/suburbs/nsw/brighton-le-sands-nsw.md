# Brighton-Le-Sands (NSW)

Slug: `brighton-le-sands-nsw`

Back in the day we'd start the weekend with a promenade saunter, squinting at Botany Bay and pretending we lived in a Mykonos postcard while arguing whether The Brighton or the Novotel made the better staycation backdrop. As Broadsheet noted, Ammos opened inside the oddly iconic Novotel at Brighton‑Le‑Sands — roving ouzo trolleys and woodfired lobsters providing excellent excuses to look cultured on a Tuesday.

Our ritual used to be predictable: pick up whatever The Fish Joint were battering that morning, walk past the takeaway window of Benetti's at The Brighton Hotel, and clock the new renovations at Neptunes — Concrete Playground loved that multimillion‑dollar revamp, and so did we when it meant more tables facing the water. Dine & Dezerts at 373 Bay Street used to be the syrupy punctuation to every outing, because nothing says seaside living like a dessert pizza you can half-share and regret in equal measure.

Petty local drama? We had our moment when three escaped horses went for a trot through the streets — Pedestrian TV didn’t make it up — and, frankly, that outranked a lot of our council fights. Otherwise our hot takes ran smaller: which corner of The Grand Parade belongs to Meet The Greek, who made the best saganaki, and whether Coco’s On The Beach really meant happy hour at 5pm sharp.

This place isn’t for everyone. If you hate breezes that carry barbecue smoke, strolls interrupted by oysters being shucked to order, or the comforting knowledge that the Mougios family have been part of the neighbourhood story for decades, you’ll want to live somewhere with fewer plates and more parking.

But if you love being by the water, enjoy a promenade that leads to kiosks and volleyball nets, and once belonged to a town where families queued for breakfast at Neptunes and kids went to Little Scribblers on England Street, Brighton‑Le‑Sands is exactly the kind of slightly sunburnt, Mediterranean‑influenced patch of Sydney we proudly call ours. And for families — the Brighton Le Sands Bunnies playgroup at the public school used to run every Tuesday — it felt, and still feels in memory, like the best kind of small‑bay community.
