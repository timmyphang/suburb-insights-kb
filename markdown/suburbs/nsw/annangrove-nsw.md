# Annangrove (NSW)

Slug: `annangrove-nsw`

We wake to the exact image everyone in Annangrove knows: a Prado reversing ceremonially across a perfectly raked gravel circle while a bow-tied Cavoodle inspects the herbs in the 'vegan-friendly' garden bed. We say we live semi-rural and hate pretension, but we will also queue at Annangrove Bakery for a soy flat white and then sprint off in Lorna Jane leggings before loading the Prado for the nursery run at Green Acre Nursery on Annangrove Road. We talk about property growth rates between bites of sourdough like it's weather talk.

Our Saturday is ritualised: farmers market (always the same barista at the market stall, always the same almond flat white), then the nursery to procure another fiddle-leaf or succulents for the verandah, followed by soccer at the primary school where the parking war is a spectator sport—Prado vs Tesla, with marathon sidestep manoeuvres on the verge and a discreet tally of who parks closest to Old Northern Road. We finish with a low-key BBQ, rosé on ice, kids in a glorified sandpit and the hens featured in the Instagram story at 4pm.

Drama is currency. The great debate of the year: should the street-name sign have a hanging pot and fairy lights — and who gets the spot in front of it on soccer Saturdays? The "Annangrove Chat" Facebook thread peaked at 200 comments, three passive-aggressive GIFs and a motion to replace the old wooden post on the community sign with stainless steel 'for longevity.' Two neighbours treated it like a referendum and now we all know one another's opinions on galvanised metals.

Our daily choreography is precise: 6am yoga on the verandah, an Instagram story of the hens, drop-off logistics that require a GPS and a small miracle, a weekly donation to the local footy club and an activewear wardrobe that could single-handedly stock both Lorna Jane and Vuori. The Mercedes GLC sits politely in its own gravel circle like a museum exhibit next to the herb planter.

Who would HATE living here? The public-transport purist with a single-speed they call a lifestyle would rage. They measure life in tram frequencies and indie bookstores, loathe the car-centric ballet, sneer at acreage as performative and will never forgive the only late-night option being a pie shop that shuts at 8pm.

Who would LOVE it? If you traded a two-bed Paddington terrace for five acres, three hens and a cache of social capital, if you adore sunrise yoga shots and footy sidelines, if your SUV is a statement and your rosemary is organic — welcome home, we’ll save you a parking spot (as long as you bring the fairy lights).
