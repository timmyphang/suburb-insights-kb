# Kingsville (NSW)

Slug: `kingsville-nsw`

Back in the day we’d wander down Williamstown Road and feel like we’d stumbled into a postcode-sized love letter to small things done very well. The corner site at 126 Williamstown Rd used to host Willow Wine Cafe — Broadsheet loved its warehouse windows and all-day vibe, and it even stood on the spot that Fisher Bats once used for crafting cricket bats. You could almost hear the echo of willow wood and a vintage wine opener in the same breath.

We remember Nabo bringing a tidy slice of Scandinavia to our corner: smorrebrod, apple and ginger porridge and rhubarb tarts — the sort of picnic food that makes you feel mildly cultured. Olive Oil & Butter Kafe earned its name honestly, serving up family-made Greek pastries and traditional fare that made us proud to have a pastry problem.

If you wanted smoke and meat back when it was an option, Burn City Smokers had its test kitchen at 31 Vernon Street, South Kingsville — Time Out and Broadsheet called it unpretentious and properly suburban, though it’s now listed as permanently closed. Nearby South Kingsville had Dukkah and Motorinos on Vernon Street too, which gave the southern strip a proper eat-your-way-across-the-street energy.

We don’t have headline scandals here; our soap operas run on playgroup RSVPs and who’s hosting the next Kingsville Mums meet-up. Speaking of which, the Kingsville Mums blog and the South Kingsville Community Centre (shoutout on Playgroup Victoria) have been where the local gossip and kinder logistics live — wholesome, loud and wonderfully specific.

Who would hate living here? People who crave non-stop nightlife, anonymous apartment living, or those who think a community centre noticeboard is beneath them. This place values small rituals: a corner cafe that remembers your order, a RSL (Spotswood-Kingsville RSL) that’s been around since 1916, and mums arranging Scienceworks outings.

Who would love it here? Lovers of earnest local cafes, anyone after a short walk to honest wine and charcuterie in a tiny wine bar, families who want playgroup referees, and those who’d rather know their neighbours’ kids’ names than the name of the latest chain. We wear our small-town credentials with pride — and a rhubarb tart in hand.
