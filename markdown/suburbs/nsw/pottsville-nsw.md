# Pottsville (NSW)

Slug: `pottsville-nsw`

Back in the day we’d tell city visitors to swing by Coronation Avenue and peek into Shop 4/8, where Pipit used to pack ten people around a counter and serve an eight-course ode to the Northern Rivers. We still like to brag that our tiny town once hosted a chef who’d worked at Paper Daisy and even had a stint at Noma in Copenhagen — Ben Devlin, no big deal, apparently.

Mornings here are less about latte art and more about Mooball Creek: our calm little waterway for kayaks and family paddles, the sort of place Time Out called “charming, relaxed and bloody beautiful.” If you want to pretend you escaped the city, zip ten minutes inland and book a night at Heartwood (architect-designed bathtub on a deck, Burringbar Ranges out the window) and we won’t tell anyone.

Local drama, you say? We’ve got the classics. The SES once issued flood evacuation warnings when Cyclone Alfred/Albert loomed, so yes, bring gumboots and a soft spot for emergency services announcements. And when a great white took a bite out of a fishing boat, the story made national headlines — suddenly our fishermen were front-page news. It’s the kind of small-town excitement that doesn’t involve traffic congestion or hipster feuds, just adrenaline and a slightly chewed dinghy.

Who would hate it here? If you need a 24/7 nightlife quadrant, artisanal everything on every corner, or crave the anonymity of a six-million-person commute, Pottsville will frustrate you. We’re not Byron Bay — we’re the quieter cousin that Time Out said you should still consider for a summer hit list. Property’s still laughingly reasonable by coastal standards (around $1.4 million, Time Out reckoned), so don’t come expecting inner-city prices or inner-city indifference.

Who’ll love it here? Food nerds who remember Pipit’s tasting menu and the NSW Tourism Awards silver it picked up in 2024, families who want Mooball Creek on the weekend, and anyone who enjoys a town that boasts world-class produce, a cheeky local headline now and then, and sunsets that make you humble. We know every corner — and we’re not ashamed to tell you about it.
