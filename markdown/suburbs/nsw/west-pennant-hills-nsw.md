# West Pennant Hills (NSW)

Slug: `west-pennant-hills-nsw`

We know the sound of small shoes on the tile at 9:50am, because that’s prime West Pennant Hills time: the frantic five minutes before the playgroup at Kids Planet Academy. Back in the day — and by that we mean all the local write-ups we could find, which are mostly parenting blogs — Playgroup NSW listed West Pennant Hills Kids Planet running Wednesdays and Thursdays, 10:00–12:00, at Shop 1/548 Pennant Hills Road (yes, they even printed the phone: 02 8417 4075 and the email wph@kidsplanetacademy.com.au). We’ve clocked those sessions; we’ve timed the nappy changes.

Our weekend ritual used to be as wholesome as it gets: a short drive to Cumberland State Forest at 95 Castle Hill Road, where 40 hectares of native trees pretend we’re properly outdoorsy. The kids love the 350m Sensory Trail (pram-friendly, so you can fake fitness) and we love that there are toilets and a visitor centre so we don’t invent reasons to skip outings. If it’s been raining, the Richard Webb Reserve waterfall walk still gets a mention in the mums’ write-ups as the perfect “ooh look, nature!” moment for toddlers and their captive parents.

For markets and community energy, Hornsby Shire Mums reminded us the DownTown Markets in Pennant Hills run every third Sunday at Wollundry Park — great for pretending we don’t already own ten wooden puzzles. Hills District Mums also flagged Sunny Side at Coonara Avenue Shopping Centre (opened 2022) for having a fully fenced play area with a cubby house and small play kitchen — the kind of place where you can let your kid lob a toy carrot while you pretend to scroll.

There’s no blockbuster council scandal or reality-TV feud archived in our feed — mostly playgroup times, centre openings (Orchard Early Learning was “coming soon” on Castle Hill Road) and helpful directories like MumsPages listing playgroups, relocation services and family support. So the local drama is domestic: someone’s missing a snack, someone’s lost a shoe, and someone’s decided the park bench is a new career.

Who would hate living here? Nightlife hunters and people who need late-night dumplings. Who would love it? Families who measure success in nap lengths, people who like a proper bush walk without leaving the shire, and anyone who thinks a fenced cubby-house is a legitimate reason to move suburbs.
