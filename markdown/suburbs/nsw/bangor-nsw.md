# Bangor (NSW)

Slug: `bangor-nsw`

She arrives at 8:42am in Lorna Jane leggings, a Toyota HiLux taking up two spots, and a piccolo on the bench at the Bangor Village Café like it’s an accessory. We see her every morning — drop at Bangor Public School, park on Menai Road, stare meaningfully at her laptop as if emails are a competitive sport. We love that image. We live for that image.

Our village rhythm is specific. Morning runs along the bush tracks. Mid-morning flat white (almond milk, naturally) at the Village Bakery. A cavoodle named Milo who knows every route better than we do. A weekly check-in on the Bangor Community Facebook group that is equal parts classifieds and passive-aggressive horticulture forum. We coach junior soccer one arvo a week. We kayak at the reserve on lazy Sundays. We do DIY at Bunnings and then host a fundraising BBQ where the sausages are judged as personal statements.

Petty local theatre is our preferred art form. The three “premium” parking bays outside the bakery have a market value measured in stink-eye and passive-aggressive note-leaving. The PTA sausage-sizzle once spiralled into a full ballot for “official sausage sizzle supplier” because apparently the wrong sausage brand is a civic threat. The legendary Facebook thread about whose labrador dug up Mrs Kelly’s hydrangeas reappears yearly, like bad begonias.

We referee carpark wars between Subaru Outback dads with roof racks full of soccer gear and Hyundai crossover mums who insist they’re “going bush” — at least until they return in time for piccolo number two. We take granite pavers versus plain concrete at the Community Hall with the fervour some people reserve for elections. Spoiler: it’s never about the pavers. It’s about prestige, and whether your driveway makes you a person.

Our weekend is curated but never smug. Saturday: piccolo, grocery run to the big-name supermarket, kids’ soccer at the oval, afternoon kayak launch. Sunday: Bunnings, a BBQ, and a long debate about fence heights while trimming hedges. We pack lunches with kale and quinoa if we remember. We’re deeply committed to passive competition.

Who would hate living here? The under-30 urban cyclist and vinyl-collecting singleton who drinks double espressos, wears thrift-store denim and thinks “community” means late-night gigs would be bewildered. They are mystified by sausage-sizzle economics, reverence for private driveways, and compulsory school-zone bragging rights. They will be very, very confused about hedge politics.

But if you love wide driveways, a village that knows your dog’s name, weekend sport that doubles as social infrastructure, and neighbours who will borrow your ladder and your casserole dish — welcome. You’ll fit right in.
