# Tyabb (NSW)

Slug: `tyabb-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed to the gentle thump of vintage furniture being shifted about and, if it was Airshow season, the RAAF Roulettes tearing up the sky above Mornington Peninsula Airport. As Broadsheet once reminded us, Peninsula Antiques — Vladimir and Anita Tankov’s empire — put Tyabb on the map for 18th‑ and 19th‑century bits and bobs while their Collingwood outpost closed with an auction. Proper local culture, right there between the hay bales and the hangars.

A typical weekend for us used to be a drive down Mornington‑Tyabb Road: drop the kids at The Busy Peacock’s messy‑play sessions (the Hungry Peacock café was next door for feeding small people and big regrets), swing past The Big Goose Adventure Farm at 233 Mornington‑Tyabb Rd in Moorooduc for tractor rides, then, if it’s December, make a detour to North Pole Christmas Tree Farm at 249 Mornington‑Tyabb Road because we’re sentimental like that. The Tyabb Fire Brigade at 12 Mornington‑Tyabb Road would post safety reminders — even a report about a suspicious fire handed to police — so we didn’t take arson lightly.

Our local scoreboard reads like a wholesome community drama: Tyabb Football Netball Club (1475 Frankston‑Flinders Road) sharing news like Mitch Plane’s 50 Club Games, the Tyabb Cricket Club still reminding everyone it dates back to 1892, and the Airshow bringing a weird but welcome military glamour to Stuart Road. As FindGlocal showed, these things were our Saturday rituals.

Who would hate it here? If you need a 24/7 neon strip, a different latte every week, or skyline views of traffic lights, Tyabb’s not for you — we traded all that for footy raffles, messy craft glue, and the occasional flypast. We’re not a fast‑fashion suburb; we’re a place where childcare options are a considered topic (Tyabb Village Children’s Centre, Possum Skin Pedagogy and all), Camp Australia runs the OSHC at Tyabb Railway Station Primary School, and parents actually know the committee names.

Who would love it? Families, antique hunters, small‑town romantics and anyone who thinks a Sunday should include tractor rides, club sausages and a bit of sky‑noise. We’re quietly proud, slightly rural, and forever ready to roast a sausage on club day — with love.
