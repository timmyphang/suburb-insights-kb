# Brunswick East (NSW)

Slug: `brunswick-east-nsw`

We start our Saturdays the way every local cliché predicts: we hop on the tram and drift along Lygon Street like we own the one‑kilometre stretch between Brunswick Road and Blyth Street. As Broadsheet once said, our restaurants cluster here — and yes, we have opinions about which window‑service burger used to be worth the wait.

Back in the day (and by ‘back’ we mean the pages of every foodie guide), Tiny Bar, Times New Roman and Daphne were the kind of names that made your feed tilt smugly. Daphne — which Concrete Playground reviewed after it opened in November 2025 — tried very hard to be our communal front room, and To Be Frank moved into East Brunswick Village with its enormous miche and Chocolatine ambitions. Padre Coffee’s on‑site roasting at the Brunswick East Project peeled a few sleepy faces awake, and Drop Shop aimed to make choosing a bottle feel less like a quiz and more like an occasion.

We’ve always been proud of our contradictions. Time Out once crowned us the sixth coolest neighbourhood in the whole world, which felt like winning a weird, very niche lottery — and a good excuse to look down our noses while ordering pizza from 400 Gradi (yes, Johnny Di Francesco’s place that helped take home a Parma prize). Junkee reminded everyone that King Gizzard & The Lizard Wizard even immortalised strolls down Lygon Street with Sketches of Brunswick East — which, frankly, makes us sound poetic while we queue.

If you hate queues, communal courtyards or the idea that a burger window (Charrd, operating out of the Yakamoz kitchen) can muster daily lines, you will not love it here. If the word ‘gentrification’ makes you roll your eyes, we can’t blame you — Sitchu’s notes about rising stakes are the kind of local history we pretend is someone else’s problem.

Who’ll actually thrive here? People who like community gardens and kid‑friendly nature play: CERES is our backyard classroom, hosting playgroups and picnics; families and makers find reasons to stick around. Wine obsessives enjoyed Drop Shop’s convenience‑store curation, and anyone who thinks a neighbourhood should have both an experimental pizza pedigree and a non‑alcoholic bar on Weston Street will find their people.

We’re messy, loud and impossibly proud — and if you don’t like queues, go somewhere that doesn’t have a better playlist than you.
