# Heatherbrae (NSW)

Slug: `heatherbrae-nsw`

We roll in like we own the highway, because for years Heatherbrae’s the place you embarrass your GPS into admitting it’s a proper destination. Back in the day, and according to everything everyone ever wrote about us, Heatherbrae’s Pies was the reason you pulled off the Pacific Hwy at the corner of Masonite Road — an Alpine pie stewed with slow‑cooked venison in mulled wine and apple cider, no less. Time Out even noted that Heatherbrae’s snagged the Official Great Aussie Pie Competition prize for best Gluten‑Free Pie with a curry beef — proof that we can be both country and fancy at once.

Sitchu called us “the gold standard for highway pie stops,” which, yes, sounds like a backhanded compliment until you taste the beef & gravy or the chicken & leek and immediately forgive them. For a place that registers on tourism maps mostly as “pull over here,” we’ve done a very classy job of being reliably delicious.

We’re also the sort of place that gets confusingly civic: the name Heatherbrae turns up in Lawson with the Heatherbrae Play & Chat and the Heatherbrae Dads and Kids Playgroup at 1 Benang Street (phone 4759 1462), which has been the cosy, paint‑stained corner for parents and small people to meet. The directory even reminds everyone to check details before arriving — fair call, because we’re not above changing a thing or two.

Listings show some practical side to us too: HV Trucks at 38 Heather Street and C3 New Life at 22 Heather Street have our name stuck on them, if you like a used prime mover or a Pentecostal Sunday service on your suburb crawl.

Who would hate it here? If you despise honest pies, roadside nostalgia, or the idea of spending Sunday afternoon explaining to your out‑of‑town friends that yes, the “best pie in the country” was once a thing here — you’ll be bored. Who loves it? Pie pilgrims, families who value a good playgroup, and anyone whose aesthetic is equal parts highway grit and unexpectedly excellent pastry. We’re small, proud and tastefully grubby — and we like it that way.
