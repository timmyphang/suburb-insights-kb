# Leppington (NSW)

Slug: `leppington-nsw`

We’re the lot who’ll tell you the best way to start the weekend in Leppington used to be a slow roll through the Western Sydney Parklands — the stretch the guides mentioned between Blacktown and Leppington — then wobble into the local strip for something ridiculous and comforting. Back in the day, Broadsheet and Urban List made a fuss about Crumb at 5 Raby Road: Leora Bablanian and Joe Bruscino's loud little sandwich shop where the lasagne toastie (six-hour ragu, enough béchamel to float a pram) shared the menu with the Goodfella’s (whipped ricotta and mortadella on a woodfired base), house-made cookies and coffee by St Dreux Coffee Roasters. Nearby, Sitchu and listings tipped Shop 2H/5 Raby Road — Cafe Levant — for dishes from Jordan, Lebanon, Syria and Palestine, so there was usually a friendly duel between sandwich anarchists and hummus enthusiasts.

For years the transport chatter was our backyard brag and moan at once: Time Out named the T2 Leppington & Inner West line among the punctual ones (86% — not perfect, but respectable), and planning pieces discussed extending rail links. Urban List also noted new bus routes tying us to Western Sydney Airport, with route 860 running about every 30 minutes between 6am and 10pm, seven days a week — which sounds glamorous until you remember “every 30 minutes” can feel like a personality test at 6am.

Our petty local drama? We had headlines. Pedestrian TV reported five men in Leppington were fined $25,000 for gathering despite testing positive for COVID — the sort of cautionary tale that made us roll our eyes, tut, and then quietly sanitise the trolley handle.

Who would hate living here: anyone convinced life should revolve around an artisanal CBD crawl or permanent nightlife. And if you’re allergic to weekend bike trails, mid-sized sandwich revelry or a T2 that’s “mostly on time,” well — you’ll be bored.

Who’d love it: families — the local childcare was pitched as community-minded (Leppington Childcare Centre offering 76 places and STEM-focused programs) and Playgroup NSW makes it easy to find something for the littlies — and anyone who likes their suburban life equal parts parkland, hearty toasties and the comforting hum of a place that’s quietly getting on with it.
