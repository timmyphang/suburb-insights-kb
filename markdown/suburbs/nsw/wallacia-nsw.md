# Wallacia (NSW)

Slug: `wallacia-nsw`

Back in the day we liked to joke that Wallacia was two things: heirloom tomatoes and hardware-store gossip. We still are — just with a cameo from science fiction. We’ll start by saying we know where our food comes from: Sinclair’s used to bring “fine Nepean flavours” to The Log Cabin, and yes, those heirloom tomatoes were credited to Golden Fields Growers in Wallacia. Fancy? A little. Humbling? Absolutely — we’re the kind of place where riverside pubs once hired chefs who cared where the tomatoes came from.

Morning routine here used to look like: someone drops the kids at school while another runs the shopping list that might include produce from within a 50‑kilometre radius, and somewhere between Mulgoa Road and the Nepean the town wakes up. The directory pages on MumsPages and CareforKids made our priorities obvious — family support, pregnancy-and-birth services, 33 childcare services listed, seven pre-schools and ten family day cares. The CareforKids snapshot even had an average cost tagged at $146.05/day. Practical, slightly expensive, and very, very child-friendly.

Petty local drama? Oh, we had a peculiar one. For a hot minute, we were the unlikely headline: the baboon colony in Wallacia — the National Health and Medical Research Council colony managed by the RPA — became the stuff of Junkee rounds. Yes, escaped baboons, controversy, and late-night group chats trying to figure out whether to put their bikes in the shed. We like to tell the story with a grin: only in Wallacia would your local gossip swing from school fetes to escaped primates.

Who would hate living here? People who loathe children, local produce, or slightly eccentric headlines. Also anyone who needs a metropolitan 24/7 scene — we’re not that.

Who would love it? Families who appreciate a village that still has listings like Warradale Wigwam Out of School Hours Care at Wallacia Public School on Mulgoa Road, foodies who remember The Log Cabin doing sourced menus, and anyone who secretly enjoys a suburb where the topic of conversation can go from princess party entertainers on MumsPages to escaped baboons in one breath. We’re small, a bit theatrical, and unashamedly family-first — and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
