# Flemington (NSW)

Slug: `flemington-nsw`

We’ve got a weird little flex: our postcode is best known for horses and queues. We live on the bit of town where the Melbourne Cup puts on a tux and then the rest of the year the place hosts whatever festival fancied a racetrack that week.

Back in the day Broadsheet’s guide (10 May 2024) flagged staples like Cafe Wolf and Hound and Cafe Pepper, and Local Goods even graduated from pop‑up to a proper matcha outpost at 50 Pin Oak Crescent. Laksa King — the place that opened in 1998 and earned the “king of laksa” tag in more than one review — used to be the sort of queue we’d proudly endure. Its swish younger spin‑off, Laksa King Kitchen, was later flagged as permanently closed (29 January 2024), which felt like a small civic heartbreak.

Weekends here are a rotating circus. The Melbourne Showgrounds stages the Melbourne Royal Show (hello funnel cakes, classic snags and Labubu bao buns), Flemington Park has hosted Laneway Festival dates, and the Racecourse doubles as festival HQ — Good Things rolled through in 2025, Rolling Loud made its Melbourne debut there in March 2026, and even a K‑pop blowout, 안녕, MELBOURNE, was announced for March 14, 2026. One minute we’re avoiding horse manure, the next we’re navigating merch queues and stage fences like expert obstacle‑course veterans.

There’s also character off the main strip: Auntie Annie’s Hotel rose from the bones of the Quiet Man Irish Pub, the Original Escape Room has been noted as a local thing to do, and community fixtures like Crown Street Stables and the Djerring Flemington Hub have been playgroup anchors.

Who would hate it here? People who crave weekend silence, hate queues or find the idea of a racetrack being a multi‑purpose stadium existentially offensive. Also anyone allergic to festivals, matcha, or the occasional horse race soundtrack outside their window.

Who’d love it? Festival people, bargain city‑access seekers (Sitchu pointed out our decent transport links and affordability beside Travancore), cafe drafters and anyone who thinks a suburb that can be both curry‑laksa famous and festival central is a perfectly good kind of chaos. We do chaos very well.
