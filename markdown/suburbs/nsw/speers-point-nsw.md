# Speers Point (NSW)

Slug: `speers-point-nsw`

We start our weekend at the very edge of the biggest saltwater puddle anyone around here will ever admit is a lake, because why be modest? Back in the day the travel pieces called it a “lakeside escape” and pointed to Speers Point Park like it was our town square—and fair enough. As Newy with Kids reminded everyone, the Lake Macquarie Variety Playground is the sort of mega-play structure that makes grown-ups consider surrendering their dignity to a 12‑metre climbing tower and a 9‑metre spiral slide so little ones can feel superior for five minutes.

Morning routine: we stroll the esplanade toward the park, past the spots travel sites liked to name-check. Urban List once fawned over The Lakeside Retreat and even flagged Mr. Sister Coffee for anyone who wanted their caffeine and postcard view in one serve — that was the vibe the mags sold. Concrete Playground described “the coffee shop at the tip of Lake Macquarie” with whitewashed walls and a tiled counter that looked like it migrated from the Inner West and never apologised.

Weekends are market choreography. Sitchu tells us that Homegrown at Speers Point Park is technically the largest market in Lake Macquarie — first and third Sundays, 120+ stalls — while Junkee also noted the Lake Macquarie City Farmers Market rolls through Speers Point Park on Saturdays. In short: there’s a market for anyone who likes buying tiny jars and artisanal things between playground runs.

Local drama? The papers mostly pitched us as either family holidayland or a development canvas. Broadsheet once described 8 at Trinity as a pop-up on the Trinity Point development — you know, the $580‑million plan with hotel, apartments, day spa and marina — which is to say the suits have long looked at our shoreline and thought, “What if we made it shinier?”

Who would hate it here: people who crave complete anonymity, absolute silence, or gardens without the distant hum of a market playlist. Who’d love it: families that treat a playground like performance art, weekend market completists, and anyone who thinks a lakeside coffee is a perfectly legitimate life choice.

Back in the day the mags had an angle on us. We like it that way.
