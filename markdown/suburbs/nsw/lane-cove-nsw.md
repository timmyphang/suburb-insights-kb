# Lane Cove (NSW)

Slug: `lane-cove-nsw`

Back in the day we’d claim Lane Cove like you’d claim a favourite park bench — possessive but polite. Saturdays used to kick off with a half-serious queue at Barrel One for their New York‑style bagels, then a wander past Birdwood if we wanted something neat and leafy, or a detour to Kosuke Ramen on 2 Austin Street when we needed to justify sweatpants.

We learned where the best excuses lived: “I’m taking the kids to the Lane Cove Riverside Walk” meant we’d actually be loafing by the river, pretending the 10‑kilometre return route counted as cardio. The national park was our backyard — boat sheds, barbecue spots (think Carter Creek and Casuarina Point) and Discovery Parks and the old Lane Cove River Tourist Park for when we fancied pretending we were camping. Playgroup parents used to swap war stories at the Meeting House on 23A Stokes Street on Tuesdays and Thursdays 9:30–11:30, and the intergenerational sessions at St Peter’s on 3 Felton Avenue made us all feel quietly virtuous.

Our village did food small and large: Harris Farm’s big shiny store at 56–60 Burns Bay Rd even had a Gelato Messina concession and Rebel Donuts, Sugarlane had sprung up at The Canopy, Daifuku’s pillowy mochi was notorious for selling out daily, and The Alcott — formerly the Lane Cove Club — got a grown‑up revamp with a spritz bar. Garçon held its mussel‑and‑fries nights like a rite of passage. And yes, Bel & Brio’s Lane Cove kitchen used to feed the late‑night pizza and pastry machine while the Barangaroo venue shuffled on.

We were briefly famous for being sensible — Time Out even crowned us Australia’s most liveable neighbourhood in 2025 — which made us as smug as a dog with two leads. The price of a bacon and egg roll at the Lane Cove National Park Café once went viral on Reddit, because even our small scandals prefer to travel light.

People who hate trees, weekend bush picnics, or the idea that a “village” can mean both boutique groceries and a nine‑week Cameraygal Festival would not last here. But if you love river walks, decent ramen, a parade of pastry options, and the comforting hum of local playgroups, welcome home — we’ve got a bench with your name on it.
