# Kiama (NSW)

Slug: `kiama-nsw`

We’re the lot who measure weekends in spray height and sourdough supply. We wake up to the kind of sea air that makes city folk talk about “escaping” while they’re still five exits from the M5. Back in the day, Time Out was calling us one of Australia’s best small towns and crowing about our blowhole — yes, the one that Time Out said can spit water up to about 25 metres — so we’ve always had that bit of celebrity about us.

A proper morning for us used to mean a walk along the Kiama Coast Walk or the Kiama to Gerringong coastal track, a glance at Cathedral Rocks and Bombo Headland, then a polite detour past Blowhole Point and the visitor centre where Out of Town café — opened by Stan Crinis — once sat, plant-filled and smugly seaside. If we weren’t queueing for Slow Dough on 19 Bong Bong Street (as Broadsheet once recommended), someone would be bragging about Parfait Patisserie’s 16-layer croissant after it snagged top croissant honours at the National Artisan Baking Awards, as Sitchu pointed out.

We’ve been the backdrop for lists, guides and gentle tourism arm-twisting: Concrete Playground and Broadsheet sent weekenders our way for ocean-bath dips, the Blowhole Burger and jewel-green pastures; Urban List even rounded up our Airbnbs (Dales Run Jamberoo and Kinosaki Bath House got a mention). The Kiama Jazz & Blues Festival used to be the volunteer-run weekend that made our pubs feel chesty, and Changing Tides brought headline acts to the Kiama Showgrounds — yes, we do concerts, apparently.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe the sound of actual waves, find hills that aren’t manicured to green postcard standards, or resent the idea that a lighthouse constitutes cultural capital, you might struggle. We’re not for the permanently impatient: the Mariyung trains rollout was supposed to fix things between us and Sydney, but even that felt like a slow coastal saunter.

Who would love it? Anyone who wants surf beaches, rainforest pockets, a 22-kilometre walk that actually rewards you, and a town of roughly 23,000 people that still remembers its markets and harbour. We wear our blowhole like a hat — slightly ridiculous, very proud — and we’ll happily show you where to stand so you get drenched for the ‘gram.
