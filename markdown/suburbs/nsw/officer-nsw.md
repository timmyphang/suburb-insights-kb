# Officer (NSW)

Slug: `officer-nsw`

We know how it looks from the highway — a sign that says “Officer” and half the city nods politely and keeps driving. But we live here, so let us unpack the gentle absurdity.

Morning: we drop the kids at the Officer public hall, the one Playgroup Victoria actually lists on its venues page (they put it up July 5, 2024, so no, it’s not a fever dream). Liz Genez gets a namecheck on the Great Start Program pages as the Great Start Program Officer, which means someone somewhere is very organised about finger-paint logistics and community playgroups — and frankly we’re here for that kind of administration.

Weekend ritual (past-tense because nostalgia is iconic): we used to see the Sitchu guide include a Third Wave Cafe location in Officer on its birthday freebies round-up — so yes, for a time you could have celebrated turning 34 with suspiciously enthusiastic staff and the promise of freebies. Around Christmas the same Sitchu list cheerfully pointed people to Moorooduc Christmas Tree Farm — 336 Brown Road, Officer — so when the suburb smells of pine and a dad is wrestling a 6ft Fraser fir into an SUV, that’s not Melbourne suburbia, that’s us.

We’ll freely own the confusion: our postcode doubles as a police rank in half the internet headlines you’ll find if you Google “officer.” So if you don’t like being accidentally funny in a national news search bar, this place will vex you.

Who would hate living here? If you’re allergic to bub-friendly public halls, community programs, or the tiny thrill of a local event being featured on a feel-good roundup — move on. If you think suburb names should only double as ranks, you’ll probably be doing a lot of explaining at dinner parties.

Who loves it here? Families who want a proper local playgroup (Playgroup Victoria will tell you where to go), people who don’t mind a seasonal pilgrimage for a Christmas tree at 336 Brown Road, and anyone who finds comfort in small, reliable community infrastructure. We’re not flashy — we’re the place where the hall doors open on a Tuesday and someone remembers your kid’s snack allergy. That kind of ordinary is underrated.
