# Londonderry (NSW)

Slug: `londonderry-nsw`

We like to think of ourselves as the quietly smug corner of greater Sydney that nobody quite expects — partly because when you Google Londonderry you get a recipe for confusion and a playlist of emotional ballads.

Back in the day, our claim to fame was humble but very satisfying: Newlay Eggs sourced from a boutique farm near Londonderry (and another in Stroud) and, as Broadsheet noted, those eggs made it all the way to kitchens like The Grounds. So yes, we’ve been smug about our provenance long before it was a hashtag. Chefs loved Newlay’s quick turnaround; we loved telling people our eggs had a résumé.

Mornings here look the same whether anyone’s writing about us or not — sun, paddocks, the faint sense that somewhere an egg has just been judged restaurant-ready. Most of the press we attract is older, so we’ve learned to enjoy the quiet: fewer journalists, more actual life. If you’re after a current lifestyle guide, well, Google will hand you threads about the name instead.

Speaking of the name — it’s a whole thing. The internet is obsessed with Derry vs. Londonderry; Junkee even reminded everyone that the “Londonderry Air” turned up in songs like Westlife’s “You Raise Me Up” and Bette Midler’s “Wind Beneath My Wings.” We’re small, but our name carries international baggage and at least three slightly baffled message boards.

Who would hate living here? People who measure a suburb by the volume of recent web articles or by the number of places to be seen in during peak brunch hour. If your hobby is checking the latest “where to” listicles, this isn’t your content strategy.

Who’d love it? Anyone who enjoys provenance over pretence, who likes the idea that something from our paddocks once ended up on a chef’s plate, and who doesn’t mind that most of our online fame is pleasantly retro. Also fun: the search results throw up playgroup pages like Little Saints Parent & Toddler Group and even MOMS Club® threads — so if you’ve got kids, you won’t be totally lonely in the listings. We’re not loud. We’re not flashy. We’re resourceful, slightly proudly obscure, and our eggs have stories.
