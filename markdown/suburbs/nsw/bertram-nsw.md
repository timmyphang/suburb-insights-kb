# Bertram (NSW)

Slug: `bertram-nsw`

You know you’re from Bertram when our main flex is the name. We wave it around like a scratched vinyl at a backyard party: proud, a bit dusty and impossible to track down in any current listings. Back in the day the internet liked “Bertram” in a lot of different places, and that’s basically our cultural résumé.

Morning for us is not a café crawl—because there’s no shiny new guide telling us to—it's a bike ride that the Urban List still fondly remembers: push off towards Sir Bertram Stevens Drive and the Lady Carrington Drive route through Royal National Park. Ten kilometres of creeks, blue gums and pretending we’re training for something. We call it our local cardio even if the only leaderboard is who yells “last stop, ice-cream” first.

If you like origin stories, Broadsheet once did a whole elegy for Biggies at Bertram — not here, mind you, but in Adelaide — where Paul and Kay Crozier turned a stamp shop into a vinyl-only bar with hip-hop, funk, disco and soul nights. As Broadsheet noted, it gathered a devoted following and then, less glamorous, the owner couldn’t find a buyer and had to sell his house to settle debts. It’s the kind of tragicomic arc we absorb with a shrug and a beer — emblematic of what happens when you try to make anything more than quietly charming.

Urban List also used to list Wolfpack Coffee Roasters at 12 Bertram Street in Mortlake and even noted Sullo parking up at 162/1 Bertram St. Which is to say: the name pops up in other people’s suburbs and we get the crumbs of their stories.

Who would hate living here? If you need a constant feed of “what’s hot now” and a postcode that gets daily lifestyle think pieces, you’ll be restless. We’re not on anyone’s curated map lately.

Who would love it? Cyclists chasing Sir Bertram Stevens Drive vistas, music nerds who appreciate a vinyl legend even if it was elsewhere, and anyone who enjoys the gentle smugness of a suburb that’s more interesting in other people’s archives than on the council agenda. We wear our obscurity well.
