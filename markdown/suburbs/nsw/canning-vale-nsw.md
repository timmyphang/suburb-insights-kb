# Canning Vale (NSW)

Slug: `canning-vale-nsw`

Back in the day our Saturday started with a queasy, glorious ritual: a lap of Market City at 280 Bannister Road, smelling of hand‑pulled roti and noodles that didn’t break the weekly grocery budget. As Broadsheet noted, Hello Spice at the Canning Vale Markets (run by Sunlong Fresh Foods founder Mark Sun) was the sort of place where restaurant‑quality noodles met hawker prices — and Kopi Time began life as a stall there, selling roti before it wandered off to Thornlie.

We’d wobble on to Unit 11/280 — Market City Gourmet Food — and pretend those sacks of mysterious rice and jars of sauces were an identity rather than just dinner. The Holy Bagel used to supply Perth with bagels; you could pick them up from the Canning Vale bakery Tuesday–Saturday mornings, which felt like a weekday miracle for the carb‑devout.

If you needed to blow off steam in a way that didn’t involve explaining yourself to council, The Kart Centre at 27 Baile Rd was our indoor adrenaline appointment — 230 metres of bends to remind you we’re excellent at controlled chaos (Urban List told us so). Party Source Canning Vale was the place to get utterly lost in sequins and foam swords; Urban List called it a massive costume warehouse and we agree, because we’ve spent entire afternoons acquiring regrets and glitter.

We even had a spot for ramen scribbles — a food court ramen shop at T6/46‑48 Catalano Circuit was mentioned alongside Ageto Ramen and Blue Jasmine, a small promise of warm broth after a long week. Phoever and Johnny’s Burgers cropped up in the guides too — comfort food options for evenings when you wanted five kinds of pho or a burger that didn’t ask your feelings.

Yes, we briefly topped the headlines for less glamorous reasons — Pedestrian reported on an arrest in Canning Vale — but that’s a footnote. What matters is we’re practical: families here could rely on childcare expansion plans (Great Beginnings opened centres including Canning Vale), playgroups at the Community Centre, My Melody Tree’s classes at Monkey Bars (1/2 Batman Road) and the odd open day at Milestones Early Learning on Comrie Road.

Who would hate it? People who need boutiques and velvet ropes every 200 metres. Who would love it? Folks who like cheap, honest food, proper bagels, noisy kids’ music, go‑karting and a community that’s quietly getting things done. We’ll take that.
