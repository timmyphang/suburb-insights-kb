# Newport (NSW)

Slug: `newport-nsw`

We live here, so yes: we will smugly admit Newport is the kind of place where your weekend plans come with an itinerary and a dress code you didn’t know you’d agreed to.

Mornings for us start with the obvious friction — one side of the suburb tumbling down to Pittwater, the other side facing the Pacific — so you’ll catch us panting up a hill after the surf or pretending the view is our second mortgage. Newport Beach is 1.3km of proper sand: two surf breaks, an ocean pool and the Surf Lifesaving Club. Pro tip we keep repeating because people still ignore it — Time Out warned about the stormwater drain in the middle after heavy rain. You’ll know the locals by the way they sideways-step that exact spot.

Weekends are when Newport stops being a suburb and becomes a concept. The Newport (once the Newport Arms) — relaunched under Merivale and made famous enough to get Justin Hemmes a private-tour write-up — was the event horizon. It was the place with cabanas, fire pits in winter, and an upstairs Bert’s that reviewers declared “the new benchmark” for lunching like you’re on a travel brochure. Concrete Playground even clocked the place for having a vintage gymnasium with ping pong, giant magnetic Scrabble and exercise bikes that charge phones. Yes, chargers on spin bikes. We live in the future.

If you arrive too eager on a Saturday, expect a queue: Time Out once told us the pub opens at 10am and the first shuttle shows up at 12:30pm. You’ll see people who treat the waterfront like a theme park and locals who watch them like a reality show.

Who would hate it here? Anyone allergic to boats, fire pits or the faint whiff of curated seaside luxury. People who crave “normal” shopping strips probably won’t forgive us.

Who loves it? Surfers, boat owners, anyone who thinks dinner should include a view and possibly lobster pasta, and families who like being half-holiday, half-home. For years the place has traded on that holiday-feel — and honestly, we’ll keep pretending it’s just ours.
