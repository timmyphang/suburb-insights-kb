# Parkerville (NSW)

Slug: `parkerville-nsw`

We live in Parkerville, and yes — before you ask, the scrapbook we dug up is mostly old travel guides who couldn’t stop raving about our Perth‑Hills alter ego. So for accuracy’s sake: these clippings are pre‑2024 and were writing about the Parkerville down in the Perth Hills — think 6 Owen Rd, Parkerville WA 6081 — but the vibes are timeless, so roll with us.

Mornings used to begin the way the guides recommended: lace up and tackle the hills. Urban List noted there’s about 59km of track in the Perth Hills, and the stretch from Bellevue (or Swan View) up to Parkerville clocks in at about 8km — which is a nice way to feel virtuous before breakfast. By arvo, the old features told us we’d be polishing off a “Parky Parmi” at The Parkerville Tavern, the heritage country pub that was repeatedly name‑checked for its sprawling beer garden, firepit and pub grub the length of a hearty sigh.

There’s a domestic, small‑town side to the scrapbook too. We found the Parkerville Playgroup Inc — because yes, face painting, balloons and bubbles were definitely on the agenda at the Parkerville Playgroup Fun Day. Makes you proud and slightly sticky.

Petty local drama? The files are scandal‑free: no council rumbles, no celebrity kerfuffles — just forgivingly warm pubs and family days. Even the pet‑friendly angle was wholesome: pet‑friendly stays used to give drink tickets for the dog‑friendly Tavern. Our biggest feud is whether the parmi is the best in Perth; sources like Sitchu and Urban List suggested it was contenders‑level.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to walking eight kilometres, allergic to dogs getting complimentary drink tickets, or allergic to genuine country pub charm. Also, people who want a scandal in their suburb newsletter — you’ll be bored.

Who would love it: hikers, families who enjoy bubbles and face paint, pub devotees after a Parky Parmi, and anyone who likes their local listed in every romantic‑dinner and best‑parmi roundup from yesteryear. Back in the day the travel guides loved us — and frankly, we still do.
