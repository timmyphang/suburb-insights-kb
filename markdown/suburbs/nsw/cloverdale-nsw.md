# Cloverdale (NSW)

Slug: `cloverdale-nsw`

Back in the day when people tried to pin down "Cloverdale" on a map, we used to get a delightful identity crisis — and honestly, we loved it. We’re the place that turns up in Urban List with Sapore Espresso Bar at 4/275 Belmont Ave being applauded for shakes and cult hot chocolates, and Cafe 2TwentyFour at 224 Belmont Avenue being the subject of freak-shake pilgrimages (Golden Gaytime megashake, pulled pork eggs benny — what a combo). As Urban List noted, our name has been cheerfully sprinkled across Perth’s food round-ups.

Mornings in our scattered imagination start with a walk through Gallagher Park, past Cloverdale Hall, where the Community League used to run everything from pub nights to ping pong, urban poling to yoga. For years the league’s calendar read like a very polite variety show: Big Bin & River Valley Clean-Up Day, memberships that buy you discounts and eligibility for the Cloverdale Resident Folk Fest, and Tuesday walking groups that meet at 9:15am by the hall sign. There’s also the mums’ corner — Mum [i]s the Word met Thursday afternoons at Cloverdale, with Kathy and Liz teaching art and digital bits and Ann producing lunch like a sainted lunch-giver.

We don’t pretend there’s a single Cloverdale narrative. Jacaranda Community Centre’s address at 146 Epsom Avenue, Belmont and the PO Box 142 CLOVERDALE WA listings remind us our name behaves like a travelling act, popping up in community-service guides and NAIDOC or Harmony Day program listings.

The petty drama? There isn’t much recorded — which is its own drama. Our town’s scandal was being pleasantly useful: dim sum at Canton Lane got a mention for durian mochi balls, and someone somewhere decorated 159 Williamson Avenue with 30,000 lights and a life-sized Santa throne (Urban List called it a spectacle). It’s hard to scandalise that.

Who would hate living here: people who crave a single, coherent postcode identity and can’t handle being everywhere at once. Who would love it: families who like weekly walking groups, jamming sessions, Ladies of Cloverdale socials, babies-and-toddlers meetups and community halls that actually get used. For years our story has been small, noisy, slightly confused — and perfectly happy being that way.
