# St Andrews (NSW)

Slug: `st-andrews-nsw`

We’re the St Andrews that gets the wrong DMs.

Morning scroll: someone tags us in a photo of a reclaimed stable and a sun‑soaked courtyard and we’ve got to tell them gently that the St Andrews with the big, family‑friendly brewery is actually St Andrews Beach — the one on the Mornington Peninsula. Broadsheet wrote about the Freedman family turning a 37‑hectare horse‑training paddock into St Andrews Beach Brewery, Concrete Playground loved that reclaimed stable vibe, and Urban List even pins it at 160 Sandy Road, Fingal. Lovely, but not our postcode.

Back in the day the interwebs also decided “St Andrews” could mean Brighton’s heritage café next to St. Andrew’s Church — Brighton Schoolhouse sitting on the corner of Church and St Andrews Street (listed at 19 St Andrews Street) with a big courtyard and playground. Nice for brunch, but nope, still not us.

We don’t have a Tom Doak green in our backyard either — Time Out gushes about St Andrews Beach Golf Course an hour south of Melbourne — which is apparently where people who like to spend their Saturdays chasing pristine greens go. Meanwhile our Saturdays are spent explaining we’re not a golf course, a Fitzroy hotel (there is a St Andrews Hotel in Fitzroy, per Concrete Playground) or a Kent playgroup on Watling Street (yes, there’s a St Andrews Playgroup over in Dartford — Nicola Chambers runs that lot, if you must know).

Petty local drama? We’ll pass. The main beef here is identity theft by association. We get complimented for other suburbs’ coastal breweries, golf courses and brunch spots, and we take the compliments because who wouldn’t? But it’s also a bit like being famous for someone else’s highlight reel.

Who would hate living here? People who move in expecting a Peninsula Hotsprings‑adjacent lifestyle or a craft beer shrine at 160 Sandy Road. They’ll be disappointed.

Who would love it? Folks who enjoy being the quiet St Andrews in a world that keeps assuming we’re beach‑facing, brewery‑backed or heritage‑brunch famous. We’re low‑key, easily amused, and annoyingly good at politely saying “that’s actually over in Victoria.”
