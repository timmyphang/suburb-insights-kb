# South Yarra (NSW)

Slug: `south-yarra-nsw`

South Yarra — we know the soundtrack: tram bells, boutique zips, and someone rehearsing a dramatic apology outside Prahran Market. Since the write-ups we've got are all a bit nostalgic (Broadsheet, Time Out and the rest did most of the heavy lifting), this is a fond flashback, not a current-events briefing.

Mornings used to start with a loop along Toorak Road and Chapel Street — the busy bits Broadsheet once described — past Como House’s manicured lawns and the Royal Botanic Gardens where you could pretend you were fitness-adjacent on The Tan. If we were treating ourselves, we’d linger near the Como Centre or duck into a cafe the guides name‑checked: Campos was part of an old itinerary, Q Le Baker and Tivoli Road Bakery got mentions, and Concrete Playground used to round up the best spots when we were still hungry for hype.

Weekends meant the classics: Prahran Market runs (Time Out loved it), a browse through 670 Chapel and the designer boutiques Sitchu rhapsodised about, then a late‑night plan hatched somewhere around 571 Chapel St — Pedestrian even listed it among 24/7 joints. For the novelty hunters, Urban List once teased build‑your‑own ramen at Ramenova and the arrival of Julietta on Toorak Road; Concrete Playground trumpeted Token’s big opening. All great headlines, all being filed under “remember when”.

Petty local drama? Oh, we had the usual: a Junkee archive still remembers that freaky train power explosion that emptied carriages at South Yarra, and one infamous Toorak woman allegedly managed to crash into a house and drive off with shredded tyres. We’re not saying it’s glamorous, but it’s never dull.

Who’d hate living here? People who loathe queues, boutique windows, and the constant hum of curated leisure — if you need a suburb that’s surgically anonymous, this isn’t it. If you love green space next to designer shops, spas and wellness temples — Time Out and Urban List gushed about Soak Bathhouse, Saint Haven and even The Commons Health Club on Toorak Road — then we’re already halfway to popping the champagne for you.

So yes, we’re slightly polished, a little performative and endlessly fond of our own address. Come for the markets and manicured lawns; stay for the gossip that even our archived headlines can’t quite retire.
