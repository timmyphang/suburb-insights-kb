# Gosnells (NSW)

Slug: `gosnells-nsw`

Back in the day we’d reckon Saturday starts at Gosnells Primary School — not because we’re terribly academic, but because the Child and Parent Centre at 173 Hicks Street (access via Clara Street) used to have the kind of toddler chaos that doubles as community theatre. We’d drop the pram, grab a coffee from somewhere that definitely wasn’t listed in the archives, and file into Toddler Time like it was church.

Weekends used to be split between wholesome options: Sonas at 69 Wheatley Street, which announced free “Messy Mat Fun” sessions for the miniature humans, and Gosnells Leisure World, the slightly too-ambitious local palace with a 25‑metre indoor pool, learner and toddler pools, a water slide and an outdoor pool plus spa, sauna, steam room and gym. If you wanted to feel like a health influencer for ten minutes, that’s where you did it — towels optional, pride mandatory.

We’ve always had our culture too. Time Out once name-checked the Gosnells Hotel for a proper pub meal — beef brisket with mash, veg and gravy, apparently the kind of thing a grown-up would Instagram if they still used Instagram for food rather than for political arguments. And if you wanted to pretend we were a festival town, there was a Katchafire show listed once upon a time; we like to keep that memory alive as proof our postcode can host reggae.

If you’re the sort of person who dislikes dogs, kids who sing unprompted, and people who think a steam room counts as “relaxation”, you will hate living here. If you loathe small-town sincerity or think Sixty Foot Falls at Ellis Brook Valley looks better in brochures than in-situ, slide on your sensible shoes and head elsewhere.

But for anyone who loves impromptu community, dog-friendly walks that actually become adventures in winter when Sixty Foot Falls runs, and child-friendly programs that make the rest of the week survivable — we’re yours. Gosnells: where the local council brochure and the local pub both have good ideas, and the rest of us do the rest with open arms and slightly damp towels.
