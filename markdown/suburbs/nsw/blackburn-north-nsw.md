# Blackburn North (NSW)

Slug: `blackburn-north-nsw`

Back in the day we’d saunter down Katrina Street like it was our tiny Scandinavian getaway — Broadsheet even listed Little Woodpecker, noting Tanya Baharian was raised nearby and the place favoured a Nordic fit-out and menu quirks like The Golden Temple and La Bamba. We weren’t pretending to be European; we were just very committed to our aesthetics.

A typical weekend used to look like this: the kids (or our inner kids) bounced off the walls at Bounce: Blackburn North on Joseph St — Time Out had the address (22 Joseph St, 3130) and all the tripping-over-trampolines details — while the rest of us wondered if we could book the 50-minute free bounce and still call it self-care. After that we’d stretch sore limbs at the Blackburn branch of Little Mandarin Yoga & Pilates (yes, Urban List mentioned their Blackburn location) or blame our lack of upper-body strength at the local Urban Climb, which Urban List says expanded into Blackburn after Collingwood.

When hunger won, we remembered the suburban pleasures Urban List flagged — an unassuming yakiniku at 55–65 Railway Road (Ichikuro) and other hot-pot-adjacent joys that made weekday monotony feel like an event.

We do have our low moments. As Broadsheet reported back on 28 August 2020, a Yarra Valley Water advisory told us to boil our drinking water — which, in true suburban fashion, made everyone suddenly very interested in the tap and very proud of their stash of spare bottled water. Small crises, big fuss. That’s our content.

Who would hate living here? If you need CBD tram links or crave neon nightlife, this sleepy jumble of Scandinavian cafés, trampolines and climbing gyms will feel like punishment. Who would love it? Families who like nearby bounce parks, climbers and people who consider a claypot binchotan grill at 55–65 Railway Road a legitimate Friday-night plan.

We know what they wrote about Blackburn North in the guides — Broadsheet, Urban List, Time Out — and sure, a lot of it feels “back in the day.” But if you like low-key suburban theatricality, wooden bridges on river walks that head up towards Burnley, and a cafe that meticulously named its menu items, you’d have fit right in.
