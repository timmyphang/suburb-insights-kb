# Geelong West (NSW)

Slug: `geelong-west-nsw`

Back in the day we’d wander down Pakington Street like it was our runway and the rest of Geelong was politely taking notes. We know every shopfront, every market stall and which cafe used to have the photogenic fern in the window — Broadsheet even had a guide to our little strip — and yes, we remember Tulip at 9/111 Pakington Street being talked about for its locally focused menu.

Our weekend routine was ritual: poke around the New To You market at 153 Pakington St, wave at the neighbourhood mums filing into playgroups, and duck into the Geelong West Social Club for a Scandinavian-ish interior and Coffee Supreme beans (Broadsheet mentioned that one). 63 Degrees on 140 Pakington Street used to be the leafy place everyone photographed. Boss Burger Co (206 Pakington Street) was the one you forgave for the queue because the burger was worth the linger — Broadsheet had that entry too.

We’re proud of our town hall — Time Out listed Geelong West Town Hall as the centre of main-street life, where Play School Concerts once charmed a thousand small people and markets still try their best. Time Out also couldn’t stop writing about the flashy things at the waterfront — the Crowne Plaza, the Nyaal Banyul convention centre and even talk of a waterfront revamp with the Giant Sky Wheel and waterfront train nudging tourism our way. We’ll take the shiny, but don’t call us tourists.

Petty drama? The papers kept talking about revamps and rooftop pizza at Murphys (the old Argyle) and a massive beer garden at Queen of the West — Time Out loved that story — but mostly our scandals are who forgot to water the communal fern.

Who would hate it here: anyone allergic to small-town confidence, to a main street that thinks it’s a boulevard, or to being unable to walk five minutes without bumping into a boutique homewares store that started life in a shipping container (yes, Concrete Playground, we remember Frankie Say Relax).

Who would love it here: families who use the Neighbourhood House, market browsers, people who rate a good local burger, and anyone who enjoys a town that’s quietly reinventing itself while keeping the odd photogenic cafe and community toy library grants in the cupboard. We’re not posh, we’re flavorful — and we know our Pakington Street better than most.
