# Middle Swan (NSW)

Slug: `middle-swan-nsw`

We know the exact moment Middle Swan hums: a weekend morning when someone spots paddleboats on the big lake and decides, very seriously, that water recreation will fix everything. Back in the day Broadsheet and UrbanList wrote about that lake — Oakover Grounds (80 Yukich Cl) got a shiny 2014 winery makeover and, yes, you could (allegedly) hire water bikes and paddleboards for free and even catch a trout. Oakover also used to roast Fiori Coffee on-site; it was one of those places that made suburban winery-chic feel effortless.

If you’re nostalgic for family-run rituals, we’ve got Taylor’s — Taylor's Art & Coffee House/Taylors In The Valley (510 Great Northern Hwy) — occupying the original Taylor family home, doing house-made, local-ish things and hosting artists. Whistlers Chocolate Company (506 Great Northern Highway) has been here for 50 years: seven hot chocolates, including chilli and peppermint. Choose wisely — indecision is a local pastime.

There’s the quieter stuff, too. UrbanList even pointed to Middle Swan Reserve as a top launch point for a protected paddle to Lilac Hill Park, where the main soundtrack is birds and the occasional proud fisher announcing a catch. For families, the Child and Parent Centre — run in partnership with Middle Swan Primary School — used to be the kind of place parents wrote home about; the contact page lists a coordinator, Tonia Leonard, so yes, organised parenting exists here.

Petty drama? We’ll point at the receipts: Houghton Winery Cafe, where chef Herb Faust ran a seasonal menu from 2017, is now marked “Permanently Closed” on Broadsheet. The valley’s hospitality carousel spins — vintage winery vibes one year, closed signs the next. We’ve learned not to get attached to menus.

Who would hate it here: anyone who thinks a paddleboat is an affront to civilisation, or who can’t choose between seven hot chocolates without experiencing a mild existential crisis. Also, if you loathe old family homes turned into cafes, this place will annoy you.

Who’d love it: kayakers, families (the Child and Parent Centre was built for you), chocolate fiends, and anyone who likes their wine with a side of retro industrial fit-out and a lake you can actually clumsy-paddle on. We’ll be here, second coffee in hand, pretending the waterbike was our idea.
