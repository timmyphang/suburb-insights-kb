# Greenmount (NSW)

Slug: `greenmount-nsw`

Back in the day the travel pages treated us like a scenic footnote — and honestly, we’ll take it. We’re Greenmount: the polite little elbow at the very end of Gold Coast beaches where Queensland waves meet New South Wales patience.

Our morning looks the same in every guide that ever bothered to write about us: a slow saunter along the shared pathways from Coolangatta, past Rainbow Bay, up the hill by Snapper Rocks and on to Point Danger. As Urban List pointed out, those pathways make the whole thing a cinch — which is code for “you can pretend the walk was the plan, not that you’re avoiding the main drag.”

If you want spectacle, have a seat on the grassy hills of Greenmount Beach and watch dolphins and whales frolic in the sea. Urban List even reminds us the season runs long — May to November — so there’s plenty of time to practice your binocular squint. The locals (and the brochures) love bragging rights about whale spotting; we love that the postcards do our bragging for us.

We’re tidy, too. Sitchu notes the beach is sheltered by headlands and actually useful: picnic tables, toilets, barbecues and showers. Translation: bring a cooler, not a sense of urgency. For years the only errand before a beach day was popping into Bundle O’Books (116 Griffith Street, Coolangatta) to grab something to read — which, yes, people did — before wandering down to our stretch of sand.

Petty drama? The files are thin. The press mostly files us under “peaceful” and “less crowded than the main drags,” which leaves little for scandal-sheet columnists. So if you want nightclub fodder, head elsewhere; we don’t have much for you besides horizon therapy.

Who would hate living here? Someone who needs non-stop neon and a queue for the next buzzy spot. Who would love it? Anyone who wants a polite beach with real headland views, whale-season excuses, and enough barbecue infrastructure to stage a low-key family reunion. We’re understated on purpose — and extremely proud of it.
