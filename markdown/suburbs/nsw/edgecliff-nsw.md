# Edgecliff (NSW)

Slug: `edgecliff-nsw`

We know the scramble at Edgecliff Station so well we could time it like a surf forecast: one late train and suddenly everyone’s auditioning for a drama about missed meetings. Back in the day the station was the village’s heart — Junkee even remembered Danny Lim, our colourful local sign-waver, standing out the front and making weekday commutes marginally less beige (until the fine, as Junkee reported).

Mornings used to look like this: we’d wobble down New South Head Road, breathe in the faint whiff of someone’s artisanal purchase, and peer into Gourmet Life — Broadsheet’s “bunker of European culinary goods” where truffles, caviar and jewel‑coloured jams once lived under Josh Rea’s careful curation. Opposite the station Pilates Physique kept us pretending to be flexible (Urban List had it listed right across the road), while The Lab Cafe had an Edgecliff branch for anyone who wanted a caffeine fix on the way to the platform.

We’ve had our small dramas — not all charming. Broadsheet flagged the time a train fire evacuated hundreds of commuters from the corridor; nothing like an impromptu footrace to remind us how communal our commute really is. And then there are the planning whispers: Time Out wrote about the Woollahra station proposal and the talk of 10,000 new homes around Woollahra and Edgecliff — the sort of shiny promise that makes us nostalgic for the quieter corners of our postcode 2027.

Who would hate living here? If you crave a backyard the size of a football field, love easy parking or want an anonymous suburban sprawl, this punchy little hub will get under your skin — Concrete Playground even noted parking can be “a bit of a punish.” If your idea of serenity is zero foot traffic and no boutique delis, we’re probably not the vibe.

Who’d love it? Apartment people chasing eastern-suburbs proximity (Time Out called Edgecliff an entry point), parents who used to rely on Thumbelina at 25 Walker Ave and KICK Learn to Swim at Level B/100 New South Head Road, or anyone who appreciates having a boujee providore like Gourmet Life on the same strip as a neighbourhood station. We’re small, a little precious, and embarrassingly proud of it — and yes, we’ll keep waving from the platform.
