# Mona Vale (NSW)

Slug: `mona-vale-nsw`

We live where the surf meets the golf course and you can have ramen and a golf simulator within a ten‑minute walk — only in Mona Vale.

Morning looks like this: we walk from the Village Park market stalls (yes, the Mona Vale Market really is a thing) past Flying Fox Park, drop the kids at Mona Vale Fun Time in the Memorial Hall (Tuesdays, 10:30am — bring stickers), then head to the rockpool off Surfview Road for a quick dip in the 30‑metre pool. By noon someone’s already softening up for Suki’s bottomless lunch (Time Out says Saturdays — we’re not arguing) or grabbing something sensible from Bare Wholefoods (plant‑protein smoothie bowl, house‑baked fruit loaf — Time Out’s notes, not ours).

Evenings are where we quietly flex coastal versatility: The Mona Social has set up camp on the Mona Vale Golf Club grounds with those sweeping ocean views and a bar that screams country‑club‑by‑the‑beach. If you want pool tables, a golf simulator and food that remembers it’s not actually a bunker, that’s your spot (Time Out/Urban List tells us so). For the quieter, booze‑and‑noodles crowd, Broadsheet once put Menya Monaka on the map — a family ramen/izakaya project that nudged Mona Vale into night‑time dining. That one’s part of our recent past; we nod respectfully.

Petty local drama? We don’t have the Kardashians, but we have arcane arguments about which terrace is the proper “view” — Cook Terrace at the Mona Vale Surf Life Saving Club vs. the golf‑course lookout. Also: who gets the last bag of artisan eggs at the market. Fierce, highly local politics.

Who would hate it here: people who detest bottomless lunches, community playgroups, and the idea that a brand new recovery space (The Resilience Circle opened in November 2024 at number 1761 on Pittwater Road) might actually be more interesting than your curated row of succulents from The Pot Warehouse on Harkeith Street.

Who would love it: families (Running Stars on Sunday mornings at Winnererremy Bay), surfers and swimmers who know the rockpool, food nerds chasing Suki or Bare Wholefoods, and anyone who thinks “village” with ocean views is the perfect kind of pretence. We’ll bring the market tote; you bring the sunscreen.
