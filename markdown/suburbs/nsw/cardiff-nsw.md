# Cardiff (NSW)

Slug: `cardiff-nsw`

We stand on the platform at Cardiff train station like it's our town square, watching the morning commute perform their tiny rituals — backpacks, earbuds, the person who always reads the same page of a book. Back in the day Sitchu listings sold this scene to the internet with the kind of optimism only found in real-estate copy: “central location close to Cardiff train station and the main shopping district,” they wrote, and even put an address on it — 3‑91 Macquarie Road — as if that made homeownership a personality trait.

Our weekend ritual used to be equally well-captioned by property photos: open-plan living, a contemporary kitchen, “spacious” rooms (which is code for “you can park a futon and a regret in here”). Macquarie Hills — apparently our tasteful suburb cousin — got a write-up as the middle child “halfway between the café strips and boutique shops of Warners Bay and the lively township of Cardiff.” We like that: we’re both the lively township and the person complaining about the lively township.

There’s no juicy council scandal in the files we dug up, no viral café feud, no barista horoscope to roast. The local narrative, as far as the archives go, is polite and useful: listings, location, lifestyle. If that sounds boring, that’s the point. Our drama is subtle. We prefer to be quietly here and let the estate agents make the metaphors.

Who would hate living here? People who need their postcode to scream “I made it.” If you want a headline, a mural, or a guaranteed Saturday brunch queue that doubles as a networking event, go elsewhere. We’re not trying to be famous; we’re trying to be functional — train station, shopping, a kitchen large enough for both a casserole and an existential crisis.

Who would love it here? Commuters who measure success in platform-to-desk times, families who appreciate a main shopping strip that remembers you, and anyone who secretly enjoys being the tidy, underrated option in Newcastle’s suburbs. For years the local listings on Sitchu promised the particulars — and honestly, those particulars have been pretty good to us.
