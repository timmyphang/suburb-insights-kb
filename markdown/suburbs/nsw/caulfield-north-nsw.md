# Caulfield North (NSW)

Slug: `caulfield-north-nsw`

We know the ritual: weekend, we shuffle down Hawthorn Road like it’s our civic duty — past the house that looks like an Edwardian set piece, past Thinkers and Makers at 126–128 Hawthorn Rd (because someone always needs a gift that looks like it came from a well-curated curiosity box). Time Out even notes Labassa Mansion opens for tours “typically on the third Sunday of each month,” so there’s always someone in a cardigan pretending they’ve discovered history.

Back in the day Broadsheet told us Mother turned a former milk bar into a cosy spot that felt like your mum’s kitchen, and Baker Bleu earned cult-bakery status for its sourdough and almond croissants (Concrete Playground and Urban List made sure we all queued like good citizens). We’ll admit we miss the simple pleasure of lining up for a loaf; nostalgia smells suspiciously like warm bread.

These days the newsprint has a new heartbeat: Ruzia’s Wine got Time Out’s nod on 29 March 2026 — romantic, Eastern‑European plates, pét‑nats and Polish meatballs — which explains why our dinner conversations now include the words “gravlax” and “pét‑nat” in the same breath. Newer-ish names on the local roll call include LENNY’S on Inkerman Road (findglocal posts in 2026) and OKAMI’s all‑you‑can‑eat spread over on Kooyong Road (listing crawled in 2025). The Potsticker, Frank and Ginger and the Tuck Shop Take Away have all been part of our corner‑store lore — Concrete Playground and Urban List did the rounds, so if you’re craving nostalgia served with triple‑cooked chips, you’ve been warned.

Petty drama? The papers mostly argued about whose almond croissant caused the queue collapse — high culture, low stakes. We’ll happily swap scandal for a well‑made sourdough and a big slide afternoon at Caulfield Park (Time Out points out that slide like it’s a local landmark).

Who would hate living here: people allergic to carbs, quiet streets, and the idea of eating continental and Polish plates in the same week. Who would love it: anyone who likes impeccably fermented bread, retro ice‑creamery vibes, and a neighbourhood that quietly thinks it’s the city’s best secret. We do — and we’ll defend our corner with a loaf in one hand and a pét‑nat in the other.
