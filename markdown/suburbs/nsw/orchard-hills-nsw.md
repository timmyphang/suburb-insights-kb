# Orchard Hills (NSW)

Slug: `orchard-hills-nsw`

Back in the day we’d pile the car with rug-clad toddlers and head to Mamre Road like it was some kind of pilgrimage. Orchard Hills Supported Playgroup was a free Thursday ritual — held at Mamre House and Farm, 181 Mamre Road — where a qualified Early Childhood Facilitator ran activities and story time under the PEEP Learning Together framework. We pretended we weren’t comparing naps while the kids actually learned how to overturn a sippy cup with precision.

Weekends were less smug brunch and more pick-your-own chaos. Hills District Mums sent us down Old Northern Road past Glenorie, right onto Canoelands Road — about 3.5km down on the left — for Canoelands Orchard: a little shop, play equipment for tiny hooligans and a big grassed picnic area. We came back sticky, sunburnt and mysteriously proud of a basket of fruit that would last approximately until Tuesday.

If you judge Orchard Hills by headlines, we’ve also been accidentally famous as a dot on a very expensive map. Concrete Playground wrote about the Western Sydney Airport metro — a 23-kilometre line planned to link the airport to Sydney via St Marys, Orchard Hills, Luddenham and the new city of Bradfield — and how it’s apparently stuck in a $12 billion limbo. Sydney Metro warned the opening date hinged on negotiations ongoing since 2024 and that costs could balloon by another $2.2 billion. Fancy signage, delayed trains and existential questions about progress: classic suburban suspense.

Who would hate living here? People who need an express metro to the airport yesterday, or anyone who thinks a playgroup that’s actually free is suspicious. Also, anyone allergic to being within polite negotiating distance of billion-dollar infrastructure drama.

Who would love it? Families who value Mamre House’s tell-it-like-it-is playgroup, people who rate a sticky-fingered Saturday at Canoelands Orchard above rooftop glamour, and anyone who likes their neighbourhood with a side of bemused infrastructure soap opera. For years we’ve been quietly living the Orchard Hills life — a little rural, a little domestic, and absolutely ready to roast anyone who calls it “just a dot on the map.”
