# Campsie (NSW)

Slug: `campsie-nsw`

Back in the day we measured our weekends in scrolls and skewers. We’d start at Foli — that little warehouse bakery on the industrial strip — for the chunky cinnamon scrolls and shio pan that Broadsheet made us obsess over. Then, because we are nothing if not gluttonous, we’d drift down Beamish Street into full hawker-mode: Albee’s Kitchen’s rendang and laksa, the endless menu at Albee’s that Time Out loved, char kway teow that spills onto the footpath, and the jianbing from Tianjin Bun Shop for breakfast if we were trying to feel cultured.

For those evenings when we wanted to audition as messy-eaters, Smoky Cravings’ Filipino barbecue and Se Jong BBQ Buffet (8 London Street, yes) provided that gloriously noisy banquet energy. No.1 BBQ House drew queues the length of the strip; we’d stand in them like it was a spectator sport and feel smug when our patience was rewarded.

Campsie wasn’t all food porn. For the families among us there were the staples — MumsPages listings for kids’ sport, Le Ray Gymnastics, Sydney Lions Soccer Academy and GKR Karate — a suburban playlist of after-school activities that kept the local economy humming. We also had the seasonal theatre of the Campsie Lantern Festival at Anzac Mall and the Campsie Street Festival that once closed Beamish Street for a weekend of roaming performers and too much food. There was even talk — Urban List remembered — of a MetroWay linking our hospo-heavy strip to the rest of southwest Sydney. Big plans, big appetites.

Who would hate living here? Anyone allergic to queues, aromatic spice or the idea that a meal is a communal, slightly chaotic event. If you need your dining room to be Scandinavian-silent, Campsie will make you twitch.

Who would love it? Food-obsessed families, people who like their dinner loud and their pastries unpretentious, and anyone who thinks a bakery on an industrial strip is peak charm. We’ll keep queuing — for the scrolls, the skewers, and the feeling that here, someone always knows where to eat next.
