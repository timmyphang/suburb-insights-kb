# Freemans Reach (NSW)

Slug: `freemans-reach-nsw`

We live here, so yes, we know where everything smells faintly of hay and ironbark: back in the day the online listings told us Sciberras Produce and Stockfeeds sat at 364 Kurmond Road, promising everything from pet basics to enough hay to keep a small horsey revolution fed across the Hawkesbury. Nearby, Bazza’s Firewood used to mill its own Vintage Ironbark at 659 Terrace Road and flog it to the Blue Mountains and our bit of the Hawkesbury — because nothing says domestic bliss like perfectly seasoned hardwood and a dozey winter ember.

Our weekend starts slow: kids off to Community Kids Freemans Reach on 4 Hibberts Lane — the centre that catered from six weeks up through preschool, with outdoor play and an Individual Learning Plan, according to the old centre notes — while the rest of us attempt to remember why we moved somewhere that needs a plan for feeding livestock as much as it needs a plan for school drop-off. If there was a village parade, it would probably pass McQuade Oval, where the Freemans Reach Cricket Club used to wrap seasons with junior and senior chatter, photos and the kind of community updates that make you proud even when your batting average does not.

We also owe a wink to the Freemans Reach Rural Fire Brigade at 361 Kurmond Road — formed in 1951, part of the Hawkesbury District — the volunteers who get honoured in the community listings and remind us that local effort isn’t a hashtag, it’s a history. As the local directories noted, these places stitched our days together; they’re the institutions you find in civic lists, not in glossy travel blurbs.

Who would hate living here? People who want cafés on every corner, footpaths lit like a runway, and instant 24/7 entertainment. We’re not that kind of fluff. Who would love it? Families who value strong childcare options, people who need proper stockfeed and a reliable pile of firewood, cricket parents who know McQuade Oval by heart, and anyone who respects volunteer brigades that have been keeping us safe since 1951.

For years the local listings told the story. We’re proud of that quiet, useful, slightly woody life — even if our postcode sounds like it should come with a pair of wellies and a sensible hat.
