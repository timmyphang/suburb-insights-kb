# Montrose (NSW)

Slug: `montrose-nsw`

We’re the lot who know every crack in the footpath around Montrose House, so let’s start with the obvious: Montrose, NSW isn’t a postcode — it’s a heritage-listed manor and the restaurant that used to live inside it. As Urban List put it, Antica Dining was “nestled inside the heritage-listed Montrose House on Miller Street, a short walk from Victoria Cross Station.” Cozy, classically North Sydney, and quietly smug about its chandeliers.

A weekend here always felt like a costume drama with good lighting. We’d stroll past the Montrose House gates on Miller Street, breathe in the smell of something Italian someone was cooking up inside (that was Antica Dining in the old write-ups), and then hop to Victoria Cross Station like responsible urban people who also appreciate parquet floors. Back in the day, if you wanted to feel like you’d wandered into a romantic manor without leaving the city, our little patch did that for you. As Urban List noted, Montrose House even gets name‑checked in accommodation and wedding roundups — yes, they like our house that much.

There’s no juicy council scandal in the clips we’ve got — no furious residents, no late-night neon battles, just a very polite, very heritage-rich corner of North Sydney that prefers its drama on the dining menu. If you were hoping for soap-opera-level petty local fights, the archive is disappointingly civil. We’ll take it.

Who would hate living here? People who crave industrial-chic and 24-hour bangs; those who think heritage is a verb. If your dream is to hear growls from a nightclub at 3am, Montrose’s timber floors and period cornices will bore you into suburbia-soberness.

Who’d love it? Anyone who wants to feel like they’re stepping into a story every time they cross Miller Street — lovers of old houses, people who appreciate a tasteful chandelier, and couples on the hunt for a romantic backdrop (we hear the house gets name‑checked in venue lists). We’re proud custodians of a crisply historic little scene — polite, perched, and just a short walk from Victoria Cross Station.
