# Mernda (NSW)

Slug: `mernda-nsw`

We can still remember the weekend the train finally made it to town — the Mernda Rail Extension that Concrete Playground wrote about, opening six months early in 2018. Suddenly we had three new excuses to leave the cul-de-sac: Mernda, Hawkstowe and Middle Gorge stations, all linked by that shiny new shared pedestrian and bike path that made the local dog-walkers look faintly athletic.

Back in the day our Saturdays used to look like this: a toddler artfully smearing paint at One Early Education Mernda, a tired parent corralling three small people into the hall at Mernda Community House, and a snack-laden stroller convoy decanting into Mernda Villages Community Centre. Playgroup Victoria’s listings read like a hit parade of community goodwill — Mernda Village Community Activity Centre even hosted a Maternal Child Health Nurse and food outreach programs in the old write-ups. For years the local life was literally parent-and-pram structured, and we liked it that way.

Our petty local drama? Public transport is our soap opera. Time Out once told everyone the Mernda line was among the best-performing, with delays under six percent — which made us feel smug at family BBQs. Then, in another chapter of the same show, Time Out also ran a piece about how parts of Melbourne’s lines were replaced by buses in April, and yes, the Mernda line featured in that particular hellscape episode. Pride and chaos in under a week — classic suburbia.

Who wouldn’t love living here? If you’re into fitness groups and morning walks, ActiveLocals’ Mernda listings show we’ve always had the lot — walking groups, yoga, dancing, you name it. If you commute to the city, Sitchu pointed out that the Mernda train gives you a direct line — historically true and the reason we can pretend we’re still doing a commute rather than school drop-off shuttle duty.

Who would hate it? If you need round-the-clock neon nightlife or zero prams on the footpath, Mernda will test you. But if you want a place that proudly wears its community centres, rail stations and shared paths on its sleeve, come for a weekend. We’ll show you where the kids used to make the best glitter dinosaurs.
