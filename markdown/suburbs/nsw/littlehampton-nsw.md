# Littlehampton (NSW)

Slug: `littlehampton-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean every search anyone outside our postcode ever bothers to do — Littlehampton mostly shows up as a seaside postcard that isn’t actually us. We’re the Littlehampton, NSW crew who have to explain this at least once a week while pretending we haven’t memorised every wrong photo Google throws at us.

Mornings for us? Picture the usual: kids in wetsuits, soggy picnic blankets and the old-fashioned arcades people keep mentioning. Mumsnet called it a "pretty little town" with a beach, seaside attractions and arcades, and yes, we’ll cop that — we like our coast and we like our stupid seaside pleasures. Other posts on the forum also warned it can feel "rundown" and have an older population; fair — we’ve accepted our status as charmingly creaky rather than aggressively shiny.

The petty drama everyone tags us with? Not local council fights, apparently, but a 1920s scandal across the pond. Concrete Playground and Time Out keep reminding the internet about the "Littlehampton libels" — the anonymous obscene letters that inspired the film Wicked Little Letters. Fun fact: Time Out says the movie didn’t even film in Littlehampton itself but used Arundel and Worthing to stand in for us. So we get to be notorious without the production budget. Honestly, it’s peak modesty.

If you’d hate living here, you’re someone who needs a slick cafe strip or a perpetual whirl of new pop-ups. Folks on Mumsnet rank local seaside towns by desirability and warn about particular estates and schools; if you’re after trendiness and uninterrupted hype, you’ll be bored.

Who’ll love it? Families who "pop down after school for a dip and a picnic" (cheers, Mumsnet), people who don't mind a bit of seaside nostalgia, and anyone content to be mistaken for a British movie set as long as the beach is good. Also, if you Google us, prepare for Wick Village Hall and a Roundabout Playgroup in Wick, Littlehampton (that’s the UK one; yes, that will happen). We are small, a little stubborn and quietly proud — and we’ll keep reminding people that being confused with someone else’s seaside drama is our best-kept compliment.
