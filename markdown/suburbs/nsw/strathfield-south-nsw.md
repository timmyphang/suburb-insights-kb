# Strathfield South (NSW)

Slug: `strathfield-south-nsw`

Back in the day our Strathfield South Saturday began like a low-key food crawl that accidentally turned into a neighbourhood reunion.

We’d wander past the Crossways Hotel at 482 Liverpool Rd — remember when Time Out wrote about Bonnie’s opening there? — and argue whether pub-Italian classics counted as dinner or a mood. If we were feeling brave we’d follow Broadsheet’s trail: hit Ho Jiak for Penang-style char kway teow and Hainanese chicken rice, or queue for Saigon Things’ broken rice and crab tapioca noodle soup (Broadsheet did call it an instant hit). There was even that weird flex told by Broadsheet about Osawa Enterprises — premium Japanese wagyu sold from a display fridge in a Strathfield garage, trading only on Saturdays. We’re still not sure if that was shopping or performance art.

Our kids — or the kids we borrowed for the day — would beg for Strathfield Superbowl, which Inner West Mums helpfully listed as a Discover-voucher-friendly outing. The local school list in the same blog name-checked Strathfield South High School, so yes, the block parties tended to include at least one teacher and a terrifyingly enthusiastic parent with a clipboard.

Petty local drama? We had a moment on the national calendar: Time Out once listed Strathfield South among ‘areas of concern’ during tightened lockdown rules. It felt like being in the group chat that gets muted and then blamed for the group photo.

Who would hate living here? If you need rooftop cocktail lounges, artisanal everything or a postcode that never gets mentioned in family blogs, you’ll find Strathfield South a patient, slightly baffling mismatch. If broken rice, dosa from Janani, homestyle Korean soups at Hansang or the idea of buying wagyu from a garage fridge don’t thrill you, this isn’t your vibe.

Who would love it? Families who want parks, cheap house possibilities (Sitchu had that 255 Homebush Road 448sqm listing), people who appreciate a good bowling alley afternoon, and anyone who likes their weekends mixed with surprising food finds and local history — including South Street’s yearly Christmas-light ambitions, which Inner West Mums once celebrated. We know this place’s small pleasures, and we’ll defend them with a bowl of crab noodle soup and a bemused shrug.
