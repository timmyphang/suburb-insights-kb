# Kellyville (NSW)

Slug: `kellyville-nsw`

Back in the day we’d meet at Bernie Mullane Reserve and pretend we were outdoorsy — while the kids actually did the hard work at Livvi’s Place, that glorious, shaded water-play wonderland the city papers once named among Sydney’s best playgrounds. We know every tunnel, trampoline and musical play-walk there because someone in our crew had to test every slide.

A typical weekend used to look like this: drop-off at Rutherford Ave Reserve for the learn-to-ride track, a detour past Kellyville Grove to feel like we’d achieved ‘modern convenience’, then a slow pilgrimage to The Local Trade (Shop 34, Belleville Village, 90 Wrights Road) which Time Out once listed as our modest little Turkish-inspired breakfast haunt. On rare, grown-up nights we’d remember the Ettamogah Hotel on Windsor Road — the cartoon-pub staple that’s been part of the Hills megapub strip since 2003 — and feel very cosmopolitan for knowing where the wood-fired pizzas once were.

We’ve seen property ads that read like an inventory for your life choices: Sitchu listings promising room for trucks, boats, even caravan storage on Windsor Road — because nothing says Kellyville like “space for your caravan.” Apartments on Thorogood Boulevard in the Grandfield development were touted as handy, close to Kellyville Station and schools; there was talk of new homes near the Carriageworks site slated to include a chunk of social housing. The Sydney Metro map used to include Kellyville as one of its northern stops, which gave commuters something to brag about at the school gate.

Petty local drama? We don’t invent it — our papers gave us practicalities instead: playgroups like MyTime Kellyville and Play Strong PlayVan supporting parents, and listings of playground openings that read like community pride notes rather than gossip columns. Which, honestly, suits us.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to slides, short school runs, or having too many playground options named in local blogs. Who would love it: families who prioritised parks, inclusive play spaces and being close to Kellyville Primary and High School, plus anyone who secretly liked the idea of room for a boat on Windsor Road. We’re Kellyville — not flashy, but reliably useful, and proudly suburban.
