# Moorabbin (NSW)

Slug: `moorabbin-nsw`

Back in the day we wandered into Moorabbin and did a double-take: the old Phillip Morris cigarette factory had shrugged off its soot and tried on a personality — Morris Moor, all neon and shared tables. We remember queuing for Tuckshop by Comma’s OTT bagels (Broadsheet did call them “over‑the‑top”) and pretending the hanger steak with potato crisps was health food. Comma Food & Wine felt like the place you’d bring someone you wanted to impress, back when we still used that strategy.

Weekends here used to run on a precise rhythm: queue, eat, then wander — sometimes ending up at Big Pig Little Pig on Ebden Street for brisket that refused to be subtle (Urban List gave it a thumbs-up). Other days the Junction would flip into neon-city for Moorabbin Junction After Dark — UV face‑painting, a silent disco and enough glow to make you suspicious you’d accidentally time‑traveled to 1989 (Time Out and Concrete Playground ran the pics).

We’ll admit Moorabbin tried hard to be everything at once. Funlab brought Holey Moley and Archie Brothers Cirque Electriq into the Morris Moor mix and Time Out basically declared mini‑golf a new civic right. Stomping Ground signed a ten‑year lease and promised a family‑friendly beer hall in the old factory — classy move, if you like your local history served with a pint.

Who would hate living here? If you despise neon, mini‑golf crowds, massive Vinnies op‑shop hauls (Time Out says ours is the biggest in Victoria) or the idea of a brewery in a converted cigarette factory, Moorabbin will grate on you like gravel in your shoe. If you can’t handle kids’ birthday chaos, you’ll also be vexed — Up Unlimited, Sniper’s Den and playgroups at St Catherine’s used to keep the calendar proud and loud.

Who would love it? People who like choices: food obsessives who fondly remember Comma and Toast It, bargain hunters who revered Vinnies on Wickham Rd, parents who booked Risky Kids or Up Unlimited for ankle‑biters, and anyone who appreciates a grimy bit of industrial soul getting a glow‑up. We’re proud of our mix of neon, smokehouses and community chaos — it’s not trying to be pretty, it’s trying to be interesting.
