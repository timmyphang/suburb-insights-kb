# Hillman (NSW)

Slug: `hillman-nsw`

Back in the day — or at least in the only press we’ve got — Hillman felt like that quiet cousin who turns up in someone else’s yearbook. We know every corner of the place by instinct: which footpath floods first, where the cat that thinks it owns the suburb naps, how to get to the mailbox without being seen. The internet? Not so much. Our dossier is mostly Urban List telling other people where to eat in Tokyo and Melbourne CBD, and Time Out New York quoting a Beth Hillman at the 9/11 Memorial & Museum. That’s it. That’s our celebrity.

Our morning routine is pure local content: we make the tea, sit on the porch and scan the headlines for any sign we've been mentioned — and find a Netflix crime-show roundup instead. If a listicle about kakigori or Melbourne’s buzzy CBD restaurants thinks to drop our name, we’ll throw a parade. Until then, we remain deliciously undercooked.

There’s no juicy petty drama in the files — which, believe us, is both a blessing and a disappointment. The archival excitement here is: did Time Out New York quote Beth Hillman this week, or was it Matt Hillman in a Knicks piece? Either way, our biggest claim to fame is existing in the margins of other cities’ culture pages. Urban List can keep their Tokyo tonkatsu; we’ll keep our quieter claim to fame.

Who would hate living here? If you need to be on the 2026 Hit List for the Best Restaurants in Melbourne CBD, or you measure a suburb’s worth by how often it’s on an international listserv, Hillman will feel like punishment. If your life requires relentless hype, curated feeds and brunch features, go elsewhere — you’ll be miserable and also annoy everyone.

Who would love it? People who prefer being out of the algorithm, who enjoy knowing the local bus timetable by memory, who find comfort in a place that doesn’t need a headlining review to be real. We’re low-key, a little proud of our anonymity, and happy to let the rest of the world argue about tacos while we get on with living here.
