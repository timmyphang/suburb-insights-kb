# Belmont (NSW)

Slug: `belmont-nsw`

We stand on the lakefront like we own the ripple — which, to be fair, we do until someone brings a kayak — watching Lake Macquarie do its quietly spectacular thing. Back in the day Concrete Playground wrote about "this charming Belmont cafe" right across from the water, praising short-order brunch plates, "loaded toasties like The Vego" and "excellent coffee." We still use that review as a humblebrag when people ask what we actually do for fun.

Morning routine here is simple: ferry our dogged optimism down to the foreshore, argue about which bench has the best view, then shuffle over to that unnamed cafe the guide liked. We won't pretend it's a foodie revolution — we're more toasted-sandwich-and-soul than degustation — but the Vego lives on in local legend and in whatever dreams the toastie-industrial complex has left for us.

There isn't much petty public drama in the clipped dataset we were handed — no council scandals, no headline-grabbing redevelopments — so our drama is domestic and very relatable: whether to take the windy track or the flat path, whether to sit lakeside or pretend you don't love the ducks. Concrete Playground's few words about us capture the vibe: small, serviceable, and stubbornly lakeside.

Who would hate living here? People who need rooftop infinity pools, neon nightlife or an argument about whose restaurant has the best truffle oil. If your idea of excitement is a 3am open mic and a tram strike, Belmont will bore you into a calmer person.

Who would love it here? Anyone who finds joy in a real view of water, a reliable short-order brunch, and a neighbourhood that ages like a comfy sweater rather than a flashy influencer feed. We are quietly proud — the sort of place guidebooks drop into a paragraph, then go back to the city to chase trendier things. That suits us just fine. Come for The Vego myth; stay because the lake will make you put your phone away, if only for a minute.
