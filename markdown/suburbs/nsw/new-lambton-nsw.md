# New Lambton (NSW)

Slug: `new-lambton-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed, shove the kids into swimmers and call it a cultural tour: Lambton Pool for the tipping bucket and water slides, a detour through Lambton Park to point out the rotunda like it’s some kind of local Louvre, then a quiet hour at Blackbutt Reserve’s Richley playground while someone argues about climbing frames like it’s parliament.

For years the local paper — The Local — has been the thing that ties us together, landing in more than 11,000 letterboxes and carrying our triumphs and tiffs. It’s the paper that told us Newcastle Basketball had big plans for a new $30 million stadium at Turton Road, and later printed the fallout: 658 submissions in the planning process, 484 opposed and 174 in favour. We still argue about that one at barbecues, which is our version of town hall meetings.

Back when food critics had opinions we couldn’t afford, Time Out called Alfie’s Italian + Wine Bar “a neighbourhood gem” and even The Urban List lumped New Lambton in with Hamilton and Wickham as somewhere the city’s hospitality scene was spilling into. Concrete Playground once wrote up Crumb in Lambton as artisanal pastry heaven. Nostalgia suits us; we like remembering when reviewers made us feel slightly metropolitan.

If you hate parks, pools, community fetes and ANZAC Day services on the Lambton Park lawns, you’ll hate living here. If you can’t handle the idea of a quiet suburb being interrupted by a stadium debate or the gentle creep of “growth” — the kind Time Out once suggested would ripple out from nearby suburbs — steer yourself elsewhere.

But if you’re a family who likes accessible green space, someone working at the nearby John Hunter Hospital, or a person who appreciates a local paper that tells you when the playgroup at 48 Regent Street meets on Wednesdays, you’ll fit right in. We’re small‑town with a touch of hipster press envy and a massive soft spot for our parks and pools. Come for the playgrounds, stay for the debates about basketball courts — and bring a folding chair for the next public submission meeting.
