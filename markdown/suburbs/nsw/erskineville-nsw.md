# Erskineville (NSW)

Slug: `erskineville-nsw`

We know the Erko like the back of our hand — the corner where Erskineville Road does its best impersonation of a high‑street, and the pubs politely elbow each other for elbow room. Back in the day Broadsheet said we set a slightly slower pace, and yeah, we’ve always liked being the cosy cousin of Newtown and Alexandria who still remembers the name of the butcher.

Mornings used to look the same: a stroll past Erskineville Oval or down to Sydney Park, a nod to the Erskineville Farmers’ Market on a weekend, and the inevitable debate about where to brunch — Café Shenkin, Erskine Villa or the places Broadsheet and Time Out used to list in their guides. We’d clock the heritage terraces, converted factories and wonder which of the old pubs would have the better story that night.

Drama? We’ve had some. Pedestrian TV reported The Imperial was once shut for 72 hours over Liquor Act breaches — yes, our famous Imperial Hotel made headlines — and there was that probe into offensive graffiti that even mentioned a sign on Erskineville Road. Concrete Playground remembers The Imperial’s flamboyant relaunch (drag shows, DJs, rooftop bar, Priscilla’s and UP) and Pizza Bros popping up on the rooftop, while The Erko got a welcome facelift in 2023. We’re small, but we’re theatrically small.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who thinks parking is a personality trait — Sitchu warned street parking can be challenging — and anyone who prefers being invisible. We sit cheek‑by‑jowl with Newtown; we were never going to be the quiet suburb your in‑laws imagined. If you can’t handle a rooftop pizza or a late‑night DJ set leaking into your dreams, this isn’t your pace.

Who would love it? Students, young families and anyone who likes pubs with stories — The Swanson, The Kurrajong, the Erko and the Imperial have all been named in the write‑ups — and people who treasure a place that reads like a village inside the city. For years the guides — Broadsheet, Time Out, Concrete Playground — have sung our praises. We’re the little suburb that refuses to be polite about being interesting. Welcome (but don’t expect to find a parking spot).
