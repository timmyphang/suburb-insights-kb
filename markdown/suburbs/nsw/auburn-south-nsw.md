# Auburn South (NSW)

Slug: `auburn-south-nsw`

We’re the ones who time our weekends by when the pink rain falls at the Auburn Botanic Gardens — remember when Time Out was all over the Sydney Cherry Blossom Festival there? Back in the day, you could plan a picnic around petals, not parking spots, and feel properly cultured before lunch.

Our morning routine used to be refreshingly simple: queue at Gaziantep Sweets AUBURN for baklava (Urban List called it a goldmine; we concur), then wander the strip where Darband once reopened after twenty years and got everyone pretending they’d always loved Persian food. If nostalgia’s your spice, Khaybar Restaurant was the sort-of-neighbourhood gem Urban List flagged for putting Afghan cooking on the city’s map — food with a community story, not just Instagram angles.

We’ve also had our thrift-store glow-up: Savers Auburn opened and Time Out wrote about the $10 bargains — yes, that place filled the exact wardrobe gaps we didn’t know we had. And for parents crunching school-fever spreadsheets, Sitchu used to note Auburn South Primary School as a local zoning perk, which is the kind of practical flex you brag about between bites of baklava.

Petty local drama? The suburb’s got a few chapters in the archive — we won’t retell every headline, but Junkee did chronicle some of Auburn’s more… theatrical moments. We like our history with a side of theatre, thank you very much.

Who would hate living here? People who think “night out” means a curated bar crawl and not a passionate debate over where to get the best stuffed vine leaf. Who would love it? Anyone who judges a suburb by its spice rack and secondhand finds — you’ll feel at home among the pastry boxes, reopened restaurants and a botanic garden that used to host a festival big enough to make Time Out take notes.

We’re not the polished postcard of the inner city, and we like it that way. We’re the suburb that smells like syrup, spice and possibility — and, frankly, we wouldn’t swap our queue for your curated latte line-up.
