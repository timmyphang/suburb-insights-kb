# Croydon Park (NSW)

Slug: `croydon-park-nsw`

We know Croydon Park like the back of our hands — the corner of Georges River Rd where we used to queue for things that sounded posh but kept our feet firmly on the pavement. By George (117 Georges River Rd) arrived in a former appliance shop and, as Broadsheet liked to remind anyone who’d listen, opened four weeks before Sydney’s June lockdown. We used to line up for Scrumpets’ sourdough crumpets, bagels from Small Talk, Tuga pasteis de nata and whatever Ona coffee was pouring that day. Urban List even remembers our shameful devotion to Lotus Biscoff “scrumpets” during lockdown.

Weekends looked like this: drop the kids at Seedling Kids on Brighton Ave (35 Brighton Ave) — yes, the centre that ran a community garden and served the tiny horticulturalists their own veg — then swing past Pure Gelato at 30–32 Brighton Ave to stock the freezer with everything from wattleseed to bubblegum because that, apparently, is how you demonstrate local love. Sitchu will tell you we’re leafy, heritage-sashed and quietly smug about it; Inner West Mums will remind you we’ve got seven bus services threading along Georges River Rd if you’re into public transport bragging rights.

The suburb isn’t immune to drama. We had a proper scare when a shooting in an apartment forced a lockdown — a man was later charged and paramedics treated multiple people. It’s the kind of thing that knits a neighbourhood together without making it less polite.

Who hates living here? People who want an eternal parade of late-night bars and skyline views — Croydon Park never pretended to be that. It’s for people who prefer a tennis court, a park upgrade, a basketball club for the kids (shout-out to the Croydon Park Rangers) and a bakery that remembers how to make a proper pastry. We’re the suburb where families linger, where community fundraisers (remember Charlie’s story?) actually matter, and where the gelato is worth hoarding.

So yes, we’re a little suburban, a bit nostalgic and quietly proud. If you liked the idea of a local that bakes, gardens and turns up when it counts — well, we always did.
