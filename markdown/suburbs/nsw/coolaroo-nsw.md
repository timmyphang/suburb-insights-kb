# Coolaroo (NSW)

Slug: `coolaroo-nsw`

We know the weekend’s arrived because the carpeted chaos starts — or at least, it used to. Back in the day The Urban List singled out the Coolaroo Hotel on the corner of Barry Road and Maffra Street as the local MVP for families, thanks to that indoor play area where kids could climb, slide and leave sticky fingerprints on everything. We miss those afternoons already.

If you wanted to launch yourself sky‑high (literally), Time Out once made a big fuss about Airborn Indoor Trampoline Park — “over 1,300 square metres of custom made trampolines,” they said — and who are we to argue with a good bounce? Those are the kinds of things that gave our postcode a particular kind of weekend swagger: carpeted pubs, trampoline parks and the quiet pride of “we know where the best play area is.”

Our resident soap‑opera subplot? Pedestrian TV reminded everyone about the drama of the so‑called ‘The Castle’ shed, pointing out the much‑debated address — 3 Highview Crescent, Coolaroo (which, they helpfully added, is actually 3 Dagonet Street, Strathmore) — and that it might be relying on a heritage listing to dodge demolition. It’s the kind of small‑scale courtroom theatre we watch with popcorn and a thinly veiled sense of civic duty.

Business listings quietly fill the rest of our story: Locale.Online had directory entries for HIYC café & Restaurant on Motto Drive, Melka Cateres and even SR Coolroom Constructions. They’re the sort of local names that say “this is a place people set up shop and get on with it,” even if the write‑ups feel a little like old phone‑book nostalgia.

Who would hate living here? If you despise the pragmatic, family‑first vibe — the pubs with play areas, the trampoline parks and the businesses that don’t pretend to be artisanal — you’ll find Coolaroo a perpetual disappointment. Who would love it? Families who want space to let the kids run, people chasing affordable suburban energy and anyone nostalgic for the kind of local institutions Time Out, The Urban List and Pedestrian TV wrote about back when their pieces landed.

Full disclosure: most of our bragging rights are based on older stories — but we wear that history with pride. After all, not every suburb gets to be remembered for its play areas and a shed with its own legal biopic.
