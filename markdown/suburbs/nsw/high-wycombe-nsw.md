# High Wycombe (NSW)

Slug: `high-wycombe-nsw`

We live in High Wycombe, NSW — and we’ll admit it: the internet keeps confusing us with our glamorous cousin in Perth. Back in the day the travel and food blogs that mentioned “High Wycombe” were actually cheering on Palmer Crescent’s over-the-top Christmas lights and a patisserie over in High Wycombe, WA. We don’t mind the mistaken identity. It gives us something to live up to.

Morning round here is quieter than the blog photos. For years the write-ups that name‑checked our suburb pointed readers to spectacles like Palmer Crescent’s massive, musical, inflatable-heavy display — Time Out and Urban List both featured it among Perth’s top Christmas lights. Urban List also used to send gaggles of costumed kids our way at Jacaranda Springs Estate and Foxton Boulevard for Halloween trick-or-treating, which explains the foam and fairy wings we keep finding in the gutters.

If you’re the type who Googles “where do babies go,” the internet did us proud: MyBump2Baby used to list an absolute fleet of local options — Monkey Music, Sweaty Mama, Baby College, Moo Music — and even flagged several Water Babies swim schools for the splash-prone set. So yes, we’ve long been quietly parenting like champions while the rest of the web argued about who had the best lights.

Foodies once pointed to the WA High Wycombe bakery scene too. Broadsheet’s pie round-up name‑checked Four Seasons Patisserie & Bakery in High Wycombe, WA as a runner-up and credited it with wins in the pork and apple pie categories, while Buddy Bakery got the brass ring elsewhere. We’ll let them have the flaky glory — we’ve got other charms.

Who would hate living here? If you’re after late-night cocktails, curated brunch queues, or a nightlife index that doubles as your social calendar, you’ll be pining for the map app that keeps redirecting people to Perth. Who would love it? Young families, bargain-hunters and anyone who likes their suburbs with a backyard, a local toddler group and the reassurance that, at least once a year, a nearby street will do something so extra it’s featured on Time Out.

We’ll keep being us — slightly confused by our own online PR, but very content with the real-life noise of prams, pool splashes and the distant hum of someone testing their Christmas lights in September.
