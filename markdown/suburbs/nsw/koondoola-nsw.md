# Koondoola (NSW)

Slug: `koondoola-nsw`

Koondoola, NSW — back in the day we’d tell newcomers the best part of living here was knowing where the playgroup was before you knew where the nearest shiny cafe was.

We start our mornings at the Child and Parent Centre — Roseworth, on the Roseworth Primary School site, where playgroups used to be the town square (and still are the map in our heads). Playgroups were held across Koondoola, Waddington and Hudson Park Primary Schools and, if a kid needed it, speech therapists were available by referral to the Child Development Centre Koondoola. That’s our local cultural calendar: finger-painting, morning tea and the kind of gentle chaos that makes you proud rather than mildly Instagrammable.

When we wanted to pretend we had taste, we’d mention the one food legend in the listings: Kum Leng Chinese Restaurant at 34 Koondoola Ave — Urban List called its Chicken Spice Box a standout and even marked the place as a cult favourite for spice bags in Perth. We say “used to” because the only articles we’ve got on file are from the archives — nostalgic clippings, not real-time openings. Broadsheet and Time Out might have their Hot Lists and wildlife features, but they mostly remind us there are whole other suburbs getting column inches.

Drama? Not much. Our local gossip in the data is the kind measured by directories: psychologists, counsellors and family day care centres listed on MumsPages, carpets and flooring services, and even a listing for Stormfront Cleaning Group. In other words, the most exciting thing we seem to argue about is whether the vacuuming rota is fair.

Who would hate it here: people whose life mission is to be featured on Broadsheet’s Hot List or Time Out’s latest “must-see” roundup. If you want rooftop cocktail bars and breaking restaurant news, you’ll get bored fast.

Who’d love it: parents and carers, the people who prize practical support over PR — the Child and Parent Centre’s programs, family day care options and local allied health listings tell you everything. We’re low-key, family-first and quietly competent. We don’t need a skyline to be a community — we’ve got playgroups, referrals, and Spice Box lore to keep us content.
