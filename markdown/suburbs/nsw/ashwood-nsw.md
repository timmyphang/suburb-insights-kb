# Ashwood (NSW)

Slug: `ashwood-nsw`

You know that moment when you tell someone you live in Ashwood and they say, “Lovely — Byron?” Yeah, us too. We’re the Ashwood that keeps getting mixed into other people’s travel stories, and honestly, we’re fine with our cameo life.

Back in the day Broadsheet kept introducing a whole lot of Julia Ashwood to the internet — the travel‑blogger‑turned‑hotelier who helped restore The Eltham Hotel and co‑created Sun Ranch and The Vista. As Broadsheet and Concrete Playground politely reminded everyone, Julia now spends most of her time in Byron, which is confusing for visitors who arrive here expecting boutique barns and themed rooms. We’ll wave her name across town like a souvenir towel and let the tourists keep searching for pools and vintage interiors.

Our actual local claim to fame — at least in the listings — was a shy little cafe called Hyde n Seek. Urban List and Broadsheet remembered it as “hiding away in Ashwood” at 6 Yertchuk Avenue, with Moroccan and Turkish spices doing all the talking. It was the sort of place you’d brag about finding, then immediately tell your mum about like you’d discovered a community garden or a small moral victory.

If you’re a family person, we’re quietly smug. Ashwood Children’s Centre has been doing the rounds since it opened on 3 February 1986, merged and renovated around 2000 with Ashwood Central Preschool, and offers long day care plus funded 3‑ and 4‑year‑old kindergarten programs. Our high school even turns up in local listings — Ashwood High School’s calendar has the usual school‑sport bragging rights (Year 9–12 Boys Interschool Netball, a 2026 Athletics Carnival — the kids love it).

We don’t have manufactured drama here — our stories are more “slow burn than scandal.” Restore Movement Physiotherapy (509 Warrigal Road) pops up when someone trips over their own enthusiasm at netball and needs fixing.

Who would hate living here? People who consider identity crises a dealbreaker — we’re the suburb that gets famous for someone else’s surname and a hidden cafe. Who’d love it? Parents, teachers, and anyone who appreciates a solid children’s centre, dependable school sports, and the smug satisfaction of knowing where Hyde n Seek used to hide. We’re low‑key, slightly confused, and perfectly ourselves.
