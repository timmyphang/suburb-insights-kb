# Treeby (NSW)

Slug: `treeby-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start Saturday like proper locals: drop the kids at a Playgroup WA session, swing past the refurbished Treeby & Bolton for a cheeky bit of “hand painted Swedish glass” (because yes, we love pretending our builder-grade kitchen is a design edit), then nip to the Tucker Fresh IGA Treeby for the staples. The internet says “every Treeby local is loved” — and honestly, they weren’t wrong about the sentiment, even if the data we’ve got is a little nostalgic.

We know every corner here: the Treeby Community Association (over at 60 Clementine Boulevard) has been the one trying to make things less cookie-cutter, “running events, and advocating for services and safety” — as their listing puts it. Nearby developers left their mark too; B1 Homes used to have a presence at 15 Cedarleaf Entrance in the Calleya Estate, so if you like newly minted front lawns and identical mailboxes, we’re your people.

There isn’t much local tabloid fodder in the clips we found — no council scandals, no café turf wars — which is actually kind of the point. Our drama is quieter: a community association that’s earnest, a playgroup network that’s working, and a shop that sells quirky lamps and hard-to-find pieces so you can art-direct your way out of suburbia’s sameness. MumAlly and Playgroup WA show up in the record too; for years the local parenting scene has been quietly organising swaps, meetups and craft ideas (yes, even the Family Tree activity made the crawl).

Who would hate living here? If you’re allergic to community notices, new estates and the faint whiff of “we’re building something nice here,” you’ll feel restless. If boutique homewares and playgroups make you twitch, maybe try elsewhere.

Who would love it? Young families, first-home buyers, people who like the optimism of a community association at 60 Clementine Boulevard, and anyone who enjoys a Saturday ritual of small local discoveries — whether that’s Tucker Fresh IGA, a refurbished Treeby & Bolton, or a playgroup idea from Playgroup WA. We’ll keep doing the rounds, finding the little things that make Treeby feel like ours, even if the web archive treats it like a fond memory.
