# Avalon (NSW)

Slug: `avalon-nsw`

We know Avalon by the sight of a Subaru Outback double-parked on Avalon Parade at 6:30am, surfboard rack balanced, Cavoodle Milo panting in the backseat while Bec sips an oat-milk flat white from The Boathouse and checks her ‘Dawn Surf’ highlight. We wear Lululemon like a uniform and Patagonia vests like badges of honour. We judge your shirt with our eyes and offer you a tip on the best reef for dawn waves with the same breath.

Our Saturday starts at 5:30am: paddle (always “for core work”), quick surf, Boathouse oat flat white, then brunch—acai bowl or avo on sourdough—at the village. We stroll through the farmers’ market with a reusable tote, Milo on a long lead, counting our 10k steps on Apple Watch and planning Pilates later at the boutique studio down the laneway. We make headphone-less phone calls about ‘community sustainability’ and buy organic lemons from Karen who knows everyone’s coffee order.

And yet, paradise is petty. The parking melee outside Avalon Surf Club is Olympic-level: real estate opens, tourists, dog-walkers and three generations of old school locals competing for curb spots. Avalon Parade looks like a car meet — two Teslas squeezed next to a Toyota Hilux, a Subaru Outback, and a BMW with surf wax on the bumper. The village café feud is our national sport. One Friday a whisper-campaign crowns a café ‘best avo’ and the rival retaliates by removing the communal newspaper rack, which sparks passive-aggressive signs taped to light poles and a furious group chat about beach umbrellas at the markets. We gossip, we sigh, we put our names on lists.

We own four yoga mats in the garage and a boutique gym membership we use religiously and sporadically. We lug a reusable cup to every place that will accept it. We complain about traffic while driving a station wagon. We’re eco-conscious like it’s a lifestyle brand.

Who would HATE living here? Connor, 24, nightclub promoter, Hyundai i30 booming with bass at 2am, fluorescent club merch and a fondness for late-night raves, would find Avalon’s dawn-and-paddle rhythm pure cruelty. He thinks community equals a stacked speaker lineup and an unsolicited permit application. We’d serenade him out of town with polite but firm notes on etiquette and repeated requests to ‘turn it down, mate.’

And yet, we welcome you. If you love sunrise surf, farmers’ market gossip, oat milk rituals, Cavoodles, and the performative gentleness of small-village drama, you’ll fit right in. Bring your board, your mat and your reusable cup. We’ll make you a flat white and tell you where the reef is.
