# Woronora (NSW)

Slug: `woronora-nsw`

We live where the river thinks it owns more real estate than the council. Back when Broadsheet filed its love letter, The Boatshed at Woronora had just been handed new owners and “a fresh look” — but, mercifully, the lolly shelves and the famous three-tier breakfast platter were still doing their civic duty. For years the Boatshed was our waterfront excuse to turn up hungover and call it breakfast culture.

Morning around here used to mean Prince Edward Park muttering to itself while canoes, kayaks and stand‑up paddle boards slipped off the bank. Urban List called the Woronora River an “absolute paradise” for paddleboarding; Concrete Playground and Sitchu kept reminding everyone you could hire a kayak from the Boatshed (131 Prince Edward Park Road, if you liked directions). The Needles — with its rope swing and sandy banks — remains the spot where we pretend we grew up doing heroic wild-swimming laps.

The petty local drama? The river has been more committed to gentrification than we are. Broadsheet also reported the Boatshed being flattened by floods — around 80% of stock and appliances gone — then patched up with a commercial kitchen and reopened. If your idea of excitement is an SES text telling you to evacuate, Pedestrian TV has nostalgic receipts of those calls for lower‑lying bits of Woronora and Bonnet Bay. The river keeps trying to reclaim the place; we keep giving it a polite eviction notice.

Who would hate living here: people who demand perfectly predictable weather, dry ground at all times, or a suburb without a rope swing that’s two metres too nostalgic for its own good. Also, anyone allergic to trees doing the heavy lifting.

Who would love it: families and nature nerds who appreciate pram‑friendly river walks, dog owners who, as Sitchu noted, can bring pups on paddles, and anyone who secretly enjoys having a café that doubles as a kayak hire desk. For years the Woronora has been our slightly soggy, gloriously dog‑friendly river nook — and honestly, we wouldn’t trade the occasional flood text for a high‑rise view.
