# St Johns Park (NSW)

Slug: `st-johns-park-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean the internet pages that mention us pre-2024 — we knew St Johns Park, 2176, as the suburb that made you consult MumsPages like it was the family bible. We’re the people who don’t scroll Instagram for brunch pics; we open “Classes and Activities for Kids” then debate whether karate or the local dance class will break first. As MumsPages lists, you can find “karate classes and lessons” and a whole raft of “businesses and service providers for mums” if you know where to look.

Our Saturday morning routine: we herd the smalls into car seats (with military precision that would shame any drill sergeant) then check the directory for NDIS providers — yes, that’s on MumsPages too — because planning is our cardio. We run on schedules, permission slips and the polite fury of parents who can spot a missed enrolment form from a mile away.

People imagine suburbs get famous with Broadsheet features and Concrete Playground shout-outs. Sure, Broadsheet has its Sydney directory and a never-ending list of new restaurants for the trend-chasers, and Concrete Playground likes to write about Wellington’s St Johns (not ours, mind you). But we’re not in that glossy lane. We’re the postcode that shows up where it matters to families: useful listings, local classes and support services. It’s less champagne reception, more community noticeboard — and we kind of like it that way.

Who would hate living here? Anyone whose emotional GPS reroutes them straight to “best new eateries” and Michelin gossip every weekend. If your relationship is with curated nightlife rather than kids’ timetables and practical directories, you’ll get twitchy.

Who’d fall in love? Families, planners, organisers, anyone who finds solace in a postcode where you can actually find a karate class and compare NDIS providers without hunting through influencer tags. We’re efficient, quietly dependable, and gloriously unbothered by being unloved by trend pieces. St Johns Park, 2176 — not flashy, but you’ll always know where to drop the kids off.
