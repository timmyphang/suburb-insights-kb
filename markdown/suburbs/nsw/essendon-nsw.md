# Essendon (NSW)

Slug: `essendon-nsw`

We wake up in Essendon, NSW and immediately argue about whether Buckley Street ever slept — back in the day Broadsheet said it was the place for top coffee and food, and honestly that hungover pilgrimage used to feel like a rite of suburbia. We’d mosey past Bam on Albion Street (Broadsheet called it a modern milk bar) wondering if the locals still queue for its lofty club sandwiches, then smell a hypnotic sourdough drift from Backhaus Food Store — Broadsheet’s ode to 48-hour-fermented loaves — and briefly consider apologising to our waistlines.

Weekends used to be a buffet of very specific pleasures. Urban List and Concrete Playground reminded us 400 Gradi planted its Neapolitan flag on Keilor Road, Scalable Fish-Op gave us a grown-up fish-and-chip option, and Sitchu named Dua Essendon among the new cafes worth the detour. Essendon Fields had its own temptations too: DFO for bargain hunters (Time Out listed the address on Bulla Rd), LaManna’s cult cinnamon scrolls (Sitchu) and even dessert detours to Pinto Tiramisu on English Street.

Of course our local soap-opera has been less pastry, more primetime. Pedestrian’s pages ran the full carousel on the Essendon doping saga — the “Essendon 34” headlines, court rulings, and the James Hird exit chatter — so we’re no strangers to being the team that can make national inboxes sweat. Concrete Playground even ranks our ANZAC Day Essendon v Collingwood clash as one of the busiest fixtures — stadium drama included.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t handle a hangar being repurposed into Bounce — yes, Hangar 4, Wirraway Rd was a trampoline fantasy according to Time Out — or if you think DFO crowds are a personality trait, this place will make you twitch. If artisan bread, boutique baths (Sóma was once name-checked) and a local gallery inside the old Essendon Incinerator sound like too many cultural choices, maybe move to a place with one bakery and no moral ambiguity.

Who would love it? Food obsessives (Broadsheet, Urban List fans), families who find the Lincoln Road playgroup hubs handy (Playgroup Victoria listings), sunset seekers at Lily Street Lookout and anyone who appreciates a suburb that wears its contradictions proudly. We know every corner — the good, the grilled and the gloriously ordinary — and wouldn’t have it any other way.
