# Austinmer (NSW)

Slug: `austinmer-nsw`

We know Austinmer by the image of a Patagonia puff jacket wedged into the driver’s seat of a Subaru Outback, SUP on the roof, foilboard in the boot, explaining solemnly that he “commutes light.”  
We start our mornings with the cliff-top carpark full of surf crews and Subaru keys jangling like a brass band, and we end them arguing over whether the caravan carpark sign on Cliff Road is ‘heritage’ or a municipal eyesore.  
We queue at the Austinmer Kiosk for a double soy flat white and judge the bloke from the new boutique cafe on the hill for charging glow-in-the-dark coffee.  
We’re the ones in Lululemon and sunglasses named after vitamins walking Cavoodles and Labradoodles—yes, Beans belongs to us—and trading low-key barbs about whose kid just got into the selective school.  
Our weekend routine is a ritual: dawn surf at Austinmer Beach, a quick BOM swell-check on the walk up the stairs, the cliff walk with a podcast about low-tox living humming in one ear and someone’s F45 squats in the other.  
We brunch at the kiosk, the double soy flat white in hand, then meander to the Balgownie antiques stalls because that vintage lamp for our coastal-themed living room simply must be found.  
We obsessively check tide times like it’s a romantic entanglement: high, low, the weird in-between when everyone who can paddle out does.  
We complain about Sydney traffic as if it’s a character flaw we all share.  
We park on side streets and leave passive-aggressive notes on windscreens when the cliff-top carpark is claimed by early-morning surf crews; it is the small-town theatre that keeps us entertained.  
We’ve survived the sausage roll war at the Austinmer Kiosk—a three-week Yelp-style review bloodbath in the local Facebook group—artisan vs classic, puff pastry vs shortcrust, and everyone has an opinion written in full stops.  
We have a membership at F45 or Anytime Fitness in Wollongong and look athletic in Barbour jackets for coastal authenticity.  
We post aesthetic sunrise photos on Instagram with the caption “local grind” and feel smug.  
We swap homeschooling tips at the playground and debate whether the new café’s menu is peak village or peak monetised.  
We know every parking spot that becomes available during the weekend parking war and we guard them like treasure.  
Someone will hate living here: the inner-city nightclub DJ who needs 24/7 kebabs, three-level car parks and a tram at 2am will find our single-lane cliff road and early-quiet curfew stifling.  
They expect nightlife, cabs at 3am and kebabs on demand; we give surf, a tight gossip network and closing times set by how offended the locals are.  
But if you love a community that argues loudly about coffee, walks the coast at sunrise, owns a Subaru and a Cavoodle called Beans, and thinks a good weekend is surf, brunch and a slow potter through Balgownie, you’ll love Austinmer. Welcome.
