# Sunshine (NSW)

Slug: `sunshine-nsw`

You’d think a place called Sunshine would come with a press arm, a festival, and at least one artisanal tote bag. Instead, we’re here, Sunnies of Sunshine, NSW, waving from a low-key postcode that the internet mostly treats like a mystery box.

Mornings here are predictably quiet: we make our own headlines. There aren’t any recent write-ups to parade around, so for context we dig through what the archives remembered. Broadsheet once chronicled a different “Sunshine” — the family-run Sunshine Asian Supermarket in Riverwood, where the Feng family’s dim sum Saturdays used to lure queues at 10am. That one’s a Riverwood story, not ours, but it does give us hope that “Sunshine” can mean siu mai fame somewhere in the region. We also found a Sunshine Garden Playgroup operating on Garigal and Gaimaragal land in Frenchs Forest — their Steiner outdoor rhythm, Harvest Fest 2023 lantern walk and Instagram @sunshine.garden.playgroup read like a postcard from the thoughtful, pram‑pushing side of suburbia.

So here’s the deal: there’s not a heap of recent journalism about our Sunshine, NSW. Back in the day (or at least in the digital past), the name was busy popping up all over Australia — from Riverwood dim sum to Melbourne’s Nana Rosa’s Kitchen and even Sunshine Coast festivals — which is charmingly confusing. We’ll own the anonymity. It’s our aesthetic.

Who’d hate living here? People who need to be top of every trending list, who confuse suburb fame with personal worth. Also anyone trying to hype their brunch brand on an audience that, frankly, prefers a quiet Saturday.

Who’d love it? Families who appreciate a low-radar life, neighbours who actually know each other, and anyone relieved that you can live in a place called Sunshine without having to Instagram it every hour. We’re content to be underrated — if the rest of the internet wants to fight over the name, let them. We’ll keep the lanterns lit and the dim sum queues a fond, nearby memory.
