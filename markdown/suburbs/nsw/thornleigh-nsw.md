# Thornleigh (NSW)

Slug: `thornleigh-nsw`

Back in the day we used to tell visitors Thornleigh was the kind of place that did one thing very well: feed families until they could no longer button their jeans. We remember Istana — Broadsheet called it a long‑running Malaysian restaurant — with its old‑fashioned service, live seafood tanks and Hainanese chicken rice portions big enough to shame a Sunday roast. Urban List even called Istana a stalwart where "you’re treated like a regular on your first visit." Hard to be snobby about that.

Our weekend routine used to involve a tactical negotiation: do we salsa our way to the Chaozhou‑style place at a7/2-10 Central Avenue (the Urban List address, popular with families), or do we keep it low‑key and play it safe with Istana's generous plates? Time Out reminds us there was also Makan@Alice’s in its original Thornleigh digs before it decamped closer to Town Hall — so yes, we’ve exported our suburban charm to the city and occasionally missed it.

Parent life here used to be very organised. Normanhurst‑Thornleigh playgroup met at Hawkins Hall, 2 Sefton Rd every Monday at 9:30am (Playgroup NSW had the details), and Hornsby Shire Mums even listed the group contact (hawkinsplaygroup@gmail.com) and a $40 per term fee. We like to say our local calendar runs on Hainanese chicken rice and playgroup timetables.

Who would hate it here? If your nightlife consists of rooftop DJs, artisanal everything and a rotating cast of pop‑up whatever, Thornleigh will feel like a slow‑motion dinner service. If you can’t stand the idea of being recognised by staff on your first visit — as Urban List bragged is our superpower — you’ll be frustrated.

Who would love it? Families, people who worship generous portions, and anyone who appreciates eateries that remember you long before Yelp does. We’re quieter than the inner rings, proudly procedural, and still the place where the best insult is to say someone “hasn’t left Thornleigh in years” — because staying is hardly a punishment.
