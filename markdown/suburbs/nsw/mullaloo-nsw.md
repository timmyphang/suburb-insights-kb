# Mullaloo (NSW)

Slug: `mullaloo-nsw`

We know the exact place to watch the sun throw its own little tantrum into the Indian Ocean — Mullaloo Beach. Back in the day Time Out even splashed us across its list as the editor’s “best hidden gem”, which felt fancy until you realised “hidden” just meant the rest of Perth hadn’t yet got off the M1.

Our morning used to look like this: a coast walk from Burns Beach to Mullaloo, sand underfoot and the Mullaloo Surf Life Saving Club already on watch, then a slow shuffle past the foreshore where the Mullaloo Beach Hotel sat, promising that sunset view everyone brags about. If we were feeling decadent we’d booked a Hush Spa & Wellbeing session beside the hotel — warm stone massage, reiki, the lot — and pretended responsibility wasn’t a thing. For sugar therapy there was Swell Mullaloo at 1 Oceanside Promenade with its Nutella hot choccy, or Dôme Mullaloo at 10 Oceanside Promenade for a brekky with the ocean as the backdrop. None of this is cutting-edge journalism; it’s just our version of coastal domestic bliss.

Local drama? We had our own. The Mullaloo Triathlon in 2026 was a proud day until organisers shortened the swim because the water had other ideas — mostly nature reminding us who’s boss. It was the sort of minor chaos we tell guests about like an island war story.

Who would hate living here? People allergic to sand in shoes, anyone who thinks “hidden gem” equals “central”. Also those who prefer their weekends without surf life savers, sunrise dips or community triathlons interrupting their yoga scroll.

Who would love it? Families and anyone who likes tidy white sand, cinematic sunsets, a surf-club tradition that’s over 60 years strong, playgroups that used to run through the years (Playgroup WA had groups here in 2014/15 and 2015/16), and the sorts who start with five friends at Mullaloo Beach for a sunrise dip and somehow grow into a national morning ritual. We’ll keep the beach; you can keep the traffic.
