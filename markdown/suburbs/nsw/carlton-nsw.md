# Carlton (NSW)

Slug: `carlton-nsw`

We know the moment: you step off the tram and Lygon Street hits you like Nonna’s garlic breath — and we wouldn’t have it any other way. We wobble past Brunetti at 380 Lygon Street pretending we aren’t eyeing the gelato, keep one eye on Tiamo at 303–307 Lygon Street like it’s part of our Sunday ritual, and maybe duck into Readings to pretend we came for literature rather than warm air-conditioning.

Full disclosure: our research reads like a beloved scrap-book — Broadsheet, Time Out and friends telling the same story of Little Italy, Cinema Nova, Carlton Gardens and the Royal Exhibition Building. Hector’s Deli at 323 Lygon Street made headlines (yes, it brought the odd Lygon-exclusive doughnut maritozzo); Garfield Pizzeria reimagined pizza in the King & Godfree building and, fun fact, was reportedly named after an orange cat with Karen Martini on the culinary team. Jamie Valmorbida’s plan to split the King & Godfree into a pizzeria, osteria and cocktail bar gave the old landmark a bit of a glow-up, or at least a new lease on hospitality life.

Local drama? It isn’t all rosemary and ricotta. The Corkman saga lives on in memory as the pub that was illegally torn down, and every guide still tiptoes around the tension between heritage and whoever’s moving into the Malt Store next — yes, Birkenstock once set up a flagship there with a repair workshop, which made for chewy conversation at the bakery queue.

Who would hate it here: people who want suburban quiet and uninterrupted sleep — Carlton is a student mecca, a museum precinct, a permanent food festival. Who would love it: anyone whose heart beats faster for pasta, bookstores that have won awards (we're looking at you, Readings), mojitos on a converted-heritage bar, or family days at the Pauline Gandel Children’s Gallery and the Carlton Learning Precinct.

We’re biased. As Time Out and the guides keep telling everyone else, this is the place where history, hunger and the Melbourne Museum rub shoulders — sometimes politely, sometimes with sauce on their sleeves. Come for the food-lore, stay because you can’t find a better excuse to eat in public.
