# Cooks Hill (NSW)

Slug: `cooks-hill-nsw`

We start our weekends like everyone else in Cooks Hill: pretending we’re just popping out for milk and ending up three hours deep on Darby Street, because that’s how temptations are here. The Autumn Rooms used to be the kind of upstairs escape where the Tea Collective ran the show (Becci Fowler and Ben Richardson’s idea, if you remember), and you’d nod at strangers like they were old friends who also collect teapots. Down below, Goldbergs — “an institution with locals” at 137 Darby St — held the corner like it always has.

Back in the day Broadsheet called High Tea with Mrs Woo a rare gem on the increasingly commercial Oxford Street; we still tell people the Foong sisters (Rowena, Juliana and Angela) can stitch a terrace-house wardrobe better than most of us can pay for one. Concrete Playground once pointed out our boutique lane-ways and name-checked Scotties and The Falcon, which is true: we keep our hidden good stuff in plain sight and wonder why anyone’s in a hurry to leave.

Parent life here is quietly elite: Centennial Park’s fort and Norfolk Pines are where we pretend to be outdoorsy, and Newy Mums has the Ninja Parc mapped between Darby and Tooke — the kids run, we caffeinate (see: Darby Street’s steady lineup of cafes noted by Urban List). St John’s Church on 107 Dawson Street used to host the playgroup, because of course our leafy church has a playground calendar.

Who would hate living here? If you crave anonymous suburban sprawl, a shopping mall you can’t walk to, or a postcode with zero art-loving types and too many chain stores, Cooks Hill will grind your gears. We’re too compact, too walkable, and we have more galleries (Newcastle Art Gallery on Laman St with its thousands of works) and boutiques than mystery-brand megastores.

Who will love it? People who like heritage terraces, a creative scene that leaks onto the footpath, and beaches within spitting distance. Time Out once put us among the up-and-coming spots — which is code for: we’ve been quietly marvellous for years and now everyone else is noticing. Come for the culture, stay because you walked past one more cute shop and couldn’t help yourself.
