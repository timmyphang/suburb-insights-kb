# Merewether (NSW)

Slug: `merewether-nsw`

We wake up to the ocean like it’s our alarm clock — not subtle, not apologetic. For years Broadsheet told visitors you can watch the sunrise over Merewether Beach from a beachfront Airbnb, and honestly, we still pretend that’s our living room view even when the weather’s grey.

Our weekend looks suspiciously simple: a quick wander down to the baths (yes, those baths). Time Out and Urban List have both been trailing around taking pictures of our pride and joy — the Merewether Ocean Baths — and calling them massive. There’s certainly a data-backed flex to it: Time Out lists them at 50 x 100 metres and says they’re the biggest ocean baths in Australia and the Southern Hemisphere, and Broadsheet notes the baths’ 1930s-era history. Locals argue whether the first baths were a 1926 prototype or the 1935 incarnation everyone now knows; historians and salty swimmers both shrug and jump in.

Back in the day Concrete Playground and Time Out would send people up to Surfhouse Merewether — “Newcastle’s answer to Bondi Icebergs,” one write-up suggested — and Urban List even name-checked Peregrin at Level 1/99 Frederick St as a seaside gem. Those were the classy snapshots; elsewhere Netflix once turned up with a colossal umbrella on Merewether Beach and gave away merch, because of course they did.

Who wouldn’t want to live here? Sitchu’s profile charts our evolution from a mining village to a surfy, sought-after suburb — cottages, contemporary builds, Bathers Way strolls and all. The community listings remind us we’re not just surf and sunsets: the Alice Ferguson Community Centre on Caldwell Street and the Merewether Ladies Probus Club meeting at Souths Leagues Club on 46 Llewellyn Street keep things genuinely local, while Carpenter Court Aged Care sits right opposite the beach.

Who would hate living here? If you despise ocean pools the size of small football fields, can’t tolerate surfers, or think Bathers Way is an unnecessary temptation to walk off a pie, this isn’t your postcode. For the rest of us — postcode 2291, roughly 10,469 people packed into 6.2 km² of coast — we’ll keep being smug, salty, and impossibly fond of our beach.
