# Kensington (NSW)

Slug: `kensington-nsw`

We get up, we stretch, we argue about whose turn it is to drive down Anzac Parade — because in Kensington, NSW, the pink building on Anzac Parade used to be the cure‑all for panic shopping. Time Out called it Peter’s of Kensington: the one‑stop pink shop for kitchenware, stationery and gifts, and we’ll fight you for the last novelty mug it used to hawk.

Weekends look like this: we stroll past hospital towers and uni backpacks (it’s what Time Out called the mix that shapes our strip), then drift toward the food bits that make everyone pretend they were first here. Back when Urban List wrote about the precincts, Alex Lee Kitchen on Kensington Street got the name check for Chatterbox Chicken Rice and Katong Laksa Noodle — we used to queue because we have poor impulse control and excellent palates. Kensington Street Social was also on the radar for its British‑Mediterranean share plates; fanciful dishes we would order and then politely argue about who gets the flatbread (we aren’t allowed to mention what you order, so you’ll have to imagine).

We’re not smug about it. We’ll tell you outright: Kensington is food corridor famous not because of one genius cafe but because of the delicious, slightly chaotic stew of students, hospitals and the neighbourhood’s many appetites — as Time Out pointed out on June 3, 2026. The nearby Spice Alley in Chippendale even added a Japanese Quarter, which only proved our point that great meals are a short walk away if you’re prepared to tolerate queues and the odd late night uni debate.

Who would hate living here? If you crave absolute quiet, meticulously trimmed suburban uniformity, or a cul‑de‑sac where no one knows your business, Kensington will make you twitch. We like the hum — ambulance sirens, delivery bikes, the mum‑and‑pop pulse — it’s part of our charm.

Who would love it? If you live for easy access to chewy noodles, impromptu markets and a corner shop that still sells things you forgot you needed, you’ll fit right in. We love it because it’s useful, just slightly ragged at the edges, and never boring — and honestly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
