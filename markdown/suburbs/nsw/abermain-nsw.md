# Abermain (NSW)

Slug: `abermain-nsw`

We know Abermain by the sound of tiny shoes on hardwood and the smell of afternoon tea that somehow becomes the town’s unofficial anthem. For years we carted small people down to the old church on the corner — Tillys Play & Development Centre — which was established in 1996 and was refurbished from a historic church building. It sat (and still sits on paper) at 44 Melbourne St — Cnr Bathurst and Melbourne St if you liked directions with a side of nostalgia.

Mornings here used to be a ritual: drop-off between 7:00am and 9:00am, wrestle with the permission slip, know that someone else would shepherd your three- to six-year-old through the Transition to School Program while you tried to remember what silence felt like. Tillys was a 28-place long day care, open 50 weeks of the year from 7:00am–6:00pm — the kind of place that quietly anchors a community. They even marked the milestone: Abermain Turns 30! — complete with an afternoon tea and a lot of reminiscing about play-based programs and local history.

There’s not much petty council drama leaking into the pages we’ve got — no greasy café rivalries, no community petitions about roundabouts — which is partly the point. Most of the local narrative in our files revolves around families and community support. The MOMS Club pages in the archive are a reminder: for at-home parents, chapters and networks are the real social calendar, even if our records don’t name a local coordinator with a clipboard.

Who would hate living here? If you’re allergic to small talk about Transition to School or you measure your week in late-night rooftop bars, this town will feel like a compromise. If your idea of excitement is a skyline of cranes, Abermain’s historic-church-turned-childcare vibe might bore you.

Who’ll love it? Young families who appreciate a centre that’s been part of the neighbourhood since ’96, people who value community rituals (yes, even afternoon tea), and anyone who finds comfort in a place where the kids are seen, the programs are practical, and the church bells were politely repurposed into a playground soundtrack. We’ll keep the tea warm.
