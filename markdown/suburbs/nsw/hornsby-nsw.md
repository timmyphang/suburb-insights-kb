# Hornsby (NSW)

Slug: `hornsby-nsw`

We wake up on Quarry Road, make a half-hearted vow to be outdoorsy, then immediately get distracted by the view from Hornsby Park’s Southern Lookout. Yes, that 60-hectare former quarry with the Crusher Plant Precinct is ours now — the gift that keeps giving: lookouts, bike tracks and the excuse to call a hill “a lifestyle choice.”

Our weekend runs look suspiciously wholesome. We’ll wander Fagan Park’s Garden of Many Nations until somebody admits they’re hungry, then lob toward Westfield Hornsby because someone in our group insists they “just need one thing.” Once, Archie Rose even rolled through Westfield Hornsby with a Tailored Spirits Live pop-up and let the whole place pretend we’re connoisseurs for about an afternoon. Good times. Past tense, obviously — it was a pop-up. We’re not posers, we’re archival.

Local soap-opera: the council got a $200,000 grant to fast-track a Special Entertainment Precinct — yes, the town square and mall are in trial territory for Hornsby After Dark. Translation: permission to be noisy for the sake of a “nightlife economy.” We’re equal parts excited and terrified. Will this mean late-night vibrancy or someone’s karaoke rendition of a 90s hit echoing off the quarry walls? Time will tell. Also, apparently the planes are taking a new arc toward Hornsby now, which makes our post-dinner alfresco dining feel like a limited-time, flyover-enhanced experience.

Who would hate it here? People who crave a never-ending, buzzy inner-city stomp. If you need skyscraper skyline drama and unlimited late-night doner kebab options, Hornsby will politely bore you. Also, anyone who can’t cope with mums organising everything — shoutout to Hornsby Shire Mums for running a better calendar than our entire suburb combined.

Who would love it here? Families who adore community — we’ve got Wollundry Park’s rebuilt nursery-rhyme playground, stargazers who’ll drive up to Mount Kuring-Gai with a blanket, gardeners who’ll lose afternoons in Fagan Park, and book-lovers who remember Tanya Kurukulasuriya’s Bookplate and its delightful “book-quets” from lockdown. We’re suburban, scenic, and quietly ambitious. Come for the lookouts, stay for the Spring Fest on Peats Ferry Road and Dural Lane — we’ll save you a spot on the hill and a good gossip.
