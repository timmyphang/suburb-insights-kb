# Blackburn (NSW)

Slug: `blackburn-nsw`

We like to think of Blackburn as the suburb that quietly refuses to be flashy. Saturday mornings, we’re the ones padding around Blackburn Lake Sanctuary, pretending the boardwalk was our idea of a detox, then admitting defeat and following the Gardiners Creek route that, yes, used to link us all the way into the Yarra Trail — as The Urban List cheerfully reminded everyone who wanted a long walk and no small talk.

Back in the day the headlines about us weren’t about rooftop bars or glossy laneways. Broadsheet once wrote up Little Woodpecker on Katrina Street like it was a tiny civic institution — a cafe that moved in where an upholsterer used to be — and Urban Climb got the big, respectful coverage it deserved when Time Out and Broadsheet reported on that gargantuan, $15‑million, 2,800 m² Blackburn centre with Olympic‑style walls, saunas and an onsite cafe. We still brag about the five‑storey speed wall at family BBQs and nobody corrects us.

If you read the local pieces, our cultural peak is a tasteful mix of outdoor calm and very earnest activity: a sun‑dappled bottle shop/bar called Natural Science Wine & Liquor — which Sitchu noted was founded by Tristan Jallais and Joyce Chua — and a quietly unassuming yakiniku at 55–65 Railway Road, Ichikuro, that Urban List shoved into a round‑up for people who like binchotan and house marinades. Not exactly clubland, but whoever needs clubland when you can debate single‑vineyard shiraz over a small rotating tap?

We’ll admit it: people who crave an always‑on, neon nightlife would hate living here. The sort who measure a suburb by the number of late‑night burger places will find Blackburn maddeningly serene. Also, if you think every local should have an opinion on the council’s scent of the month, you’ll be disappointed — we prefer trees.

But who loves Blackburn? Young families and professionals who want good schools and parks (Sitchu’s suburb profiles had that right), climbers who treat Urban Climb like a second home, and anyone who finds the idea of a retro bottle shop/bar more thrilling than a rooftop photo op. For years the coverage we’ve got focused on walks, climbing and thoughtful little venues — and frankly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
