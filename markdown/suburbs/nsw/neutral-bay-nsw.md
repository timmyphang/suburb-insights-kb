# Neutral Bay (NSW)

Slug: `neutral-bay-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start Saturday by bickering about parking on Military Road like it was a competitive sport, then wander down the artery Time Out once called Neutral Bay’s main strip to see who’s queuing for lunch. For years the local guide pages — Broadsheet, Time Out, Concrete Playground — have treated our village like the place to pretend you’re on a short holiday without leaving the Lower North Shore.

We used to pop into Brother Fox Espresso on the way to a class at Neutral Bay School of Dance, scope out the new-ish pop-up vibes in Grosvenor Lane where Kabocha and Kyushu kept the sashimi faithful happy, and argue whether Henrys was the perfect date-night flex or just very good at curated cocktails. Weekend wellness felt indulgent and faintly theatrical: Rimba Sweat’s Neutral Bay studio promised infrared suites and communal ice baths for the sort of restorative show-off sessions Urban List and Broadsheet wrote about.

Our local institutions have their own novels. The Oaks Hotel — yes, the one with the 70-year-old oak and a beer garden Broadsheet and Time Out both couldn’t help but adore — used to be where generations chose to notch their Friday evenings. We had small, neat discoveries like At Neutral and the pick-me-up lunch spots Concrete Playground listed, plus the ever-present promise of rooftop SoCal and Pocket Pizza if you were feeling blasé about authenticity.

Petty local drama? Remember the temporary plaza trial on Young Street — North Shore Mums covered the cone-and-planter experiment and the community feedback period like it was a soap opera. Our mums’ groups also kept busy: Neutral Bay Mums and Bubs were once being refurbished and asked locals to email manager@nbaycommunity.com.au if they cared.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to brunch ecosystems, alfresco terraces and the faint hum of “is this the best katsu in Sydney?” arguments outside K-Town Korean BBQ House. Who would love it: professionals and families who appreciate a twenty-minute commute to the city, a clutch of reliable cafes, a bewildering number of Japanese spots (Tobikiri, Jugemu/Shimbashi) and a village that stages tasteful living-room-level drama with a smile. We know every corner — and we’ll happily judge you for parking on Military Road.
