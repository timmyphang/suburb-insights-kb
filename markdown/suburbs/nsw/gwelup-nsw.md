# Gwelup (NSW)

Slug: `gwelup-nsw`

Back in the day we’d chart the evening like a pilgrimage: playgroup at the Colin Moore Community Centre, a detour past Umberto’s for something flaky, then a queue at Gwelup Fish & Chips on North Beach Road. Broadsheet reminded us the chippie only opened four hours a night — which somehow made the battered fish taste like an exclusive event. 

Our mornings were routine in the loveliest suburban way. The volunteer-run Gwelup Community Corner Playgroup at the Colin Moore Community Centre (corner of Lagonda Drive and North Beach Road) kept the small people entertained while we swapped tips on enrolments and free trial sessions. For a while EB Beauty at 707 North Beach Road was our go-to for tidy nails before the school concert; the Karrinyup Flower Shed on North Beach Road had our last-minute posies. Wild Gecko in Gwelup Plaza was where you bought the novelty present you didn’t need but absolutely wanted.

The local food scene had its brief celebrity moment — Magnolia BBQ’s Anisha Halik and Jacob D’Vauz swapping pop-ups for a permanent slot at Gwelup Fish & Chips lured the crowds, as Broadsheet wrote. It was the kind of mild local drama we cherish: queues, excited social posts, and the occasional review that made our small strip feel very big-city for a weekend.

Who would hate it here? If you loathe trick-or-treaters, don’t move to GWELUP — Urban List literally listed our streets, Farmer Jacks and Lake Gwelup Primary School as a Halloween stop. If you’re after late-night fine dining or a buzzing laneway bar, our most dramatic decision is whether to have crumbed or grilled.

Who loves it here? Families, obviously — the playgroup, Lake Gwelup Primary, the local bakery legends, and the one fish’n’chip place that made an evening feel like a ritual. We’re quietly proud of our patchwork of practical shops and community corners. We may not be flashy, but we’re the suburb that knows how to queue properly, bring lunch to the school fair, and wave at neighbours on North Beach Road.
