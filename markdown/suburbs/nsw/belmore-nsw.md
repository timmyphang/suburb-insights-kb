# Belmore (NSW)

Slug: `belmore-nsw`

We open every Saturday with the image of a faded Holden Commodore idling outside the servo on Burwood Road, a Canterbury Bulldogs sticker on the bumper while the bloke inside orders a long mac and a sausage roll like it’s a tactical play. We know our weekend by the hum of that engine. We plan our mornings around Belmore Oval the way other suburbs plan solar panels.

Three rows of cars angle‑parked on the verge, two distant relatives loudly insisting “I was here first,” and a furious PTA rep waving a laminated schedule — that’s our ritual. Mums in Lorna Jane leggings time the school‑run squeeze for a gap in the oval parking, swapping gossip about the next fundraiser while timing the kids with military precision. Kids in shorts, socks pulled up, and junior blokes practising the chant that will echo until half‑time.

Our corner café on Burwood Road is where the compromise lives: long macs, firm handshakes, and a barista who knows the Bulldogs injury list better than the sports desk. Until last month, when the cafe swapped the 'double‑shot long mac' for a 'single‑origin pour‑over' and our local internet transformed into a committee. One‑star versus five‑star reviews on Google and Facebook, complete with screenshots and alt‑texted grievances — the thread reads like a parliamentary inquiry into taste. “Too posh for Belmore,” someone declared. “Finally, good coffee,” someone else replied. We kept sipping.

Our ideal local is a 40‑something footy dad/coffee connoisseur who drives a Toyota HiLux for weekend gear runs, wears a Bulldogs cap to brunch, and keeps a soft spot for the milk bar’s meat pies. Saturday: drop the kids at juniors, park at the oval, catch the game, grab a long mac from the corner cafe, then a “quick” run to Bunnings that swallows the arvo. Daily: check the Bulldogs injury list, walk a staffy crossbreed down the main strip, greet everyone on the footpath, and offer Tim Tams and instant coffee “for guests” like it’s hospitality doctrine.

Our small dramas are sweeter than they are vicious: the weekly parking war, a strata spat over wheelie bin placement, and the cafe loyalty quarrel are part of the suburb’s heartbeat. We love the volume of life here.

Who would hate living here? The 28‑year‑old artisan candle‑maker who cycles everywhere in designer cycling shorts, orders cold brew on repeat, and insists SUVs are a “personal branding statement” would combust. They’d see loud engines, sausage rolls and footy chants as aesthetic offences; our curated chaos looks to them like unpaid council tax.

And who would love it? Anyone who wants a suburb that smells like meat pies and strong coffee, where people know your name, your dog’s name, and whether your Commodore “still has life in it.” Welcome to Belmore — loud, loyal and unapologetically ours.
