# Rivervale (NSW)

Slug: `rivervale-nsw`

We live in Rivervale, NSW — the kind of suburb whose biggest flex is sharing a name with a couple of more famous Rivervales. Back in the day, most of the press that bothered to type the word “Rivervale” was actually writing about other places — which, frankly, suits us just fine.

If you Google a Rivervale weekend, the results are a weird travel brochure mash-up. UrbanList once touted Aloft Perth in Rivervale and its Springs Kitchen (they even loved the 24-hour pantry), and Sitchu remembered The Springs at 27 Rowe Avenue. UrbanList also recommended iFLY in Rivervale as an indoor skydiving novelty — because nothing says “local weekend” like floating in a vertical wind tunnel in a different state. Time Out, meanwhile, had notes on a Rivervale Mall branch in Singapore and its 24-hour Whale Tea — proof our name travels well.

As Junkee cheerfully documented, “Rivervale” also exists as Riverdale’s spooky parallel-universe storyline, which is handy when you want to imagine your suburb with neon fog and secret cults. Broadsheet once had the most local-sounding breadcrumb: an interviewee who mentioned relatives moving to Rivervale and a grandmother visiting from Singapore — sentimental, brief, and very much from another Rivervale’s scrapbook.

There isn’t much spicy council drama in the clips we have — which is either a blessing or boring, depending on how many community Facebook rants you subscribe to. The only local personality here is ambiguity, and we wear it like an ironic hoodie.

Who would hate living here? People who need a constant stream of brunch clout, celebrity chefs, or metropolitan scandals. They’ll be disappointed when their Rivervale feed points them to hotels in Perth, a sushi train in Thornlie, or a fictional small town on TV.

Who would love living here? Folks who like being low-key, who enjoy the idea that our name connects us to a hotel with sweeping views, a 24-hour bubble-tea shrine in Singapore, an indoor skydiving wind tunnel, and a soap-opera parallel universe. We’re happily obscure — and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
