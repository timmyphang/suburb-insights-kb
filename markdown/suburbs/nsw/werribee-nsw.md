# Werribee (NSW)

Slug: `werribee-nsw`

Back in the day, we’d roll out of bed, still dazed from whatever Chirnside Park had put on the night before, and treat ourselves to the small triumphs Werribee quietly promised. Tuckers for a loaded sanga and one of those Shilling Shakes, a wander past Wyndham Park Playground to Wolf On Watton if the kids were behaving, then a slow pub crawl that reluctantly included The Park — yes, the one a few steps from Werribee Station with around 30 taps — and the long-suffering, gloriously revived Bridge Hotel with its 1926 art‑deco face and three crackling fireplaces. The Marquis of Lorne team did a thing with that place and we all pretended we planned to be regulars.

We used to feel like explorers at Werribee Open Range Zoo, especially since that Massive Elephant Trail opened — you could convince yourself you were on safari without leaving the western suburbs. For quieter pretence of grandeur there was Werribee Park Mansion and its Italianate gardens; Lancemore Mansion and the L.M Spa got the nod when we wanted to feel posh without the city queues. Even the local arts types got their moment: remember 24, the free 24-hour festival at Chirnside Park? LIT at Wyndham Park lit up the place too — we liked to say it was culture that didn’t make us change out of our shorts.

Petty local drama? Oh, we’ve had our hits. There was the bloke fined $1,652 for driving 30km to fetch butter chicken — legend or local cautionary tale, take your pick. And who could forget when a Tufted Duck earned the title “goth duck” after turning up in the sewage ponds and briefly stealing birdwatchers’ hearts?

Who’d hate it here: anyone allergic to craft beer, outdoor art that refuses to clock out, or open‑air safari buses. Who’d love it: families (Lollypop Creek Community Centre, Elements Café and the playground-friendly spots know us), walkers and geology nerds at Werribee Gorge, spa seekers at Lancemore, and bargain hunters — Werribee kept getting name‑checked as proper value with riverside charm. We still smile when the train grinds in or the M1 spits you out — this place has always been a good kind of messy, and we wouldn’t trade it.
