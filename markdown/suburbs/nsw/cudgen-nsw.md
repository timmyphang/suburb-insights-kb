# Cudgen (NSW)

Slug: `cudgen-nsw`

Back in the day we’d wake to glass‑clear water and the sort of birdlife that makes city folk do embarrassing double takes. Cudgen Creek used to be our calm‑before‑the‑Instagram storm: Urban List called it “breathtakingly transparent” and yes, if you were lucky you’d get a dolphin to photobomb your paddle. We’ve even done the Family Centre’s kayaking session — proper local curriculum, not a city workshop.

Weekends used to stretch like the sunflower rows at Farm & Co (529 Cudgen Road) — you know, the place Urban List made famous for sunflower fields, farm‑to‑plate vibes and friendly farm animals. If you missed peak bloom between December and March, blame crop rotation, not us. Out on 592 Cudgen Road there was Earth Beer Company — started as an old tin packing shed on an avocado farm overlooking Wollumbin — and Red Earth Brewing cropped up in the rural landscape promising fire‑pit dinners and indigenous bush tucker. These were our low‑impact, high‑ambience distractions when the surf wasn’t doing anything dramatic.

Our community calendar never had a gossip column; our big headlines were Reconciliation Week walks from Cudgera Creek to the Cudgen Beach Surf Lifesaving Club and the 18 war widows who met at Cudgen Leagues Club every third Thursday. That’s the level of civic life here — earnest, practical and occasionally sunburnt.

Who would hate it here? If you loathe kayaks, can’t cope with sunflower selfies, or are allergic to low‑key breweries that started life as farm sheds, this isn’t your postcode. If you pine for tram lines and a proper taxi rank, best look elsewhere.

Who would love it? Families who turn up to Playgroup NSW meetups, paddlers who know Cudgen Creek’s secret calm, people who appreciate a beer that remembers its farm origins, and anyone who thinks community means walking together in Reconciliation Week. We’re small, a bit rural, quietly proud — and if that sounds like the sort of place you’d bring a picnic (and a hat), you’ll belong here.
