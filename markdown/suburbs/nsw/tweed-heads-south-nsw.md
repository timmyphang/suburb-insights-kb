# Tweed Heads South (NSW)

Slug: `tweed-heads-south-nsw`

Back in the day we’d wake up on the Tweed side with sand still in our shoes and a plan that involved minimal effort and maximum seafood. As Broadsheet once cheekily put it, there’s more than enough to fill a weekend where Queensland and New South Wales meet — and we’ve milked that border-town energy for years.

Our morning routine used to be embarrassingly predictable: queue for a coffee at Next Door Espresso on Dry Dock Rd (56 Dry Dock Rd, if you’re digging through old directories), admire the white Mediterranean fitout, order whatever Moonshine coffee the menu promised, and argue about whether the sourdough french toast or the acai bowl deserved the praise Urban List gave it. If we were feeling virtuous we’d pop into The Bread Social down by the river for pastries; if we wanted to be pampered like we’d earned it, Lan Spa on Minjungbal Dr (54 Minjungbal Dr) was the “new” wellness option promising head spa enlightenment.

For the outdoorsy among us, Concrete Playground’s write-ups used to send you out on Tweed Eco Cruises with Luther Cora — a proper Bundjalung Nation–led trip through the Terranora Lakes mangroves — or the slightly less mystical Catch a Crab Tour where you learned to snare spanner, mud and sand crabs, pump yabbies and watch the resident pelicans judge your technique. Foodies who remember the Catseye Pool Club pieces still talk about Tweed Heads rock lobster and Bowen mud crab pie like they were local religion.

We’re not pretending life here is all beachfront bliss. We’ve sat through cyclone warnings and grant cuts that made the rounds on the news, and the local parenting network keeps us honest: The Family Centre used to list an office on Amber Rd (Unit 1/14 Amber Rd) and the playgroups — including MyTime’s Thursday meet at Unit 3/6-8 Amber Road — make sure the tiny residents are louder than any late-night party.

Who’d hate it here? People allergic to pelicans, crab claws and community groups that meet at 9.30am on Mondays. Who’d love it? Anyone who worships good coastline, edible spoils from the river, and a border town that’ll happily send you into Queensland for one thing and keep you in NSW for the next. We’ll keep the sand, you keep the sandals.
