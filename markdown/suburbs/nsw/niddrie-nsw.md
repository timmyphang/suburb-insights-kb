# Niddrie (NSW)

Slug: `niddrie-nsw`

We know the line at the quarry like it’s our own neighbourhood footy team: kids on scooters, dogs on the deck and people pretending they discovered the lake last weekend. Back in the day the new-lakeside dream came with a name — Peregrine Social Club — the Holmes Hall team’s waterfront spot in the old Niddrie Quarry at 45 Valley Lake Boulevard, part of the Valley Lake estate. We watched it go from “coming soon” to the place you Instagram from the boardwalk.

If you’ve lived here long enough you’ll remember Mister Hoffman — the little neighbourhood cafe at 85A Hoffmans Road that used to be the go-to for a family-friendly feed (Broadsheet’s directory had Petra and Dean Parker listed). These days the listings read like a who’s-who of places that put Niddrie on someone else’s weekend map: Charlie’s got a shout from Sitchu for “some of Melbourne’s best” sandwiches, and even El Jannah made a play and opened a site here in the chain’s Victorian push.

We’re not all brunch selfies and quarry sunsets. Time Out once put us on a list — Niddrie made their 2025 highest-growth suburbs with an 11.8% jump — so yes, the property spreadsheets are whispering sweetly. That growth came with a side of fuss: early maps for a 2025 high‑density housing plan even listed a joint development for Niddrie and North Essendon, which stirred the usual mix of “about time” and “hold your horses” reactions.

We’ve got the practical stuff too: Niddrie Gymnastics Club still turns preschool energy into backflips (they’re listed at Building 44, 235 Wirraway Road, Essendon Fields), local family networks and the Greater Craigmillar Community Calendar stitch Craigmillar and Niddrie together for events. Our state member, Mr Carroll, has even put playgroup wins into Hansard — so yes, we lobby with stickers and scones.

Who wouldn’t love it here? Families who need decent kids’ activities, people who like a lakeside beer, and anyone who wants to be slightly removed from the city without losing the occasional sandwich that actually impresses. Who would hate it? If you crave inner‑city anonymity or flat‑out refuse to share a lake with a stroller, maybe pass.

We’re proud, a little gentrified, and very aware that our quarry has more views than our real estate agent’s brochure lets on.
