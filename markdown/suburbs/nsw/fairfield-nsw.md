# Fairfield (NSW)

Slug: `fairfield-nsw`

We start our Saturdays like proper locals: a quick detour to King of Pickles and Spices — where $15 will score you a kilo of pickles, plus seeds, spices and even Iraqi buffalo‑milk cream if you’re feeling extravagant. We clatter past the showground, wave at the playground that made headlines, and pretend the $630‑million Fairfield Hospital redevelopment is our morning cultural landmark (Time Out did say it was the biggest investment on the site in almost 40 years, so we’ll take that prestige).

By 10am the kids have been deposited at Deerbush Park — yes, the one next to Fairfield Showground with the Liberty Swing and wheelchair‑accessible carousel — and we perch on a bench pretending we’re just supervising. Play Strong PlayVan was even running sessions from Deerbush Park (443 Smithfield Road, Prairiewood), so there’s always someone masking parenting panic with a thermos of coffee.

Petty local drama? We’ve had our moments. Remember the Paradiso Receptions blow‑up — about 700 people, a $5,000 fine and a long evening of “who invited whom”? It’s the sort of thing that gives us stories for years and provides free entertainment for anyone who enjoys a civic soap opera.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t handle kids shrieking on an inclusive carousel, an adventurous Fairfield Heights Park playground that opened after a $4 million spruik, or a food precinct where queues form (Urban List even noted lines at the Fairfield Chase Plaza birria spot), this isn’t your vibe. If pickles, playgrounds, and occasional wedding chaos provoke a fainting spell, move along to somewhere quieter.

Who will love it? Families who want Aquatopia waterpark days, people who appreciate accessible public spaces (Deerbush Park was a proper step forward), and anyone who likes their neighbourhood to be full of honest, useful things rather than curated aesthetics. We know every nook — from the showground to the park entries — and we’ll happily trade a slightly raucous Saturday for community that actually shows up. Come for the pickles, stay because the kids have taken over the swings.
