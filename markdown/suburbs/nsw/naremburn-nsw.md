# Naremburn (NSW)

Slug: `naremburn-nsw`

We start our Saturday on Willoughby Road, noses full of frying oil and the faint whiff of someone attempting a “healthy” smoothie. Back in the day our local press preferred to file fond little profiles rather than scandals, so what we’ve got to work with is mostly nice things about shops, houses and the odd very good chippy.

Our weekend circuit used to be gloriously simple: drop past Maslow’s in its Naremburn days at 286 Willoughby Road — Broadsheet remembered chef Jack New and the Mr Pig brioche long after the refit — then duck into Forsyth Cafe at 284 Willoughby Rd for specialty coffee curiosities (Time Out even name‑checked owner Rob Forsyth and the rare monkey‑parchment tales). If we felt virtuous we’d swing by Sprout Wholefood Café & Grocer at 272–274 Willoughby Road for a salad bowl and a Little Marionette hit; if we felt honest, we ended up at Old English Fish ’N’ Chips on Willoughby Rd polishing off potato scallops and plump New Zealand cod as Time Out recommended. On a good night we bragged about Karl Riseborough’s tinkering: Flat Rock Brew Cafe, converted from an old fruit-and-veg shop into a tiny brewpub with a 200‑litre setup, steaks, schnitzels and burgers — the kind of project local press loves to romanticise.

There’s no juicy council feud or bougie-versus-boat-ramp war in the archives — mostly directories, property listings and gentle rave reviews. Sitchu’s estate pages gush about Art Deco blocks on Park Road, Federation charm on Oxley Street and how everything is a short stroll to Crows Nest Village and St Leonards train station. And yes, Time Out even slipped Naremburn into a 2024 affordability-and-liveability roundup, which made us feel smug and faintly scandalous.

Who would hate it here? People who need 24/7 neon, rooftop DJs and a new revolution every weekend. Who would love it? Families who remember the Naremburn Les Petits Loups French playgroup at the Community Centre on 7 Central Street, anyone who appreciates a solid chippy, and those who like their local scene low-key, walkable and just a bit smug about being underrated.
