# Sandringham (NSW)

Slug: `sandringham-nsw`

We wake up to the smell of salt and the faint clink of rigging — this is Sandringham, South Sydney, where the bay sets the tempo and we know which tide is doing the rounds before the Met Office does. Mornings here used to be a choice between a dip at Sandringham Baths on Watson Street (you know, the one near Grand Parade and Dolls Point Baths) or pretending we’re on a yacht when the Georges River Sailing Club riggers go past. The sailing club has been here since 1927 and, if you read the write‑ups, it recently went through a $12 million-ish glow‑up and started serving waterfront dining that makes us feel very fancy for a few hours each weekend.

Weekends are simple: we potter to the foreshore, ogle the revamped club, and complain about how the harbour used to be quieter. There’s a particular local sport of watching tourists try to figure out where the water ends and the expensive sand begins. We also trade notes about the old Sandringham Hotel in Newtown — it’s been through its reinventions (Time Out had it on a heritage list) and Junkee says it closed amid Sydney’s “current regulatory climate” and was sold on for a mini‑golf makeover. The live music crowd didn’t love that chapter, and neither did we; there’s nothing like a sudden urban metamorphosis to give the village grapevine something to do.

Who would hate it here? If you think boats are noisy, water is wet and community‑minded places are suspiciously neighbourly, Sandringham will feel suffocating. If you want a suburb with zero change, zero foreshore restaurants and absolutely no chance of a quirky post‑pub mini‑golf takeover, we’ll reluctantly agree you should look elsewhere.

Who’d love it? Sailors, people who measure mornings in tides, anyone who likes baths on Watson Street and the idea of waterfront dining post‑refurbishment. And us? We’ll keep claiming the foreshore as ours, laughing when the weekend crowd realises the tide chart is effectively our local timetable. Sandringham — small suburb, big water, and just enough controversy to make tea taste better.
