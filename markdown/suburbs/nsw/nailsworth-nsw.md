# Nailsworth (NSW)

Slug: `nailsworth-nsw`

We like to tell visitors we live in the suburb with a secret toastie economy. Back in the day, the hole-in-the-wall on 1/60 Howard Street used to be our proof: Around the Corner — opened by Ahmed Azimi and Suem Valiyff — arrived with kofta and chicken toasties and a rotation of Parwana-made sweets that made the street smell like someone had punted on dessert and won.

We still remember the little ritual: shuffle in, try not to stare at the narrow counter, choose between a kofta twist or whatever chicken thing they were doing that week, and walk off with something wrapped in paper and a small smugness that this was Nailsworth, not Rundle Street. Broadsheet wrote about it like it was a national treasure, even noting “customs, coffee and community” as its defining three Cs — which, honestly, is just a posh way of saying it fed us very well.

Sitchu even said it “already felt like it’s been part of Nailsworth forever” when it popped up — and for a suburb that rarely flashes newness, that felt accurate. It also got a nod in Broadsheet’s roundup of new Adelaide openings in June 2026, which meant we had to pretend we weren’t chuffed to see our tiny cafe on a list with places we’d never get into if we hadn’t once lived here.

Who would hate living here? People who need a neon franchise on every corner, or anyone allergic to queues that form for small, specialist things. If your weekend plan requires lots of table availability and very predictable toasties, you’ll grumble. Also, if you only move somewhere for status, Nailsworth will make you rethink your priorities — and you’ll leave confused and hangry.

Who would love it here? The kind of people who like surprises in their sandwich, appreciate a tight little food scene, and value a local that feels like it was stitched into the street rather than plastered on it. We keep the memory of that tiny cafe like a high school anthem: sentimental, a bit embarrassed, and very, very proud.
