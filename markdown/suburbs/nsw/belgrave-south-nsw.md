# Belgrave South (NSW)

Slug: `belgrave-south-nsw`

We like to think of Belgrave South as the place you drift to when Melbourne gets too tidy — draped in forested hills and a particular kind of creative eccentricity, as Sitchu put it. Back in the day the internet loved reminding us of that: there aren’t any fresh news scoops in our feed, so we’re leaning on the classics.

Picture a weekend where we used to pop into Unwrapped Pantry — yes, the tiny place Broadsheet mentioned — to buy mushrooms from Unearthed Co, and then pretend we were foraging. The Community House used to run classes that made the hills feel neighbourly; Time Out once bookmarked the Belgrave South Community House as proof we’re more than just trees. If you wanted to be very festive, a Christmas-tree farm was listed at 186 Belgrave‑Hallam Road, which made our December smell like Pine-Scented Good Decisions.

Our surrounding Belgrave cousins get the glossy write-ups — Broadsheet has the Belgrave Emporium and the odd listing for Babaji’s Kitchen and Killik Handcrafted Rum — and Sitchu has always enjoyed pointing out the cafe–cinema energy over the hill: Earthly Pleasures + Cameo Cinema. Meanwhile we’ve got playgroup pages (the Belgrave Heights and South Community Playgroup) and community listings that read like a map of local resilience: Belgrave Optometry Centre, Nutritional Balance (Megs who “moved to Belgrave South” according to the directory), A Woman’s Space, and even a women’s self‑defence class once held at the 1st Belgrave South Scout Group Hall, 28 Station Street.

Petty drama? The archives are mercifully quiet, which suits us. We’d rather be known for community halls, small‑scale therapists, and a suspiciously good mushroom supply chain than for any council scandals.

Who would hate living here? People who need constant neon and chain stores or who bristle at the smell of a real Christmas tree — or at being upstaged by a property listing like 75 Altson Road popping up in Sitchu’s pages. Who would love it? Anyone who wants trees, a village beat, and the excuse to say “We used to…” while sipping something local and smug. We call that living.
