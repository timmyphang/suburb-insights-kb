# Unley (NSW)

Slug: `unley-nsw`

Back in the day — and by “back in the day” we mean the only stories we’ve been handed are from the archives — Unley felt like the sort of leafy village that grew an excellent wine list and a cult following before you could say “King William Road.” We know it because the papers told us so.

We start our Saturday like locals used to: a wander down Unley Road past Pickle In The Middle at 134 Unley Road (yes, the Central Market pop‑up that planted itself here), then a respectful nod to the glasshouse aesthetic of Just Down the Road and the Whistle & Flute crew — Broadsheet made a whole directory out of our caffeinated ambitions. If you liked plants, The Conservatory Garden and Home was the place your great‑aunt would approve of; it’s been selling William Morris style cushions and topiary since 1975, apparently with more dignity than we ever manage.

Nightlife? We were spoilt for choices you could brag about. Dolly, from the Lune team, served crab soldiers and grilled cheese with mushroom and Vegemite — because we’re classy like that — and the neighbouring Spread did deli‑by‑day, drink‑by‑night duties. And if you wanted to be very grown‑up, Alt’s wine bar on King William Rd had the DJ sets and trivia nights to go with your by‑the‑glass existentialism.

There’s community theatre, too: the Unley Gala used to turn King William Road into a runway of three fashion shows, Gospo Collective singalongs and a kids zone that made parents feel very organised for approximately two hours. Families found their centres here — NumberWorks’nWords ran local tutoring; Little Miracles Playgroup met at St Spyridon College on Oxford Terrace; Unley Swimming Centre at Forestville Reserve kept the tiny people mostly alive.

Who would hate living here? If you despise Victorian bungalows, tree‑lined streets and the quiet thrill of pretending you’re “walking into the city” in ten minutes, this place will frustrate you. Also, anybody allergic to good wine and boutique topiary.

Who would love it? People who like their weekends curated: good coffee that once made headlines, a deli that became a bar, sunday swims and an annual gala worth telling grandkids about. We’re quietly smug about that.
