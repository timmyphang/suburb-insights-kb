# Templestowe Lower (NSW)

Slug: `templestowe-lower-nsw`

We know the sound of the gulls over the Yarra and the smell of eucalyptus on a Sunday, even if the travel guides treat us like a sleepy side note. Back in the day we’d roll out of bed and wander toward Macedon Square, where Broadsheet wrote about Riddik — an all-day eatery that used to live in an old ANZ bank at 1–3 The Mall (3 The Mall in some listings). It felt delightfully audacious for somewhere tucked into our undulating hills.

Weekends used to look like this: someone would mutter about catching the 907/908 to nab a table at Aura Cafe (Concrete Playground name‑checked it), a couple of kids would insist on the indoor playground at the Templestowe Hotel on 23 Parker Street while the adults pretended to be interested in happy hour, and others would escape to Westerfolds Park or Petty’s Orchard for a walk that made us feel very virtuous.

We’ve had our small-town melodrama — mostly in press clippings. Yarra Valley Water once issued a water‑quality warning that listed Templestowe and Templestowe Lower among affected suburbs; it was the kind of thing that made us check the taps and ask our neighbours if their pasta tasted funny. Otherwise, gossip here ages like the wine you hide behind the Ben & Jerry’s: slow, familiar and comforting.

Who would hate living here? People who can’t stomach hills, eucalypts and wide, quiet streets; anyone allergic to kids’ laughter because the local pub had a kids’ play HQ literally next to the bar; and anyone who thinks all good dining must be in the city — they never warmed to the idea that a gutsy, all‑day diner could open in a former bank and be headline news.

Who would love it? Families who value parks and playgrounds, walkers and cyclists who know Westerfolds by heart, plant‑based diners who once found solace at Power Plant, and anyone who appreciates a neighbourhood where the biggest scandal is a water warning or a new cafe moving into an old bank. For years the guides — Time Out, Concrete Playground, Broadsheet — treated us like a pleasant discovery. We’ll keep being that, thank you very much.
