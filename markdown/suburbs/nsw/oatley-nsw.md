# Oatley (NSW)

Slug: `oatley-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start Saturday by pretending Oatley Park was our backyard — the 45 hectares of ovals, lookouts, pier and those bafflingly nice river beaches made us smug about being 21km from the city. As Urban List wrote, the park was "truly iconic" and even had spots like Dracula's Canteen for when you needed something to gossip about on the walk home. We’d then wheel out the fixie (or at least fantasise about doing so) and ride over the old Como Railway Bridge like we were in some boutique cycling ad Time Out used to mention.

For years the village centre read like a neighbourhood anthology: Scrummies was the providore doing a roaring trade in Toby’s Estate coffee and fritters, Edmonds & Greer used to tempt us with candles and ceramics and an onsite café, and Bitton Oatley had that large outdoor space with a kids' play area where parents could feel seen. Postcard From Napoli — run by Davide Piscopo and Dino Fonzone — even put Oatley on the map for Neapolitan-approved pizza, while Broadsheet reminded us Taste of Tang had been part of the fabric since 1985 (and yes, the Singapore chilli mud crab had to be ordered 24 hours in advance).

Our local pub, The Oatley Hotel, used to lean into country‑pub charm with comedy, trivia and beer pong, and Adora once gave the suburb a chocolatey monopoly. Then there was the small-town reality TV moment: a hoarder home in Oatley sold for $1.8 million at a charity auction for the Animal Welfare League NSW — because where else do you get real estate and community service rolled into one headline?

Who would hate living here? If you crave neon nightlife, non-stop CBD energy or the thrill of not knowing your neighbour’s business, you’ll be miserable. Who would love it? Families who use the Oatley Otters intergenerational playgroup at Carino Care, the kids at St George J Kids at the Uniting Church, walkers who worship the park, and anyone who thinks ordering a mud crab a day ahead is a perfectly reasonable life plan.

We’re small, leafy, and not trying to be anything else — and frankly, we like it that way.
