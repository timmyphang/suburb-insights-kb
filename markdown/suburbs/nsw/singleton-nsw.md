# Singleton (NSW)

Slug: `singleton-nsw`

At dusk we stand on the wrong side of nostalgia and watch the white-and-orange floodlights from the mines glow uneasy in the distance — Junkee put it best: at Singleton the lights look unearthly. It’s a strangely cinematic welcome to our town, like the end of a western if the cattle were coal seams.

Full disclosure: most of what we’re riffing on comes from older pieces — this is more “back in the day” than “what’s open this weekend.” So consider this a fond, slightly dusty postcard.

Morning routine: we drop the bub at the Singleton Library playgroup (listed at 8–10 Queen Street, Fridays 9:30am in Playgroup NSW) and trade parenting hacks with the crew from SingletonMoms.org. Someone will probably remind you about the Super Bolt Run or point you to the archives from the Singleton Gala 2017. Our support network also includes Singleton Family Support — “The Stables” on the corner of Bishopgate & Market Streets — which has quietly been doing the heavy lifting for two decades. We say “quietly” because loud is for floodlights, not good social work.

If you’re here for boutique hospitality, the internet will try to distract you with John Singleton’s projects — Saddles at Mount White with its sandstone homestead and dam-side deck, or the Bon Pavilion at Gosford — reminders that the name “Singleton” on a byline is sometimes a different postcode entirely. And whisky nerds will remember The Singleton and the old “Plus Two” serve from Junkee; yes, we have a dram-shaped legacy too.

Who would hate living here? If you need constant new restaurants, neon city sidewalks, or silence instead of mining glow, this won’t placate you. If you’re allergic to community halls, family fundraisers and a town that remembers its galas, steer clear.

Who would love it here? Families who value an actual playgroup, people who want a proper local support network, and anyone amused by living in the place that both inspired a whisky serve and hosts a dam-deck restaurant a little way out at Mount White. We’ll take the floodlights and the playgroups — and keep roasting ourselves with love.
