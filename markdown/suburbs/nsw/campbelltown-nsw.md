# Campbelltown (NSW)

Slug: `campbelltown-nsw`

Back in the day we’d kick off a Saturday in Campbelltown by arguing over whether to get artisan gelato from Scoop in McCarthur Square or a banh mi from Le’ss Bakery on Hoddle Avenue — both local legends, depending on how hangry we were. After that it was an obligatory stroll past the Campbelltown Arts Centre’s quiet courtyard, where Shaun Gladwell once revisited Leumeah skate park and our town’s art scene felt improbably metropolitan. Urban List even put the Arts Centre on a “best galleries” list for 2026, which made us beam and mutter about finally getting some cultural clout.

Weekends have always been pragmatic. If you wanted cheap cinematic thrills we’d ghost up to Dumaresq Street Cinema — the $8.50 sessions felt like a civic service. For blockbuster drama, Event Cinemas Campbelltown once debuted ScreenX here, bringing 270-degree chaos to our local multiplex. Markets on Lithgow Street — Handmade and Homegrown — were where we pretended to be arty while buying breakfast and dodging the parents from playgroups at Pembroke Park and Airds.

The petty local drama file is good value. Back then a reimagined Romeo & Juliet at Campbelltown Performing Arts High got a Mark Latham-shaped kerfuffle in parliament over a lesbian kiss — classic Western Sydney publicity pivot. On a lighter note, Mickey the cockatoo briefly became our unofficial mascot when he got stuck inside Coles and everyone fell in love with his four-week residency.

Who would hate living here? People who can’t handle choice: we’ve got Dharawal National Park and the Australian Botanic Garden if you fancy bushwalking, Club Menangle if you like harness racing, and a surprising mix of microbreweries and Polish delis if you’re indecisive about dinner. If you need your weekends curated by glossy suburb guides, sorry — we prefer a DIY itinerary.

Who would love it here? Families (hello Play Strong sessions at Pembroke Park and NurtureGroup support at St Helens Park), art obsessives, bargain cinema hunters and anyone who likes their culture served with a side of community markets. We’re Campbelltown: a bit rough-around-the-edges, embarrassingly proud, and always ready to surprise you with a scoop of gelato or a ghost tour at Quondong Cottage.
