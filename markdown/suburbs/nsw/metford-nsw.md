# Metford (NSW)

Slug: `metford-nsw`

Back in the day, our Saturday started with glitter in the carpet and questions about whether glue sticks are a food group. Playgroup NSW used to list Metford Community meeting Wednesday mornings at 9:30am at Metford Community Hall — the little corner of the world at Cnr Ingall & Lowe Sts, 2323 — and if you weren’t there, you missed Jane Georgopoulos handing out the weekly craft like it was an Olympic sport. She volunteered for the same playgroup she attended in the late 1980s and somehow made paper plates feel like a legacy; she prepared projects for about 12–13 very committed tiny artists.

If we’re honest, Metford has always been quietly family-run. MumsPages used to keep a rolodex of what mums needed here — from service providers to kids’ karate classes — because when you live where Willow Drive Inclusive Community Playspace (aka Wattle Close Park) is a feature, you plan your life around slides and toddler swings. Newy with Kids catalogued that playground’s group swings, low slides and climbing frame/cubby; it’s the sort of place where ankle-high council bureaucracy and high-vis vests quietly make inclusivity happen.

For the more adventurous among us, Tenambit BMX Track — on Metford Road at Tenambit, near East Maitland — was the local “little gem” according to local guides. It sat inside the Beryl Humble Sporting Complex with a proper starting ramp, picnic spots under gum trees and the consoling practicality of public toilets and water fountains. The guide even noted you could ride it at your own risk, with organised sessions via Maitland City Council if you felt like being sensible.

Petty drama? The archives are disappointingly wholesome. We don’t have headlines about nightlife spats or boutique wars — mostly playgroups, directories and BMX jump lists. So if you loathe community singalongs, glitter craft and inclusive playground planning meetings, Metford will test your patience.

Who would love it here? Young families, grandparents who like to recycle their childhoods (we see you, Jane), and anyone who thinks a BMX track under gum trees sounds like a weekend well-spent. For years the local story has been the same: small, practical, and utterly built for kids — in case you’re wondering, that’s exactly the point, and we wouldn’t change it.
