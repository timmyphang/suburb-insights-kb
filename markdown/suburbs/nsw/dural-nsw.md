# Dural (NSW)

Slug: `dural-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start a weekend like proper locals: one of us would be at Swayne’s Dural (490 Galston Road) before 9am, pretending we only needed a single succulent and walking out with a trolly full of roses. Another would claim they were “just popping to the market” and end up at the Dural Growers Market at Dural Country Club (662a Old Northern Road) buying enough sourdough to feed a minor sporting league.

For brunch we used to fancy ourselves very cultured — Wild Pear Cafe (658 Old Northern Road) for the view and Instagram-friendly desserts, or Wolfe & Co when we wanted to feel like we’d stumbled into a Hills District food moment. There was also The Vicar, the old sheep-shearing sheds grown into a very big pub with Mrs Goldsmith wine bar and Wakefield’s restaurant, which made pretending to be fancy embarrassingly easy. And yes, the Benzin folks put a thing or two here before branching out into Pitt Street.

We’re not all rustic idyll, mind you. Council papers and coffee-shop conversations once circled the Derriwong Road & Old Northern Road rezoning proposal — remember the plan for 110 low‑density dwellings and a 4,000 sqm park? Then there was the first stage approval for the Skyline Dural gateway at 488 Old Northern Road: road upgrades, a supermarket, parking. It gave the suburb the official “coming-of-age” glow and provided ample material for letters to the council and sighing over the footpath.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who needs trams at midnight, 24/7 laneway espresso or a rooftop bar within walking distance. If your idea of Saturday is back-to-back galleries and cabaret, you’ll be lonely between the nurseries and the Christmas tree farms (Dural Christmas Tree Farm, 879 Old Northern Road — yes, that one) — though you might admit the novelty of picking a fir still wins on nostalgia.

Who would love it? Families who adore The Centre Dural and the Dural Musical Society at Dural Soldiers Memorial Hall (604 Old Northern Road), gardeners who worship Swayne’s or Honeysuckle Garden’s production, and anyone who finds comfort in a sprawling pub (Duke of Dural included), a farmer’s market, and a playground like Moorfield Hills Reserve on Sebastian Drive. We’ll roast you gently, Dural, because we live here and we wouldn’t trade the acreage, the markets and the absurdly large venues for anything.
