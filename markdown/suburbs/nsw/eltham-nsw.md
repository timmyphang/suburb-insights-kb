# Eltham (NSW)

Slug: `eltham-nsw`

We’re the lot who know the Eltham that isn’t a suburb on a Melbourne train map — the one tucked in the lush tropical hills between Byron Bay and Lismore. Back in the day the internet mostly had one particular headline for us: yes, the Eltham Hotel, with its retro paint job and towering palm trees, is the thing the guidebooks fell in love with.

Our weekend looks the same as it’s always been in those snippets: we idle into the Eltham Hotel’s expansive outdoor area, pretend we meant to book a room in one of the five guest rooms, and pretend even harder that we understand the chef’s new menu. Time Out tells us the place was built early 1900s, restored in 2019 and had its food given a polish by ex-Saint Peter chef Alanna Sapwell-Stone — so when the plates arrive we nod like we’ve been reading the reviews all morning.

There’s not much local scandal in our inbox of articles — the coverage reads more like a love letter to the pub than any pitched suburban drama. Most of the “news” is: lovely pub, live local acts most Sundays, upgraded food, arrive hungry. Urban List and Time Out both did that thing where they point at our pub and say “postcard charm”; we don’t blame them. It’s hard to roast a place that practically pays in sunsets and palm fronds.

Who would hate living here? If you need tram timetables, cocktail bars on every corner or a farmers’ market that’s actually a boutique food festival, you’ll be bored stiff. If you think “country pub” is a metaphor for michelin-star pretension, you’ll find our retro paint job offensive.

Who loves it here? People who want a proper getaway without pretending to be on a vineyard retreat: those who like live music on a Sunday, an honest meal that’s been tidied up by a proper chef, and a pub that still looks like a postcard. We’re small, leafy and unbothered — and if that’s too quaint, well, there are worse places to be ignored by the travel pages.
