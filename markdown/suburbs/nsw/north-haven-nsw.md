# North Haven (NSW)

Slug: `north-haven-nsw`

We stand on the footpath and squint across the inlet like everyone’s watching the tide for a plot twist — the Camden Haven Inlet does most of the heavy lifting around here. Back in the day the internet’s idea of North Haven usually boiled down to one thing: the bowling green. As Urban List noted, Club North Haven sat roughly a kilometre from North Haven Beach, about 30 minutes south of Port Macquarie, and after a multi‑million dollar renovation in 2017 it was the kind of waterfront bowling club other towns named their best-case scenario after.

Our weekend routine is gloriously undemanding. We check the inlet, argue (politely) about whether the pelicans are conspiring, and remember that the club used to be the place for the kind of coastal social life that doesn’t require pretence — just a lawn, a glass, and someone who can keep a straight scorecard. The beach is a minute‑matter away and the horizon does the aesthetic work for us; no artisanal backstory required.

The internet gets confusing about which North Haven is which. Time Out and Sitchu love a good coast‑park yarn — they keep talking about a continuous walking and cycling corridor from North Haven to Sellicks Beach — but that’s a South Australian thing, not our local geography. Which is probably for the best; we like that people have to double‑check which state we’re in before they try to book a coastal hike.

Who would hate living here? People who need a buzzy nightlife map with neon arrows and three cocktail bars per intersection. Also anyone allergic to lawn bowls, which is a very specific population but, frankly, we respect their taste.

Who’d love it here? Bowlers who appreciate a renovated club with a view, people who consider a kilometre stroll to the beach an acceptable daily pilgrimage, and anyone who thinks a quiet inlet and an honest bowling green are underrated forms of culture. We’re not trying to be everything — we do the calm, the sea, and the odd bit of dignified competition very well. Come for the inlet; stay because you forgot where else you were meant to be.
