# Long Jetty (NSW)

Slug: `long-jetty-nsw`

We know the feeling: you step onto that jetty and it keeps going — Sitchu reckons it’s “the longest in NSW”, jutting more than 350 metres out over Tuggerah Lake — and by the time you get to the end we’ve already decided whether you’re here for nostalgia or for the bakery crumbs.

Back in the day our weekend routine was very specific. We used to swing past Burnt Honey Bakery (Time Out’s favourite carb shop on the main drag), then wander up The Entrance Road to Green Tangerine — the bakery-on-site cafe at 393 The Entrance Road that opened in 2014 — and pretend we weren’t being judged by our thrifted shoes. Broadsheet once wrote about the Glass Onion Society’s rescue-furniture charm and all-day breakfast; Concrete Playground later listed it as closed, and we still miss the place when a live gig was the cure for a small-town Tuesday.

The Savoy has always been our indulgent drama: a 1950s picture palace that ran for 20 years before closing for four decades, and later revived into a two-tiered bar with a balcony you could half expect to see a movie reel hanging from. Concrete Playground gave that revival the local thumbs-up — because nothing says civic pride like fixing up a giant neon sign and pretending we invented nostalgia.

We’ve got boutiques and oddities too — Tiki La La and Thanks Tattoo were doing their curated thing in town, and Komuniti and Albie’s Coffee Bar were the sort of one-block-back gems Sitchu celebrated. Hernando’s Hideaway Mexican Kitchen even made the local dining map on someone’s home-tour roundup.

Who would hate living here? If you need a shiny, anonymous high street full of chain predictability, or you can’t cope with thrift-store furniture making you feel like you’re being stylistically assessed, Long Jetty will make you twitch. If 350 metres of pier and a main street that smells of fresh bread and second-hand vinyl gives you existential dread, move to a suburb with less character.

Who would love it here? First-home buyers hunting the Central Coast vibe, anyone who rates an eclectic cafe scene and vintage shops over mall-slick sameness, and people who think a long walk on a very long jetty is a perfectly reasonable afternoon. We do, obviously — we live here.
