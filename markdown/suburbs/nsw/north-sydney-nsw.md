# North Sydney (NSW)

Slug: `north-sydney-nsw`

Back in the day we used to brag that North Sydney was the sensible sibling of the Harbour Bridge — all suits, sensible pubs and the occasional rooftop pretending to be Tulum. These days we still are, except our Tulum is called Four Hundred and our rooftop cocktails started turning up on lists.

Mornings for us mean a circuit: market day at Civic Centre Park (220 Miller Street) for the North Sydney Market — second Saturday of the month, unless it’s January, because even markets need a holiday — then maybe a walk through St Leonards Park to the weekly parkrun on Ridge Street if we’re pretending we like endorphins. If you were into first-looks, you’d remember the Walker Street precinct arriving with Poetica, Bar Lettera and Rafi making the concrete feel a touch tropical. And yes, Mensho Tokyo once pitched up here with its farm-to-bowl chicken soup; we read about it, admired it and nodded like proud hosts.

Our petty local drama reads like a council memo: North Sydney Council’s road-safety campaign — the much-discussed “Take A Look Around” — got roasted in the press for being, well, ambitious. Then there was the Shore School table-throwing incident; kids will be kids, but we still prefer our fights to be about trivia nights, not classroom furniture. We also had the novelty of a big festival — the North Sydney Festival with Ben Lee and Sarah McLeod — streets closed, kids entertained, and the CBD briefly jealous.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe walkability, harbour-adjacent parks, and the faint whiff of corporate shirts colliding with bakers’ aprons, North Sydney will make you twitch. If you want anonymity, forget it — everyone here has an opinion about the next dining precinct, Victoria Cross plans, or which bakery used to sell the best babka.

Who loves it? Families who use Stanton Library and community playgroups at 3 Cunningham Street, runners who queue for parkrun, food people who remember Genzo, Rafi or Blessed Fruit Pies on Miller Street (yes, the bakery that used to have two entrances into Greenwood Plaza), and anyone who thinks having a roof over your rooftop bar is an optional extra. We’re the place where the Harbour Bridge commute ends and the good life begins — slightly officious, secretly warm, and very fond of markets.
