# Collaroy Plateau (NSW)

Slug: `collaroy-plateau-nsw`

We stand on the Plateau like we own the horizon — which, technically, we sort of do: one glance down Pittwater Road and there’s Collaroy Beach stretching its beginner-surf welcome, the rock pool quietly bragging a proper 50m lap, and the playground doing its civic duty to tire out every toddler within a three-kilometre radius.

Our morning is a well-choreographed ritual. We used to swing by Upper Crust for a pie (as Time Out and Urban List reminded everyone who asked where real Northern Beaches pastry lives), wander past the ocean baths at Collaroy Rock Pool for a half-hour of pretending to be fit, then loop back along the sand where Merivale’s reshuffle — The Collaroy and the sandside Surf Deck — utterly rearranged our sunset options. Merivale’s makeover, and the tiki-leaning Surf Deck that followed, didn’t exactly sneak in; it politely elbowed the old pub into something glossy, seaside and very, very Instagrammable. Concrete Playground and Broadsheet ran the receipts.

The small local scandal — if you call tasteful tropical cocktails and a multi-storey seaside venture a scandal — was that the beachfront got a glow-up and we all had to agree it looked lovely. No feral back-alley paste-ups, just woodfired pizza options and north-facing views that made the whole community suddenly very proud and suspiciously well-dressed for a surf-adjacent venue.

Who would hate living here? Someone allergic to playgrounds, lap pools, and families. Someone who resents daytime pie culture, long lunches with ocean views, or the fact that a homewares shop like Arikaya used to double as an excuse to snoop at teak sideboards and then get coffee. Also, if you crave 24/7 neon nightlife, this isn’t your postcode — the Plateau favours sunsets and surf clubs over club queues.

Who would love it? Families who walk to Collaroy Plateau Public School, people who like long walks to Narrabeen Lake, the serious swimmers who worship the rock pool, the pilates crowd who’d name-drop Plank Pilates Studio, and anyone who thinks beachside dining — however Merivale-shaped — is a perfectly reasonable way to spend an afternoon. Back in the day we were quiet; now we’re quietly smug.
