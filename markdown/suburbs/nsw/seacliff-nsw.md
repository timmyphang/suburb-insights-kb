# Seacliff (NSW)

Slug: `seacliff-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start the weekend by pretending we live in a postcard: the Sea Cliff Bridge skimming the horizon, the wind trying to rearrange our hair into something artisanal. We’ll take the walk because Broadsheet once lumped the Glenelg to Seacliff Walk into a national list of “walks that finish with a swim,” and who are we to argue with a list? It’s the kind of walk that makes you feel virtuous for 45 minutes, then very wet and justified.

By mid-morning you’d end up at Bald Hill Lookout at Stanwell — yes, the one Sitchu name‑checked — buying ice cream and pretending we meant to watch para‑gliders, not just take content for Instagram. The view does the heavy lifting: dramatic coastline, brave people launching themselves and our small talk into the air, and us applauding like we’d arranged the whole thing.

The local nightlife used to be very simple: the Seacliff Beer Garden was the place to be, at least according to older guides. Those were the halcyon days of tables, huts and a beer list we could pronounce with minimal shame. These mentions are from older roundups, though, so think nostalgic brochure rather than live events calendar — we’re more “remember when” than “here’s tonight’s trivia host.”

Petty drama? The archives are shy. The stories that exist are the gentle kind — walking routes, lookout ice‑cream, a beer garden that earned a write‑up — not the council feuds or reality-TV levels of chaos. Which, to be honest, suits us. We’re low on scandal and high on scenic.

Who would hate living here: anyone who needs a 24/7 novelty spinner or can’t cope with para‑gliders stealing their thunder. Also the sort who insists on up‑to‑the-minute foodie hot takes — our press clippings are mainly older travel and camping roundups (Concrete Playground’s coastal camping guide even reminds you to BYO water), so plan accordingly.

Who would love it: people who want coastlines that pull their shoulders down, walks that end in salty hair, and a hometown that reads like an affectionate travelogue. We’ll keep the bridge selfies warm for you.
