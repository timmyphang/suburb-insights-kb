# Moolap (NSW)

Slug: `moolap-nsw`

Back in the day we used to roll up to OneHope Baptist Church on the Bellarine Highway like it was the town square — not because we were particularly devout, but because the playgroup had indoor toys, an undercover area and a grassed patch with a sandpit that made us feel like we’d won at suburban parenting. As OneHope Playgroups noted (Apr 2, 2024), entry was at the front and the car parks were “adequate”, which in local language means you could squeeze in the ute and still pretend you’re organised.

If you want proof Moolap likes being part of a gang, look no further than the community notice boards. Find It Locally cheerfully lumps us in with Newcomb, Breakwater and Whittington — identity crisis solved, we’re the postcode that’s happy to share. The Moolap Community Notice Board (postcode 3221) used to be where you found everything from sausage sizzles to lost chook notices, or at least that’s what the hub page implied.

Childcare? We weren’t short on options. CareforKids listed 17 childcare services in Moolap (3224) with vacancies and an average cost of $148.00/day, and even the family day care roster included Little Aussie Sprouts FDC and Julie’s Family Day Care. K 4 Kids Early Learning at 158–160 Bellarine Hwy showed up on the listings with long hours (6:30am–6:30pm) — handy if your life had decided to become a roster sheet.

Who would hate living here? Someone who insists their suburb be a standalone celebrity. If you want a postcode that struts alone, Moolap’s comfortable with being the bit in the spreadsheet that supports everyone else. Also, if you’re allergic to playgroups, noticeboards or sensible childcare hours, this won’t be your scene.

Who would love it here? Young families and anyone who likes a community that pins its life up on a noticeboard, queues at a playgroup and treats the Bellarine Highway like an extended driveway. We’re low-key, practical and oddly proud of it — and yes, the sandpit was excellent.
