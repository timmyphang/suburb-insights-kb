# Duffys Forest (NSW)

Slug: `duffys-forest-nsw`

We roll out of the suburbs like we own the bush — practically do. Our Saturday begins with a bumpy turn off Birramal Road to Namba Road, where, back in the day, Sydney Christmas Tree Farm used to line up hundreds of pines and make festive indecision into a full-contact sport. The Urban List and Time Out both pointed to the place at 6 Namba Road (enter from Birramal Road), and yes, we remember the booking scramble — Time Out even noted the calendar opened at 8pm on a Monday in November, like some arboreal ticket drop.

If you want refinement, you’ll have to travel. Time Out once suggested setting out from the edge of Duffys Forest for day walks that plunge you into bushland that “feels a million miles from the hustle.” That’s not marketing hyperbole — it’s a promise our locals keep. We know every track that disappears into the gumtrees and every spot where the mozzies file their union paperwork.

There’s no juicy local council soap in the clippings — the press about us reads like a suburban catalogue: Christmas trees, easy hikes, property listings and directories. Sitchu treated Duffys Forest like a boutique listing market — remember that 15 Kinka Road snippet? — and even the dog-lovers got a mention: 413 Killawarra Road appeared in a round-up of luxury dog boarding options. MumsPages quietly catalogued our childcare, party suppliers and mums’ services, because yes, families live here and yes, they need birthday balloons too.

Who would hate it here? If you crave streetlight nightlife, late-night shopping or a cafe strip where you can be judged for your weekend sunglasses, this is not your vibe. If folding yourself into a booking queue for a Christmas tree gives you hives, best stick to the city.

Who’d love it? Bushwalkers, people buying their first proper patch of bushland, families who appreciate a good directory link on MumsPages, and anyone who wants their dog treated like royalty — Sitchu’s dog-boarding mentions didn’t lie. We’re low-key, a touch rural and stubbornly proud of it. Come for the trees (or the memory of them), stay for the silence between the birds.
