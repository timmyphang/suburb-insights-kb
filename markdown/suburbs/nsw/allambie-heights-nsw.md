# Allambie Heights (NSW)

Slug: `allambie-heights-nsw`

We begin every Saturday at the Allambie Heights Village Café, where three Subaru Foresters, a mud-specked Toyota Prado and a gleaming Tesla Model Y argue about parking etiquette outside while someone orders an almond flat white and sighs into their oat milk cup. We are the parents who time our drop-offs to the school bell, double-knot our Lululemon, and pretend the Fitbit’s “active minutes” came from a trail run rather than the school gate shuffle.

Our morning routine is a ritual: 6:30am F45 at the studio on Allambie Road, a quick scroll through the neighbourhood Facebook group while we sip single-origin coffee, then farmers market for ethically-sourced sourdough and organic spinach. By 9am we’re walking the reserve track—Allambie Heights Reserve, if you must—Cavoodle on a retractable leash, comparing dog-park anecdotes about whose pup finally mastered “leave it.” Sunday is prune-the-grevillea DIY time, a tidy of the native garden beds and a barbecue with neighbours who swear their Weber is merely “for social purposes.”

Our petty local drama reads like soap opera: a fortnight-long online spat about whether the Village Café should serve “proper milk” after one person dared to complain about oat milk flat whites. There’s also the weekend parking war outside the reserve during junior soccer—complete with a pencilled-in roster on the community page dictating who may use which verge. Someone once planted an unauthorised veggie patch on a nature strip and the fallout lasted three weeks; we still bring it up at the post-Pilates catch-up.

We watch for badges of belonging: the person in black Lululemon leggings with a Subaru Forester and a reusable coffee cup; the household with both a mud-splattered Prado and a bike rack for “mountain” aspirations; the new family who joined the Allambie Heights Village Mums group and immediately offered to coordinate the sausage sizzle.

Who would hate living here? The festival-loving single with a mop-top haircut who cycles a fixie, refuses to own a ute and thinks activewear is a lifestyle choice rather than a uniform would last two weekends before missing the inner-city chaos—because they’ll hate the school-gate small talk, the verge parking politics and everyone’s gentle insistence that 6am bootcamps are normal.

Who would love it? If you’re a 38-year-old school-gate captain who treasures early F45 classes, ethically-sourced sourdough, Cavoodle-walk afternoons and neighbours who will borrow sugar and return a casserole, you’ve found your tribe. Welcome to Allambie Heights.
