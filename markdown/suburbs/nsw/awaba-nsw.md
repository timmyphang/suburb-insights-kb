# Awaba (NSW)

Slug: `awaba-nsw`

We know we’re home when the piccolo arrives at Gully Grind at 7am, a Toyota Hilux idling outside with a single reusable cup and a sticker that reads “Tree Hugger, Off‑Road Edition.” We queue, we smile, we post the selfie at the Awaba State Forest lookout five minutes later and call it humblebragging with eucalyptus. We call our front yards gallery lawns and whisper “bush corridor” while edging out the hydrangea hedge on Wattle Crescent — environmental stewardship, as long as it matches our Instagram grid.

Our morning is ritual: the same piccolo, ordered with exact cadence, tiny latte art admired like a trophy. The school drop‑off is a Lululemon parade at Station Street, Ford Rangers and Prado rows gleaming because Sundays mean polishing the Prado until it tells the neighbours its life story. By 8:10am a neat line of Prado, Ranger and the occasional Hilux is doing an intricate ballet at Awaba station — coffee cups on dashboards as provenance, seats reserved like Wimbledon finals, family pride on the line.

We are passionately territorial about bins. The great Green‑Lid Feud of last winter started when Tina put hers out on Tuesday instead of Wednesday. It escalated to labeled photos posted on the Awaba Community Facebook group, then a printed letter nailed to the noticeboard on Glenview Drive; someone photocopied the thread and left it at the bakery. We’ve had more drama over recycling days than a reality TV reunion, and yes, we will bring receipts.

Weekends are carefully curated: piccolo at Gully Grind, school sports drop‑off, a 10am bushwalk at Awaba State Forest for five photos and two boomerangs, then hammock downtime with a craft beer or a cold‑press juice. Myrtle, the Labradoodle, does her part with impeccable timing for the slow‑motion sunset clip. There’s ritual herbicide‑free mowing for biodiversity optics and an annual Weber deployment in the Ranger tray, because community is grill smoke and shared sausages.

If you commute by lycra and scoot — the kind who cycles everywhere, drinks instant coffee and has a sourdough starter but no BBQ or ute — you will hate us. You’ll be baffled by hedge height social currency, outraged that footpaths are often secondary to our relationship with four‑wheel‑drive, and scandalised by the market produce passion.

If you like polished utes, structured outdoor selfies, a Labradoodle that knows the morning route and a town where everyone signs petitions after checking the lighting on their feed, you’ll absolutely love Awaba. Welcome.
