# Greenwich (NSW)

Slug: `greenwich-nsw`

Back in the day we’d catch the ferry over, fold out a deck chair and pretend we were on permanent holiday — Time Out even told people to “take a ferry to sleepy Greenwich and relax at the suburb's enclosed harbour baths.” The ferry still smells faintly of sunscreen and ambition, and the kiosk hands out gelato like it’s a civic duty. We know which bench gets the sun at low tide and which slip‑way has the best view of the Lane Cove River — yes, some Sitchu listings brag about “sweeping vistas over Lane Cove River” and we’re not arguing.

Our weekend looks suspiciously similar every week: ferry in, wander the foreshore parks, lob a picnic in Binney Park or head to the Bendheim Western Greenwich Civic Center for whatever the kids are into this month. GreenwichMoms keeps a firmer calendar than most councils — their day‑camp and preschool guides are the kind of local intel we’d actually share (begrudgingly) with newcomers.

We like to remind people that Greenwich isn’t all showhomes and harbour porn. The Hairdresser has been in the village since 1982, doing chemical‑free work on curls and fine hair while quietly outlasting trends. And yes, Sitchu will sell you the beachfront penthouse view if you’re into that sort of thing; we’ll wave from the foreshore while you sign the contract.

There’s also the political wardrobe: our name keeps turning up in headlines because Alex Greenwich, our local MP, is visible — he’s marched in Mardi Gras and been the subject of the odd online kerfuffle (the Mark Latham tweet saga pops up in the archives if you want drama). We don’t mind; we’ll wear the spotlight like a linen shirt at a harbour lunch.

Who would hate it here? If you crave 24/7 nightlife, endless hipster reinvention, or a postcode where every laneway has its own Instagram filter, Greenwich will bore you blissfully. Who’d love it? Families, ferry commuters, people who want baths, parks and a salon that remembers your name. For years we’ve loved the quiet parade of small pleasures — and we’ll keep pretending that gelato on a deck chair is peak metropolitan luxury.
