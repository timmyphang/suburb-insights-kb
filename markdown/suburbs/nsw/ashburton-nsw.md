# Ashburton (NSW)

Slug: `ashburton-nsw`

Back in the day we’d stroll down from the train, jug of toddler snacks in one hand and a scruffy dog on the other, and queue at Joe Frank like it was the only Italian-Aussie pilgrimage worth making. Concrete Playground called it “the new go-to” after it took over the old Resident Cafe — and yes, we still brag about their homemade crumpets and the sunny courtyard that lets your mutt feel like a café influencer. (As Concrete Playground put it, it’s “across the road from Ashburton Train Station,” so don’t blame us for the morning scrum.)

Our weekend loop was honest and efficient: High Street — aka Ashburton Village — for the essentials, a detour to Markham Reserve for the kids to burn off sugar on the adventure playground, and maybe a wine stop at McGlashan’s Estate if we were pretending this was a write-up for adults. Time Out called Markham Reserve a proper family hit, with BBQs, skate spots and an off-leash area, which is code for “bring sunscreen and the dog’s squeaky ball.”

We had tasteful shopping, too. Kiss With Style used to stage our small-home styling fantasies like a showroom for people who liked cushions more than chaos. Mitch Studio cropped haircuts into our lives (Time Out mentioned Tara‑Lee Mitchell) when we wanted to look like we’d made an effort without actually trying. And for a while the Mill and Bakery did its thing on our lunchtime radar — Time Out even flagged it — though the listing now reads (CLOSED), which is the adult version of someone moving away.

Petty local drama? Mostly renovation logistics and playgroup relocations. The Camberwell Structured Playgroup moved into the Children’s Centre at Ashburton Uniting Church in 2023 while its hall got a makeover; we RSVP’d to the chaos and brought more snacks. The playgroup’s 2026 raffle even bundled up Ashburton Pool & Recreation memberships and Real Toys Ashburton vouchers — small-town prizes, big-town pride.

Who would hate it here: nightclub rats and anyone who thinks “village” means a late-night parade of neon. Who would love it: walkers, dog owners, families who like good coffee and better cheesecake (Urban List once name-checked Joe Frank’s), and people who appreciate a suburb that trades trend-for-trend authenticity.

We know our streets, our cafes (past and present), and our park bench politics. We’ll keep the afternoon espresso nostalgia and the suburban brag — you bring the sunscreen.
