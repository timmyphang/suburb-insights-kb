# Airport West (NSW)

Slug: `airport-west-nsw`

Airport West, NSW — back in the day we’d brag about two things: being annoyingly close to an international terminal and having a bakery that took bread very seriously. For years the local story was more back-of-house than boulevard: Broadsheet told us Backhaus Bakery set up wholesale in Airport West in 2022 (with Melissa Matthew), and that its loaves and sticky cinnamon scrolls were being delivered down the road to Essendon. We still talk about those 48‑hour ferments like it’s a local miracle.

Our weekend routine used to look like this: someone would drop at the old Skyways Hotel on 113 Matthews Ave for a function that was definitely not fancy, the kids would be dragged to the Airport West Shopping Centre to have their Santa photos taken, and the deli run would include a pit-stop at Deli Boy on 22A Fraser Street if you’d been good enough to bring the dog. As Sitchu put it, Deli Boy was one of the spots dog-owners could claim as their own — and we did.

We are small enough to have eccentricities. Pedestrian once put Sexyland — the niche little adult store tucked by the airport/Tullamarine fringe — on a list of hyper-niche places, which is the kind of notoriety we wear like a neon sign. Time Out reminds us Cesar Duran’s El Cielo started its tortilleria days here in 2012, so yes, we’ve quietly fed Melbourne’s taco curiosity more than once.

Local drama? Not much recent ink — mostly playgroups (shout-out to APW Cuties) and directories listing psychologists, art classes at The Studio School of Art (Level 1, 13–15 King Street) and the usual services you tap when life gets real. No scandal to narrate, just a steady spool of families, shift workers and people who like their cinnamon scrolls flaky.

Who would hate it here? If you need boutique boulevards and perpetual brunch columns, you’ll feel cheated. Who would love it? Anyone who prizes practicality, decent baked goods and an honest function room at Skyways for a Saturday night with awkward speeches.

We’re not flashy. We’re the place that quietly delivers the goods — and the scrolls.
