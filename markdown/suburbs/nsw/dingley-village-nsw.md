# Dingley Village (NSW)

Slug: `dingley-village-nsw`

We live in Dingley Village, and nothing says suburban plot twist like being the quiet place mums bookmarked for soft-play while, apparently, also being earmarked for the Southern Hemisphere’s biggest wave park. Back in the day we’d drop the kids at SuperZu or the Kid Factory — volcanos, trampolines and themed party rooms — then try to have a “coffee” at Strange Servant, the inner‑city‑style cafe that opened in December 2017 with Industry Beans on the go, a mural by WA WA WA Designs and Danielle Weber, and the kind of menu that made us feel a little fancy for the whole block. As Broadsheet noted, the same owner even spawned Mr Patterson in Patterson Lakes a few weeks later, because why stop at one boutique caffeine clone?

Weekends were a choreography of kid-centred commerce: a quick round of Goanna Golf at Spring Park Golf Course (yes, The Burrow and The Gully were listed among Melbourne’s best mini-golf) before the circus under the big top at Boundary Rd & Centre Dandenong Road when Elmo rolled through. Then there was Delta Force Paintball at 1C Grange Rd — movie-set arenas and a real USAF aircraft for backdrop — which, as Time Out reported, had been closed temporarily with plans to reopen. And for the parents who like Pilates-adjacent calm, State of Soul had the kids’ yoga listing on MumsPages so someone could at least touch their toes between snack runs.

The local headline-grabber, of course, was SurfnPlay (or Surf n Play/Surf 'n' Play depending on the outlet) — Pellicano’s mega aqua-park that Broadsheet said had plans lodged in 2019 and was sitting with Kingston City Council. We’re an unassuming pocket between Moorabbin and Mordialloc and somehow on the shortlist to host a wave machine that would embarrass coastal towns. Try explaining that at a school gate.

Who would hate living here? People who can’t stand murals, mini-golf quirks, the persistent hum of toddler birthday parties, or the faint possibility that a six-wave-per-minute surf machine might appear next door. Who will love it? Families, playdate planners, mural appreciators, paintball tacticians and anyone who enjoys being the unexpectedly big thing in a quietly ordinary postcode. We’ll keep the cafes cosy and the party rooms booked — and if the wave park ever arrives, we’ll sell mullets and popcorn with ironic pride.
