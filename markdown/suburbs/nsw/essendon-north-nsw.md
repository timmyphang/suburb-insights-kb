# Essendon North (NSW)

Slug: `essendon-north-nsw`

We stand on Keilor Road like it's our own tiny Champs-Élysées and admit it: we still talk about the queue at Parisian Patisserie Boulangerie. As Broadsheet once put it, the place at 19 Keilor Rd was "a little slice of Paris" and, well, we treated it like an arrondissement. Back in the day you could time your morning by when the brioche ran out.

Our weekend vibe used to be very simple: amble down Buckley Street, argue about which old Broadsheet write-up was more accurate, then pick a table at North and Eight (285–287 Buckley Street) while pretending we were on a spacious European laneway. Urban List and Broadsheet battled the same patch of pavement for years — North and Eight, Cafe St Rose, even Hey Jude got their day in the sun — and we loved watching the local guide wars like they were footy finals.

If you want petty local drama, we’ll give you a mild one: food writers deciding whether Dua at 324 Keilor Road was the shiny new thing (Sitchu mentioned it among 2025 openings) or just the latest entrant in the long-running Essendon cafe carousel. It’s the kind of debate that really divides us — along the line of "baked-in-the-morning" versus "trendy new menu."

Who would hate living here? People who want nonstop nightlife, anonymous CBD anonymity or zero queues for pastries. Also, anyone allergic to small-town boasting about how our local playgroup scene is actually a community asset. The Lincoln Rd Early Years Centre in the heart of the North Essendon shopping precinct and the Moonee Valley Toy Library at 129 Lincoln Road used to host the kind of organised chaos that makes you either start a parenting blog or move to Tasmania.

Who would love it here? Families who like accessible playgroups and a village feel, food nerds who can recite which guide recommended which cafe, and anyone who appreciates that our biggest scandals are which bakery’s croissant was flakiest. We’re proud, a bit precious and still advertising our best bits through coffee reviews and directory listings — and honestly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
