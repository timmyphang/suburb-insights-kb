# Oak Park (NSW)

Slug: `oak-park-nsw`

We start our weekends like proper locals: someone suggests a “quick” run along the Cronulla Esplanade, and two hours later we’re arguing about whether Oak Park Beach was actually our idea. For years the travel write-ups have been confused and delighted by us — Time Out tucked Oak Park into the Bate Bay roll-call, Urban List gave the Esplanade a nod for scenic runs that pass our stretch of sand, and Concrete Playground proudly lists Oak Park as one of Sydney’s big marine sites. Back in the day (by which we mean whenever those articles were written), that meant one thing: snorkels out, ego in check.

Our morning loop is very specific: jog the Esplanade, detour to Oak Park Beach to inspect the tidepools, then stroll up to the Oak Park Pool on Jibbon St — yes, the natural‑floored swimming enclosure Time Out describes, the one that’s technically more “ocean habitat with lane ropes” than your average pool. If you like your mornings with possible seahorse sightings and the odd turtle cameo (Concrete Playground tells us they’re a regular), this is your jurisdiction. And if an octopus decides to photobomb your lane swim at Jibbon St, well, we always say hello for you.

Our petty local identity crisis — and it’s a small, charming one — is that the guides can’t decide if we’re a runner’s detour, a snorkeler’s secret, or a beach on a national-best list. We lean into it. We’ll take all three, thanks.

Who would hate living here? People who think beaches should come with a dress code, or anyone allergic to sand in every pocket. Also, if you’re the sort who needs your pool to be chlorinated and predictable, Oak Park Pool’s “natural‑floored” vibe will give you the vapours.

Who would love it here? Families who like low-key wonder (tidepools, turtles), runners who appreciate a scenic detour, snorkellers chasing seahorses, and anyone who enjoys bragging that their local beach made it into the Bate Bay conversation. As Time Out and Concrete Playground remind us — we’re not flashy, just reliably full of small marine miracles and big local pride.
