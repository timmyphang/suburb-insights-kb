# Lawson (NSW)

Slug: `lawson-nsw`

Back in the day we thought sharing a name with a famous food writer would mean limos and lined-up reservations. Instead, Lawson hums along like the polite cousin Nigella forgot to follow on Instagram. While Broadsheet and Concrete Playground were busy telling Sydney about Nigella’s Vivid dinners, we were following a different kind of itinerary: tracks, toddlers and a very good pie.

Our Saturday ritual — at least according to Blue Mountains Mums — was simple and stubbornly local. We used to plan a morning around the Five Waterfalls Walk (South Lawson / Waterfalls Loop Track): a neat 3km, easy–medium loop that lets you pretend you’re fit until the picnic rug reveals the truth. If we fancied a shorter plunge into the ferns, Dantes Glen Walking Track’s 2.1km return was our rainforest fix: three tiny waterfalls, a few smug selfie spots and enough leaf litter to make city folk squeal.

For years the local listings on Blue Mountains Mums kept us honest: Heatherbrae Play & Chat (1 Benang Street) was where new parents traded survival tips and spare nappies. And if someone boasted about “discovering” a bakery on the drive home, we’d point them to the one Urban List once praised — Black Cockatoo Bakery at 1 Staples Crescent — which featured in a roundup of country bakeries in NSW. That write-up felt like a Michelin star for people who wear hiking boots to lunch.

Petty local drama? There isn’t much. Our biggest argument is whether the Five Waterfalls Walk counts as a bushwalk or brunch warm-up — a debate that ends when the scone arrives. The internet’s interest in Nigella’s Sydney dinners was entertaining, but it didn’t change our version of Lawson: no red carpets, just real ones you wipe sand off.

Who would hate it here: anyone who needs a 24/7 cocktail bar, or who judges a suburb on the number of boutique gyms. Who would love it: families who actually read the Blue Mountains Mums page, hikers who measure success in waterfalls not espresso shots, and anyone who likes their bakery stops to come with a view. We’re small, a bit wet underfoot after rain, and perfectly unapologetic about it.
