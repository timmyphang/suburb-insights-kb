# Como (NSW)

Slug: `como-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start Saturday at the Como Pleasure Grounds like it’s our civic duty: pack a picnic, steer kids toward the boat structure and climbing net, and stake a patch of grass before the place fills up. As Mums of the Shire noted, the playground is a proper preschool magnet — swings, a climbing net and a boat kids pretend is a yacht they will inherit one day. There’s even a small public pool run by the Council for summer, which is adorable until it’s elbow-to-elbow and someone’s sunscreen is doing overtime.

Our weekend loop used to be ridiculously simple: playground, then one of the local eats. Mums of the Shire mentioned Waterfront Como Cafe, Thai Rim Nam and Blackfish Cafe and Grill; the Como Grill with its verandah and water views gets a shout when we’re feeling fancy-but-not-fancy. Revolution cafe and the little local shops are the pit stops that keep the weekday hustle honest. None of this is hot-take gossip — it’s the itinerary.

For those who like their social calendar organised by a spreadsheet and a thermos, Playgroup NSW once listed the School Of Arts Hall‑Como on Novara Crescent as the place to be, with a Monday session (fortnightly) and a Wednesday weekly session. We love that level of structure for tiny humans — and the endless parade of craft-making skills we never knew we had.

There isn’t much petty public feuding in our files (which is honestly preferable), so the real awkwardness here is practical: the upgraded reserve is brilliant for kids under seven, but some spots have no BBQs or toilets, so plan accordingly or accept your fate as the kombucha courier for a toddler army.

Who’d hate it here? If you loathe playgrounds, fenced toddler kingdoms, and the faint whiff of hot chips and craft stall glue at a winter market, Como will test your tolerance. Who’d love it? Families, picnic romantics and market-day converts — the Como Riverview Winter Market used to pack the place with craft, food and children’s entertainment and that’s exactly our vibe.

We’re proud of our small-park, big-heart suburb. It’s quieter than the city and more communal than you’d expect — and for a lot of us, that’s exactly the point.
