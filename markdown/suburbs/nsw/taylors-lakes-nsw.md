# Taylors Lakes (NSW)

Slug: `taylors-lakes-nsw`

We know the exact angle the sun hits the carpark at Watergardens on a Saturday — it’s the moment we decide whether we’re here for the parenting bingo or the retail therapy. We roll into 399 Melton Highway like it’s a second CBD: Big W, Target, Coles, Woolies all under one roof at Watergardens Shopping Centre, and now, if you like the idea of buying shampoo between kids’ craft workshops, Adore Beauty has set up shop — Shop T137, 13–14 Watergardens Town Centre — opened its second bricks‑and‑mortar here in 2025 and yes, we’ll queue for the in‑store treatments without shame.

Morning routine: drop the little ones at a school holiday camp listed on MumsPages, sneak into the library at Sydenham — Brimbank Libraries’ Sydenham Library is at 1 Station Street, where we occasionally find a family film screening or, in May 2026, an Arabic calligraphy workshop for 8–12 year olds. That’s our culture fix: small, earnest, and frequently sponsored by a community noticeboard.

Petty local drama? Oh, we’ve got lore. We still bring up the infamous “eviction season” email from Professionals Taylors Lakes — PedestrianTV ran the whole thing when a property manager called Christmas a season for rent collectors and a realtor, Jordan van den Berg (Purplepingers), found himself in the crosshairs. We gossip about it at the playground like it’s our version of Shakespeare.

Who would hate living here: anyone who needs artisanal everything or thinks “mall” is a dirty word. If you crave laneway coffee and boutique streetscapes, you’ll feel like Watergardens swallowed your inner-city aesthetic and spat out a carpark.

Who would love it here: families who want everything in one convenient postcode (3038, if you’re collecting trivia), parents who actually use school holiday listings on MumsPages, bargain hunters who cheer for a new supermarket, and anyone who appreciates that our cultural calendar ranges from Halloween at the Town Square to Arabic calligraphy at the library. We’re suburban, occasionally scandalous, and very good at family film nights. Come for the shops, stay for the community noticeboard drama.
