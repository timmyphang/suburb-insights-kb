# Blacksmiths (NSW)

Slug: `blacksmiths-nsw`

Back in the day the guides had an opinion and we listened — which is lucky, because otherwise we’d still be telling people the best way to find the beach was by following the smell of sunscreen. We’re the ones who’ve walked the Blacksmiths Breakwall at dawn, watched the tide play coy with Moon Island and warned the tourists that Grannies Pool used to be called Chanty’s Pool (yes, really).

Our morning routine is simple: park, if you can, near the boat ramp (it’s a pleasant 15-minute stroll from Swansea Bridge — 30 minutes return if you stop to take photos like a proper amateur), follow Gommera Street from the Pacific Highway into the roundabout and let the kids loose at Blacksmiths Playground. Lifeguards clock on from the September/October school holidays through to April, 8:30–5:00, so the beach’s patrolled stretch is almost as reliable as someone bringing fairy bread to a school fete.

We own being family-friendly. Newy with Kids and Lake Mac Family Life have said as much: this is the southerly end of Nine Mile Beach, calm enough for small humans and stubbornly honest about how much sand will end up in your car. The Swansea Bridge walk up to the top of the Breakwall takes about an hour if you like exertion; if you prefer exertion with snacks, make it two.

If you’re the sort who hates minor adventure — the people who faint at the thought of 30,000 vehicles crossing the Swansea Bridge every day — this isn’t your postcode. If you needed every latte named after a Scandinavian concept or insist that every lunch be photographed under foliage, you’ll be bored. Back in old guides, The Yoga Place Cafe (next to The Yoga Place Blacksmiths) and mentions of Villa Cafe and Mr. Sister Coffee were the local nods to civilisation; treat those as charming footnotes rather than gospel.

Who’ll love it here? Families, dog-walkers who appreciate a proper breakwall view, folk who like their beaches with lifeguards and a side order of tidal creek serenity. We’re small, salty and unshowy — and if that sounds like your kind of suburb, welcome to Blacksmiths.
