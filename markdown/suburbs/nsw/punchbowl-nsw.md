# Punchbowl (NSW)

Slug: `punchbowl-nsw`

You know the bit just after you get off the train and the air smells faintly of garlic sauce and baklava? That’s our welcome mat. We’ve been around long enough to watch Al Afrah’s sweet shop — the one Mohammed Saker’s folks opened in 1982 after leaving Lebanon — turn into the kind of landmark other suburbs try to photocopy. Nearby, Awafi Chicken’s original Lebanese charcoal chicken (and that famously punchy garlic sauce) put us on the takeaway map, and Lahori Dhaba used to set the Saturday morning tempo with Pakistani breakfasts and lamb shanks that make you forgive people for leaving the TV on too loud.

Full disclosure: all our source material is a bit of a time capsule — these pieces are from a few years back, so we’re nostalgically reporting rather than handing out current opening hours. Back in the day Broadsheet and Time Out documented our strip; Broadsheet even paired Andrew Quilty’s black-and-white photos with Jackie Dent’s prose to accompany SBS’s Once Upon a Time in Punchbowl. There’s been a lot of good press about how we do food and thrift: Afandi’s knafeh got Urban List’s affectionate nod, Uturn’s big outlet (95 Bonds Road if you like a postcode pilgrimage) made Time Out’s op-shop list, and Pedestrian still remembers our rack-city thrift cred.

Local drama? The internet wrote the loudest bit: the Trent from Punchy clip went full NFT for a minute, because of course it did. Otherwise it’s more of a slow-burn identity thing — club rooms buzzing on World Cup nights (Club Punchbowl got a shoutout), a mosque that features in city attraction guides, and a bus company name in the electric rollout chatter (Punchbowl Bus Company — hello, future).

Who would hate living here? If you’re allergic to good-value group dinners, relentless pastry temptation or the idea that your neighbour might know five places that do garlic sauce better than your local cafe, you’ll be miserable. Who would love it? People who want food worth queueing for, thrift stores you can lose an afternoon in, and a suburb that’s unapologetically itself — loud, generous and stubbornly delicious.
