# Gawler (NSW)

Slug: `gawler-nsw`

We live in Gawler, NSW — which, frankly, gets mistaken online for Gawler, South Australia so often we've started answering to both. Back in the day our Google trail was mostly throwbacks: Broadsheet ran that iconic lemon chicken recipe credited to Gawler Palace (the SA one), Junkee remembered Peter Brinkworth’s family rotisserie in Gawler where chicken salt first showed up in the 1970s, and travel pieces kept dragging us into the Barossa loop like we’d ordered it.

Our weekend ritual — if by “ritual” you mean clicking through a regional travel guide — involves looking at the Barossa Trail that links Gawler to Angaston and gawking at the list of cellar doors stretching from Gawler to Nuriootpa. Concrete Playground’s cycling-and-wine route treats Gawler’s heritage-listed buildings like a checkpoint on a very cultured pub crawl. Meanwhile, Sitchu used to describe Gawler as South Australia’s first country town and a magnet for tree‑changers and families, and even the humble visitor centre got a mention as “an invaluable first stop” with handy parking.

We don’t have any hot, current scoop in our files — everything above is from older write-ups — so this is half nostalgia, half public-service announcement: the Town of Gawler was listed with its council office at 43 High St, Gawler East, the Civic Centre at 89–91 Murray St, and the Civic Centre Library used to publish its hours and services (internet access, book groups, Justice of the Peace). There’s also a Zion Mother’s Group note from the halcyon 2009 days, for anyone cataloguing local grassroots history.

Who would hate living here? People who crave instant inner‑city coffee clout and refuse to be confused with a place that shows up on wine trails. Misplaced influencers who need a daily artisan precinct to document will grumble.

Who would love it? Families, tree‑changers, anyone who enjoys civic libraries that run book groups, and those who secretly adore being mistaken for the Barossa’s extended family. We’ll take the gentle identity crisis — it’s cheaper than a wine tour and the lemon chicken recipes are worth the mix‑ups.
