# Epping (NSW)

Slug: `epping-nsw`

Sunday arvo in Epping, we’re the ones who’ll tell you the good bits with a cheeky grin. Back in the day we’d wander down to Boronia Park for a picnic, or lace up for the Terrys Creek bushwalk — the kids loved the waterfall and the mums loved that Ryde and Parramatta Councils did a bit of fixing up (ParraParents, as if you needed reminding). 

Weekends used to follow a predictable, delicious loop: Sweetness the Patisserie on Oxford Street for something sugar-focused, then elbowing through the 30-seat squeeze at Penang Cuisine for a laksa that people kept writing home about (Broadsheet, Urban List, Sitchu all nodded). The Epping Club tried to fancy things up too — Rawson’s opened there and the club’s bistro ambition, fronted by chef Nick Whitehouse, felt like our suburb deciding it could be posher than its strip mall socks permitted (Broadsheet/Time Out).

We even flirted with nocturnal cult status: Time Out and Urban List covered our first-ever Epping Night Market at Boronia Park — kicked off April 24, ran on the last Friday of the month with more than 30 stalls from 5–9pm. For a minute there we were a destination, or at least a place you’d tell your out-of-town friend didn’t smell like an arterial road.

Petty local drama? Oh yes — we endured transport melodrama. Broadsheet once warned commuters the Chatswood–Epping rail line shut down for seven months and, if you lived here then, you remember the creative carpooling strategies and the mutual hold-the-line misery. Beschico sat opposite the station doing a steady takeaway trade while we navigated the chaos (Time Out).

Who’d hate living here: anyone craving late-night cocktail bars on every corner, or a suburb without a sensible bit of civic chaos — the rail disruptions and community-market hustle will test your patience. 

Who’d love it: families who want leafy parks, bushwalks and predictable laksa queues; people who like their suburb equal parts sensible and quietly ambitious. We’re not pretending to be Paddington, but we make a mean dessert, a fierce curry laksa and we’ll still show you a waterfall if you ask nicely.
