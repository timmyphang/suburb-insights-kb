# Clifton Hill (NSW)

Slug: `clifton-hill-nsw`

We like to pretend our mornings are cinematic: we used to amble down Queens Parade, past the neat Victorian shopfronts Broadsheet once praised, stopping for something housemade before ducking into a yoga class. Broadsheet and Time Out both made a fuss about how the cafes and restaurants cluster along Queens Parade — and, honestly, they were right. For years the local strip quietly did the boutique-and-bakery thing very well.

On a weekend we’d scout the old favourites: Open House (122 Roseneath Street) earned a lot of goodwill as a sunlit community hub while the team were working to get their liquor licence approved, and Uncle Drew (23 Groom Street) was the sunny corner cafe Concrete Playground kept writing home about for its rotating specials and Dukes coffee. Time Out even had a soft spot for Some Velvet Morning, the little venue that used to do live music five nights a week. Curious Grace’s Clifton Hill showroom was the place to go if you wanted your couch Scandinavian and your cushions existential.

Local drama? Mild and very tasteful. The biggest soap-opera subplot we had in the listings was the Open House liquor-licence saga — less shouting, more paperwork. We’ll take it.

Who would hate it here? Anyone who needs their nightlife to look like a festival lineup every night. Time Out and Broadsheet both noted we have fewer venues than neighbouring suburbs; if your life goal is to sample twenty bars in one evening, Clifton Hill will sigh and hand you a leaflet about leafy quiet. People who can’t abide a compact, curated strip will find our “small but mighty” claim underwhelming.

Who loves it? Families and small-crew weekenders — Playgroup Victoria points to Quarries Park Playground and the Yarra Bend spots for the kids. Yogis will miss the steady hum of Clifton Hill Yoga Studio (129 Queens Parade), which was still being written up as a big local hub. Pickleballers: Clifton Hill Tennis Club (2 Hoddle Street) was even getting social sessions. And if you’re after good pizza or charcoal chicken, Time Out’s write-ups of Gertrude Hotel (47 Spensley Street) and Cookshop Rotisserie had mouths watering.

Back in the day, this place was content to be cosy, leafy and quietly excellent. We wouldn’t have it any other way.
