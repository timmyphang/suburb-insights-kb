# Glendalough (NSW)

Slug: `glendalough-nsw`

We live in Glendalough, NSW — yes, that Glendalough, the one Google keeps sending tourists to and then apologising for. Back in the day the only solid thing on the internet about our name was everybody else’s Glendaloughs: the Irish valley with its sixth‑century monastic ruins and the Spinc and Glenealo Valley Walk (hello, Poulanass Waterfall and that very dramatic boardwalk), the Perth pocket that boasts Lake Monger and a 3.5 km trail running alongside the Mitchell Freeway, and even a cocktail mention in Sydney when Urban List flagged Bar Conte’s rhubarb Negroni made with Glendalough Rose gin.

Our morning routine is basically deflecting identity theft. We empty the bins, check the mail, and explain, for the hundredth time, that no, we don’t have glacial lakes or a medieval round tower in our backyard. People bring up Braveheart and P.S. I Love You as if we’ve got a film crew on permanent retainer — Time Out did point to those movies using the Irish Glendalough as backdrop, and suddenly we’re expected to fill the role of historic moorland.

The local petty drama is mostly metaphysical: are we the scenic ruin, the Perth lakeside stroll, or a brand of gin fancy enough to star in a Surry Hills Negroni? Time Out Atlanta even ran a Glendalough Whiskey Dinner listing, so somewhere out there someone’s pairing our name with single malts and small plates. We’re flattered, confused, and slightly concerned about needing a sommeliers’ subcommittee.

Who would hate living here? People who crave a single, unambiguous identity — nod politely as they search for the Spinc and then move to the next suburb when they realise the nearest waterfall isn’t in our postcode. Who would love it? Anyone who enjoys being part of a global family of place names: the ones who like trivia about monastic settlements, the ones who’ll sip a rhubarb Negroni and say, “Isn’t that named after us?” and the people who appreciate a suburb whose main scandal is identity theft by geography.

So we wear the mix-up with pride. When someone asks where we’re from, we smile and say: “Glendalough — pick your continent.”
