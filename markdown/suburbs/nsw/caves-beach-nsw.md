# Caves Beach (NSW)

Slug: `caves-beach-nsw`

Back in the day we used to time our lives around the tide chart like it was a school bell. We’ll stand on the clifftop where Swansea Peninsula takes a dramatic bite out of the horizon, squint down at the southern cliff and wait for the sea to do its thing — at low tide a wide, hollowed‑out cave appears and suddenly everyone’s spelunking in their swimmers. As Time Out and Urban List reminded us, the caves are the whole act; the nice stretch of beach to the north is the encore.

Our weekend routine? A slow coastal walk — the five‑kilometre Caves Beach Coastal Walking Track — that ends with the view that Concrete Playground swears is brilliant for whale watching (northern migration, May–July, if you’re into long telescopic stares). We used to nod appreciatively at the Caves Coastal Bar & Bungalows noted in the travel guides, the kind of place that made the area feel like a holiday ad in real life. The surf club is the social spine: Caves Beach Surf Life Saving Club features in the P&C’s community calendar (remember that kids’ disco at the surf club in August 2025?) and the local P&C has kept the school doing its thing.

Petty local drama? We don’t have much gossip in the clifftops — our drama is binary and very tidal: you either checked the tide tables or you didn’t. Newy With Kids and Lake Mac Family Life have the public service announcement down pat — check tide times, mind the rock pools, and be careful exploring the caves. The walk to Pinny Beach via Spoon Rocks isn’t for the faint of heart, either: steep terrain, staircases and one‑hour return if you’re brisk; Spoon Rock Bay even has a breakwater and is explicitly not patrolled, so don’t treat it like a supervised playground.

Who would hate living here? People who despise stairs, schedules and natural surprises: if you loathe checking tide charts, hate a bit of sand under your shoes, or need every beach to come with a lifeguard on duty all year, you’ll be miserable.

Who’d love it? Families who swim during patrol season, dogged beach campers and surfers (Concrete Playground’s suggestions don’t lie), whale‑watchers with patience, and anyone who thinks a network of sea caves counts as legitimate weekend entertainment. We know our coastline’s quirks — and we wouldn’t trade a single tidal drama for it.
