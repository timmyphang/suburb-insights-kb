# Bateman (NSW)

Slug: `bateman-nsw`

We’re Bateman — or, more accurately, every travel guide and theatre review that can’t be bothered with spelling is talking about Batemans Bay, and frankly we’re fine with that. Back in the day the mags painted us as a tidy coastal escape: Clyde River oysters on your lap, kayak tours for the Instagram-adverse, and a marina that promises sea air and vague responsibility.

Our weekend routine used to look like this: up early, pretend we’re on a day trip (Urban List cheerfully reminded readers we’re a four‑hour drive from Sydney), then stumble toward whichever waterfront spot Broadly called out — The Boatshed, The Sandbar, Mami’s Bar — and attempt to eat Raymond Ng’s fried rice like we aren’t still thinking about the ocean. Sitchu once crowed about boutique stays — Bay Ridge House or Caseys Beach House if you were pretending to be sophisticated — and there was even that retro revival, The Isla, which a Canberra team revived in 2022 for anyone who likes their motel with a side of neon nostalgia.

The petty cultural identity crisis? We don’t have one, the arts pages do. Broadsheet and Time Out kept dragging Patrick Bateman into the conversation — musicals at the Opera House, themed venues with very weird mannequins — so if you ever wondered whether our name has better PR than we do, yes: Hollywood and theatre folk have looser naming conventions than GPS.

Who would hate living here: people who despise oysters, quiet marinas, and the fact that “day trip” requires a small pilgrimage. Also anyone who can’t forgive a place for being repeatedly photographed with its shoes off at low tide.

Who’d love it: families and people after a soft reboot. Sitchu’s family-friendly round-ups and Urban List’s “best stays” told us the truth — this is a place for slow mornings, seafood that refuses to pretend it’s anything other than seafood, and motels with personality. We don’t pretend to be a metropolis; we’re the comfortable coastal cousin who turns up to the reunion smelling like salt and carrying an oyster knife. Come for the Clyde River, stay for the absurdity of sharing a headline with a fictional banker — we’ll save you a seat at The Sandbar.
