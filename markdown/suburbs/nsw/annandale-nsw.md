# Annandale (NSW)

Slug: `annandale-nsw`

Back in the day we’d spill out of our terraces and stroll Johnston Street like we own it — because, well, we kind of do. As Concrete Playground once said, Annandale “feels more like a village than a city suburb,” and we still enjoy pretending the rest of Sydney hasn’t noticed our Booth Street shops and federation homes.

Our weekend routine used to involve queueing for sourdough at Stoneground Bakery (their Booth Street seating always attracted the lingerers), buying something ridiculously leafy at the Annandale Garden Centre, and pretending we weren’t paying premium for provenance while clutching a bottle from Annandale Cellars. Broadsheet’s old guides and Time Out’s area features made sure newcomers knew where to look, and the neighbourhood cafés — the Drop, Clover Café, Cherry Moon General Store with its woodfired oven — used to set the rhythm of our mornings.

Petty drama? Oh, we had a headline to chew on. Pedestrian.tv ran the soap-opera version of our nightlife when The Annandale Hotel’s finances and fate were splashed across their pages, and Concrete Playground reported plans to turn The Empire into The Federal with Maybe Frank pizzeria on the cards. We argued about it over coffee and then promptly forgot the details because the fig‑leaf sourdough sold out.

Who would hate living here? People who want neon high-rises, 24/7 convenience and a skyline view would be baffled by our century-old mansions, Trafalgar Studios quirks and insistence on boutique everything. If you crave anonymous chain stores, Annandale’s curation — Jim and Jane’s colour‑coordinated boutique, Académie Salon’s appointed calm, Fat Fish at 109 Johnston Street’s old-school fish-and-chips cred — will feel like mild punishment.

Who would love it here? Lovers of village vibes, of curated shops and cafes that have stories in their tiles. If you like buying pot plants, stopping for a slow coffee, and arguing about whether the local pub should stay a pub or become boutique accommodation, you’ll fit right in. We’ll be at the corner, pretending the council can’t touch our gumtree.
