# St Albans (NSW)

Slug: `st-albans-nsw`

We roll off the M2 like it’s a confession and turn onto the Old Northern Road as if we’re following a map to a better temperament. Back in the day — and by that we mean the only pieces we could dig up — St Albans was the sort of place Concrete Playground recommended as a “pleasant day‑trip destination”: slow, scenic and stubbornly lovely. You’ll skim the Hawkesbury River, thread through the Macdonald Valley and feel your shoulders drop two centimetres.

Our weekend looks predictable and perfect: a lazy drive through farmland, a stop where the road narrows and someone always waves, and the kind of pub moment Sitchu once flagged when it called the Settlers Arms Inn a “Best Country Pub Close to Sydney.” In the old listings there was also St Josephs Guesthouse at 1029 Saint Albans Road — a converted 19th‑century church with private bedrooms, a deck and even a plunge pool, if you wanted to punctuate your pastoral fantasy with a bit of civilised decadence.

We’ll admit we’re working from archives here — the stories about St Albans we’ve got are the reminiscing kind, not a live feed. So this is us luxuriating in past suggestions rather than endorsing tonight’s booking.

Local drama? Not much to report beyond the eternal argument: should we hurry back to the city or stay until the light over the river goes pink? The guides nudged visitors to move at a relaxed pace; you’ll either resent that or learn to love it.

Who would hate living here: anyone who thinks “weekend” means “shopping precinct” or gets twitchy about quiet roads and one‑pub decisions. If you need a 24/7 city soundtrack, St Albans will treat you like background noise.

Who would love it: people after a proper escape — walkers, folks who like their pubs tied to river views, or anyone whose ideal day ends with a country drive and no decisions about which boutique to visit. As Concrete Playground and those old travel columns hinted, we’re not trying to be everywhere; we’re trying to be exactly this. Come for the river, stay for the sunset, leave the hurry at the roundabout.
