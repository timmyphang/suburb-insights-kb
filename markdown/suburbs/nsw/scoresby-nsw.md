# Scoresby (NSW)

Slug: `scoresby-nsw`

Back in the day we’d line up like it was a royal opening — at least that’s what the guides said. As Broadsheet noted, Drom Bakery (listed at 1/19 Scoresby Road, Bayswater) used to draw early queues for croissants, pastries and savoury toasties; Urban List even renamed it Dröm and praised its lunar-shaped croissants, cinnamon scrolls and country-style pies. We’re the kind of place where pastry bragging rights are a local sport and someone will always show you an article to prove it.

Weekends smelled like butter and engine oil. You could wander from The Hatter and the Hare (21 Scoresby Road, Bayswater) — yes, the Alice-in-Wonderland-themed cafe Broadsheet wrote up with Down the Rabbit Hole acai bowls and the Knave of Heart’s Treat French toast — and stumble into the enormous Caribbean Gardens & Markets. Pedestrian reported the MPAA called those markets “notorious” for physical piracy; they’re also Australia’s largest undercover market, apparently over 10,000 sqm, open Wednesdays and Sundays. Only here could artisanal croissants and dubious DVDs exist in peace, separated by the width of a caravan park.

We’ve got family things too. Playgroup Victoria listed Scoresby Primary School’s play sessions and, more recently, entries about One Hope Community Church running playgroups out of Scoresby Secondary College and Community@Cavell. The mums keep the calendar full — Scoresby Steamfest 2025 was on our radar as the 57th annual bash at the National Steam Centre over Labour Day: vintage steam, gas, petrol and diesel engine displays. If you like your heritage loud and oily, this is your shrine.

Who would hate it here? People who prefer their suburbs quiet, polished and free of market vendors with questionable DVD boxes and engines that still hiss. Who would love it? Families who want playgroups and Steamfest on the same long weekend, pastry hunters armed with Urban List clippings, and anyone who enjoys a bit of local colour that reads equal parts Good Food column and rural swap meet.

We’re a patchwork: a bakery people wrote love letters about, a Wonderland cafe that once charmed the guides, and a market the MPAA noticed. We’d say that’s a vibe — and we wouldn’t trade it for a uniform postcode.
