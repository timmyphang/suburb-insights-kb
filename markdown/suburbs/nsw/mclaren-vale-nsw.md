# Mclaren Vale (NSW)

Slug: `mclaren-vale-nsw`

We wake up in McLaren Vale and the first thing we do is check if the vines are sulking or just hungover from a weekend of cellar‑door tourists. For years the weekend riff has been the same: a slow drive past the Visitor Information Centre on Main Rd (yes, 796 Main Rd), a coastal detour if we’re feeling dramatic, and a grazing plan that reads more like a marriage contract than a to‑do list.

Back in the day Broadsheet sent people here for hikes in Onkaparinga River National Park and Chapel Hill tastings; Time Out plastered our faces across lists of the best wineries because someone thought a cube‑shaped tasting room at d’Arenberg was a brilliant idea (and the toilets apparently are a national attraction — we’re not making that up). Concrete Playground celebrated a local hero moment when Beresford Estate’s Classic Shiraz 2023 went on to embarrass nicer regions overseas. Sitchu and Urban List used to give out route suggestions like wedding invitations: bring a coat, bring a designated driver, bring cash for Pizzateca on Chalk Hill Rd.

There isn’t much scandal in the files — mostly winemakers, markets and a relentless calendar of Good Things To Do. The McLaren Vale Business & Tourism Association and the long‑running Grape, Wine & Tourism Association keep the machine greased: markets (yes, the Collective Christmas Market crops up in lists), piazzas, and events that make our little Institute Hall and Piazza Della Valle feel like the civic heart of a place that sells itself by the bottle.

Who would hate living here? People allergic to queues, vineyards, or conversations that begin with “Do you prefer grenache or shiraz?” Also anyone who thinks a double‑decker wine bus is the height of subtlety.

Who would love it? Couples after The Strand at the Vineyard vibes, families who appreciate playground‑friendly cellar doors, cyclists wanting to ride the Shiraz Trail, and anyone who enjoys being able to brag that our shiraz once knocked the socks off the judges. We’ll keep pouring — grudgingly proud and ever so slightly smug.
