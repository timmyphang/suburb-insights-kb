# Kingsgrove (NSW)

Slug: `kingsgrove-nsw`

Back in the day we’d start a weekend like it was a low-effort art project: queue at Ickle Coffee — Broadsheet called it “educational, colourful and welcoming” — grab a Portuguese tart or something gluten‑free from Little Secrets Bakehouse, and pretend we’re learning about single‑origin beans while actually arguing about who left the shopping bags in the hallway.

This place reads like a culinary yearbook. Sisters Rowena and Kate Chansiri used their Kingsgrove pop‑up at Ickle to hawk their grandmother’s beef‑noodle‑soup as Ama, and that little idea eventually wandered off to Surry Hills. Nearby you could find Bask Blends Cafe’s tiramisu pancakes on a dessert list, and Quattro Stelle’s cured meats earned a Time Out nod for its salami. All Good Things Eatery was the kind of café that bragged about in‑house pickling and smoking — artisanal puttering we’d quietly applaud between sips.

We can’t pretend there’s a mayoral scandal hiding in these clippings — the archive is mostly cafés, playgroups and cured meat cred. Playgroup NSW recorded our weekly Wednesday meet‑ups at Kingsgrove Community Aid Centre, 30 Morgan St, so if you judged a suburb by the number of prams and singing toys, we’d win. Bambini Health turned up in the mum blogs as the local paediatric allied‑health spot for speech and occupational therapy (for ages 0–18), which is the kind of boringly brilliant thing families thank the universe for. There’s even kids’ theatre and party‑entertainer listings (Em’s Entertainment; a sports party entry at 8/23 Garema Circuit) and a Saturday morning French club via LCF Fun Languages if you want your toddler to sound classier than you feel.

Who would hate it here? If you loathe communal tables, cured‑meat shops at 11/192a Kingsgrove Rd, and the faint whiff of pickling jars, you’ll be miserable. Also, if you wanted an always‑open souvlaki tavern, fair warning: Time Out listed Kefi Greek Tavern as closed (though Urban List still remembered the 48‑skewer grill glory days).

Who’d love it? Young families, small‑town food obsessives and anyone who likes their weekends a little artisanal and a little practical: playgroups at 10am, a decent slice of salami, and the comforting knowledge that a pop‑up here can become someone’s Surry Hills success story. We’ll raise a recyclable coffee bag to that.
