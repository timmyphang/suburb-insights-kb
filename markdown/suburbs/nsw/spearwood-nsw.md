# Spearwood (NSW)

Slug: `spearwood-nsw`

We roll out of bed and into Spearwood like it's a suburbally choreographed waltz: the post from 280 Spearwood Avenue goes on the kitchen bench, the ute (not ours to name) heads off, and we remember that for a while Coco + Boho used to be the place people mentioned when they wanted breakfast that sounded fancier than it actually was. Urban List even had them in their directory — crab cake, vanilla‑bean-and‑spiced French toast and all — which is peak Spearwood aspirational dining, bless it.

Weekends here have always been stubbornly practical. Back in the day (and on the records), Manning Park hosted the Spearwood Playgroup in the Park — you registered via TryBooking, the layout map had a Baby Zone, Water Play and, yes, Little Bull Espresso on the map next to an animal farm. We like that: organised chaos with coffee plotted on a PDF.

Housing listings read like a survival guide for people who like elbow room. Places on Etherington Avenue and Scales Way were sold with promises of "huge outdoor patio and entertaining area", "lock‑up garage for three cars, a workshop" and enough space for "a boat and caravan". We are the suburb that says, if you’re going to own a boat, you may as well build a house around it.

If you’re looking for local drama, the articles we’ve got are refreshingly low‑petrol‑spirit: property listings, a playgroup map, an Urban List entry. No council scandals, no barista wars. For some of us that’s maddening; for the rest, it’s peak relief. We’ll roast the place, then admit we prefer no headline‑sized scandal.

Who would hate it here? People who need constant culture‑industry stimulation or think a PDF map counts as a festival program. Who would love it? Families and practical romantics — Spearwood Alternative School and Newton Primary are within arm’s reach, Stargate Shopping Centre makes weekly errands a bore in the best way, and beaches are described in listings as "stunning" and "just moments away" — which, yes, we remind visitors smugly every time.

We’re not the flashiest postcode, but we’ve got space, sensible cafés that once made a fuss, and a park with a plotted coffee point. We’d call that modest, useful, and very Spearwood.
