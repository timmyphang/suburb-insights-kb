# Mount Riverview (NSW)

Slug: `mount-riverview-nsw`

We know this place — we’re the ones who queue the esky, park the double pram like it’s a civic duty, and judge shawarma portions on principle. Back in the day, if you wanted intel on Mount Riverview you didn’t scroll the city feeds, you checked Blue Mountains Mums. Their listings were our bible.

Morning routine (nostalgia edition): we’d drop the kids at Riv Kids Playgroup on 11 Calver Avenue — the home‑school crew where primary‑school aged kids were meant to “play freely, learn alongside peers and form friendships,” as Blue Mountains Mums put it — then wander past DCH Mount Riverview, which used to be the reliable place with a fenced play space, outside seating and a kids’ menu. Perfect for the parent negotiating carrot sticks like it’s international diplomacy.

If you’re after local event flexing, we’ll remind you the Mount Riverview Community Hall was the venue of choice for parties that wouldn’t fill a suburban hall anywhere else: listed for up to 63 people, with an outdoor area that flows onto a small parkland. Yes, that’s 63 people — intimate, like a wedding where everyone actually stays for the speeches.

Who would hate living here? People who require a non‑stop nightlife, artisanal everything or a postcode that says “we’re pretending to be a beach suburb.” Who would love it? Families who like a playgroup that knows your kid’s second name, anyone who appreciates a hall that can fit exactly 63 of your nearest and nerdiest, and those who find quiet parks suspiciously underrated.

For years the local listings on Blue Mountains Mums told the real story — small, practical, slightly smug, and very much ours.
