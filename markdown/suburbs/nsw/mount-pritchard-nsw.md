# Mount Pritchard (NSW)

Slug: `mount-pritchard-nsw`

Back in the day we’d brag that Mount Pritchard was the sort of place where your Sunday plans involved a bowl of something steaming and a safe spot for the kids while you ate it. We still tell the story like it’s an itinerary.

Morning: we used to queue for pho at 2 Foodies—Urban List even pointed out it sits “next to a car wash,” which is peak suburbia and somehow only makes the broth taste better. Broadsheet reminded everyone that Bau Truong’s operators have ties to Mount Pritchard too, so if you wanted your Vietnamese fix, the suburb wasn’t exactly starving for options.

Weekends: someone would park the kids at Monkey Mania (yep — the Freshwater & Mount Pritchard locations were called out in round-ups for play centres) and the childcare roulette would begin. For years the data shows we’ve had a lot to choose from: Mt Pritchard Early Learning Centre, Mount Pritchard Kindergarten & Child Care Centre, Young Minds Academy, and Young Academics at 85 Hemphill Avenue. CareforKids’ listings even clocked 124 childcare services across 2170 — apparently 87 had vacancies once — and the average cost was quoted at $121.08/day. There was also talk of a Mount Pritchard Montessori Academy slated to open at 12 Hemphill Avenue in mid-2026, if you’re into planning your life around future openings.

Local colour: Pritchard’s Hotel was on the maps — Time Out’s old listing gives the address as 360 Elizabeth Drv and remembers the member’s draws and Friday night extras. And yes, metro media didn’t always stick to feel‑good food pieces: Time Out’s coverage of the tobacco-shop boom referenced worrying incidents near Mount Pritchard, a reminder that the headlines sometimes visit our postcode.

Who would hate it here? People who want a suburb with one quiet, predictable vibe. We’ve got pho next to a car wash, play centres, dozens of child-care listings, and a pub with a history — pick your soundtrack.

Who would love it? Families who need options, people who take their noodle soup seriously, and anyone who likes a suburb that wears its practical, slightly chaotic heart on its sleeve. We’ll keep the bowls hot and the playdates rolling — even if the reviews about us come in from Broadsheet, Urban List and Time Out.
