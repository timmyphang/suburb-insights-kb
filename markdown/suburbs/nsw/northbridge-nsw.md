# Northbridge (NSW)

Slug: `northbridge-nsw`

We can still feel the gravel under our shoes as we weave back from a Jack Rabbit Slim’s DJ set — or at least we remember when Jack Rabbit Slim’s used to be the spot for late‑night mixes on Aberdeen Street. Back in the day this place was a slow-motion relay: Picabar was the launch point, bodies basking under that giant fig tree, then a stagger through laneways to hidden spots like Astral Weeks or Convenients (the latter famously entered through a working convenience store, as Time Out cheekily noted).

Mornings were a conciliatory ritual. Broadsheet used to point us to Get Ya Fix, Livingstone’s Urban Jungle and Holy Bagel as the daytime anchors — a gentler scene to nurse both hangovers and egos. Families and small children turned up, too: Playgroup NSW listed Northbridge Intergenerational at Catholic Health Care St Pauls on Eastern Valley Way, and North Shore Mums still name‑checked Northbridge Baths as a picnic escape from the mayhem.

If you’re after our local biography, Sitchu and Time Out did a lovely job stitching it together — gritty past, artist‑driven comeback, and a nickname as one of Australia’s coolest neighbourhoods in 2024. Concrete Playground reminded us that Connections Nightclub has been spinning disco balls and rooftop terraces since 1975, and that the Brass Monkey Hotel was the kind of place to rally for big games. Urban List and Broadsheet would argue about who’s got the best bar list — Ezra Pound, The Bird, Lot Twenty, Northbridge Brewing Co., Palace Arcade — and nobody wins because we love them all in different states of sobriety.

Who would hate living here? People who crave curfewed streets, tidy hedges and absolute silence at 2am. If you recoil at rooftop cinemas on carparks, themed adult play arenas in old IMAX shells, or a surprise Tokyo‑style listening bar turning up at a festival, Northbridge will give you hives.

Who would love it? Artists, night‑owls, professionals with a soft spot for divey charm, and families who don’t mind a little soundtrack to life — the precinct’s always been capable of serving both a late taco and a toddler’s playgroup. We’re loud, slightly sticky, and endlessly interesting. We’d have it no other way.
