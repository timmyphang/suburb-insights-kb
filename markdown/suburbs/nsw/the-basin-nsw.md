# The Basin (NSW)

Slug: `the-basin-nsw`

Back in the day every guide seemed to agree: we’re the smug little slice of Sydney where you can pitch a tent without leaving the city. We know. We see the caravans and the overexcited first-timers lugging kayaks onto Pittwater’s western shore and thinking they’ve discovered Oz’s answer to paradise.

Our morning routine is a small ritual. If we’re feeling civilised we glide out for a paddle — Urban List and other travel types have long called The Basin perfect for stand up paddleboarding and kayaking — if we’re feeling industrious we hoof the 2.8‑kilometre track from West Head Road and earn our breakfast. The campground was described as holding about 400 campers, with showers, toilets, barbecues, drinking water and picnic tables, so someone always has the fire going and someone else has forgotten the matches.

Concrete Playground once made a whole fuss about our underwater wardrobe: seagrass beds where you can spy seahorses, starfish, cuttlefish and bream. We don’t mention it to every passer-by, but yes, the lagoon’s turquoise, yes it’s listed among Sydney’s best snorkelling spots, and yes, a guide even recommended packing a snorkel. We will also happily remind you that you can technically camp on the beach while still being in Sydney — if you want an argument about jurisdiction, we’ll win it.

There’s no hedge-worthy council feud to rehearse here, just a low-level competition for the best picnic table and the eternal question: ferry or hike? The Basin’s only real drama is deciding whether you’re dehydrated from the swim or from pretending you’re rugged.

Who would hate it here? People who demand asphalt to their tent, who need a café on every corner, or who can’t fathom getting to a beach by anything other than driving right up to it. Who would adore it? Kayakers, snorkellers, families after a tidal pocket of calm, and anyone who likes turquoise water, kooky marine life and barbecues in national-park surrounds.

We’re small, we’re sheltered, and for those who know the 2.8‑kilometre secret and our seagrass show, life at The Basin still feels like a private joke the city hasn’t caught up with yet.
