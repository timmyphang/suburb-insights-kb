# Banora Point (NSW)

Slug: `banora-point-nsw`

We know Banora Point by the sight of a Prado reversing into a canal-side townhouse driveway while someone snaps a flat-white selfie under a jacaranda. We call it waterfront living — because owning a boat plot and Instagramming pavers counts as a lifestyle. We queue at Banora Village Cafe for a double oat flat white like it’s our civic duty, and we judge your coffee order with the same intensity we reserve for council debates.  

Our mornings are ritual. At 6:30am we’re at the chain gym on Tweed Coast Road in Lorna Jane leggings (men included) pretending the class is for fitness and not the social run sheet. By 8:30 the Prado or HiLux is loaded with jet-ski gear or timber from Bunnings Tweed Heads, and by 9am the Banora shopping strip has become tactical ops. Neighbours argue in the Facebook group about dibs on the 9am Coles/Bunnings parking spots like it’s a minor war. We refresh the thread. We strategise. We mourn.  

Weekends are sacred and slightly sawdusty. Saturday: oat flat white from the Banora Village Cafe, a quick spin on the Tweed canal (jet-ski or beginner surf), then four earnest hours at Bunnings fixing the wobbly deck with “eco-friendly” decking. Sunday: school sport sideline, Staffordshire Terrier on a lead, sausage sizzle and an Instagram story thanking local businesses with suspicious sincerity. Our BBQ recipes get swapped like hot tips, and we wear activewear from sunrise to sunset — because it’s practical and also who has time for chinos?  

Drama is our cottage industry. The council’s proposal to “beautify” the roundabout on Banora Point Drive turns into petitions, a letter to the editor, and three passive-aggressive lawn signs that read FRIENDS OF THE ROUNDABOUT and NO MORE EUCALYPTUS LEAVES. Strata fights about paint colours escalate faster than you can say “neutral palette.” And yes, someone once tried to reserve a 9am spot by putting out a folding chair. The chair lost. Relations soured.  

Who would HATE living here? The inner-city cocktail sommelier who cycles everywhere, drinks only orange wine and believes SUVs are toxic status symbols. They’d be enraged by our love affair with 4WDs, our annual Bunnings pilgrimage and the conviction that a weekend project is a personality trait. They wouldn’t survive the small-talk economy.  

Who would LOVE it? The mid‑40s weekend warriors, tradesman-turned-golf-dads, FIFO families and anyone who measures success in jet-ski hours, Bunnings loyalty points and school-sport attendance. You’ll love the canals, the community outrage, and the way everyone will help when you need it — right after they finish their sausage sizzle. Welcome.
