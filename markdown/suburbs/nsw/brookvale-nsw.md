# Brookvale (NSW)

Slug: `brookvale-nsw`

Back in the day Brookvale was the place you passed on the way to the beach — an honest industrial cousin of the Northern Beaches. Then the mags started sniffing around and, before we’d finished complaining, the warehouses sprouted taprooms, roasteries and a festival called the Brookie Trail that somehow made our ugly backstreets feel curated.

Our weekend used to be simple: a trundle past Warringah Mall, a rummage through Vinnies behind the mall, and the occasional pilgrimage to Lorraine’s Patisserie on William Street for something worth pretending was artisan. Then Concrete Playground and Broadsheet began listing the likes of Freshwater Brewing Co., Dad & Dave’s Brewing, 7th Day Brewery and Manly Spirits Co. as if we’d always meant to be a craft-beer hub. Bazaar & Bar turned up on 10 Orchard Road offering curry and wine; Tommy Panini opened in July 2024; Wilburs eventually expanded to 10–12 Roger Street — all of which made our industrial strip feel oddly cosmopolitan. Coffee Supreme even tried the arts-district dream before Broadsheet’s entry flagged it as “Permanently Closed.”

We’re not squeamish about history either. We still tell the story of the Battle of Brookvale — that 2011 NRL brawl that landed four sin bins and a send-off — with the same pride you’d reserve for a dodgy family heirloom. It’s part of the folklore here, like the National Grid Gallery exhibitions, the Brookvale Heritage Walk, or the bloke who ran Stunt Gym and taught people how not to set themselves on fire.

Who would hate us? If you crave quiet cul‑de‑sacs, pristine lawns and a suburb that smells faintly of nothing but eucalyptus, this isn’t your Eden. We’re loud, we host BrookieFest (three days of dachshund dashes, esky races and skate demos in 2025), we have Chalky’s pool-hall vibes and an electric bus depot that made sustainability bureaucrats very happy.

Who would love it? Families who use Brookvale Park Playground, In2Swim and All In Arts; bargain-hunters at Vinnies; and anyone who rather enjoys ordering a beer beside a reclaimed warehouse. We’re the suburb that used to make secretive industrial workers shrug — now we’re the place they brag about. Come for the breweries, stay for the weird little markets and the fact we’ll always be unapologetically the Brookie.
