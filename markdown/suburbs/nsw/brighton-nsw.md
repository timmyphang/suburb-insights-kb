# Brighton (NSW)

Slug: `brighton-nsw`

Back in the day — at least according to the patchy press archive we’re allowed to brag about — we were strolling the Brighton-Le-Sands foreshore when a trio of escaped horses decided to audition for Mad Max: Equestrian Edition. As Pedestrian TV reported, they galloped through the streets of Brighton-Le-Sands and Taren Point and yes, we were there, holding our paper coffee cups like it was all part of the Sunday plan.

Our weekend routine used to be gloriously uncomplicated. We’d wander down to Neptunes for those Mediterranean-ish long lunches the Concrete Playground remembered fondly, then drag ourselves (begrudgingly) to Bay Vista for views that made our phones look like art galleries. Benetti’s, per that same Concrete Playground review, moved into a new seaside home and served woodfire pizza and aperitivo sunsets you told your relatives about.

The Brighton hotel had a bit of a glow-up too — Time Out wrote about its Mykonos-coded revamp and reopening under the MGallery banner in October 2025 — so there was the odd weekend where we tried very hard to be tasteful poolside while pretending we weren’t secretly impressed.

Local drama? We don’t need to invent it. The biggest flex is that our suburb will occasionally be galloped through by horses for no reason you can explain over lunch, and the rest is tasteful coastal living: seafood, pizza, and a hotel that’s had more rebrands than we’ve had rainy Sundays. As Concrete Playground reminded us, Brighton-Le-Sands wears its seaside-ness proudly.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to clifftop Instagram feeds, Mediterranean menus or the occasional equine interruption. Also, if you can’t handle a hotel that’s been polished to within an inch of its glorious tile, maybe don’t move next door.

Who’ll love it: lovers of long lunches, families who appreciate a waterfront setting, and anyone who’s ever wanted to tell a dinner party that once a horse ran past their table. We’ll be on the foreshore — slightly soggy from laughter, pretending we meant for all of this to happen.
