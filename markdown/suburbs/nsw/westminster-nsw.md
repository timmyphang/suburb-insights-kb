# Westminster (NSW)

Slug: `westminster-nsw`

We’re standing on the corner of “Westminster, NSW” — or at least that’s what our postcode keeps being told online — watching tourists try to find the nearest abbey and locals trying to explain we don’t have Poets’ Corner. Every morning we refresh the feed and get a carousel of Westminsters that aren’t us: Urban List’s guide to London Airbnbs, Concrete Playground’s reverent deep dive into Westminster Abbey (40 coronations, 3,300 burials — very dramatic), and a glowing roundup that insists Sweet Society in Westminster, WA, makes the best cookies. Back in the day the internet had identity issues; apparently it still does.

Our weekend ritual now involves a kind of cultural whack‑a‑mole: one tab wants to book The Peers’ Dining Room at the Palace of Westminster, another is nostalgically rewatching Junkee’s bits on the Westminster Dog Show, and a local forum reminds us there’s a Westminster in Maryland with a Mona Becker mayor who actually has council meetings at 7pm. We haven’t met any lords, terriers, or mayors — but we’ve become very good at clarifying, “Not that one.”

There’s no petty council feud in the files we’ve been given — shocking, we know — so the real drama is genealogical: are we the abbey with stained glass, the Perth suburb with Brookies, or the Maryland town with a Public Library at 50 E. Main Street? As Concrete Playground and Urban List repeatedly show, the name Westminster is doing all the heavy lifting for other places while we sit here and enjoy our anonymity.

Who would hate living here? If you thrive on being recognised for coronations, VIP dining or being the location of a famous dog show, you’ll feel perpetually miscast. Also, if you’re the type who needs your suburb to show up correctly on every travel guide, prepare to be mildly furious.

Who’ll love it? People who like being delightfully nondescript, who appreciate that our postcode sends everyone else on a miniature scavenger hunt, and who prefer a life without guided tours through Poets’ Corner. If anonymity is the new prestige, we’re practically Westminster Abbey by stealth.
