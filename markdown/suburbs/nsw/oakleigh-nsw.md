# Oakleigh (NSW)

Slug: `oakleigh-nsw`

We know the walk down Eaton Mall like it's our backyard — the kind of place where you can tell the time by which cake shop is shouting loudest. Back in the day Vanilla Cakes and Lounge used to pack its sprawling outdoor seating and publish a quarterly mag about how many pastries one person could reasonably consume. Nikos Quality Cakes has been doing the reliable loukoumades-and-baklava thing since 1987, so if your nana's looking for authenticity, she already lives here.

Weekends used to begin with the sweet siren call of The Oakleigh Doughnut Co — opened in 2017 by brothers Nic and Yani Kabylakis — and their Galakdough inspired by galaktoboureko. There was a time we'd queue for Halva-flavoured doughnuts like we were queuing for the footy. Broadsheet and Urban List catalogued all of this lovingly; we all felt seen.

By arvo we’d wander up to Oakleigh Market and, if we were feeling pretentious, perch where Olympia used to be — that carpark-turned-rooftop with whitewashed walls, olive trees and a downstairs booth that made you believe you were on holiday within a 15-minute tram ride. Time Out and Concrete Playground raved about the Greek wines and saganaki croquettes; we still tell people about the rooftop as if it’s a local myth.

Then there’s Kalimera Souvlaki Art — the tiny shop the NYT apparently lost its mind over, and Time Out cheekily calling its pork souvlaki “pork perfection.” If you wanted a lesson in how the suburbs quietly started running the culinary world, that was it.

If you hate oregano, you will loathe Oakleigh. If you despise queues, rooftop bars, or being gently judged for ordering more cake, maybe consider somewhere with fewer pastry cabinets and more anonymity. But if you love earnest Greek baking, souvlaki that has critics losing their composure, doughnuts with a backstory, paintball at World Series Paintball or a novelty spree at Creative Costumes — well, Oakleigh has always been very good to us.

We grew up on Eaton Mall, argued about the best spanakopita at Nikos, and somehow still feel proud when a visitor says, “You’ve got to show me around.” Back in the day, we did — and we’d do it again.
