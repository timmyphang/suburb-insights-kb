# Inglewood (NSW)

Slug: `inglewood-nsw`

Back in the day — and by that we mean every weekend the papers wrote love letters about Beaufort Street — we’d amble down the strip like we owned the footpath.

We’d start at Finlay & Sons, the corner cafe Time Out once said had an eggs benedict “none compare with” (that perfect ooze, remember?) then drift past Yoke and La Luna, pretending we needed another throw rug or a scented candle. For lunch we’d argue about whether Hoodburger’s second outpost (and its wine‑bar extension, Hoodburger 848) was the peak of civilisation or merely excellent marketing. There was always talk of the new bistro — Bistro Massilia — bringing a slice of Marseille to Beaufort Street and, yes, a béarnaise burger for two that Sitchu thought worth shouting about.

Our local used to be reliable in the way only the best suburbs are: the Inglewood Hotel (listed as a “tried-and-true local” by Concrete Playground) kept watch over pre- and post-game loyalties, while Pedestrian.TV documented the Inglewood Inn morphing from a 30‑seat country pub into something with a lot more bells and multi‑million dollar sparkle. The night markets and the Terry Tyzack Aquatic Centre gave us weekend rhythm; the Town Square, library and Bob Daniel Community Centre kept the small‑town civic vibes honest. Even the playgroup was Proper Community — it scored a Lotterywest grant and ran a tidy program out of the Children’s Centre next to the library.

Petty local drama? Mostly arguments about whose parking spot on Beaufort Street was actually theirs and whether Cecchi’s rustic‑chic approach to Italian was the future of dining or just a very nice plate of duck tortellini. Oh, and the eternal debate: Everyday Bread — artisan hero or overpriced sourdough?

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to foot traffic, curious restaurants, or tasteful homewares. If you need a silent cul‑de‑sac, move along.

Who’d love it: families who like the playgroup village life, food‑obsessed folks who read Broadsheet and Time Out, and people who think a minor renovation can turn a pub into a destination. We may be boutique‑curated and occasionally smug — but Beaufort Street taught us how to brunch, browse and keep our communal drama charmingly local.
