# Chippendale (NSW)

Slug: `chippendale-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed, stumble past the never‑ending rumble of Cleveland Street and Broadway, and convince ourselves Kensington Street’s Spice Alley wasn’t just an excuse to eat twice before lunch. This was the loop: a cheeky detour past Prince Alfred Park, a nod to the White Rabbit Gallery on the way to Carriageworks, and the comforting sight of The Old Clare’s heritage bricks telling us we were somewhere that remembered its own history.

We used to queue for tiny, life‑changing things — Radio Taco at 67 Abercrombie Street, that 12‑seat birria joint that sold out daily; Toby’s Estate, whose roastery bar was even crowned in an international guide; Buttered in The Brewery that made the viral Korean salt bread. The Chippo — the Chippendale Hotel — got a whole new identity too, relaunching as an all‑vegan pub, which, yes, even the bar was vegan. For a while it felt like the suburb’s secret handshake.

Petty local drama? We had art that argued with itself. Remember that Kanye mural that became a worldwide thing before the artist painted it over? Classic Chippo: big statements, then paint thinner. And the precinct always had its theatrical moments — Phoenix Central Park used to host those free, ballot‑only performances that made us pretend we were cultured on a budget.

Who would hate it here? People who crave suburbia with identical fences and predictable Sunday afternoons. If you can’t handle a laneway that doubles as a hawker precinct, a boutique hotel playing soundtrack to a private party, or the perpetual possibility of a popup eating your parking spot, Chippendale was never going to be your thing.

Who would love it? The restless, the hungry, the curious. Folks who appreciate wood‑fired cooking at Ester, a ramen‑roll call at Spice Alley, galleries that smell faintly of solvent and ambition, and a compact walkable pocket that Time Out once named Australia’s coolest neighbourhood and seventh‑coolest in the world. We’ll always be proud of that messy, brilliant mix — equal parts grit, gallery, and gourmet — even if we’re only reminiscing about the good old pop‑up days.
