# Winston Hills (NSW)

Slug: `winston-hills-nsw`

We start our Saturday like proper locals used to: dropping the kids at the Winston Hills Mall food court play area so we can pretend we're here for a bargain and not for the slime workshop. As Hills District Mums cheerfully documented, the mall even used to have Santa’s letterbox next to the Colour Splash Zone outside Suzanne Grae — which, yes, made our annual debate about Santa proximity oddly civic.

Back in the day the guides liked to pat us on the head. Urban List once listed Café Els at 180–190 Caroline Chisholm Drive as a modern-Australian brunch spot; Time Out remembered June as a friendly neighbourhood café with a sun-soaked alfresco and a signature “Avo Good Day” toast. For people who count their wins in pastry crumbs, that was something. Concrete Playground and Sitchu tucked Winston Hills into broader Hills District round-ups, praising inventive breakfasts, burgers and the odd sun-drenched terrace.

We are also secretly proud of our humble culinary triumphs: Urban List name‑checked Chook A Licious — family‑owned since 2011 — for cheap potato scallops (they were $1.60 each in that feature), which is peak suburban joy right there. Meanwhile The Winston at 170 Caroline Chisholm Drive went through renovations and, as Time Out noted, left us with an outdoor terrace, a decent bottleshop and live‑music ambitions that made Friday night slightly less about catching up on the washing.

Our parks are the kind of places where kids learn bike chops and parents learn PTA diplomacy. ParraParents points to Water Dragon Way’s 2km bushwalk along Northmead Gully, plus Winston Hills Lions Park and John Curtin Reserve for playgrounds, picnics and the annual argument over whose sausage sizzles better.

Who would hate it here? If you loathe mall Santas, dislike playground choreography or can’t bear the smell of BBQ chook drifting across a weekend car park, you’ll be vexed. Who would love it? Families who want easy playgroups, walkers who like a bush stroll without leaving the postcode, and anyone who thinks $1.60 potato scallops are a legitimate food group.

Yes, our press shout-outs are mostly from pre-2024 — but that’s alright. We wear our suburban honours like a slightly squashed paper crown.
