# Bickley (NSW)

Slug: `bickley-nsw`

Back in the day we’d pile into the car for an autumn weekend and pretend we were country folk for 48 hours — Bickley’s harvest vibes were irresistible. As Broadsheet noted, the Bickley Harvest Festival used to turn the valley into a tasting menu on legs (they even listed the May 3–4, 2025 run in a festival round-up), with over 20 venues opening for long lunches, fruit picking at Core Cider House and whatever grown-up merriment passes for culture in wine country.

Our weekend routine was embarrassingly precise: slow drive out the scenic routes, stop at Hainault Vineyard and Cafe on a whim (the Urban List said it was open weekends and public holidays, which felt like a personal invitation), and flop on the verandah at The Packing Shed — 19 Loaring Rd, if you’re the sort who likes exactitudes — where country-style plates met a distillery and cellar door. We read somewhere (thank you, Urban List) about Airbnb decks so enormous they made us feel like extra furniture, gazing over the Bickley Valley. Romantic, then mildly smug.

For a town that’s quietly proud of its heritage, we’ve got contradictions: Mistelle used to call 255 Walnut Road home before it decamped to Shenton Park, and the valley still boasts three heritage-listed sites crammed into a beginner-friendly 7km trail, according to Urban List. Sitchu kept reminding us this is a roughly 35-minute scoot from the city, for those who like pretending remoteness is a lifestyle choice.

Who would hate it here? People who need 24/7 neon and a tram every five minutes — the kind who count nightlife in espresso bars and footpath murals. Who would love it? Hikers, wine-and-cider devotees, harvest-festival romantics and anyone who thinks “weekend” should include fruit picking and a very sincere verandah nap.

For the record, our sources are mostly older write-ups — so this is a fond, slightly grainy postcard from the Bickley Valley’s heyday, not a current directory. Bring a jacket and your best picnic manners.
