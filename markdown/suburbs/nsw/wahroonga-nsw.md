# Wahroonga (NSW)

Slug: `wahroonga-nsw`

We know exactly which footpath tile will fake a sprain outside Bonjour French Patisserie on Railway Avenue — it’s the one you’ll limp on while pretending you weren’t there for the croissant. Back in the day (or at least according to the internet’s scrapbook), this is the rhythm of Wahroonga: slow-lane breakfasts, mid‑century pilgrimages and a park that stages our annual excuse to drink wine in public.

Morning here used to mean a circuit of former-butcher-turned-café lore. The Butcher’s Block has been the place road‑trippers stopping on the drive to the Hunter Valley were told to hit; Concrete Playground and Time Out have written paeans to its butcher‑themed fitout and Allpress coffee. If you wanted something a touch more continental, Bonjour at 17 Railway Avenue got the nod for its pastries. Sofe Bakery cropped up in lists as the Korean bakehouse making viral chocolate‑walnut baguettes and tiger‑salt rolls. Danes earned its old‑school rep with sunny al‑fresco tables and its on‑site roaster. Karoo & Co was even name‑checked for bacon and egg roll excellence — the kind of thing that keeps our ambling Sunday queues socially acceptable.

We don’t have any fresh scoops in the data pile — most of the stories about us are archival — so instead we boast about our past: the Rose Seidler House gets regular museum‑style worship (Broadsheet loved it), and designers have used it as event fodder. Wahroonga Park has been home to the Food and Wine Festival, bringing more than 30 winemakers, market stalls and tasting packages (think about $38–$40) to the grass while kids get face‑painted and parents pretend it’s educational.

Who would hate living here? If you crave neon, midnight kebabs or a postcode that never shuts up, Wahroonga’s leafy conservatism will bore you rigid. We’re not for people who need the CBD on tap; we’re for those who appreciate a restored playground, local playgroups at St Lucy’s School House, and a village fair that can still return and feel like an event.

Who would love it? Design nerds who worship Seidler, families who use Wahroonga Park, bakery hunters and anyone who thinks being known well enough to have a Launceston Airbnb called “Wahroonga on Bourke” is peak suburb celebrity. We’ll wave you in — but bring patience for the pastry queue.
