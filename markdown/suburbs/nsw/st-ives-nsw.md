# St Ives (NSW)

Slug: `st-ives-nsw`

Back in the day we’d roll out of bed, hop on the T1 to Gordon and catch the 582 to the Acron Road stop like we owned the place — destination: The Cascades and Bungaroo Pool, because yes, St Ives has actual rock pools you can brag about to your inner‑west friends. We know the tracks, the quiet gullies and the smug satisfaction of beating the crowds to the good swimming ledges.

Weekend routine here used to look like this: a stroll through Ku‑ring‑gai Wildflower Garden (420 Mona Vale Road) when the wildflowers were peaking, a picnic at St Ives Showground, and if it was a cinematic season you’d park up for Sunset Drive‑In or swing by during the Medieval Faire when the showground turned into seven centuries of costume drama. If you wanted quieter, Artnest Potter Atelier offered pottery classes and those BYO Cork & Clay nights for anyone who liked shaping clay while pretending they weren’t just making wine‑stained bowls. Even Sous Le Soleil used to feel like the cottage in the bush where someone always had a jam tart on hand.

There’s no juicy local scandal in our paperwork — the only real dispute seems to be whether to cut across to the Senses Track or linger at the Wildflower Art and Garden Festival. Concrete Playground and Time Out told the same stories: big outdoor events, treetop adventures adding high‑ropes courses, and the showground doubling as stunt‑and‑circus central. We’ll take that kind of “drama.”

Who would hate it here? If you need 24/7 bars, neon laneways and that rushed CBD adrenaline, you’ll find us quaint to the point of smug. If you can’t stand buses to Gordon, north‑coast shrubbery or people who actually like a community fete, move along.

Who would love it here? Families and anyone who appreciates being 20 kilometres north of the CBD for the exact reason it feels like a leafy secret. Bushwalkers, wildflower obsessives, medieval re‑enactors and people who once queued for The Big Bounce at the showground will feel at home. We’re the suburb that quietly stages drive‑ins, fairs, pottery nights and treetop antics — and we’ll still wave as you catch the 582 back to the city.
