# Nedlands (WA)

Slug: `nedlands-wa`

We know the bend in the river, the hill where everyone pretends not to jog, and exactly which cafe queue used to make the school mums late. We’ve been Nedlands long enough to remember when the foreshore was just ours and not a curated Instagram loop.

Morning used to start with a gentle stroll on the Nedlands Foreshore Walk — the old Law Walk Loop — a tidy 4.7km that made us feel virtuous before Mortadeli’s lunch rush tempted even the most resolute among us. Back in the day Rocket Fuel at 6 Stirling Highway was the drive-thru lifeline for bleary-eyed commuters (it sat just past the Caltex Star Mart, if you were keeping score). If you had a tiny person in tow, Jo Wheatley All Abilities Play Space quietly did the inclusive-park thing better than anyone else in town.

We’ll own our little foodie flexes: Kith Eatery used to be the cult cafe everyone blamed for traffic; yes, the tropical iced matcha and chilli scrambled eggs were the things we queued for and the council supposedly solved by promising more parking. Marumo was the place you swore you’d book for a special night — the bijou, 30-seat omakase that made getting a seat feel like winning a dinner lottery. Then there was Masa Mesa’s dream of pupusas and parrilla from Erick Martinez, which felt like a delicious answer to suburban predictability.

Petty local drama? It wasn’t scandalous — mostly about parking, bookings and whether The Resident Bar’s white-on-white aesthetic and its huge boab-tree beer-garden actually made us feel cultured or just mildly judged. Chez Pierre used to be where romantic bistro dates happened, Santis fed pancake pilgrimages, and Mortadeli kept hospital staff very happy in the lunchtime scramble.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe waterfront walks, cottagey golf course mornings (Nedlands Golf Club’s nine-hole charm), yacht-club skyline views, or people who bring picnic rugs to sunset, this isn’t your postcode.

Who would love it? Families who appreciate inclusive playgrounds, amateur sailors who like a Nedlands Yacht Club view of the Swan River, and anyone who still gets a thrill from booking something “hard-to-get.” We’re not flashy about it — we’re the quietly smug suburb that brings good sandwiches, better walks and a stubborn love for the bend in the river.
