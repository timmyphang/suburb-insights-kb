# Hammond Park (WA)

Slug: `hammond-park-wa`

We open the blinds on Healy Road and, as proud locals, salute the one time of year our street outshines the CBD: Halloween. As UrbanList once kindly noted, Healy Road and the houses around Botany Oval used to be the place to go trick-or-treating — which is code for “bring a wheelie bin and a sense of humour.”

Weekends here used to look like this: a family pilgrimage out to Christmas Trees of Hammond Park (listed at 331 Wattleup Road in Wattleup in a few seasonal round-ups) to wrestle a noble pine into the car, followed by a slow lap of Rotary Park and Yeo Park while someone explains for the hundredth time where the ducks like to sunbake. Sitchu and The Urban List have both name-checked that tree farm as one of Perth’s wholesome options, so yes, we have the festive logistical nightmares to prove it.

We don’t have glossy nightlife features or a skyline, but MumsPages has long treated Hammond Park as a proper category — gyms, kids’ activities, and directories that say, in so many polite web pages, “this is where families go.” Even swim lessons made the index: Swim Mechanix Success was listed on MumsPages among local providers, showing that if you want your toddler to survive the pool, someone once had that covered.

There isn’t much local scandal in our file of articles — mostly friendly, practical mentions — so the drama is low-key. If you’re the sort who needs a bespoke boutique on every corner, or you believe excitement is defined by rooftop bars and artisanal anything, Hammond Park will disappoint. If you hate the sight of kangaroos, emus and families of birds (yes, Time Out once suggested a stroll through Hammond Park toward an animal sanctuary), maybe rethink your suburb choice.

But if you like wide lawns, sensible directories that actually list useful things, a street that goes all-in for Halloween, and the seasonal chaos of hauling a tree home from 331 Wattleup Road, you’ll fit right in. We’re the kind of place that doesn’t need to shout — we’ve been quietly on lists long enough to know what we are.
