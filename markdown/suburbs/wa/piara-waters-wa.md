# Piara Waters (WA)

Slug: `piara-waters-wa`

Back in the day our suburb was the sort of place that turned up on community directories and parenting forums and felt pleased about it. We know Piara Waters by postcode (6112), by the 7 km² that squeezes us between Banjup, Canning Vale, Forrestdale, Harrisdale and Treeby, and by the City of Armadale’s proud list of libraries — yes, the Piara Waters Library at 352 Wright Road used to be on every council listing. As My Community Directory noted, we’re in the electorate of Darling Range (federal Burt) and we wear that postcode like a school badge.

Mornings here used to involve a pilgrimage of sorts: the playgroup that met at Piara Waters Pavilion on the corner of Wright Rd and Nicholson Rd, where volunteer parents ran a warm chaos we’d call community. BabyCenter threads remember us as the place new mums trawled for coffee groups and “mums with bubs” fitness sessions — there was chatter, plans and the occasional hopeful meetup.

Weekend vibe: the kind of place kids write home about. PerthMumsgroup gushed about the Robot Playground in Newhaven Estate — a giant robot-head cubby, flying fox, hamster-wheel thing and water play — the sort of conspicuous play equipment that makes you both proud and a little jealous of your own childhood. Rossiter Pavilion Playground made the shortlist too, with mini trampolines, an accessible swing and picnic barbecues for the practical among us.

Petty local drama? We don’t have a headline scandal in the files — mostly logistical grievances: limited parking at Robot Park and the eternal debate about where the best picnic spot is. We’ll take those fights.

Who would hate living here? If you crave pulsating laneway culture or a permanent line at a trendy café, you’ll find our brochure thrillingly low-key. Who would love it? Families who want playgrounds that read like sci‑fi set design, volunteers who run playgroups from a pavilion, and anyone who enjoys knowing their suburb is on the community map — even if the mentions are a little vintage now.

So yes, we’re a tidy slice of Armadale with a robot playground in our back pocket and a library that used to be on the map. We’re not flashy. We’re reliably wholesome. And frankly, we’ll take that.
