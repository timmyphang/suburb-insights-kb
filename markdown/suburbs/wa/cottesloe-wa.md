# Cottesloe (WA)

Slug: `cottesloe-wa`

We know the angle: golden sun, salty hair, the kind of sunset that makes you briefly forgive people for liking sculpture parks. We’ve spent enough evenings parked on the shaded grassy tiers of Marine Parade to know exactly where the light hits the limestone for the best selfie — and which bench to glare from when someone brings a drone.

Back in the day the big moment was Sculpture by the Sea — the show that was meant to be a regular heartbreak when 2025 was cancelled amid funding delays, only for federal money to step in and for the exhibition to return in March 2026. It ran March 6–23, 2026, allegedly hosted about 230,000 visiting art pilgrims, and even handed out People’s Choice honours (Tim Storrier and Drew McDonald made the winners list). For two weeks the shoreline was equal parts high art and high SPF.

Our mornings used to be reliably simple: a brisk dip at Cottesloe Beach, followed by a wander to the Boatshed Market (they kept reasonable hours — 6:30am–8:00pm) for fruit, or maybe a stop at the Cottesloe General Store on Marine Parade for something warm and hand-baked. If you were more of a sandwich-and-salt-life type, Ol’ Buoy used to sit below the Indiana Tea House and do seasonal hours, while Eric Chamberlain made a claim to the local cafè map on Eric Street.

Petty local drama? We’ve had it. Nothing screams community like watching funding spreadsheets decide whether the coastline gets a temporary sculpture forest or just more holiday umbrellas. We’re into that sort of tense civic theatre — with better views.

Who would hate living here? People who can’t stand sunsets, quiet prestige that smells faintly of seaweed and good wine, or the kind of weekend where sunset cocktails at a beachfront venue like the Cottesloe Beach Club, the Cottesloe Beach Hotel or the Ocean Beach Hotel were considered very reasonable plans. Also, if you loathe bumping into mums from the playgroup at the playground, this isn’t your postcode.

Who would love it? Families, art nerds who’ll traipse along the shore to see 70-odd installations, and anyone nostalgic for that postcard-perfect stretch of sand where dining rooms like Lamont’s, Gibney, Indigo Oscar and even Tigerfish used to punctuate the evening. We’ll keep the towel down for you.
