# Halls Head (WA)

Slug: `halls-head-wa`

Back in the day — and by that we mean the handful of articles anyone bothered to write about us — we were mainly a coastline you could cycle along without dodging scooters. We know this place by smell: salty breeze off the Indian Ocean, sunscreen, and the faint pride of a bakery that once made the rest of the country admit we can do pastry.

Our weekend looks like this: we roll out of bed, clip in at Doddi’s Beach on Halls Head Parade and pedal the Halls Head Coastal Trail because yes, the 12km of coastline really does give you dolphins, seals and the occasional whale cameo (Urban List swore on it). Halfway through the trail you remember the thing that made national papers — as Time Out and Broadsheet noted, Jack’s Bakery in Halls Head snagged a stack of Great Aussie Pie Competition trophies: Gourmet Pie, red meat, Shepherd’s and even brekkie pie. We’ll never shut up about it. It’s our small-town Olympics.

Local life isn’t all glittering pies and pelicans. We’ve got a community infrastructure that reads like a friendly checklist: Halls Head Central (opened 2001; had a 2016 development that added Kmart, Aldi and more shops), a bowling green or four at Halls Head Bowling & Recreation Club, and the dependable hum of community groups — Playgroup WA is where the littlies learn to be tiny humans and WA Mums Cottage runs raffles out at Bunnings Halls Head (yes, the one on 25 Rutland Dr) when they’re not organising Mother’s Day or Easter fundraisers.

Who would hate living here? If your soul is only satisfied by inner-city laneways, cocktail menus with names you can’t pronounce, or absolute silence away from kids, bowls and seagulls stealing chips — Halls Head will test you. Also, if you despise seeing people actually use a coastal trail, this isn’t your vibe.

Who’d love it here? Families who want playgroups and parks, bargain-hunters who appreciate a Kmart run, bowlers looking for a club night, or anyone who thinks a nationally-awarded pie is a perfectly reasonable reason to brag at the pub. We’ll keep cycling, watching for dolphins, and reminding strangers that our pies once shut down the internet.
