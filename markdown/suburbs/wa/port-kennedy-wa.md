# Port Kennedy (WA)

Slug: `port-kennedy-wa`

Back in the day we’d launch our kayaks from the Port Kennedy boat ramp, wobble past the shallows and pretend we were doing something noble. Urban List once told us to paddle from Port Kennedy to Secret Harbour — and not to be reckless about it, so yes, life jackets. We are, officially, the sort of place that thinks a sensible Saturday can include salt on your face and bravery on your shoulders.

Broadsheet also noted that Pinjarra Bakery had an outpost in Port Kennedy, which explains why we can feign sophistication with country-bakery credentials when relatives come to stay. And when the weather turns grim, Urban List helpfully reminded the big kids among us there’s Lazer Blaze in Port Kennedy for peak laser-tag catharsis. It’s wholesome, noisy, and oddly competitive — like our local PTA, but with more neon.

We were even name‑checked by Time Out as one of WA’s pet-packed suburbs alongside Waikiki, so yes: dogs know the footpaths better than we do. Junkee told the story of the Songline skateboard team rolling into town for the Rockingham Rumble at Port Kennedy, complete with demos and workshops — proof we can host something genuinely cool when we try.

If you’re raising tiny humans, the directories have always had us covered: Playgroup WA’s finder lists playgroups nearby, and MumsPages used to list local options like GKR Karate, Acrodanz and Footwork Dance Australia (they were advertising a new air‑conditioned dance studio on Main Street). In short, we’re where parents go to get kids into something that involves either a uniform, a tutu or a sense of impending tumble.

Who would hate living here? People who loathe the sight of a leash, the sound of a skateboard, or the sudden cheer of a laser-tag victory. Also anyone who thinks every suburb should come with a buzzy laneway bar and five avocado brunch spots — sorry, we’re more kayaking then cocktail.

Who would love it? Families who want activities for little feet, dog owners who consider the foreshore their second living room, and anyone who likes a local that’s quietly good at being ordinary in the best ways. We’re Port Kennedy: low‑key, pet‑packed, and secretly proud of it.
