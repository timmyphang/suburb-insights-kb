# Cooloongup (WA)

Slug: `cooloongup-wa`

Back in the day — and by "back in the day" we mean the only stuff we could find was from the dusty pre-2024 listings — Cooloongup was the sort of place that winked at you from the directory and politely offered a community hall, a Chinese takeaway and a chicken shop. We know the corners: Westerly Family Centre (opened 27 October 1990) sits next door to Cooloongup Primary School and, per its contact page, used to list (08) 9592 3650 and info@westerlyfamilycentre.com.au if you wanted to book a room or a cuddle.

A proper Cooloongup morning used to look like this in the archives: someone drops the kids at the primary, then wanders into Westerly for Mad Hatter Stampers or Chattastitches Sewing Group — yes, Stitching Witches are on the roll call too — and by noon the venue-hire page had our old-school, air-conditioned rooms booked out by a knitting posse who could probably stitch a scarf faster than the council could approve a new sign.

If you rummage through the business listings that still haunt the web, you’ll find Yong’s Chinese Kitchen, Hank’s Bar and Bistro, and Chicken Treat Cooloongup gazing back at you like familiar shopfront ghosts. The Rockingham Rejuvenation Centre even has an address at 4/2 Elanora Dr, which is the sort of precise trivia we recite at family barbecues when we can’t think of anything else to argue about.

Petty local drama? The files we have are shockingly wholesome — venue hire rates, group names, and a directory for mums — so the spiciest thing we can roast is bureaucracy and sensible community spirit. Who would hate it here? Serial trend-chasers who need novelty every fortnight, people who measure suburbs by cafe aesthetics, and anyone who gets restless without an artisanal label on their hobby group.

Who would love it? Families who value a proper community centre, crafters who actually want to meet other humans instead of influencers, and anyone who enjoys the comforting knowledge that you can still hire a room with reverse-cycle air-conditioning and a kitchen without pretending it’s vintage. We’re Cooloongup, 6168: quietly civic, a little domestic, and stubbornly community-minded — even if only the archives can prove it.
