# Ardross (WA)

Slug: `ardross-wa`

Back in the day our intel came from the archives, so think of this as a nostalgia roast: we’re the lot who know every corner of Ardross even if the newspapers last checked in a few years ago.

Weekends start like this: we mosey toward the buzzing Ardross Street café strip over in Applecross — yes, it’s technically Applecross, but come on, we share the vibes — where Typeface at 7 Ardross St used to be the cosy bookshop you’d duck into with a shortlist and come out pretending you only bought a birthday card. If we were feeling decadent we’d remember when Hoodburger opened its flagship at 31 Kearns Cres in 2021, all retro diner booths and American soft drinks, serving smashed burgers and chicken tenders that made us feel like we were in an ’80s road trip montage (as Broadsheet wrote).

For a quieter afternoon there was Bad Apples Bar — tucked in at Shop 6, 16 Riseley Street — dishing duck tacos, wagyu sliders and an alfresco beer garden that made us briefly believe we were sophisticated. And yes, Canteen Pizza popped up at 32 Ardross Street over the way, which meant our dinner arguments escalated from “thin crust” to “which artisanal tomato is more characteristic.”

We don’t have anything juicy in the files about council feuds or scandal — the archives are annoyingly wholesome — so our petty drama is aesthetic: we’d rather roast the tasteful retro diner fit-out than invent a soap opera. Sitchu’s real-estate listings reminded us there are executive homes (hello, 26 Searle Road) that make the rest of us feel like we’re living in the “before” photo of a renovation show.

Who would hate living here? If you’re a late-night club-hopper who measures nightlife in decibels, Ardross (and its polite small bars and café strip) will bore you stiff. If you loathe bookstores, small-batch pizzas and the idea of duck tacos turning up at your local, this suburb will test your patience.

Who would love it? Parents who once checked the Family Tree Child Development Centre at 15 Willcock Street (listed as caring for 62 kids, Mon–Fri 7am–6pm, phone (08) 9364 7211) will sleep easier; burger nostalgics will reminisce about Hoodburger’s diner dreams; and anyone who enjoys a low-key street with good eats and a bookshop on the corner will feel right at home.
