# Colonel Light Gardens (SA)

Slug: `colonel-light-gardens-sa`

We wake up to the sound of our own gumtrees negotiating with the breeze — rounded street corners, wide, tree-lined streets and a suspicion that every lawn has been personally judged by at least one neighbour. Colonel Light Gardens sits about 7 km south of the city, planned as a proper garden suburb so tidy it could give you a guilt trip about your indoor plants. Time Out called it a model of postwar bungalows and manicured open space; we call it home.

Weekends here are a slow parade: kids evaporate into the playgrounds at Hillview Reserve, Kent Road Reserve, Portland Place or Mortlock Park, while the rest of us drift to the oval or the BMX track. The City of Mitcham lists Mortlock Park with an oval, baseball diamond, BMX track, BBQs and picnic tables — handy, because someone always forgets the snags. For years the local cafe scene had Bond & Lane Canteen doing the neighbourly-larder thing — Lou Rodwell built a courtyard where produce from next door somehow ended up on plates (Broadsheet used to like that).

We do have our civic soap opera: the Residents Association meets every 3rd Thursday at 7pm and exists to preserve the suburb’s character — think gentle emails about paint colours and volunteers turning up to garden the public reserves. The Historical Society keeps the folklore orderly, publishing a “walk of discovery” brochure and offering talks and renovating guides so your 1920s bungalow doesn’t get modernised into a box that offends the plan.

Who would hate it here? If you crave high-density change, late-night neon, or the thrill of never knowing who lives next door, Colonel Light Gardens will bore you senseless. We’re low-density, tightly held and proud of it.

Who loves it? Families (Sitchu gave us a tick for raising kids), people who like secret lanes and tennis courts (Play & Go still raves about our hidden spaces), sports nuts who appreciate Mortlock Park’s facilities, and anyone who enjoys a suburb where the RSL on Prince George Parade still marks ANZAC and Remembrance Day properly. We’re quaint, we’re picky, and we’ll still show you the best tree-lined street in town — just don’t try to turn the front yard into a second living room.
