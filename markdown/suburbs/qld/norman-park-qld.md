# Norman Park (QLD)

Slug: `norman-park-qld`

Back in the day — and by that we mean the only write-ups we've got are pre-2024 — Norman Park read like a meditation on coffee and lawn bowls. We know every corner because we used to queue at the spots the food pages loved.

Morning ritual, nostalgia edition: we’d shuffle down Overend Street to Dovetail on Overend (85 Overend Street), that tiny galley cafe Broadsheet and Concrete Playground raved about — coeliac-friendly, recycled timber tables, the kind of place where the menu was short and the coffee "hit the mark." Nearby was Clancy’s Espresso on Wynnum Road (198 Wynnum Road), which Broadsheet noted served Single O beans and Jocelyn’s Provisions pastries. Jacu espresso popped up in the early lists too (172 Bennetts Road) for the bleary-eyed. Yes, this is a roast and a tribute; we loved that the internet kept telling everyone how nicely we brewed our mornings.

Weekends: the sport of the suburbs. Norman Park Bowls Club isn’t just for the perfectly ironed shirts — Concrete Playground listed it among top places for lawn bowls, with trivia twice a week and live music on Sundays. We’ll own that our social calendar could be mistaken for a community hall brochure and we’re not embarrassed about it.

For those who like their neighbourhood to smell of fresh blooms, Time Out flagged Poppy Rose at Shop 4, 50 Hume Street, and Sitchu mentioned Flour & Bloom on the same street — both reminders that yes, we take gifting plants seriously.

Schools and small-life conveniences cropped up in listings: Norman Park State School and local catchments that lean to Coorparoo Secondary College, plus Hampton Swim School’s Hipwood Street location for the kids who came home chlorine-scented and triumphant.

We don’t have fresh scoops — these are the clips that loved our cafes, our bowls, our florists and our playgroups. So who would hate it here? Someone who thinks a good weekend should involve a nightclub and a skyline — you’ll be reflexively offended by our trivia nights. Who’d love it? Families, folks who measure Saturdays in coffee and lawn bowls, and anyone who finds joy in small, reliably charming suburban things. We’re Norman Park: quietly proud, fond of our cafes, and perfectly happy being a little bit old-fashioned.
