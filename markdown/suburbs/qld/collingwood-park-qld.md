# Collingwood Park (QLD)

Slug: `collingwood-park-qld`

We know the turn-off onto Collingwood Drive like it’s our local ritual. We’ve navigated the school-run squeeze at Collingwood Park State School on the corner of Burrel and Hannant Streets, watched the suburb stretch across its roughly 7–7.62 km² like a neighbourhood giving itself elbow room, and grumbled fondly about the postcode 4301 being one of Ipswich’s best-kept secrets.

Weekends here used to look simple and small-town: drop-ins at the Collingwood Park Shopping Centre (the one that boasted over 200 car parks and a Woolworths among its eight specialty stores), a quick consult at Collingwood Park Family Medical Centre on Collingwood Drive when the kids invented new ways to test gravity, and the steady hum of a place that’s been growing — the Collingwood Park State Secondary College officially opened its gates at the start of Term 1, 2025, because apparently we needed one more thing to fight over at the school gate.

We throw up our hands and laugh at the suburb’s ambitions. Paul Tully Avenue made The Urban List’s Brisbane Christmas lights roundup for 2025 — yes, our street goes full festive, possibly decking the cul-de-sac enough to be seen from space. And the lottery shop Cignall had a dramatic moment too: a temporary closure after ex‑Cyclone Alfred and a later reopening, because even our lotto outlets have seasonal plot twists.

Who would hate living here? If you crave laneway espresso snobbery, underground cool that can’t be GPS’d, or a nightlife that requires a passport to the inner-city, Collingwood Park will feel politely provincial. If your idea of excitement is a boutique bar crawl, this isn’t your rom-com location.

Who would love it? Families who like breathing room, locals who appreciate a proper Woolworths car park, parents who now have a brand-new secondary college down the road, and anyone who values community directories that actually list the GP, the school, and sensible trivia like our surround-squad suburbs — New Chum, Redbank, Redbank Plains and Riverview. We’re Ipswich Council’s growing little patch: unflashy, unexpectedly festive, and stubbornly practical — and frankly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
