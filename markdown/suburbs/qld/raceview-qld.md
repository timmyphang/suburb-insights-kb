# Raceview (QLD)

Slug: `raceview-qld`

Back in the day, our morning looked less like a brunch flex and more like an orienteering course for small humans. We’d pore over CareforKids listings like it was real estate: 79 childcare services in Raceview 4305 — yes, 79 — and apparently 49 of them had vacancies. Practical, overwhelming, and very Raceview.

We remember the big names that used to anchor the drop-off circuit. Raceview Kindergarten & Child Care Centre used to be at 219 Whitehill Road, open 6am–6pm and charging $145/day (a cheeky step above the area average). Expeditions Early Learning Journey was at 80 Thornton Street, also 6:00am–6:00pm, with its 4.2 rating wobbling nicely in the middle of the pack. The pre-school tally read 22 centres, with 17 showing vacancies — which translated into furious spreadsheeting by sleep-deprived parents.

Petty local drama? The data is refreshingly drama-light, so instead we’ll roast the numbers: an average cost of about $129.81/day, which, depending on your math, is either a bargain or the suburb’s covert way of funding the playground upgrades we still haven’t seen. There’s no council meeting quoted here yelling at anyone — just calm, efficient provisioning of nappies and structured playtime, as documented by careforkids.com.au.

Who would hate living here: anyone whose idea of suburbia is a series of curated cafés and boutique yoga studios. If you’re allergic to pram routes, roster swaps and negotiating who gets Monday pick-up, Raceview will test your tolerance. Also, if you like your neighbourhood to be a mystery of “what’s open late?” — this place historically ran on family timetables, not 24/7 artisan hours.

Who’d love it: parents who want options and predictability. Families who appreciated that 22 pre-schools were listed and 17 had places — and that some centres explicitly ran full-day hours for working people. Even the internet gets confused by our name: there used to be a “Raceview Kids” — but it was over in Alberton, Gauteng (19 Winnipeg St), so if you emailed them about our local kindy, consider that a mini adventure.

We’re not posh. We’re practical, loud, and gloriously on a schedule. If your ideal suburb features more handprints than latte art, you’ll feel right at home.
