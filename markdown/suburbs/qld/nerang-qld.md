# Nerang (QLD)

Slug: `nerang-qld`

Back in the day when we would point the car down the M1 and pretend we were on a pilgrimage, Broadsheet told us to keep going to Nerang for the suburban-gem Chanoyu. We believed them, because what else do you do on a Wednesday except trust a travel edit and the promise of matcha-adjacent vibes?

Our weekend routine used to look like this: a detour to Little Kiwi Bakehouse at 8 Lavelle St for a pie that’s earned a cult following, then a quiet, slightly smug stop at First Harvest on 33 Hinkler Drive — the gated indoor playground that sits in the same building as the Hillsong Church and, famously, is often eerily quiet. If we were feeling fancy (or peckish for umami), we’d remember Hanakyo at 2 Riverview Rd — the “tasty little number” that snuck onto the ramen map — or that unnamed Japanese‑Aussie spot the Urban List loved for black sesame French toast and karaage burgers. River House used to be the riverside option for the times we wanted to pretend we were on holiday without leaving the Nerang banks.

We don’t have much in the way of local scandal in the clippings — the biggest excitement seems to be festivals and new openings. Concrete Playground wrote about Isla Cantina bringing SoCal‑meets‑Mex to the Isle of Capri on the Nerang River, and River of Gold likes to light up our river with a floating 15‑metre stage. Southport’s Queen Street Village even runs along Nerang Street, so if you like a little mixed‑suburb infrastructure, we’ve got you covered.

Who would hate living here? People who need neon nightlife at 2am, or anyone allergic to kids’ playdates — Social Mums Club meetups and the Mama Tribe make sure the pram brigade are always on hand. Also, if you loathe the idea of choosing between a cult bakery pie and a perfectly slurpable bowl of ramen, this place will ruin your decisiveness.

Who would love it? Families, festival folk, riverside graziers, and anyone who appreciates a quiet suburban charm with pockets of properly good food. We know every corner, and honestly — we wouldn’t have it any other way.
