# Camp Hill (QLD)

Slug: `camp-hill-qld`

Back in the day we’d start Saturday at the little white corner store on Martha Street — Florence used to do those artfully crafted brunch plates that made us pretend we were effortless — then wander down to Picnic on 12 Martha Street for the smoked Wagyu brisket if we were feeling indulgent. If you had kids in tow, Nodo at Market Square (25 Samuel Street) was the move: gluten-free doughnuts, concrete-chic seating and a grassy play patch where small humans practiced being loud.

By lunch we’d be doom-scrolling wine lists at The Old Seven on Old Cleveland Road — yes, the French‑inspired 35‑seat spot Matt Lucas and Nellie Barker opened, famous for a rosé-forward selection and more than 100 bottles. Or we’d pick up something from Pippa’s Pantry on 565 Old Cleveland Road if we wanted our pastry to come with a side of plant-based virtue and a viral ‘crookie’ story to tell.

We don’t have council scandals ready to hand, so our drama is quieter: Community meet-ups run by Social Mums Club (Debbie and Pippa are names you actually see on the roster) and the steady hum of local businesses being quietly excellent. Concrete Playground even remembered Rays on Newman Avenue when it opened as a European-style corner bar — schnitzel and cocktails, they said — and the Camp Hill Bowls Club kept its old-school charm while doubling as our low-cost Friday-night social hub (two hours for about $6, apparently).

Who would hate living here? If your idea of a weekend is club queues and neon haze, Camp Hill will think you’re dramatic. If you can’t cope with people who prioritise good salads (Botanica’s third spot landed here), small pizza experiments (hello, Spread on Bennetts Rd) and an enormous patch of green called Whites Hill Reserve with the Sankey’s Mountain Summit track and a fenced dog park — then move along.

Who would love it? Families — Sitchu has long called Camp Hill one of the most family-friendly suburbs — vintage hunters (Camp Hill Antique Centre with 70+ dealers and an on-site cafe called Tart), dog walkers, rosé aficionados and anyone who likes their weekends to end with a bowl, a bakery box or a lazy picnic. We’ll be here, quietly smug, waiting for you to notice.
