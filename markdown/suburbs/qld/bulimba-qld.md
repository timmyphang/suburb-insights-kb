# Bulimba (QLD)

Slug: `bulimba-qld`

Back in the day we’d saunter down Oxford Street like we owned the footpath — pram wheels squeaking, tote bags half-full of gelato and grudgingly respectable paperback finds from Riverbend Books. Oxford Street used to be our main drag: Sugo Mi’s pizzas and gelato were the kind of thing you’d whisper about at dinner parties, Bellissimo Coffee’s riverside warehouse was where we pretended brunch was for the culture, and Luckie’s Kitchen served up Cantonese‑inspired whimsy that made us feel worldly for about 20 minutes.

Weekends were a ritual: CityCat or Cross River ferries in the morning, a picnic at Bulimba Memorial Park in the arvo, and a stroll past the shops looking for something that wasn’t quite on the high street yet. The Bulimba Barracks plans — remember when Brisbane City Council approved turning that 1943 Fabrication Workshop into a riverside lifestyle hub? We watched that one like it was our new communal backyard, all promises of parkland, riverwalk and pop-ups.

Petty local drama? Mostly development chat and menus. Romeo — yes, the Simon Prest project that followed My Kitchen Rules 2024 — was the kind of “very soon” opening that gave our group chat whiplash. New residences at Bhode, 360 Riding Road, were being teased by builders and planners; we vowed to love the views and hate the parking in equal measure.

Who would hate living here? People who loathe eye contact with impeccably ironed linen, those allergic to riverside promenades, or anyone who can’t handle a suburb where the cafés and boutiques quietly compete for who can look the most relaxed while being very curated. If you prefer anonymous streets and zero community notices, this is not your jam.

Who would love it here? Families who actually use the Bulimba Community Centre’s programs — Wee Make Music, Little Kickers, Young@Heart — and anyone who likes books, pizza and the idea of a weekend that involves a ferry ride and a perfect bit of local theatre called Oxford Street. We’ll keep the tea warm and the opinions louder than the riverboat horn.
