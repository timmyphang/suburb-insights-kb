# Beachmere (QLD)

Slug: `beachmere-qld`

We open our mornings with a Hilux reversing down to the Pumicestone Passage, tinny clinking on its trailer like a maritime alarm clock. We queue for the one reliable coffee at Beachmere Café & Deli — double-shot flat white, no fuss — and watch a Nabbed-Nissan Navara owner argue with a pelican over yesterday’s catch. We wear Rip Curl hoodies with R.M.Williams boots because function won out over fashion the moment we saw tide charts. We call the Labradoodle “Mullet” and mean it.

Sundays run on a predictable loop: up at sunrise for a run along Pumicestone Passage, pop into the bait shop for pilchards, swing past Beachmere IGA for milk and a loaf, check the tinny and trailer on Beachmere Road, fish until lunch, then a late arvo beer at the bowls club while someone posts a drone-ish photo of the mudflats captioned “blessed”. We count a successful Saturday by bait purchased and sausages eaten at the Bunnings sausage sizzle. We compare outboard motors like they’re vintage watches and knock back the bowls club raffle like it’s a civic duty.

The petty theatre keeps us amused. Parking wars at the boat ramp escalate quickly; windscreens collect passive-aggressive notes (“MOVE YOUR TRAILER — IT’S A BOAT RAMP, NOT A DISPLAY YARD”) and Facebook threads titled “Boat Ramp Etiquette 101” reach Olympic levels of etiquette. A proposed housing development once summoned 27 council submissions — mostly caps-lock manifestos signed only with first names. There’s also the ongoing lamington cold war: Beachmere Café & Deli insists on local bakery supply, the bowls club is lobbying for its own confectioner, and the strata on Waterside Crescent still haven’t agreed whose wheelie bins go out on which side of the street.

We buy hardware on a whim, do the school-pick-up shuffle for visiting grandkids, and argue passionately about whether the best fishing is off Holmes Street or the old jetty. Our drama is artisanal and loud in small doses. We are proud of it.

Who would hate living here? The Brompton-riding, oat-milk cold-brew start-up founder craving a 24/7 cafe scene and boutique coworking within a five-minute walk will rage. They’ll be outraged by pelicans, by pelican-stolen lunches, by the slow-gear tempo and the inevitability of Saturday afternoon bowls club functions.

Who would love it? The retired nurse-turned-weekend-angler in a Rip Curl hoodie with R.M.Williams boots, Labradoodle called Mullet, trailer ready and heart content — they will thrive here. We’ll save a chair at the bowls club raffle for them. Welcome.
