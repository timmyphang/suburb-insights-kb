# Kuraby (QLD)

Slug: `kuraby-qld`

Back in the day — yes, proper throwback energy — we used to set the alarm for 6am and head down to the Kuraby Markets on Beenleigh Road. Concrete Playground remembers the fourth birthday (May 2017), when the stalls promised fresh fruit and veg, baked goods, seafood and arts and crafts, and they even handed out free birthday cake from 9:30am. If that won’t get you out of bed, nothing will.

Our weekend looks like this in memory: a slow stroll from the car to the market, a legacy of Goodwill Projects’ footprint — they used to run the Kuraby Farmers Markets alongside the Beenleigh Night Markets, HOTA Farmers and Artisan Markets and even the Surfers Paradise Beachfront Markets. We’d bump into families using the MumsPages listings to find martial-arts classes for kids or the nearest party and event venue, because yes, Kuraby is on the mums’ map for practical things like fencing contractors and after-school activities.

We’re low on scandal and high on community programming. Concrete Playground also flagged Pasifika Made at Wally Tate Park — a September 21 slot in the festival that brought Polynesian, Melanesian, Micronesian, Māori and Indigenous performers. No council brawls, no gentrification op-eds — just performances, markets and the kind of local logic that quietly refuses to be flashy.

If you hate dawn markets, wetlands and festivals with actual people in them, Kuraby will test your patience. If you think every suburb must come with a boutique coffee drag-race or a constant stream of hot-new-openings, this place will make you homesick for hype. Also, if you despise a 900-hectare patch of bushland with wetlands and lagoons nearby — the Karawatha/Kuraby parcel mentioned for hikes — you probably won’t last a year.

Who will love it here? Families who actually use directories, hikers happy to lose an afternoon in Karawatha’s wetland trails, market regulars who remember cake at 9:30am, and anyone who prefers neighbourhood rhythm over neighbourhood trend. We’ll keep the markets in our stories whether they’re on the calendar or just a fond memory — Kuraby’s charm is quietly communal, and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
