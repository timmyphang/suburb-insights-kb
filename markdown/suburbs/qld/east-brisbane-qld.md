# East Brisbane (QLD)

Slug: `east-brisbane-qld`

Saturday morning in our bit of the river bend means dodging prams at Mowbray Park, pretending the two wooden boats in the playground are our private yacht, and inhaling the smell of someone else’s takeaway coffee. To be clear: we’re reading older coverage here, so this is nostalgia, not a hipster travel itinerary. Back in the day — or at least according to the listings and reviews on Broadsheet, Time Out and the like — East Brisbane liked to turn up for flavour and personality.

Weekends used to look like this: we’d amble past the Allpress Espresso roastery/café on Stanley Street (yes, the open warehouse one with wooden floors), maybe stop for Cups On Wellington on the corner of Wellington and Waterloo Street if we fancied coral‑pink walls and a jaffle, or queue for The Smug Fig’s shipping‑container charm. If we were feeling fancy and a little smug, we’d squeeze into Shunsai’s 18‑seat kaiseki room in The Wellington building. On market days the Gabba corridor — Stanley Street and Lytton Road — had options from The Wolfe at 989 Stanley St to The Barra Boys by the BP at 1007 Stanley St East.

Our suburb is a study in contrast: Kiku started life as a yakitori stall then became a cosy izakaya serving mazesoba and ochazuke; Future Magic Brewing Co crowdfunded its way into a cavernous Manilla Street warehouse and turned it into a 150‑seat beer haven with pizzas. Sunny Side Sandwiches even opened a big third outpost with a shokupan viewing window and an East Brisbane–exclusive cinnamon‑and‑orange scroll — all stuff the papers liked to note.

Petty drama? The data’s light on tea‑room feuds, so the real story is the split personality: riverside family life at Mowbray Park and then, a block over, immersive dining, breweries and roasteries vying for column inches. That’s our charm.

Who’d hate living here? Someone craving total anonymity or zero queues — we’re a small suburb, and everyone knows where the good jams are. Who’d love it? Families who worship shade and boats at Mowbray Park, food obsessives who remember Shunsai or Kiku, and anyone who appreciates a crowd‑funded brewery on an old warehouse block. We’ll keep holding the footpath for you.
