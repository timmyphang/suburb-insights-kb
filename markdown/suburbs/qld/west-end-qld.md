# West End (QLD)

Slug: `west-end-qld`

Back in the day we’d stagger out of bed at 5.59am and be elbow-to-elbow at Davies Park by 6am, because as Time Out cheekily reminded everyone the West End Markets were the place to be — more than 150 stalls, the polite chaos of produce, art and the person who always brings a drum. From there it’s a Boundary Street crawl: you could still—and we mean in the glory days Broadsheet chronicled—grab a porchetta roll from Swiss Gourmet Deli, pop into Semi Semi for a Korean shaved‑ice experiment, and then wonder how we ended up at a 136‑year‑old church on Dornoch Terrace being lectured on classic French sauces at August.

We know where the secret doors are. +81 Sushi Kappo opened as that 12‑seat omakase behind the curtain at Aizome Bar—Brisbane’s version of “shh, we only serve very expensive fish and slightly smug nods.” The West End Electric landed on Boundary Street too, because of course our cabaret needs to be 400+ seats of razzle-dazzle. Function Well set up a multi‑level wellness bunker in West Village, and Soak built a rooftop bathhouse so you could scrub off the previous night’s mistakes with a city view.

There’s no scandal to report here—unless you count arguing over whose pasta spot is the real deal—but that’s because the stories are in the streets, not in tabloids. Broadsheet, Urban List and Concrete Playground all made pilgrimages to catalogue our menus and venues; Sitchu even nominated us as a reliable melting pot of culture and vintage finds like Mappins Nursery, Neo‑Generalist and the Avid Reader bookstore.

Who would hate living here? Someone allergic to live music, markets, or the gnawing urge to sample every single bar Francine/BY.ARTISANS/Brew Baron/Catchment Brewing Co on a single night. If you like your suburb predictable, quiet and beige, we’re not for you.

Who would love it? People who like walking the riverside at Riverside Lands Park, who want a neighbourhood that tastes like the world but smells occasionally of too much passionfruit. We know every corner, every late opening, every tiny theatre seat—and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
