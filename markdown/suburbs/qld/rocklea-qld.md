# Rocklea (QLD)

Slug: `rocklea-qld`

Back in the day we’d drag ourselves out before dawn and swear Rocklea was Brisbane’s best-kept secret — mostly because you had to be awake to find it. The Saturday Fresh Market used to take over 385 Sherwood Road, and that’s where we’d do everything from sourcing cheap plants to pretending we knew what heirloom veg looked like. You might remember the Brisbane Flower Market with its frosty cold room and per‑bloom pricing; flowers felt like contraband, and we treated them accordingly.

We’re proud of our market bona fides. Sprout Artisan Bakery made its debut at the Saturday Fresh Market before getting a proper shopfront on James Street, and Michael Nguyen — yes, the grocer to the stars — used to supply specialist produce to Agnes, Joy, Gerard’s Bistro, Bianca, Otto, Same Same, Stanley, Yoko and Melrose. If you wanted culinary weirdness, Rocklea quietly supplied the city while everyone else was queuing for the next big café.

Friday nights were a different beast: Rocklea By Night, aka the Brisbane Night Market, turned the Showgrounds into a food-and-stall circus from 4pm to 10pm. We’d blame the pedestrian fee at the Brisbane Market Place for any lingering guilt, then wander in anyway. Plantapalooza once filled the markets with thousands of extra plants (and, yes, entry and parking fees) — because nothing says local pride like lugging a fiddle‑leaf fig home on public transport.

Who would hate living here? If your perfect weekend involves polished laneway selfies and boutique minimalism, Rocklea will test your patience. If you can’t fathom paying for parking at a plant festival or find the idea of a mega garage sale abhorrent, this isn’t your vibe — remember the Sunday Discovery Market mega garage sale we all picked through back in 2021?

Who would love it? Market lovers, bargain hunters, florists, plant hoarders and anyone buying their first house (Time Out once pointed out Rocklea as one of the more affordable Brisbane options, with houses around $673,000). We’re rough around the edges, loud at dawn and weirdly indispensable to the city. Come for the banh mi at An Cafe’s Rocklea stall, stay for the plants, and let us pretend we don’t know you bought the last cactus.
