# Forest Glen (QLD)

Slug: `forest-glen-qld`

Back in the day we’d claim the whole place was named after our stunning canopy, then point at a brochure and nod solemnly. Broadsheet once called something “the colour of a forest glen” — flattering, if a little on the nose for a suburb literally called Forest Glen.

Our Saturday usually started with a convoy to Ninja Jungle at 35 Owen Creek Road — because nothing says “relaxing weekend” like a giant Ninja Warrior–style obstacle course for the kids. After we’d lob their small bodies into the foam pits, we limped over to Kunara Organic Marketplace at 330 Mons Road. Kunara used to be the place with 18,000 products, a café, garden centre and naturopathic services; the Kunara Cafe served vegan-friendly food from a certified organic kitchen, and we loved pretending we were on a documentary about mindful shopping while buying yet another jar of something fermented.

If we weren’t being domesticated at Kunara, we were being domesticated by beer: Glass House Brewery in Forest Glen used to brew in-house pale ale, black lager, session ale, red IPA and an organic saison, and the Italian menu and function space gave us all the excuses to book a table and behave like we were on a very local food tour. Local Social had a Forest Glen sister café, which made us feel marginally metropolitan.

There’s not much real local scandal in the public record — no council coups, no boutique-shopping turf wars — which suits us. The closest we get to drama is deciding whether the kids go to Ninja Jungle or the Kids Camp at Park | Forest Glen (that “Kingdom Quest” week in late July 2026 sounded ambitious). Park | Forest Glen also used to put on Summer Nights from June to August, which was peak neighbourly awkwardness and free sausage roll energy.

Who would hate it here? Anyone allergic to organic aisles, beer taps or the sound of small humans being heroic on obstacles. Who would love it? Families who like messy weekends, vegans who appreciate an honest-certified organic cafe, and anyone chasing a low-key Sunshine Coast vibe with surprisingly good beer. We’ll raise a can to that — retrospectively, of course.
