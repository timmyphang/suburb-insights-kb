# Gilston (QLD)

Slug: `gilston-qld`

Back in the day we’d roll down Worongary Road like it was a pilgrimage — 590 Worongary Road was the drop-off point for tiny rebels at Kids World Gilston, the childcare centre Kerryn Warner set up in 1993 on roughly two acres of what felt like glorious, slightly muddy freedom. Kids World was the sort of place that earned an “Exceeding” on the National Quality Standard and had us quietly bragging at school pick‑up while clutching laminated enrolment forms for 2025 like they were concert tickets.

If you search the internet for Gilston you’ll discover our identity crisis is impressive. Urban List once had a “Gilston Four” (in Broadbeach — yes, not here) and even listed a Crampton Social on Gilston Street over in Keperra. Concrete Playground has spent time waxing lyrical about a Gilson bistro in Melbourne (spelled differently) and Time Out took the liberty of reporting on a whole new town called Gilston outside London. We like to think this proves we’re internationally confusing — or at least that our name ages well in copy-editors’ nightmares.

Netmums has pages advising on “things to do with kids” and childcare options in Gilston, which is handy because our press coverage basically boils down to: childcare, more childcare, and family days out. That’s our vibe. We’re not trying to be the next Chapel Street; we’re the place your kids will come home with a surprising number of nature-themed paintings and a mysterious streak of paint on their forehead.

Who would hate living here? If you require a Michelin-starred night out on your doorstep, someone to walk your dog at 2am, or a postcode that doubles as a status symbol, Gilston will quietly disappoint you. Also, if you love being the centre of the universe, you’ll find our two-acre pace a little too charmingly anonymous.

Who would love it? Families who want a proper bush-y childhood, anyone after low-key community energy, and those who appreciate that our best headlines are about playgroups, kindy enrolments and a childcare centre founded by someone who clearly knew what families needed. We’ll keep the paint on the kids and the calm in our streets — you’re welcome.
