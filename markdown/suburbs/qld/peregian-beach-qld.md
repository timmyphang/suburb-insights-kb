# Peregian Beach (QLD)

Slug: `peregian-beach-qld`

Back in the day we’d tell visitors to leave their spreadsheets at the gate and put on something that could handle sand and a cheeky sea breeze. We’re Peregian Beach — the sleepy seaside village Time Out fancied up, the “barefoot boho” Sitchu profile swore by, and the kind of place where the town square has more character than your last Zoom background.

Mornings used to be simple: a stroll past the square, the idea of a French pâtisserie lingering because Le Bon Delice was the small café known for macaroons and éclairs, and the thought that if you were chasing a paddock‑to‑plate long lunch you’d make a beeline for Bask — which, as Broadsheet noted, moved from Eumundi down to our coast and became “long, lazy lunches are their jam.” Evenings had the Peregian Beach Hotel out on its massive deck doing live music and sun-burnt singalongs, while Mariella Mexicantina brought mezcal and share plates to the breezy cantina mood.

We’ve also had the less glamourous bits — Pedestrian.TV covered bushfires near here that reportedly destroyed homes and forced evacuations, and yes, there’s that jellyfish footage that made national headlines. We don’t make light of the hard stuff; we just fold it into our history like a surfboard into a roof rack.

Petty local drama? The kind worth a raised eyebrow: a surf shop/collective called Dessa used to hawk boards and curated threads, Bedouin Traders cropped up in the home‑tour gloss, and Urban List once recommended Essence Peregian Beach as barefoot‑luxury—so suddenly our sleepy village had options for both flip‑flops and linen blazers. We’ve seen houses on Old Emu Mountain Road that look like they were staged by the sea gods themselves.

Who would hate it here? People who live for noise, neon and 24/7 city urgency. If your ideal weekend involves a food festival every hour, you’ll be twitchy.

Who would love it? Folks who want beach walks, a village square that feels like an actual village, paddock‑to‑plate long lunches, quiet surf breaks and the kind of community that knows when to ring a neighbour. Come for the sand, stay because you lost your watch and can’t be bothered finding it.
