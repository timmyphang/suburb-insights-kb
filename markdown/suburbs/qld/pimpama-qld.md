# Pimpama (QLD)

Slug: `pimpama-qld`

Back in the day we used to brag that Pimpama was where convenience met ambition — and by that we mean we were home to Australia’s first drive-thru ramen. As Concrete Playground reported, Ramen Danbo’s Pimpama outpost on Pimpama Jacobs Well Road (Shop 3, 62–68) seriously leaned into the “we’ll feed you noodles without making you unbuckle” ethos. It was the sort of thing you either loved or used as proof we’d win any “how far from anywhere” contest.

Our weekend ritual, for anyone who remembers, was gloriously practical: pick up a roast pork banh mi from BM Corner at 53 Collingrove Cct (Urban List called it a Northern Gold Coast favourite), then head to Slideways Go Karting World for some slightly over-ambitious overtaking on their 700-metre, high-grip outdoor track. If you were feeling nautical, The Fish Tank at 2 Soldier Bird Ave showed us we could do sustainably sourced fish and chips without crossing the bridge. Meanwhile Pimpama Pavilion on Dixon Drive did its best to keep us cultured — Time Out Entertainment listing shows it hosted nights like InFusion at The Park Coomera.

We’re not all tacky drive-thrus and karting banshee-screams, either. For the parent brigade, “Baby Sensory – Pimpama” was a thing at Pimpama Sports Hub on 139 Rifle Range Rd (whatson4kids listed it), which is code for: yes, we have tiny humans’ classes and someone will applaud when your kid sits up. And for the people who like a bit of luxe in the postcode, Sitchu once put 7 McInnes Street in Pacific Cove on a pedestal — 600m² block, posh photos, the usual estate aspirational nonsense.

Who would hate living here? If you need tramlines and boutique small-batch everything within a five-minute walk, we’ll make you very cross. Who would love it? Families who want space, people who enjoy impulsive karting and sustained banh mi devotion, and anyone who appreciates a town that makes bold moves — like inventing a ramen drive-thru just to see if the internet would notice.

As Urban List and Concrete Playground showed us, Pimpama’s always had ideas bigger than our postcode. We’ve embraced them, laughed at them, and occasionally queued for them.
