# Nambour (QLD)

Slug: `nambour-qld`

We start our weekend at the landmark that refuses to be subtle: the Big Pineapple. It reopened after renovations with a new cafe and viewing platform — the sort of oversized fruit confidence you either love or pretend you’re too cool for. As Concrete Playground reported, you’ll find it on Nambour Connection Road; it’s basically our skyline.

Back in the guidebooks, trains from Brisbane were the polite way to arrive — “trains from Brisbane stop at Nambour and Cooroy,” as one travel piece put it — which is handy if you like the idea of a day trip that ends with a 1km loop to Robinson Falls. Robinson Falls, fed by Tuckers Creek, is our modest swimming hole with BBQ facilities and a playground: perfect for people who prefer their wilderness served with a picnic bench.

On quieter days we used to choose between two very particular therapies: pottery classes at The Pottery Studio (Pottery & Chill, Sunday Wine Down, Clay & Wine — yes, that’s a thing) or rage therapy at The Smash Pit, Queensland’s first destruction room where you can don safety gear, take a bat, and pretend the week never happened. Both are very Nambour: hands-on, slightly theatrical, and blessedly unpretentious.

Our social calendar has always been Showgrounds-heavy. The Nambour Showgrounds on Coronation Avenue hosts everything from drive-in festivals headlined by The Chats to the Queensland Garden Show (they’ve brought it here for July events). Kids get in free for some of it, which makes us very smug.

There used to be a food scene that made the brochures: Urban List once tipped Nambour Social at 59 Currie St for its house-made truffle hollandaise, and Sprinkles brought vintage diner vibes with menu hits like the Chocolate Biscuit Bowl and the Crumble Sundae. Whether they’re still the local favourites depends who you ask — and which brochure you’re reading.

Who would hate it here? People who need their suburbs to be anonymous glass towers and nothing that could be mistaken for an icon. Who would love it? Families, makers, people who like festivals, and anyone who thinks smashing a crate of crockery counts as wellness. We do too.
