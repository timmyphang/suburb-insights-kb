# Flagstone (QLD)

Slug: `flagstone-qld`

Back in the day we’d say Flagstone was the kind of place where the busiest intersection was the footpath outside 19 Trailblazer Drive — that’s where the community really happened. For years the Flagstone Community Centre used to be our town square: kids in Little Butterflies Playgroup & First Five Forever, someone teaching Arts & Crafts, a seniors posse turning up for the over‑55s group, and the occasional haka practice from Te Kapahaka O Te Ngakau Mahaki that made you stand a little straighter.

If you lived here, your week was scheduled around clinic times like a weird suburban ritual. Every second Monday, Logan City Council ran its immunisation clinic 9am–12pm and, on Tuesdays, Queensland Health turned the centre into a Child Health Clinic. We used to joke that if you missed the clinic, you’d have to wait until the next soap‑opera cliffhanger to get your appointment — but at least the paperwork came with a cuppa from whoever was folding the newsletters.

Our favourite thing was the collisions: seniors chatting over knitting needles while teenagers rolled dice at the fortnightly RPG games and toddlers smeared paint into a modern art masterpiece labelled “Parenting, 2022”. It was gloriously chaotic and completely uncurated — heaven if you like your suburbs multi‑age and slightly sticky.

Who would hate living here? If you crave polished anonymity, 24/7 shopping precincts or a suburb with no chances of being roped into a kindergarten performance, Flagstone probably tested your patience. If you wanted your mail to arrive with a side of someone else’s craft glue, well — apologies in advance.

Who would love it here? Families who appreciate handy Child Health Clinics, people who actually like community groups (yes, even the fortnightly RPGs), and anyone who thinks a local centre should be a place where cultural dance and seniors’ bingo share the same foyer. For years, if you needed info, you could reach the centre at flagstonecommunitycentre@southmoreton.org.au or ring 0491 711 398 — and that, more than any glossy development, is what made us feel like a place, not a postcode.
