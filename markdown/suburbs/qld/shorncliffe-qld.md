# Shorncliffe (QLD)

Slug: `shorncliffe-qld`

Back in the day we’d wheel the pram down the Shorncliffe foreshore like it was our runway, because apparently nothing says suburban chic like a 6km, pram-friendly return trip. As Urban List and Concrete Playground remembered for us, the Shorncliffe Walk — the Sandgate–Shorncliffe stretch — is basically our backyard catwalk: playgrounds, picnic patches and that 350-metre Shorncliffe Pier where the keen ones bait hooks by the pylons at sunrise.

Mornings used to be a ritual of sorts: a circuit along the foreshore, a detour to Lower Moora Park for the calmer family swim spots, then a scout for fresh prawns because Concrete Playground once told us we could buy peel‑your‑own prawns right here. If we were feeling fancypants we’d point a camera at the pier because, yes, Time Out and Urban List both agree the pier is the place for a decent sunrise or sunset snap. The Billai Dha-gun vista from Boondall Wetlands even gives you a bird’s-eye of Shorncliffe for those who like feathered company (the wetlands host a crazy number of birds from September to March, in case you needed another excuse to leave the house).

Local drama? The archive doesn’t give us a juicy saga — mostly walk guides, fishing tips and picnic suggestions. Which is either a blessing or proof that everyone’s too busy fishing off the pier pylons to start a scandal.

Who would hate living here: people allergic to slow bayside life. If you scorn a 6km pram-friendly promenade, can’t stomach the idea of someone catching dinner off the pier, or think rotundas and cliffside picnic spots are passé, Shorncliffe will feel like punishment. Also, if you think furniture shops on Sinbad Street are the worst kind of charm, you missed the mention of Smith & Gray Furniture Makers in old local roundups.

Who’d love it: parents chasing safe walks and playgrounds, anglers hunting Bramble Bay’s best spot, sunrise nerds and anyone who enjoys peeling prawns by the esplanade. For years the local listings — from market notes at 95 Brighton Road to the cheeky nods in Sitchu — have been saying the same thing: it’s quietly lovely, and we’ll defend our pier with a smug smile.
