# Logan Village (QLD)

Slug: `logan-village-qld`

Back in the day — and yes, our reading pack is a touch nostalgic — we still know how to find the good bits of Logan Village.

We start the morning at the Logan Village Green: kids on the mega slide, someone testing the flying fox like it’s an Olympic trial, and scooters carving past the skate park that, as Brisbane Kids noted, sits cheek-by-jowl with the Logan Village police station. By 10am we’ve dropped a toddler at the in-ground trampolines and wandered down Wharf Street to the little library that My Community Directory lists on Wharf Street (Logan Village QLD 4207). It’s small, it’s useful, and it smells faintly of community noticeboards.

If you’re into organised chaos, the Village Rockers Inc. still get a shout in the community listings — Rock‑n‑Roll classes and monthly socials, box‑15 nostalgia and all — while the Logan Village Community Centre at 30 Wharf Street quietly runs the kinds of affordable programs our bank accounts respect. Mama Tribe even includes Brisbane Logan + Surrounds, so the mums meet up and trade survival tips over whatever juice boxes pass for coffee in our era.

Urban List once rounded up eateries in Logan, mentioning places like Extraction Artisan Coffee and Bufwin as part of the wider Logan dining story — so yes, we’ve been counted among the “accessible south-of-Brisbane” crowd. Urban List also flagged Logan City Council opening evacuation centres in Crestmead, Bethania, Jimboomba and Logan Village during the floods, which is to say: when the weather throws a tantrum, this place looks after its own.

Who would hate living here? People who need late-night restaurants on every corner, curated boutique lanes and the kind of buzz you only get inside a tram timetable. If your hobby is complaining about having to park on your own street, move on.

Who would love it? Families who treat a giant slide like heritage, walkers who enjoy the Logan Village to Yarrabilba Rail Trail’s 3.5km of native bush, mums who meet through Mama Tribe, and anyone who appreciates a place where the community centre and the skate park are both civic landmarks. We’d rather be mocked than overlooked — and besides, the flying fox always wins.
