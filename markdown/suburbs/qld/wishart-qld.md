# Wishart (QLD)

Slug: `wishart-qld`

We know the exact moment Wishart remembers summer: when someone realises The Gift Shop has gone and the neighbourhood collectively sighs. Urban List even called it out — “Still mourning the closure of Wishart’s The Gift Shop?” — and yes, we are. We’re sentimental but practical: we cried, then we got in the car.

Back in the day the crew behind that little haunt didn’t sulk — they opened Deli Dakota in Mt Gravatt. We used to hop a suburb over to 30 Badminton Street, Mount Gravatt East, for sandwiches that needed two hands and a prayer. Deli Dakota’s brisket reuben was the kind of thing Urban List wrote love poems about: slow-cooked brisket, BBQ sauce, chilli mayo, sauerkraut, pickles and American cheese on thick malted barley bread. It felt like a civic event every time someone managed to get the thing into their mouth without losing a pickle.

Our weekend routine now reads like an archaeologist’s notes: remember where the hatch was, recalibrate the coffee run, and nod to anyone who’s still part of the Social Mums Club listing that used to cover Mansfield, Mt Gravatt and Wishart. For years the local mums were doing most of the heavy civic work — organising, supporting and making the place feel like a suburb rather than a postcode. We still see that energy in the park, even if the cafe came and went.

Petty drama? That’s simple: the ongoing debate about whether we should pine for what closed or subsidise a pilgrimage to Mt Gravatt for a proper sanga. We have strong opinions and a short memory.

Who would hate living here? If you want an ever-present inner-city hospitality scene on every corner, you’ll grumble — we make our own good things and then sometimes watch them move a street or two away.

Who would love it? Mums who want community, sandwich hunters who don’t mind a short drive to reach gastronomic salvation at 30 Badminton Street, and anyone who likes being part of a south-side suburb that remembers its small pleasures even after they shut their doors. We’re quiet, cheeky and forever nostalgic — and yes, we still talk about The Gift Shop.
