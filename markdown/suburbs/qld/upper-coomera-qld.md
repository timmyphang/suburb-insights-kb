# Upper Coomera (QLD)

Slug: `upper-coomera-qld`

We roll our windows down on Tamborine-Oxenford Road and know exactly what kind of day Upper Coomera is serving: neighbourhood energy, a sprinkle of suburbia spectacle, and someone already prepping their Forest Oak Drive lights like it's Coachella for Christmas.

Back in the day, the weekend looked like this: we’d wander past 382 Tamborine Oxenford Rd where Genki Cafe & Sushi was the sort of community hub that ran art classes and sold locally made gifts — you could almost hear toddlers from Social Mums Club playgroups trying to out-enthuse each other. Cedar Anderson, who ran the Social Mums Club for Coomera & surrounds, was the kind of organiser who made sure mums had somewhere to decant coffee and gossip. We used to lean on that small-town convenience like it was purpose-built.

If we wanted dinner, 388 Tamborine-Oxenford Road housed Charm Thai — uncomplicated, delicious, the kind of takeaway you’d claim was “for the whole family” while you actually scoffed it solo on the couch. And for the slightly outdoorsy or stubbornly fit among us, Cutloose Indoor Climbing wasn’t even open yet but had already stirred the town with promises of a Kilter Board and the 2024 MoonBoard — our hands were tingling at the thought.

Come December, Forest Oak Drive used to be the place you dragged visitors for a festive light tour; the whole street would glow like it was trying to win a suburban Nobel Prize in Christmas. The Christmas Twilight Markets were even slated to make a return for 2025 on Saturday 15 November — a proper bonanza for anyone who likes artisanal candles and awkwardly earnest carol singers.

Time Out named Upper Coomera among Queensland’s most pet-packed suburbs, which explained why half our street knows every dog’s name but not their owner’s. That’s our vibe: a few too many pets, a lot of local goodwill and a penchant for community-run meetups.

Who would hate living here? Someone who needs late-night metropolitan drama, Michelin lists, or an opinionated tram system. Who would love it? Young families, pet owners, the “weekend market” crowd, and anyone who judges a suburb by its Christmas lights and sushi spots with art classes.

We know our corner of the Gold Coast — loud in the best ways, earnest where it counts, and forever ready to light up Forest Oak Drive on a good night.
