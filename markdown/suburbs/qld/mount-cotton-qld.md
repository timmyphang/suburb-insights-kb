# Mount Cotton (QLD)

Slug: `mount-cotton-qld`

Back in the day we told visitors Mount Cotton was where you go when you need the kind of quiet that makes the city feel like a noisy prank. These days the papers still know us — but only because we keep turning up in wholesome stories about wineries, wildlife and playgrounds.

Our weekend looks like this: we caravan out to 51–63 Valley Way and shepherd the smalls through Mount Cotton Village Community Park — the two massive sand-based playgrounds, tube slides, climbing walls and the huge spiderweb thing that makes everyone feel athletic for a hot second. Then there’s the duck pond situation (yes, the one with the perfect viewing deck) where we practice our very unrefined loaves-of-bread throwing technique.

If you want drama, look elsewhere. The local headlines have been kinder: Sirromet Wines at 850 Mount Cotton Road has been the real celebrity of the postcode — cellar-door tastings, Restaurant Lurleens and the Tuscan Terrace, the ‘Sanctuary by Sirromet’ glamping tents that claimed to be Queensland’s first vineyard glamp, and even a run of events that read like a festival brochure (Slideapalooza slid through once, Harry Potter: A Forbidden Forest Experience lit the woods there, and Concrete Playground listed a Fiery Winter Warmers with private firepits that came with a price tag in the press). We once saw a Fatboy Slim listing for an outdoor show at Sirromet too — because why not DJing among vines?

We’re also the kind of place that keeps koalas in our storylines — Venman Bushland National Park gets name-checked as protected koala habitat, so bring your binoculars and your etiquette.

Who would hate living here? People who need three bars of nightlife and hourly trams. People allergic to sand in every conceivable crevice will lament the park’s generous playground sand policy. Who would love it? Families who know the park, couples who booked the glamping tents, wine nerds who liked the cellar door write-ups and anyone who wants koalas and country air without actually owning a farm.

We know every corner because the writing about Mount Cotton is blissfully earnest: playgrounds, wineries, and a nature trail or two. We’ll happily keep it that way.
