# Tarragindi (QLD)

Slug: `tarragindi-qld`

We’ll be honest: our favourite Sunday starts with a muddy pair of shoes and Toohey Forest in our lungs. Tarragindi is the sort of place where you can lose an hour on a meandering track and still be back in time for something artisanal — or at least artisanal-adjacent.

Back in the day (and by that we mean: all the press we’ve got is from before 2024), the papers made Tarragindi sound like the quiet sibling who suddenly discovered small-batch cred. Sitchu reminded everyone we’re under 10km from the city with Toohey Forest on our doorstep; Broadsheet and Concrete Playground celebrated L.P.O., the tiny wine store run by Matt Okine and Dan Wilson with a rotating handful of rare drops to taste. The old Tarragindi Fruit Barn made way for Good Things Grocer — Kymberlee and Sarah Stone’s nostalgic corner‑store project, born after their café was hit in the 2022 Lismore floods. We used to brag about that kind of resilience.

Our weekend ritual used to be very Tarragindi: forest walk, then somewhere that takes toasties infuriatingly seriously. Dijon (from the Deedot / Never Been folks) was the kind of place that made you reassess what a sangas could be; Bruno’s BNE — Bruno Silva Franca’s long‑running local — served things like banoffee French toast and convinced half the suburb to bring their dogs. Black Lab Coffee and Esher St Cafe and Deli kept the rustic, neighbourly vibe going, and Leaf Espresso would flip from salads by day to burgers and live music by night. Oh Monk even waved a slightly more ambitious flag with Chef Batt’s Bangkok‑to‑Brisbane résumé.

Petty local drama? There isn’t any juicy council scandal here in the files — just a mild identity crisis: are we a leafy refuge for walkers, or a boutique toastie-and-wine precinct? We’d say both, with generous amounts of dog hair.

Who would hate it here: anyone allergic to dogs, small-batch grocery aisles, or the idea of being asked which of eight tiny wines you’d like to taste. Who would love it: people who want a walk in the gumtrees, then a serious sandwich, then a nostalgic grocer to justify the whole outing. Tarragindi — quaint, green, and quietly excellent.
