# Elimbah (QLD)

Slug: `elimbah-qld`

Back in the day we’d split our weekends between two very Australian extremes: tractor rides and strawberries. We’re the people who queued for a basket at Rolin Farm — you know, the pick‑your‑own patch they wrote up as “moments from the Glass House Mountains” — and who could talk at length about the strict “no eating in the patch” rule like it was sacred. We’d wander Hamilton Rd to White Ridge Farm (130 Hamilton Rd, as the guides helpfully remind you) on Wednesdays and Sundays, where the llamas, alpacas, horses, goats, ducks, sheep and yes, even a camel, used to be the sort of celebrity we actually cared about. Urban List and family blogs loved that bit of wholesome chaos.

We’ll admit we have a soft spot for the local hardware vibe too — Elimbah Co‑Op on English St and Glasshouse Orchids were the kinds of places you’d pop into for something you’d inevitably then turn into a gardening problem. For years the local listings and mums’ guides were all we needed: school holiday activities, Grasshopper Soccer covering the area, farmstays and strawberry advice — gumboots were optional to think about, but only if you read the right forum.

Then, in the realm of “please explain”, a 68‑hectare proposal called Infinity Planet was floated off the Bruce Highway and lodged with Moreton Bay City Council. Time Out and Urban List wrote up this $2.6 billion entertainment-city-that-might-be, promising to “bring the world to Australia”. We love a big idea, but there’s something delicious about imagining a camel and a theme park queue sharing a postcode. It’s peak Elimbah contradiction and we own it.

Who would hate living here? If you need a sushi bar on every corner, a tram outside your door, or you recoil at the sight of a fossil dig and putt‑putt golf next to a petting paddock, this won’t be your jam.

Who would love it? Families who liked the old-school farm days, people who garden and don’t mind driving past the Glass House Mountains for strawberries, and anyone who enjoys a suburb that can be modestly rural one minute and wildly aspirational the next. We’ll take the llamas, the seedlings and the wild idea of Infinity Planet — preferably in that order.
