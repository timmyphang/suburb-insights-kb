# Wamuran (QLD)

Slug: `wamuran-qld`

Back in the day — or at least according to the listings we could dig up — Wamuran was the kind of place where your Saturday started with a highway, a sunflower field and a meat counter that knew your surname.

We remember the D'Aguilar Highway stretch where Wamuran Country Markets used to set up (4th Saturday of the month, and the 3rd Saturday in December for the Christmas Market, if you were the sort who planned ahead). The stalls were very on-brand: plants, honey, handcrafted bits, candles and jams, plus the inevitable array of small-business hustle you’d find on a FindGlocal page. Nearby, Sunflower Sue’s Sunflower Farm on OSHEA Road used to fling open the gates for seasonal visitors — Mother’s Day was a thing there, with a $15 entry and kids under 10 getting in for free. There was even a contact phone for the curious.

If you liked your local commerce served up in directory form, Wamuran had options. FindGlocal and LocalBusinessGuide listed everyone from Fur Addiction to Hermes Strawberries, Martial Life Gear, Kenzie’s Cricut Designs and Wamuran Quality Palms and Plants. And for the practical among us, LocalBusinessGuide catalogued excavators like Cg Mini Earth Moving, G & K Gartner Wamuran Excavations and Turbo Track Excavations — because someone has to dig for the next market carpark.

Wamuran Country Meats at 1058 D'Aguilar Highway used to be the anecdotal heart of the place: established in late 2011 by Anthony and Amy Carney, they billed themselves on great value, a gourmet range and even gluten‑free lines. Their listings showed posts about shop renovations and specials — proof we weren’t the only ones keeping tabs.

Who would hate living here? People who need a sparkling late-night mall and a tram every five minutes. If your idea of leisure is a curated chain store, Wamuran will make you twitch.

Who would love it? Folks who like small markets, sunflower selfies, plants you can actually afford, and the comforting bureaucracy of local directories recommending party entertainers and school holiday camps. We’ll keep the highway, the pumpkins and the phone numbers — you bring the folding chair.
