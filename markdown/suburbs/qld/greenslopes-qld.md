# Greenslopes (QLD)

Slug: `greenslopes-qld`

We know Greenslopes like the back of our sunburnt hands — the short walk from Greenslopes Mall to the hospital, the bus stop gossip on Logan Road, the way a Saturday feels both incongruously calm and quietly competitive. Weirdly, everything we dug up about our patch is a bit of a dusty scrapbook — so consider this part roast, part nostalgia tour.

Back in the day we’d line up at The Jam Pantry — 2/575 Logan Road — for Naomi Zavackas’s preserves and whatever brilliant left‑of‑field breakfast she was serving, until Broadsheet reminded us it served its last customers on Sunday March 29. We still talk about it at the bus stop like it’s a lost ex. Down the strip 5 Boroughs at 401‑405 Logan Road wore its New York inspiration proudly until the listings quietly labelled it “Permanently Closed.” Classic Greenslopes energy: try hard, close eventually, swap stories about it for years.

Our corner used to be a parade of niche fixes: Souffle Japancakes’ wobblier-than-life stacks, Charlie’s Raw Squeeze at 47 Old Cleveland Road promising acai salvation, Rock’n’Roll Bakery wedged on Logan Road pretending to be a deli and a rock star at once. Cheeky Poke Bar even sprouted a Greenslopes outlet in the chain expansion files — which, in our archive, reads like proof we were briefly very cosmopolitan.

Real estate agents love whispering Sackville Street and galleries of Sitchu listings promise you’ll be “steps from IGA, Jam Pantry, and CBD buses” — a succinct way of saying you can afford the commute and maybe a croissant if you skip one latte. Greenslopes Private Hospital on Newdegate Street is our reliable constant — Ramsay’s 24‑hour pharmacy there gets name‑checked in the mums’ guides, and maternity lists have pointed people our way. Lavelle Hair used to get a shoutout as a family‑friendly cut spot. Social Mums Club keeps the playground politics civil.

Who’d hate it here? If you can’t handle a suburb that remembers its small wins and mourns its cafes, you’ll be bored. Who’d love it? Families, bargain‑hunting house hunters and anyone who likes their local history served with a side of fond exasperation. We’ll keep talking about the places that left a mark — even if the internet only left us the receipts.
