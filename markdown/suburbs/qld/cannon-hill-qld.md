# Cannon Hill (QLD)

Slug: `cannon-hill-qld`

Back in the day we used to start Saturdays with the sensible chaos Cannon Hill does best: a muddy Cannon Hill parkrun, a wander through Minnippi Parklands and then a strategic negotiation about lunch that inevitably ended at Billy’s Pine and Bamboo. As Broadsheet remembered, the Wong family’s Peking duck was a tableside ritual for 35 years — the sort of thing you brag about at school pickups like it’s an Olympic sport.

We live where people measure convenience in park benches and butcher counters. Cannon Central’s certified gluten‑free butcher (yes, really) used to be our place for sausages in 20 flavours and dry‑aged wagyu that made the neighbours suspiciously impressed. If you fancied something more charcoal‑forward, Maru Grill House on Mary Street has long been part of the local dinner rotation, while Time Out pointed out our Wynnum Road Soonta as a reason to make the trip for bánh mì and bowls.

Concrete Playground once ran with the headline that Cooee Cafe & Restaurant at 1825 Creek Road — the 200‑seat spot overlooking Minnippi Golf & Range — was opening in February 2024. We watched that announcement like proud relatives at a christening, and then kept walking the parklands anyway. Between Porter's Paddock and the golf course, our weekend hangouts read like a brochure for moderate outdoor guilt.

If you hate kids, green spaces, sensible commutes and people who actually have a weekly routine, Cannon Hill will be your ongoing disappointment. If you need late‑night house parties and neon, you’ll be mildly perplexed by how many playgrounds we manage to fit into one suburb.

Who would actually love it here? Growing families, parkrun pilgrims, anyone who enjoys a proper roast duck, and bargain‑hunters who appreciate a speciality butcher. The Social Mums Club and local services like Hamptons Swim School used to keep the baby brigade entertained, and for anyone who likes being 15 minutes from the city while still smelling grass at dinner, Cannon Hill is our perfectly ordinary, quietly proud little kingdom.
