# Stafford (QLD)

Slug: `stafford-qld`

Back in the day we’d set our alarms for 7am like it was the footy, queueing at Bella & Tortie (12/240 Stafford Road) because, yes, a line formed from 7am on Fridays and Saturdays for that siu mai‑inspired sausage roll — doors listed as opening 7:30am–midday, but nobody cared about punctuality once the smell hit Stafford Road. 

Our weekend routine used to be embarrassingly simple: wander the Soul Pantry nursery (the plant people among us will tell you it was a ritual), linger in the cosy corner cafe after a botanical browse — the place even had a colourful makeover that made us feel cultured for a few hours — then negotiate brunch territory with Enoki Coffee & Co, which, bless it, was Stafford’s “newest breakfast spot” with a mint‑green interior and footpath seating and a menu that was almost all vego. For late nights we’d console ourselves with Miss Sophia’s Gelateria at 240 Stafford Road, which was rumoured to do rotating flavours into the late hours.

We also watched the local icons shift. King Of The Wings finally landed at Stafford Central — a food‑truck legend graduating to indoor dining and an outdoor laneway — while a much older relic, the Stafford Skate Centre on Stafford Road, was reported to be set to close after four decades once the building was sold, the lease due to end in September and a gym lined up to move in. Change, obviously, is our sport.

Who would hate living here? If you need everything open seven days, hate a two‑day bakery cult, or believe a roller rink is non‑negotiable nightlife, you’ll be miserable. If historic roller disco vibes matter more than a great wing spot, this place will test you.

Who would love it? Plant obsessives, weekend bakery pilgrims, vego breakfast devotees and anyone who likes their neighbourhood to feel like it’s quietly earned its charms — with a few closed chapters and a few new menus to argue about. We’re Stafford, QLD: small‑town rhythms with a stubborn appetite for good food and slow change.
