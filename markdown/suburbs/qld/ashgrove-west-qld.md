# Ashgrove West (QLD)

Slug: `ashgrove-west-qld`

We start our mornings like any proper Ashgrove West native: wandering past 498 Waterworks Road and stopping to squint at the mural Joel Birch once slapped on the wall — back when Broadsheet did a first look at Macho Macho and the place was a travel agency turned pink-and-exposed-brick shrine. Macho Macho used to serve dukkah‑crusted eggplant, fall‑off‑the‑bone beef rib and zesty pancakes with honeycomb; we say “used to” because, honest to goodness, all our sources are from back in the day — there hasn’t been much recent ink.

Weekends follow a dependable loop that reads like a local guide from the archives: we used to pick up bread from Banneton and cakes from Gerbino’s, grab something wholesome from The Produce Bulk Foods, and drop the kids at the Home Cafe playroom on 20 Stewart Road while someone in the group pretended to be surprised by how relaxed Revive’s pilates made them feel. Urban List and Concrete Playground liked to remind us that Osaka Japanese Dining Bar was worth the ten‑minute drive, and the Brisbane Mums Group catalogued our collective life — Corn Street Cafe, Cafe 63, Ashgrove Carols By Candlelight and even the Halloween Outdoor Cinema.

There’s no juicy council scandal in the clips we’ve got. If you were after a petty local drama, the files are disappointingly wholesome: playgrounds with “enchanted forest” vibes, seed pods collected under the leopard trees at Dorrington Park off Frasers Road, pilates studios and bakeries. We respect the dullness; it’s part of the charm.

Who would hate living here? Night‑owls after a proper city buzz, anyone allergic to artisan bread or community carol services, and people who can’t stomach a mural by a touring metal vocalist in their suburb. You’ll grumble at the benign civility.

Who would love it? Families, people who like their weekends organised and their options listed in a mum‑blog, bakery devotees, pilates converts and, frankly, anyone who thinks a neighbourhood should come with an enchanted playground and a neatly catalogued set of local habits. Back in the day, that’s what made Ashgrove West ours — quiet, slightly fancy, and very, very well documented in older weekend reads.
