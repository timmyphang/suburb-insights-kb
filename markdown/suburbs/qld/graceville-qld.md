# Graceville (QLD)

Slug: `graceville-qld`

Back in the day we’d cross the Walter Taylor Bridge like it was a pilgrimage — Honour Avenue on the other side looking like a theatrical set of Queenslanders, bakeries and baby strollers. We know every bench and tree here: the village strip used to be a proud little lineup of places Broadsheet and UrbanList took photos of — Plentiful (opened March 2020, apparently worth the bridge-run), Hunter & Scout’s heritage courtyard, Miettes doing continental mornings and Ananas Espresso at 4 Central Avenue after Marissa and Paul Robins gave it a new life. Even the kids had their routine: Lick! Ice Cream, started by Grant and Carina Lew, whose strawberry flavour the little critics adored.

Our version of excitement was never a nightclub queue but a new patisserie, a bottle shop with personality or someone calling dibs on the best picnic patch at Simpsons Playground. Speaking of which, Simpsons — by the river, with shade, toilets and the long paved path — is where Brisbane Canoeing Inc. ran intro-to-kayaking sessions and where families learned to paddle without capsizing the dinner conversation.

Then the soap-opera one-act: Ardo’s rolled into town promising neighbourhood wine wisdom, only for the City Winery story to go sideways when the company entered voluntary liquidation and the Ardo’s venues closed. We watched that one like a slow-motion grape stomp.

Who would hate living here? If you crave anonymous high-rise drama, or you need a late-night neon strip, Graceville will bore you dumb with its tree-lined calm, heritage homes and too-many-friendly nods from people who know your dog’s name. If you loathe slow weekends, riverside strolls and cafes that double as community noticeboards, this is not your postcode.

Who’d love it? Families, kayakers, people who measure success in playgrounds visited and festivals attended — remember the inaugural Graceville Festival that popped up in 2024 — and investors (Sitchu noted property lifts of about 23.4% and values nudging into seven figures). We’ll keep our little village charm, our foreshore walks and our cafés-in-memory, thank you very much — and if the bridge is closed, we’ll still manage to complain about it together.
