# Tewantin (QLD)

Slug: `tewantin-qld`

Back in the day our idea of a Saturday was simple: a river breeze, someone from Hastings Street boarding a cruise that would cough and spit them out at Tewantin, and us pretending we weren’t eyeing the waterfront lunch spots. The Noosa Marina (2 Parkyn Court) is our front yard — you can still see where Downward Dog Hot Yoga used to sweat it out in Shop 11A, and where Lucio’s Marina holds court at 3/2 Parkyn Ct (Urban List even name‑checked it in a 2026 restaurants roundup). Peli’s Noosa was slated to open on 12 September 2024, which is fancy way of saying: we were expecting a new bistro to stare at the water with us.

Our local soap opera? The Royal Mail Hotel’s reinvention. It reopened and swallowed the neighbouring Liquorland to become part timber‑clad bistro, part beer garden — picnic tables now look out over the Noosa River while big screens make sure no rugby, cricket or footy moment goes uncelebrated. If that’s not community revitalisation, it’s at least very useful on a Tuesday night.

For a small place we’re suspiciously well catered for: the Tewantin Market Garden on 93 Poinciana Avenue does the local-produce thing, the Noosa Outlook IGA and Tewantin Newsagency keep us fed and lettered, and the Tewantin Noosa Tennis Club on McKinnon Drive has ten Mod‑Grass courts and even a Wimbledon‑themed tournament on the calendar. Little kids get spoiled too — Tewantin Community Kindergarten on Lake Doonella boasts a nature‑based playground and, yes, people actually rave about its Exceeding rating.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe river cruises, beer gardens that doubled as communal sofas, or Christmas carnivals that explode into fireworks (the 2025 Tewantin Christmas Carnival sounded like a full sensory operation), then no, this isn’t your urban jungle. Also, if you can’t abide community festivals, you’ll roll your eyes at NOOSA alive! — auditions were open for its STARS alive! finals at Tewantin‑Noosa Lions Park.

Who’d love it? Families who want lake and river life, weekend tennis players, people who like their dinner with a marina view, and anyone nostalgic for a town that still thinks turning Liquorland into a beer garden is peak progress. We know every corner here, and honestly — we wouldn’t trade our little riverside parade for the world.
