# Caloundra (QLD)

Slug: `caloundra-qld`

Back in the day we’d start Saturdays like it’s a ritual: toes in the sand at Kings Beach, a cautious wander past the rock pools, then a slow drift along Bulcock and Moffat looking for a patch of sky that isn’t hosting a kite or a tourist. Caloundra has always been that awkwardly proud little seaside village that grew up — the kind of place that wears its coastal charisma like sunscreen: generous, a bit patchy, but impossible to ignore.

We used to lose afternoons following the Lamkin Lane and Dingle Ave mural trail, ducking into the Caloundra Regional Gallery when the weather turned soggy, and timing our day so we hit the Caloundra Twilight Markets or Currimundi Lake Twilight Market just as the sun decided whether we were having dinner or dessert. Broadsheet couldn’t get enough of The Angry Baker’s Cincinnati Beer Chilli and Cheese Pie — and nor could we pretend not to love it.

Our community was never just sand and surf. Library+ Caloundra opened up a whole new chapter at 1 Omrah Avenue with kids’ zones and all the stuff parents can brag about; the Caloundra Community Centre at 58A Queen Street kept the real, weekday-life running with playgroups, ukulele, book clubs and the kind of local programs that make you feel like you belong. Jujuba Play Café arrived for the small humans and the exhausted grown-ups who needed a chair.

For music-heads we had real bragging rights: the Caloundra Music Festival took over Kings Beach Amphitheatre — remember when it marked its 15th year in 2022 and even had camping at Caloundra State Primary School? — and festivals like The Curated Plate parked fine dining on buses around Happy Valley to remind us we were also culinary-adjacent. Even Motel Caloundra got in on the reinvention act, turning a tired stay into something boutique and oddly eco‑serious.

Who would hate it here? People who want 3am nightclub drama, or who think a coastal town’s job is to be eternally anonymous. If you can’t cope with markets, murals and the constant possibility of whale‑spotting in Pumicestone Passage, this isn’t your scene.

Who’d love it? Families, mural hunters, festival goers and anyone who likes their sunsets with a side of pie, surf and a surprisingly good dinner — because Caloundra has always been quietly excellent at being itself.
