# St Lucia (QLD)

Slug: `st-lucia-qld`

Back in the day our weekday ritual was very particular: we’d weave between jacaranda blossoms on the University of Queensland campus, cross the Eleanor Schonell (Green) Bridge and argue — politely, in academic tones — about whether St Lucia’s proper breakfast came from a six‑seat espresso bar or a new-ish vegan grab‑and‑go.

We say "six‑seat" because Broadsheet once reminded everyone that If You Say So squeezed into a former 1900s tea house and served house‑baked croissants with Black Lab Coffee. Yonder Espresso, which opened in September 2021, tried to fill the specialty‑coffee gap with Single O on espresso and rotating guest roasters — and we noticed. Nearby Hawken Drive has been a mood: Sorelle Eatery (from the Tognini team) set up shop with tiramisu cruffins and mortadella toasties; meanwhile Time Out quietly kept recommending A Salt & Battery at 1/224 Hawken Dr as the students’ fish‑and‑chips go‑to. La Porchetta? The concreteplayground entry says it’s closed — so yes, some traditions truly do die.

Weekends look like one of our favourite contrasts: the manicured calm of St Lucia Golf Links on Carawa Street and Hillstone St Lucia’s wedding manicures sit cheek‑by‑cheek with kid chaos. Guyatt Park — aka Warrior Park, as Brisbane Kids called it — has Brisbane’s first free outdoor ninja warrior course and two playgrounds, and the UQ Playground is an inclusive spectacle of slides and a flying fox. Playhouse Childcare UQ on 27 Keith Street even backs onto the sports oval, which is handy for parents who enjoy pretending their lives are under control.

There isn’t any juicy council scandal in the clips we’ve seen — just a suburb that likes its grammar as much as its green spaces. For people who would hate living here: if you loathe riverside runs, jacaranda season, or the idea of mini golf (St Lucia Mini Golf, 12 holes, yes, it exists on Carawa Street), move inland fast.

Who’d love it? Students, families, golfers, and anyone who appreciates a riverside promenade, a university with sandstone charm, and the quiet thrill of a licensed bar that looks over putting greens (we’re looking at you, Hundred Acre/Hillstone mentions). We’re biased, of course — but when the jacarandas bloom, even our most pretentious lectures go soft.
