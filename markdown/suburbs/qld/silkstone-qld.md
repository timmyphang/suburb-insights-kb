# Silkstone (QLD)

Slug: `silkstone-qld`

We wake up, make a cuppa (quietly, because we’re trying not to disturb the mystery), and check the internet to see how many people in the world think about Silkstone, QLD. Answer: almost none. Back in the day Concrete Playground gave us a cameo — not here, mind you, but as an echo across the Pacific. They wrote about Half Acre in South Melbourne and described Adam Wright‑Smith as ex‑Fat Radish and ex‑"Silkstone NYC". Yes, our name once turned up in print next to a greenhouse restaurant review. We frame that, naturally.

If you’re hoping for a glossy Time Out spread your name, good luck. Time Out prefers skull‑shaped rocks three hours from Melbourne and compiling "The 37 Best Places To Visit in Australia" (updated 2026) — and while we admire Skull Rock’s theatrical sunset, it’s not on our street corner. Melissa Woodley’s solstice and musicals pieces are lovely reads, but they’re not about us either. Which, frankly, suits us.

There’s no petty council saga recorded here, no viral café fight, no bar that made national headlines. The only recorded local scandal is that we’re reliably under‑mentioned. We own that. We like being the place major outlets forget to write about. It keeps the noise down and the backyard barbecues uncrowded.

Who would hate living here? If your identity requires being on a Time Out list, or if you need your suburb to have a repeat cameo in national food press, you’ll be restless. You’ll pace, refreshing feeds, waiting for someone to declare us the next big thing.

Who would love it here? People who like a postcode with a low profile, that’s who. Folks who like keeping things simple, who treat "ex‑Silkstone NYC" as a curious footnote rather than a promise, and who’d rather discover a place themselves than have it curated for them. We’re not a spectacle; we’re a quiet, stubborn little suburb with a weird name history and a lot of elbow room. Come for the anonymity, stay because you accidentally forgot to move.
