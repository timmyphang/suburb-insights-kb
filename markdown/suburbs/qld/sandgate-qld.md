# Sandgate (QLD)

Slug: `sandgate-qld`

Back in the day we’d start a weekend with the kind of slow, salty routine only the northside can do: mosey along the Shorncliffe–Sandgate foreshore walk, nod at the 350-metre Shorncliffe Pier and pretend we were on holiday while the kids argued over who sat where on the esplanade bench. Concrete Playground even called us “the Atlantic City of Queensland” — which we laughed at, then queued for fish and chips like the sophisticated coastal mob we are.

Our version of culture used to include everything from waterside picnics at Sandgate Foreshores Park to small, earnest markets where you could buy a plant, a vintage tee and a bad coffee in the same stall. Sitchu used to brag about our flourishing café scene and markets; Broadsheet once listed the Sandgate Aquatic Centre among public pools offering $2 entry — literally cheaper than chips, which felt about right.

We had a few neighbourhood characters in the news: Junkee collected the tale of someone putting a turtle into Einbunpin Lagoon, sparking the kind of local conspiracy theories that make community Facebook groups feel like law courts. There was also the time a family-friendly chain was said to be “dropping anchor” here — Urban List wrote that Bubba Gump Shrimp Co. was set to open at 94 Rainbow Street — and yes, that made the afternoon walk feel slightly more themed-park-adjacent.

If you hate gentle seaside weirdness, small-town markets, and pubs with playgrounds, you’ll despise us. Our Full Moon Hotel got name-checked as a kid-friendly pub; that’s code for sticky floors and two-plate meal deals — not everyone’s cup of tea. Likewise, people who need a tram to the nearest Michelin star will find our paced, community-first vibe painfully slow.

If you love bay breezes, old-school fishmongers and local history, this is your jam. We used to send visitors to Sandgate Fishmonger on Flinders Parade, point them toward Arthur Davis Park and the Sandgate Historical Museum on Rainbow Street, and watch them leave thinking they’d found a seaside village inside the city. We’ll keep handing out that particular northside welcome: a bench on the esplanade, a suspiciously well-seasoned barbecue, and the knowledge that Sandgate has always been quietly doing its own thing.
