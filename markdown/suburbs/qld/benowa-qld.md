# Benowa (QLD)

Slug: `benowa-qld`

There’s a silver Prado idling on Benowa Road with a Cavoodle named Biscuit on a designer cushion, while its owner scrolls the Benowa State School Facebook thread like it’s the morning paper. We’re that neat line of SUVs (Prado, LandCruiser, CR‑V) that performs a solemn drop‑off ballet at school, each driver insisting they “just popped in for a coffee” on their way to F45. We call it laid‑back Gold Coast; our lawns call it a full‑time hobby.

Saturday at 8:50 we’re at the little café by Woolworths — yes, the one everyone swears by — ordering oat flat whites, extra shot, and comparing Royal Pines tee times. At 9:00 the kids are on the oval for junior soccer, and by noon we’ve scrolled three real estate listings with hinterland views and mentally pencilled in suburb bragging for Sunday’s meat raffle at Benowa Bowls Club. Sunday is a slow Nerang River walk, a piano lesson, then a quiet BBQ where someone inevitably whispers about the proposed apartment on the riverfront and whether it will “change the vibe.”

Our community has heated debates that would mortify a diplomat. The Facebook parent group exploded on Tuesday over shade sails versus more Instagram‑friendly swings — entire threads devolved into memes. In the strip outside Woolworths, there’s an ongoing parking war: someone gets a passive‑aggressive note tucked under a windscreen wiper and the offender is shunned on social media for a week. The council kerfuffle over that new development by the Nerang River is our Saturday night dinner theatre.

We obsess over small certainties. We trim our front lawns to two centimetres, book Royal Pines tee times like early bird specials, alternate between true‑crime and self‑help podcasts on the school run, and rehearse “we’re looking at private schooling” in case the right person asks. Our wardrobes are 70 percent Lululemon; our cookie tins are full of Anzac biscuit recipes; our pets are cavoodles with names that are also pastries.

Who would hate it here? The hard‑party, skateboard‑without‑a‑helmet, I‑haven’t‑been‑to‑a‑PTA‑meeting type would be a walking cultural mismatch. They’d be aghast at immaculate hedges, baffled by the passive‑aggressive note economy, and offended by how seriously we negotiate a 3:15 pick‑up like a UN summit.

But if you love punctual school runs, hedge perfection, a decent flat white and a bowls club where everyone knows your name — and Biscuit the Cavoodle might be your new best mate — welcome home.
