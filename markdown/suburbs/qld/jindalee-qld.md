# Jindalee (QLD)

Slug: `jindalee-qld`

We know exactly how a Jindalee Saturday starts: we’d park at the Home Centre and wander over to that improbably placed floating cafe—Bare Bones Society—Kym Machin’s back‑to‑basics experiment that used to sit in the middle of the carpark like a chef’s island in a sea of trolleys. Back in the day we’d nurse a pastry from Two Teaspoons on Goggs Road and wonder whether the rainbow latte at Piggy Back on Curragundi Road was a cry for help or just very committed brunch theatre. (As Urban List noted.)

If you were watching local business theatre, the Kym and Jade Machin saga was our soap: Broadsheet recorded their Corinda pop-up Same Same But Different opening, then closing with its last service on August 16 — the kind of small‑town headline that makes the supermarket aisles feel like front page real estate. They even opened a grab‑and‑go around the corner from their Jindalee spot; we kept an eye on the revolving door of café ideas like proud, mildly judgmental neighbours.

We’ve also got the suburban luxuries: a six‑screen Reading Cinema at DFO Jindalee — fully reclining seats and a Titan Luxe room — which Concrete Playground wrote up when it opened on Boxing Day. National Cinema Day came through here, too, because nothing says community spirit like synchronized reclining.

And yes, we’ll shout about the water: Jindalee Pool featured in the $2 summer dip scheme and, as Concrete Playground reported, attendance doubled in the 2023–24 season. We’ll tack on the 3.6‑kilometre riverside walk that passes the pool and finishes at that long 800‑metre promenade, because we like pretending we earned our weekend snacks.

Who would hate living here? If your idea of a renaissance is a CBD cocktail bar and you can’t abide prams, river walks, or mums on a mission, Jindalee will feel small-town cosy in the worst way. Who would love it? Families, walk‑obsessed weekend warriors, bargain cinema fans and anyone who appreciates a community that shows up — whether it’s for $2 pool dips or a Wednesday meet‑up with the Social Mums Club.

We’re proud of this place, even when we roast it. After all, where else do you get boutique pastry drama, reclining cinema superiority, and a riverside promenade all within earshot of a shopping centre carpark?
