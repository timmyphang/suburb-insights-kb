# Alexandra Hills (QLD)

Slug: `alexandra-hills-qld`

Back in the day — or at least according to a bunch of older write-ups — Alexandra Hills was the sort of place that politely insisted on two things: lit-up cul‑de­sacs and very good playgrounds. We know every glowing house on Ashbury Court and Oates Crescent; Time Out and Urban List nudged tourists our way to gawp at the Christmas lights, and honestly, we’ve never been so smug about a suburban power bill.

A typical weekend for us used to look like this: the kids run riot at Hanover Drive Park or O’Gorman Street Park while we argue about whether the painted activity paths are secretly for competitive scooters. The Social Mums Club had our calendar sorted — meetups, self‑care listings like Sanctuary of Self, and handy directories that even name local essentials such as Terry White Chemmart Alexandra Hills. For a beat, we felt very organised.

If you liked a pint with a side of putt, the Alexandra Hills Hotel on 332 Finucane Road used to be our multi‑tasking hero — a mega‑pub with a bistro, 40 rooms and a swish mini‑golf that wrapped itself around the Fairway Beer Garden so parents could sip and supervise. Urban List and Time Out both made a point of it, which somehow made our local date nights feel internationally approved.

We’ll also own that we’ve been the sort of suburb critics could tuck into a feature piece about cafes and Christmas hams. Broadsheet name‑checked Pattemore’s Meats — pick‑up only from Alexandra Hills Shopping Centre — as a top ham source, and The Mailman on 2 Bluebell Street got the brunch write‑ups (it was described as cosy and drawy in its heyday). These mentions are from older pieces, so we’re being nostalgic, not making a current events list.

Who would hate it here? If you loathe kids’ laughter, festive cul‑de‑sacs, or the sound of someone sinking a satisfying putt while your neighbour lights up every gumtree, this place will feel like a sensory attack.

Who would love it? Families, Christmas pilgrims, mini‑golf addicts, and anyone who appreciates a well‑stocked local directory (or a good ham pick‑up story). We’re tidy, we’re bright, and our parks are embarrassingly good.
