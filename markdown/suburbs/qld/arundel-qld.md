# Arundel (QLD)

Slug: `arundel-qld`

We’ll admit it up front: there aren’t any recent pieces in our dataset about Arundel, so back in the day is where most of our receipts come from. Which suits us — we’re the kind of place that prefers a local legend to a trending hashtag.

Mornings here start with running the school drop circuit: AB Paterson School, then a detour past the train/light rail station because someone’s forgotten the lunchbox again. If you’re nostalgic for weekdays, Sitchu used to brag about houses like the 3‑bed, 2‑bath on 5 Lani Court — roomy enough for mismatched trophies and that mysterious second piano. Shopping days mean a short drive to Westfield Helensvale or Harbour Town; uni kids still remember Griffith University as the reason their rent is a saga.

For weekends we used to lean family-first: Urban List once named Inflatable World at 61 Newheath Drive as an indoor-party king for the small people, and the same site lumped Arundel into its rainy-day roundups (line dancing, anyone?). Frosted Kitchen at 1 Marble Arch Place was flashed around as the local cake saviour for birthdays that refuse to be boring. And when we wanted a big night out, Urban List had us pencilled in for Hound & Stag Brewing Co. at 13 Wrights Place — a proper taproom with live music, if memory serves.

There’s local lore, too. Junkee recalled the night two blokes intercepted a robbery while “having a laugh over a fingered (broken) plugga” outside an Arundel service station and Oporto — proof that our version of heroism involves socks, sandals and bad timing.

Who would hate living here? People who need beachfront Instagram shots before breakfast, or anyone who panics at the sight of a kids’ birthday inflatable, a community noticeboard, or a brewery that doubles as a music venue. Also, if you can’t cope with helpful local networks — yes, we have a Social Mums Club run by Sharyn Watson that keeps the gossip tidy and the baby clothes swapping efficient — this won’t be your thing.

Who would love it? Families, cake-buyers, brewery-goers, Griffith students dodging campus deadlines, and anyone who prefers a suburb that feels lived-in rather than curated. We’re practical, a touch loud, and full of conveniences — even the wheelchair‑accessible rental at 10 Demand Ave used to get a shout‑out. Come for the haul of shops and services; stay because someone’s always got spare fairy‑bread.
