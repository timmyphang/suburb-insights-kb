# Bellbird Park (QLD)

Slug: `bellbird-park-qld`

We know Bellbird Park by the sight of a diesel Ford Ranger parked on Galah Crescent with a pram in the tray and Mum balancing a skinny oat flat white in one hand and a Lululemon tote in the other. We drink our soy piccolos from Morning Roost Café, wave to Baxter the Cavoodle, and judge your recycling like it's our civic duty. We are equal parts Bunnings-obsessed tradie and pilates‑class perfectionist. We will fix your fence and tell you which lawn mower is objectively superior at 7am on a Sunday.

Our mornings are a ritual. We do the school run to Bellbird Park State School, dodge the Prado that's half on the footpath, and scroll the community Facebook group while reversing into a non-existent parking spot. At 8:02am someone posts a screenshot of the Prado. The passive-aggressive comments arrive by 8:05. Someone nominates the Prado owner for the next sausage sizzle and the thread explodes. This is theatre. This is us.

Weekends look like this: Saturday, two hours at Bunnings where the sausage sizzle is mandatory and the $20 wallet balance is considered a badge of honour. Then Orion Springfield City for groceries, footy practice, and that sense of achievement when the trailer is loaded for the sunset run to the bottle shop on Old Logan Road. Sunday is lawn mowing at 7am, a 30-minute HIIT at Goodlife (or Anytime Fitness if you prefer fluorescent lighting), and a fence-line debate about house prices that would put any economist to shame.

Our local dramas are petty and proud. There was an epic Facebook spat about who left the council bins out for three days that ended with two neighbours refusing to speak at the local footy barbecue. There is a sacred order for who hosts the next sausage sizzle. There are parking wars on Station Road that could be a new sport. These battles are fought with screenshots, emojis, and the kind of thinly veiled compliments that double as accusations.

Who would hate living here? The downtown, minimal‑design vegan cyclist who thinks a car is for peasants and 'weekend projects' are existential performance art. They will be traumatised by 7am mowing, the ubiquity of diesel utes, and our communal economy of sausages.

Who would love it? The late‑30s tradie‑turned‑dad with a Prado for the kids, a Lululemon mum in pilates leggings, Baxter the Cavoodle, and a permanent $20 Bunnings wallet. They will love the practical help, the footy, the coffee, and the fact we can fix your fence and your day. Welcome to Bellbird Park.
