# Ferny Hills (QLD)

Slug: `ferny-hills-qld`

We wake up to the same soft problem every Ferny Hills morning — too much local green, not enough reason to wear heels. By 8am we’re on the path to George Willmore Park, dodging a barbecue someone’s claimed for a “family event” and pretending we don’t know whose birthday it really is. The park has been our picnic stage for years; it’s where kids run off steam and older kids pretend they’re coaching the under-10s.

This May, though, the suburb gets its moment: Hills Festival 2026 is back (1–31 May), and for the month Arana Hills, Ferny Hills, Everton Hills, Bunya and Draper pretend to be cool and cultured all at once. We’ll be there, folding ourselves into whatever tiny tented market or community performance someone has hastily organised — because if there’s one thing we love, it’s a local festival that needs three volunteer coordinators and a good playlist.

In the quieter, museum-adjacent part of town, Urban List once name-checked the Tramway Museum Ferny Hills — yes, the one you could actually jump aboard and take a historic tram for a spin. We’ll always brag about that to visitors, even if it sounds like the sort of niche flex only we appreciate. Concrete Playground noted an Ash Barty mural at Pure Tennis Academy, 144 Samford Road; it felt very civic-pride-y at the time. There was also a kids’ play café attached to the Pure Tennis centre, if you were chasing a birthday venue that doubles as a cardio session for exhausted adults.

We used to vote the Ferny Hills Swimming Pool our municipal spa: a 50‑metre heated lap pool, a 20‑metre learn‑to‑swim pool and a toddler splash zone with free barbecues on the side — the sort of place where you can judge parenting skill by the efficiency of sunscreen application.

Who would hate living here? If you’re after non-stop nightlife, boutique shopping or neighbours who never borrow your lawn mower, move along. But who would love it? Families who like parks, people who secretly enjoy small-town festivals, and anyone who thinks riding an old tram is innately satisfying. We’re not fancy. We just have trees, a decent community festival, and a tram you can ride when you need to prove we’ve got culture.
