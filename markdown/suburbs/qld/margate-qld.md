# Margate (QLD)

Slug: `margate-qld`

Back in the day we thought Margate, QLD was a secret only the peninsula knew — the kind of place you roll up to with sandy knees and a smug grin. Walks along Margate Beach, framed by Norfolk Pines and that wide boardwalk everyone raves about, felt like our little coastal flex. The breeze off Moreton Bay does the heavy lifting: you don’t need to shout, the sea does it for you.

Our weekend ritual used to be embarrassingly simple. Paddle or kayak out for five minutes of calm, then stagger back for what the guides called “post‑beach snacks.” Sunny’s Margate Beach — the rooftop at The Sebel that Urban List leaned on — used to promise comfy seating, sea breezes and a bit of rooftop glamour for anyone pretending they’d booked a hotel. And when the hungover cravings hit, aKAH Chicken at 139 Margate Parade was that hole‑in‑the‑wall salvation: crispy chicken, loaded fries and the kind of sauces that forgive poor life choices.

We’ll admit it: Margate isn’t trying to be something it’s not. Sitchu reminds us it’s one of the quiet siblings of the Redcliffe Peninsula, sitting alongside Scarborough, Redcliffe and Woody Point, which means we get the seaside without the relentless city soundtrack. The guides described paddling, kayaking, relaxing and then grabbing something nearby — and honestly, that’s been our vibe.

Who would hate living here? People who need a nightclub on every corner, or those who measure a suburb by how many neon latté innovations it hosts. Also anyone allergic to sea air, Norfolk Pines, or the mild public embarrassment of wearing boardshorts to an actual rooftop bar.

Who would love it here? Families who like a proper beach with a boardwalk, weekend paddlers, people who think a good day is a kayak then a cheeky feed from a beloved local chicken shop, and anyone who appreciates a sunset that doesn’t charge cover. For years the listings were modest and a little smug about it — and honestly, we wouldn’t have it any other way.
