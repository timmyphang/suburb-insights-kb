# Mudgeeraba (QLD)

Slug: `mudgeeraba-qld`

Back in the day we’d stroll down Railway Street and pretend we weren’t a Gold Coast village with a secretly sophisticated appetite. As Urban List once profiled us — calling Mudgeeraba “quaint”, with leafy streets and a slow-town vibe — we took it as a compliment and a dare.

Weekends used to start with a queue (or at least a polite nod) at Mudjira Village Eatery — the long‑running café that quietly got a renovation but kept its community-first energy. If you were feeling fancy or needed to impress an out-of-towner, you’d claim you were driving out for scenery and end up at Cook Club on Boomerang Farm, an open-air, 200‑acre spot that paired hinterland views with food that thought it belonged in a postcard. Weddings? Kwila Lodge on its 5.5-acre plot — the 1923 house given an eco-luxe glow-up — would handle that bit of theatre so we could all take credit.

We even had a Japanese moment. Anekawa, opened by Koki Anekawa, was the kind of place that served signature clay pot rice and kinako ice cream and made you consider replacing your usual dinner routine with something that involved chopsticks and slightly more dignity.

And yes, we weren’t all plates and panoramas. There was a softer lane: Earth Mothers Community — the holistic sanctuary at Shop 14, 58 Railway Street — used to host circles and classes that made some of us whisper things like “grounding” while secretly enjoying being grounded together.

Who would hate living here? People who thrive on neon-lit, 24/7 chaos and think “slow-town vibe” is code for “no new bars.” If you need a city that never pauses, you’ll be stamping your foot in our roundabout. Also, anyone allergic to local history, hinterland sunsets or the idea of a café being more than a caffeine transaction.

Who would love it? Folks who like their weekends leafy, their celebrations scenic, and their community spaces earnest — whether that’s a renovated village eatery, a farm with a view, or a sanctuary on Railway Street. We’re small-town enough to know your name, big enough to feed you kinako ice cream and pretend it’s haute cuisine.
