# Sherwood (QLD)

Slug: `sherwood-qld`

Back in the day we’d tell visitors to meet us under the big fig at Sherwood Arboretum — the heritage‑listed park the guides still gush about with its thousand‑plus trees and river views. It’s the sort of place where our weekend routine was basically: picnic, nap, judge the picnic next to you. Concrete Playground and Sitchu have both called it a gem, so yes, we’re officially smug about our foliage.

Our commercial strip used to be described as “boutiques and cafés along Sherwood Road,” which sounds romantic until you realise most of the press angle here was either property listings or lifestyle copy. Remember those townhouses that came with a year of free avocado on toast? Pedestrian ran that one and yes, that stunt felt very much like Sherwood trying on poshness for size.

If you liked a low‑key, domestic kind of outing, Sherwood delivered. Urban List once pointed to Roma Roma as a playful local pasta option, and Concrete Playground catalogued a yoga studio at Shop 1/641 Sherwood Road — the sort of things we used to file under “Sunday plans.” Sitchu’s profile painted us with broad strokes: wide, tree‑lined streets, classic Queenslander homes, and a proper local community vibe. The property sites liked to show off Lilly Street and Primrose Street when they needed aspirational photos.

We’ll be honest: the media archive here reads like it was assembled by someone with a fondness for real estate photography and a yoga mat. There’s no front‑page scandal, no neon nightlife, no civic upheaval — just good old suburban pride and a stubborn refusal to be flashy. That’s our drama: not enough drama.

Who would hate it here? If you crave 2am bars, flashing signs and a parade of new bars trying to out‑Instagram each other, Sherwood will make you twitch. We’re more arboretum than after‑party.

Who would love it here? Families who value St Aidan’s Anglican Girls’ School and Sherwood Primary School proximity, people who want riverfront picnics and Queenslander charm, the yoga‑mat crowd and anyone who enjoys a quiet weekend that involves real trees, not just curated plant pots. We’re not trying to be the city — we’re the place you escape to when the city gets loud, and honestly, we’d have it no other way.
