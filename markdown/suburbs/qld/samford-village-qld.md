# Samford Village (QLD)

Slug: `samford-village-qld`

Back in the day we’d push through a wrought-iron gate, wander up a winding garden path and pretend we weren’t just here for breakfast. The Flying Nun — yes, the former moveable council funeral shelter that somehow ended up in a churchyard — used to serve brekkie beneath stained-glass windows while a blue-and-gold macaw called Jasper judged our life choices. Concrete Playground and Broadsheet both thought it was very cute; we thought it smelled faintly of hymnals and toasted sourdough.

A typical weekend meant circuiting Station Street: the Store of Requirement popped up on Harry’s birthday (opened July 31) and turned part of the village into a low-key Diagon Alley. Next door, The Owlery Cafe staged its Potter-adjacent vibe inside the old church. You could buy a wand, eat a themed cake, and then wander past Samford Valley Antique & Vintage on Main Street hunting for a misshapen teacup to match your wand collection. Baffies Oan Books on Mount Glorious Road was our pre-loved book siren — for people who like their paperbacks with a side of country air.

This isn’t a secret anymore: travel lists called it a super-cute day-trip — 30 to 45 minutes from Brisbane depending on whether you take the scenic route via Mt Nebo — and Time Out sends you through here on your way to Cedar Creek Falls like we’re some sort of enchanted service station.

If you can’t handle border collie puppies being raised on-site and daily puppy socialisation sessions at Collies And Co, or you get uncomfortable with genteel country charm, you’ll hate it. If the idea of a century-old pub, a train-themed playground, pony trails and people selling painted furniture makes you want to scream into your kombucha, maybe move back to the city.

Who’ll love it? Folks who want country-with-coffee: antique hunters, book browsers, families who still think a fairy-tale bridge is peak destination, and anyone who enjoys small-town eccentricity — a macaw, a wizard shop, a church-turned-cafe and all. We know every cracked verandah and market stall because we live here. Back in the day the guides loved to boast about our quaintness; we’ll keep doing the same, only less politely.
