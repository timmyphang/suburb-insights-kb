# Lota (QLD)

Slug: `lota-qld`

For years we’ve treated weekends in Lota like a low-key seaside ritual: sunhat, suspiciously competitive esky, and a deliberate detour to the playground before we’ll admit we’re at the water. Back in the day the kids ran riot through the beach‑inspired forts and sand play at Lota Park (Fig Tree Point Park), which brisbanekids.com.au helpfully noted is fully fenced and even has accessible toilets, barbecues and undercover seating for when the in-laws pop in unexpectedly.

Our morning walk is embarrassingly wholesome: we’ll amble the Wynnum to Manly Esplanade from Elanora Park all the way to the Lota Creek Boardwalk because Concrete Playground once listed that exact stretch among the ten best walks around Brisbane. It’s the sort of view that makes you feel virtuous about not having to cross the bridge to the city — plus the sea breeze does a lot of our laundry for us.

Time Out, in its 2023 review, even slotted the Manly Boathouse into the “Manly - Lota” breadcrumb trail — a reminder that if we feel like seafood and a busy outdoor fish‑and‑chips counter, there’s an old favourite nearby that critics once praised for waterfront views. We mention it like a family anecdote, because our sources here are telling us this is the Lota narrative… but also: note to followers, our article list is all a little historic, so treat the recs like fond memories rather than live listings.

The council once earmarked our foreshore for a 20‑year masterplan as part of lead‑up works for the 2032 Olympics — Concrete Playground wrote about Wynnum, Lota and Manly being on the list — which means planners whisper “revitalisation” and we whisper “please don’t ruin our fishing spots.”

Who would hate living here? Nightclub hunters and anyone who needs a skyline of neon. If you measure your life in late‑night reservation confirmations and firework displays, you’ll be bored by our gentle bayside tempo.

Who would love it? Families who actually read playground reviews, dog walkers who know the Lota Creek Boardwalk by heart, and people who enjoy a long walk that ends at water and a remembered fish‑and‑chip counter. We’re not flashy. We’re just Lota: quietly seaworthy, slightly sandy, and enduringly proud.
