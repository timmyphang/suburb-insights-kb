# Banyo (QLD)

Slug: `banyo-qld`

We know Banyo like the back of our hand — the kind of place where a Saturday arvo could mean choosing between a brewery birthday and a bowls club barbecue, and both feel like family reunions you didn’t know you’d RSVP’d to.

Back in the day we’d drift down to 189 Elliott Road where All Inn Brewing Co threw parties that smelled faintly of craft and community. Concrete Playground called it “environmentally focused” — solar panels, repurposed tanks, spent grains handed to local farmers — and yes, Harley Goodacre’s brewery (started in 2012) used to pack the place with food trucks, live music and dogs on the deck. Nearby the Banyo Bowls Club was the reliable weekend plot twist: one green for actual bowls, another for food trucks and craft beer line-ups. Urban List had a soft spot for that.

If you wanted culture with your caffeine, Site Cafe used to pitch a tent for monthly art exhibitions, book swaps and a kids’ corner — Urban List loved that community vibe. For those of us who like our cheap eats without pretense, Sitchu flagged Vietnamese Pho Ever at 66 Blinzinger Road as a bowl that did the job without breaking the bank. And when people asked what to do with a stubborn Sunday, Brisbane Kids reminded us the Brisbane Motor Museum — which opened in 2023 — quietly became a “hidden gem” for anyone who likes a bit of chrome nostalgia.

We’re also the suburb that’s been described as handily close to the Banyo train station, the Gateway Motorway and Brisbane Airport — handy, if your commute is a story rather than a plot.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe low-key pleasures — sustainable breweries, communal bowls clubs, libraries actually serving the neighbourhood (Banyo Library was listed at 284 St Vincents Road) — then yes, you’d find Banyo boringly wholesome. Also, if you demand rooftop bars with fog machines, look elsewhere.

Who’d love it? Families (Earnshaw State College is our local prep–Year 12), dog owners who once dragged a pup to a brewery birthday, bargain pho seekers, and anyone who prefers their weekend plans to involve a skatepark, a museum of old motors and a slow, dependable sense of place. We’re modest, practical and quietly proud — and that, frankly, suits us down to the last grain of spent malt.
