# Wellington Point (QLD)

Slug: `wellington-point-qld`

Back in the day we’d swear Wellington Point was Brisbane’s best-kept weekend alibi — and the old reviews agree. We’re the bunch who time the tide, park near the jetty and pretend we sleep in, then watch the sunrise over Moreton Bay like it’s our personal screensaver. Time Out used to recommend the jetty and the sand causeway to King Island (Erobin) for family walks; we’ll take the scenic bragging rights, thanks.

Our morning ritual? A slow stroll from the Wellington Point Recreation Reserve Playground under those enormous figs, past Plantation Place Park, and if the kids are cooperating we might try the sand bridge at low tide — the very walk Time Out and Urban List swore was worth the calendar reminder. The playgrounds are the sort that make you feel noble for bringing snacks.

For years the food guides did the heavy lifting. Broadsheet fawned over Citron — the light-filled, Mediterranean-leaning cafe on the corner of Main Road and Christina Street serving Darvella pastries and Lost Race Coffee — and Urban List listed Wellington Point Farmhouse at 623 Main Rd as the farm-to-table spot where you could picnic on the lawn. Concrete Playground reviewed Manny’s Italian at 3/391 Main Road and even pointed out the pizza and the waterfront views. Jaipur Palace gets a respectful nod from Time Out as a local curry institution (Dharminder and Sheena’s, since 1998). And yes, Concrete Playground also immortalised the Off The Wall Diner’s “Hottest Burger in the World” as local legend — for anyone into ego-sized challenges.

We don’t have much petty council soap opera in the clippings — the real bustle here is community-run: Social Mums Club and local noticeboards keep the baby-grow economy humming, and finditlocally pages make sure everyone knows where the sausage sizzle is.

Who would hate us? People who wanted a big surf break and a club till dawn; Wellington Point is a reserve-with-water-on-both-sides kind of romance, not a neon nightlife frenzy. Who would love it? Families, sunrise stalkers, amateur sandbar explorers, and anyone who appreciates a cafe review you can actually walk to after the paper.

Back in the day the reviews did the name-dropping — we’re still doing the same slow, salty routine.
