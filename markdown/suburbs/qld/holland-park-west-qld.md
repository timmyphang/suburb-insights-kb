# Holland Park West (QLD)

Slug: `holland-park-west-qld`

We know Holland Park West by the rhythm of Logan Road: the slow shuffle to Deedot Coffee House at 973 Logan Road (we used to think their breakfasts were the reason to set an alarm), then the polite detour to Child’s Play QLD at 1034 Logan Road for whatever makes the small humans and their parents slightly less chaotic. This is the suburb where errands line up like a good weekend parade.

On a Sunday we’d pretend we were being active — a quick game of pickleball at Joachim Street Park (yes, that Joachim Street Park the lists keep recommending) — then try to lose ourselves at Puzzled Room Escape (5/1177 Logan Road) when reality got too predictable. For a night out there was talk of Clover, the neighbourhood bar Broadsheet wrote up, with its six taps and pub classics; and The Ukrainian Club Brisbane, which Urban List said finally arrived and officially opened on 12 May. Small plates, warm fit-outs, a dash of ambition: very Holland Park West.

There isn’t much full-throttle suburb drama in the clippings — no council coups, no water cooler scandals. Mostly we have property listings gawking at our leafy ridgelines (Dagmar Street, Albert Street, Sterculia Avenue make regular cameos), and Sitchu reminding buyers they can glimpse the city and Mt Coot‑Tha from elevated living. The loudest civic noise here is the community notice board — finditlocally counts us among 1,500 community groups and frankly we wear it like a badge.

Who would hate it here? People who crave absolute anonymity, the kind who need a nightlife that never stops and zero local noticeboards. Also anyone allergic to trees, school catchment competition (hello Cavendish Rd SHS mentions) or the idea of a neighbourhood bar that remembers your face.

Who would love it? Young families who like nearby child psychology and therapy services, pickleball converts, escape-room enthusiasts, anyone seduced by city-view hills and local spots with a proper backstory. We’ll mock the postcode’s modesty, but we’ll defend its green gutters and Logan Road corner cafés to the last parking meter.
