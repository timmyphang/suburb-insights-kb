# Wooloowin (QLD)

Slug: `wooloowin-qld`

We know the walk: Eagle Junction train, a hop past Lodge Rd at Clark Street (stop 34), and suddenly we’re squinting at bright yellow chairs on Dickson Street like it’s a tiny sun colony. Back in the day the Little Corner Cafe at 86 Dickson Street was the spot we bragged about — Concrete Playground liked its sunny outdoor setup and that espresso smoothie with honey and yoghurt. We miss the cafe chitchat that felt like a neighbourhood meeting, minus the agenda.

For years Wooloowin’s rhythm has been quietly civic: The Community Place used to run programs and even hired out space for birthday parties from its Kalinga/Wooloowin base at 20 Clark Street (they still give train-and-bus directions on their site, which tells you everything about the suburb’s emphasis on being useful, not flashy). If you wanted to pretend to be sporty before committing, Shaw Park Tennis Centre got a nod in Urban List for hosting pickleball ‘Come and Try’ sessions — friendly crew, zero ego, maximum accidental backhanding of your neighbour.

We’ll also own our taste for low-key luxe: Kent Road Bistro on 85 Kent Road made it into BYO round-ups with French-Italian-inspired dishes — yes, someone in Wooloowin once served fillet mignon with hash brown and onion jam and we all pretended not to be impressed while secretly wanting two helpings. And if you like books without the social pressure, Shelf Lovers Adult Book Circle at the Shelf Lovers bookstore was the sort of thing Urban List praised — read whatever you like, no shame, no assigned titles. Pure Wooloowin.

Petty drama? The archives don’t give us juicy council spats or scandalous rooftop parties, so we’ll admit it: our drama is community-centre-level — practical, kind, and slightly beige. That’s OK. Who would hate living here? If you want loud nightlife, pop-up notoriety or weekly celebrity sightings, you’ll be bored senseless. Who would love it? Families, people who like organised hobbies, anyone who appreciates a BYO bistro memory, a sunny corner cafe table, and a book club that lets you actually read.

Back in the day we were small, neighbourly and oddly proud of it — and that’s still our vibe, even if the recent headlines mostly skip over us.
