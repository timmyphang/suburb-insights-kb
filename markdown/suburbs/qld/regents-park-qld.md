# Regents Park (QLD)

Slug: `regents-park-qld`

We like to start the week by checking our own press—because who doesn't?—and then remember: we barely have one. Our scrapbook of stories for Regents Park, QLD is basically a postcard rack of other people's Regent(s): Time Out’s love letter to Regent’s Park in London, Concrete Playground’s nostalgia for Sydney’s neon-bright Regent Place, and a stray Urban List entry pointing to Gipsy Boy at 68 Regents Park Rd in Joondalup. All the clippings are older (pre‑2024 or undated), so yes, "Back in the day..." is the vibe here.

Morning routine: we brew whatever we have in the cupboard, pull up Concrete Playground’s piece about Regent Place and imagine a laneway behind George Street full of tiny Japanese spots. Concrete Playground once wrote about LeTAO’s Sydney store in that precinct, and Broadsheet ran a feature on Yappari Steak arriving at Regent Place — that sizzling lava‑stone theatre sounds very cinematic, and we envy the theatrics while our own gossip column is a blank page.

Petty local drama? Our press file is mercifully drama‑free. The only controversies we can point to are about other cities: Time Out planning permissions and a Queen Elizabeth II memorial garden in London; Broadsheet’s previews of ramen houses and new openings. So if you wanted neighbourhood feuds and council melodrama to roast, you're asking the wrong postcode.

Who would hate living here: the kind of person who needs an editorial pipeline of new bars, curated superfood cafés and endless Instagrammable openings. They’ll bail after one day of scrolling city guides that talk about Primrose Hill views, the Regent’s Park Open Air Theatre or Frieze in the park instead of our dog park and local footpath.

Who would love it here: people who enjoy being under the radar. We’re the quiet cousin in a family of very loud parks and precincts. If you like not being constantly discovered, if you prefer living in the kind of place that gets mistaken for someone else’s headlines, welcome. We’re perfectly content borrowing other cities’ glamour while keeping our own, very modest routines.
