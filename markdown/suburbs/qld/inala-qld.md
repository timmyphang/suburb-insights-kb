# Inala (QLD)

Slug: `inala-qld`

We know the sound of a Sunday here: soup steam, clinking spoons and someone in the queue explaining why their pho is the one to beat. We’ve long been the sort of place food writers can’t resist — beef brisket pho at Pho An made the list, Tan Thanh used to have lines from 8am seven days for takeaways, and our local markets (shout-out to Thai Hoa Grocer) were the sort of places chefs rang when Brisbane needed top produce. Yes, we buy rambutans like they’re going out of fashion.

Our weekend routine used to look like this: someone grabs a bowl from Tan Thanh, somebody else returns with banh mi made from Biota Hot Bread (Banh Mi Now’s owner once settled on Biota in Inala for her loaves), and the rest of us shop the stalls at Thai Hoa or argue over who supplied what to the flood‑relief dinners that once named Thai Hoa Grocer and Inala Seafood Market on the credits.

There was a bit of local drama — you might remember the fuss when an SBS crew wanted to film a Struggle Street episode here and the application got rejected. We won’t re-litigate it, just say: we’re not in a box, we’re not a TV idea.

Who would hate living here? Anyone who needs a rooftop cocktail bar, a curated laneway mural for their selfie, or a precinct where the main cultural activity is choosing which pho is the answer to life. People who can’t stand community noticeboards, skateboards and festivals will be perplexed — our skate park at 10 Inala Avenue (refurbished and open since late 2015) is unapologetically for actual use, not for aesthetics.

Who’d love it here? Families who turn up to the Inala Multicultural Festival at Dogs Queensland in Durack, those who rely on the HUB Neighbourhood Centre (79 Poinsettia Street) for everything from legal help to playgroups, and anyone who believes a neighbourhood is measured by its bowls, markets and the way people show up for each other. Also: if you Google “Inala” and expect dinosaurs, note the Inala Jurassic Garden is actually over on Cloudy Bay Road, South Bruny — only proves this place punches above its postcode on the map.

We’re messy, tasty and stubbornly community-minded. Come for the pho, stay because we’re already halfway through your story.
