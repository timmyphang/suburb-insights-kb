# Kippa-Ring (QLD)

Slug: `kippa-ring-qld`

For years we’ve told people Kippa‑Ring is where the peninsula keeps its feet up — not flashy, just comfortable. Walk Anzac Ave and you’ll see what we mean: the Kippa‑Ring Shopping Centre at 284 Anzac Ave doing the reliable suburban thing, Repco at 302 Anzac Ave fixing whatever you swore was fine, and an ANZ Kippa Ring Specialist Hub quietly sorting out people’s money problems. Nothing screams glamour, and we like it that way.

Back in the day Concrete Playground ran a piece when Slim’s Quality Burger rolled into town — mid‑September 2023, they said — at 403 Elizabeth Avenue. Retro drive‑thru, jukebox vibes, breakfast menu debuting for the brand. It felt like someone shipped in a 50s movie set and parked it next to the servo — or the place where the servo would be if we bothered with that level of urban drama. Property listings from Sitchu reminded us we’re also a proper family suburb: high‑set houses on Wanganui Street, that pool‑ready Claret Court place with side access for boats and caravans, the sort of listings that make planners sigh and parents nod.

We don’t have endless thinkpieces about cultural reinvention here. Our lifestyle press lanes are mostly childcare writeups and directories — SunRise Kids runs a centre in Kippa‑Ring and the mums’ sites point to acrobatics classes and Matt Fiddes Martial Arts nearby. For summers, Redcliffe War Memorial Pool and the Redcliffe Jetty Markets (and the annual Redcliffe Festival) are the sort of community events that prove we do weekends properly.

Who wouldn’t love it here? If you’re after backyard barbecues, room for a boat, and childcare that knows the kid’s name by Thursday, you’ll fit right in. Who would hate it? If you demand late‑night glamour, boutique everything, and a constant stream of trend pieces declaring your suburb “the new [insert suburb here],” this won’t scratch that itch.

We’re Kippa‑Ring: not trying to be anything but a proper slice of the Redcliffe Peninsula. Modest, useful, and quietly stubborn — and yes, we remember when a drive‑thru burger counted as a cultural event.
