# Redland Bay (QLD)

Slug: `redland-bay-qld`

Back in the day we’d call Broadwater Terrace our promenade and pretend we were sophisticated — there was a cafe called Merak at 55–56 Broadwater Terrace that made gluten-free rosti eggs benedict the kind of thing you’d brag about on a Monday. Kids ran wild at the playground at the Redland Bay Hotel (160 Broadwater Terrace); Urban List used to point out the kids’ menu like it was a selling point, which of course it was.

Saturdays looked the same: someone packed a platter for a luxury catamaran cruise (Aria Cruises and Brisbane Yacht Charters rated highly in the guides), others booked whale-watching with Yura Tours or drove out to Straddie Point Lookout for the horizon. If you were after calm water and a place for the dog to paddle, Sitchu kept telling us Ruster’s Reserve was the go-to — enclosed swimming, gentle wind, zero pretence.

We like to remind visitors that famous things started small here — as Broadsheet noted, Jack “Jacko” Van Issum from Beddy Rays met his bandmates on a primary school oval in Redland Bay. So when people ask if we’re a sleepy coastal town, we shrug and say, “sleepy, theatrical, and sometimes musically dangerous.”

There isn’t much local scandal in these pages — more baby massage classes at Redland Bay Golf Club, Keeping Mum and Zest Mums & Bubs running programs, and a friendly glut of community mums’ groups — which suits us. Petty drama would be someone complaining about too many Christmas lights: Hunterly Place, Dory Close and Ebony Crescent used to be the addresses Time Out and Urban List loved to point tourists toward when December rolled around. RedFest at the Redlands Showgrounds on Long Street, Cleveland, provided our annual excuse for strawberry food and carnival rides; we’d be there with show bags in hand.

Who would hate it here? If you draw energy from permanent city buzz, midnight bars and a refusal to ever look at a bay, this place will make you restless. Who would love it? Families, boat people, mums who want a village of classes and coffee-adjacent meetups, and anyone who believes that a good Christmas street display is public art.

We’re proud, understated and slightly glittery at Christmas. Welcome to our tide-marked slice of the Redlands.
