# Goodna (QLD)

Slug: `goodna-qld`

Back in the day we’d set the alarm for the slow glow of jacaranda purple and pretend we were in a music video. As Urban List once pointed out, Evan Margison Park and even George Street were the kind of places where the petals do the heavy lifting for your weekend photos — very pretty, very public.

Our mornings used to be a polite traipse past those trees, a quick look at the noticeboard on Find It Locally to see who’s selling lawn mowers and who needs a babysitter, and then the local gossip: did you hear Lisi — our own Goodna rapper — get a run with Red Bull Symphonic back when that was happening? Concrete Playground reminded us we’ve punched above our postcode in culture before anyone could spell “main stage.”

We’re not all jacarandas and community notices though. Goodna Cemetery has a reputation — Urban List’s haunted spots piece gave it top-tier chills after a visitor claimed their car wondered off with new scratches. If you like your local lore with a side of forensic scratches, we’re your spooky suburb.

We’ve also been useful in crises. During floods the Ipswich Showgrounds, Shiloh Church in Goodna and nearby Rosewood State High School were opened as refuges, as Urban List noted — we pull together when the river remembers us. And, because life is never boring, Concrete Playground’s round-ups once put us on a public-health map with a Caltex in Goodna on a list to watch. Glamorous.

Who would hate living here? People who panic at being named in alerts, who can’t handle cemeteries with personality, or those who need every café recommended as “open now” in a weekend guide. Also, if you loathe popularity: Time Out once reported Goodna’s postcode as top for apartment enquiries in 2025 — we’re apparently desirable now, which ruins everything.

Who would love it? Anyone who enjoys jacaranda afternoons, a tight-knit noticeboard culture, and the quiet flex of a local artist stepping onto bigger things. For a touch of retro shopping theatre, Sitchu’s old round-up even listed When Freddie Met Lilly at 37 Smiths Road. We’re a bit rough, a bit purple, and oddly proud of it.
