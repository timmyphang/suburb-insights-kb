# Noosaville (QLD)

Slug: `noosaville-qld`

We wake up to the river like it's on display for our scrutiny — the Noosa River doing its gentle thing while someone practises paddleboarding somewhere between the foreshore parks and Gympie Terrace. Back in the day this was our quiet flex: riverside picnics at Pirate Park, a quick walk past Lot 4,5 & 6 on Gympie Terrace (as Sitchu helpfully mapped out) and a soft, smug nod to anyone still trying to find a view.

If you read Broadsheet you’d know Noosaville used to be the local hub Broadsheet filed under Noosa — the kind of place that collected breweries, distilleries and riverside restaurants like curious shells. Land & Sea Brewery at 19 Venture Drive used to be the relaxed answer to any complicated week, sharing a taproom with Noosa Heads Distillery (as the listings put it). Heads of Noosa Brewing Co. used to pour lagers down on 85 Rene Street, and Urban List once flagged Boiling Pot Brewing Co. as a refurbished warehouse doing local beers — yes, Noosaville has carried its own modest beer pride parade.

We used to queue for Gusto’s terrace views and Euro-style prawn ravioli, and even Sum Yung Guys — once linked to MasterChef runner-up Matt Sinclair — turned up in Broadsheet’s roundups as the high-energy, pan-Asian option that people liked to mention over dinner. Concrete Playground also reminded us that the Noosa Chocolate Factory’s operations are based in Noosaville, even if the factory itself wasn’t open for nose-led tourism.

There’s not a scandal in sight in the files we dug through — no council coups, no market theatre — which is probably why the mums and kids guide pages are full of practical joy: Pirate Park, Noosa Civic’s Tuesday Playday, Mostly Music sessions. We’re recreational, not revolutionary.

Who would hate it here? People who think serenity is boring, who despise weekends that begin with river walks and end with riverside meals. Who would love it? Anyone who understands that being a Noosaville local means choosing calm over chaos, breweries over brashness, and a picnic blanket over a podium.

Back in the day, that was enough for us.
