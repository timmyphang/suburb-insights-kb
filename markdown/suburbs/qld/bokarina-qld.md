# Bokarina (QLD)

Slug: `bokarina-qld`

Back in the day we’d stroll the strip, sandy feet and all, because Bokarina used to feel like the Sunshine Coast’s low-effort chic: a playground a blink from the sea, a decent gelato joint and a bloke called Tony doing very serious pizzas by the beachfront.

Morning routine (in the good old days): we’d walk the roughly 50 metres from Bokarina Beach to the supersized playground at Bokarina Park, watch the kids launch themselves down the wide slide and across the pump track, then realise there still appeared to be no toilet facilities on site and question every life choice. After that, a pit stop at Bok Beach Cafe—right on the corner of Longboard Parade and Bokarina Boulevard—for Merlo coffee used to be the civilised thing to do. As The Urban List noted, Wallace & Wood’s “little kitchen that could” churned out ice cream that justified skipping breakfast. And if you were feeling fancy, Bocca Italian Bokarina used to be the place for beachfront pasta and a stone-base Moretti oven pizza, all with a view.

Local amenities we bragged about: the Kawana Aquatic Centre on 45 Sportsmans Parade, where Rackley Swimming ran lessons and kids learned how to kick without panicking; the skatepark down Sportsmans Parade; and the Lake Kawana Community Centre hosting Kaleidoscope Events that made markets feel like proper things to do with human children and not just scrolling for bargains.

Petty local drama (past edition): there was a serious road incident at the corner of Nicklin Way and Main Drive in 2025 — a grim reminder that our little pocket of paradise sits beside a proper thoroughfare and that traffic will always vouch for drama even when the surf is calm.

Who would hate living here: people who detest sand in shoes, playgrounds with inconvenient plumbing and the faint whiff of small-town scheduling. Also anyone who thinks “beachside” means non-stop nightlife.

Who would have loved it: families who wanted swimming lessons, skate sessions and a beach an arm’s reach away; ice-cream devotees who believed Wallace & Wood lived up to the hype; and anyone who thought a beachfront Italian from Tony Kelly sounded exactly like a very good idea. We remember it fondly — salt in the hair, crumbs on the floor and all.
