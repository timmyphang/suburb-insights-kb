# Surfers Paradise (QLD)

Slug: `surfers-paradise-qld`

Back in the day we’d stagger onto Cavill Avenue with sand between our toes and a skyline of crystalline towers staring down like disapproving parents. Surfers Paradise beach — that 2km stretch everyone pictures when they think of the Gold Coast — still does the turquoise-waves-and-high-rises thing better than most postcards, and if you squint you can spot the Q1 and remember the SkyPoint Climb that sends people 270 metres up to admire the whole glitter strip.

Our weekend ritual used to be predictably tourist-friendly: a walk along the wide sandy beach, a gawp at the high-rises, then a pilgrimage past Paradise Centre — which, as Concrete Playground reported, even hosted Wendy’s when the chain opened its first Australian store there in January 2025. The old Hard Rock Cafe got a glow-up too, morphing into The Sporting Globe x 4 Pines after a mid-2024 makeover, because nothing says seaside glamour like 80 taps and an island bar the size of a small continent.

Petty local drama? Oh yes. For a while our local Anglican church made national ink with a sign advising that “forgiveness is swallowing when you’d rather spit,” which prompted commentary from our member John‑Paul Langbroek and gave us exactly the wholesome confusion we expect from our strip. And if you needed more spectacle, remember the People’s Esplanade pop-up and whispers of Miami‑inspired projects like South Beach — Surfers Paradise has always flirted with reinvention.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to queues, neon, or a cavalcade of visitors during peak times like Schoolies. If you crave hushed suburban streets and zero people-watching, you’ll want somewhere with fewer towers and more lawn.

Who’d love it: families who know the Gold Coast can be brilliant on a budget — Beanstalk Mums still swears by the free-play aspects of the beach and indoor rescues like Timezone, Level Up Arcade and Strike Bowling for when the weather turns. Community groups such as Surfers Paradise Rotary and charities like Motherhood Village keep a softer, steady pulse beneath the glitter.

We’re not shy about our sparkle. Sure, it’s loud and occasionally absurd — but where else do skyline selfies, surf lessons and strange church signs all fit so comfortably on one postcard?
