# Capalaba (QLD)

Slug: `capalaba-qld`

Back in the day we used to think Capalaba was the quiet cousin of Brisbane who turned up to the family barbecue with a new haircut and a $3-million pub revamp. These days, when we stroll the old comforts of town in our heads, it’s a very specific kind of weekend: grab something from the big grocer, breathe in some native bush, then punt the kids towards the playground and the adults towards a beer that tastes suspiciously like dessert.

Our Saturday ritual used to start at Fresh Sensations in Capalaba Park Shopping Centre (Shop 200, Redland Bay Road) where the fruit, veg and deli made us feel slightly smug about our food choices. Then we’d head to the Redlands IndigiScapes Centre for a gentle walk through the native botanical gardens and the Discovery Centre — perfect for pretending we were outdoorsy. For real kid wrangling, Capalaba Regional Park and Brosnan Drive Park gave the ankle-biters enough swings, climbing gear and a sensory play area to exhaust them for a full week.

When the arvo hit, the Koala Tavern was the place to watch the footy — the place got a fairly lightning makeover: fairy lights over a huge beer garden, a new bistro and a kids’ play area themed after Aussie animals. For those of us with a talent for turning any outing into a pub crawl, Capalaba even had its own bespoke microbrewery, Bacchus Brewing at 1/2 Christine Place, flogging dessert-inspired brews like Java the Nut and Ferrerro Superbeast. And if someone insists on rewarding the fam with fish and chips, Costa’s Seafood at 1B/108 Old Cleveland Road was even named Queensland’s best in 2019 — not a bad flex.

We’ll also admit to enjoying Capalaba’s quieter charms: Capalaba Produce for the farm-shop vibes and ice-cream, and the festive chaos of Christmas-light devotees crowding places like 6 Tipperary Court back when the suburb fancied itself seasonal.

Who would hate it here? People who need late-night cocktail bars, permanent CBD energy or silence that isn’t occasionally broken by someone cheering at the Koala Tavern. Who’d love it? Families, beer nerds who like their stouts with a side of nostalgia, and anyone who appreciates parks, produce and a town that knows how to dress a backyard in fairy lights.
