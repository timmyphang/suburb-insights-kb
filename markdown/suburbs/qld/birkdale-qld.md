# Birkdale (QLD)

Slug: `birkdale-qld`

Saturday, 8:07am: a Toyota Kluger idles outside Birkdale Railway Station, the PTA president in Lululemon and a designer puffer, Cavoodle perched on her knee, oat flat white steaming in a reusable cup. We’d call it the town’s signature still life if it weren’t part of our morning routine. We live in a place where activewear is formalwear, mid-sized SUVs are status symbols and every third dog is a perfectly coiffed Cavoodle called something like Rupert or Mr Fluffles. 

Our Saturday looks the same every week. Drop the kids at Birkdale State School to a soundtrack of 2000s pop. Swing by the coffee cart outside the station for a single-origin oat latte — yes, the same barista, yes, the same order. Pilates at Studio Birkdale, a Birkdale Bay walk with Rupert, lunch at the café by the station (we like Station Espresso for its smashed avo and managerial eye-roll). Sunday is Bunnings for a new native and a communal barbecue in the park. Mazda CX-5s and Klugers hum like suburban bees. 

We take our small-town civic duties seriously. There’s a multi-day WhatsApp stoush about the coveted Saturday spot outside Birkdale Shopping Village where the instigator claims sovereignty at the “espresso window.” We have kerbside bin placement rules that would make a navy officer proud. The Great BBQ Sauce Incident at the community hall — someone brought homemade chipotle and the committee called an emergency meeting — is still a cautionary tale. Someone painted “community art” on the footpath and the school-drop-off became a debating society: visionary or vandal? We voted with our eyebrows. 

Retired tradies now in Lululemon can be found nursing oat flat whites at the station cart while scrolling the local Facebook group for “parking issues.” The local strata meeting doubles as a soap opera and a garden club. Small dramas, large opinions. 

Who would hate living here? The nocturnal EDM DJ on a scooter, tattoo sleeves flashing, who thinks “local scene” means club crawl not community sausage sizzle. They’d be baffled by our reverence for tidy hedges, bin-rotation schedules and the mythic “good park bench.” They’d hate that our nightlife peaks at the school fete.

Who would love it? Jess, 38, weekend warrior, Mazda CX-5 owner, Cavoodle named Rupert, subscribes to the mum-run noticeboard, shops at the little strip and treats every Saturday like a ritual. We love that Jess can borrow sugar at 9am and a ladder by 3pm and that someone will always save her a park — as long as she respects the espresso window sovereignty. Welcome to Birkdale.
