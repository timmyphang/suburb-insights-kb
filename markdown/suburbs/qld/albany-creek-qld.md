# Albany Creek (QLD)

Slug: `albany-creek-qld`

We know it’s Saturday in Albany Creek when three Prados line the kerb outside Albany Creek Village, their owners clutching piccolo (oat) cups and whispering like this is real bush survival training. We wear Lululemon to walk the South Pine River and call it ‘adventure training’; we drive a 2016 Toyota Prado and call it ‘practical’ while secretly pretending the rear-view mirror reads “bush cred.” We queue at Albany Creek Village Cafe for avo on sourdough and stage a small, polite war over who does it best — the bakery on the corner or that new spot that insists their eggs are ‘heritage’ — and we will defend our choice in the comments until bedtime.

Our weekend script is precise: 6am hot lap by the South Pine River, 8:30am piccolo (oat) and avo at the village cafe, 11am sports drop-off with two car seats still warm, and the boot full of muffin trays headed for Bunnings by 2pm. Sunday is soccer, the farmers market, and an hour of moralising about screen time while scrolling on an iPhone. We hit 10,000 steps; our Apple Watch cheers us on. We patrol the Facebook group like it’s a neighbourhood watch, ensuring the footpath is clean, the dog park rules are enforced, and someone compliments the P&C for their latest bake sale.

Drama is our cardio. The big fig outside the shops is a local celebrity: one camp calls it character, the other calls it ‘mess’ — and our Facebook thread about pruning reached 700 comments last month, plus six passive-aggressive letterbox flyers. Saturday mornings bring a guerrilla parking war outside the bakery: tactical three-point turns, a politely worded note on your windscreen, and later a candle-lit PTA meeting where we discuss decibels at sporting events as if it’s national policy.

Dogs are a vibe here: Cavoodles and Labradors outnumber other breeds and will judge you if your pooch is anything less photogenic. We shop ‘local’ between servo runs on Albany Creek Road, and yes, we stock up at the big servo too — because convenience is also part of our aesthetic.

Who would hate living here? A late-20s inner-city single who loves clubs, takes public transport, wears thrift-store goth and thinks “weekend plans” mean last-minute flights. They’ll despise three-car driveways, the sacred sports calendar, and the unspoken rule that you RSVP to garage sales. They’ll never understand why we queue for avo like it’s a civic duty.

Who would love it? Mid-30s parents who wear Lululemon to everything, drive a Prado, keep a Cavoodle Instagram, and believe brunch is a competitive sport — welcome home.
