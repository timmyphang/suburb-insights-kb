# Highland Park (QLD)

Slug: `highland-park-qld`

We know the walk from the carpark on Alexander Dr like it's our Saturday ritual — the short shuffle, the sunscreen top-up, the muttered debate over whether the dog gets the slider. Urban List used to gush about Hudson & Rosie’s Table (Shop 16/106 Alexander Dr, Highland Park QLD 4211), and honestly, we leaned into it. They wrote about salted caramel popcorn pancakes and those gloriously ridiculous smashed cheeseburger toasties. They even put Hudson & Rosie’s on lists for dog-friendly cafes and high teas — yes, high tea, on two tiers, but with hash bites and sliders, because this is Highland Park, not Buckingham Palace.

We’ll cop it: our media footprint is politely uneven. While we were getting picked for “best breakfast” roundups and an Easter hours mention, other Highland Parks were busy being Hollywood and Auckland attractions in Time Out and Concrete Playground. It’s flattering, in a “someone else’s fame reflects on us slightly” kind of way. As Urban List noted, our place on the map is the kind of suburban claim to fame that involves good coffee and very committed dog owners — or at least dogs that have opinions about pancakes.

There’s not a lot of local scandal in the clippings — no council melodramas, no heated market fights — which is probably why we’ve become the sort of suburb that gets curated lists rather than headlines. That’s fine. It means our biggest civic dispute is whether to book a weekend table at the cafe that used to serve that decadent high tea.

Who would hate living here? People who crave constant metropolitan frenzy or who think a “high tea” without tiny cucumber sandwiches is a cultural crime. Also, if you can’t stand being judged by your dog’s manners at a café, this is not your scene.

Who would love it? Dog owners who appreciate tiers of comfort food masquerading as refinement, morning people who treat Alexander Dr like a local runway, and anyone who wants a low-drama suburb that still sneaks onto national lists. We’re Highland Park, QLD — modest, slightly smug about our brunch, and quietly pleased whenever anyone outside our postcode remembers our name.
