# Wynnum West (QLD)

Slug: `wynnum-west-qld`

We know the bit — the corner of Wynnum Road where you could once pick up a paper and a bag of chips without questioning your life choices. We used to head to Triton Seafood at 2/2021 Wynnum Road for classic fish-and-chips that Urban List tipped as a local favourite, then wander off toward the Wynnum‑Manly foreshore that Concrete Playground praised for its kilometres of coastline, long jetty and that ocean pool.

A typical weekend (back when we bothered): someone would mention Munja Pizza or Adelita Wine Bar in passing — both name‑checked in those Wynnum/Manly roundups — and before you knew it we were debating whether woodfired pizza trumped seasonal wine lists while ignoring the council’s plans to gussy up the foreshore ahead of the 2032 Olympics. Yes, Brisbane City Council once earmarked the Wynnum, Manly and Lota foreshores for park precinct revamps; whether that means more benches or a boutique olive oil tasting kiosk is still up for local conjecture.

Real estate tells the other story: we’re part post‑war charm, part generous block. Sitchu once listed a 3 Ulagree Street post‑war gem on a 607m² block and had a few other Wynnum West placemarks like 10 Plaza Street doing the rounds — so if you like space and heritage bones, this is the kind of humble flexibility our streets were built for. Family logistics? Sitchu even referenced indoor play options around here, like Inflatable World on Preston Road, so the small people are catered for (or exhausted into silence).

Who would hate it here? If you loathe shoreline walks, long jetties, or being within shouting distance of pizza vs wine bar arguments, Wynnum West won’t make your shortlist. If you’re allergic to the idea that suburbs sometimes wear their past proudly, you’ll feel like you’ve wandered into a history class with a playground.

Who’ll love it? Lovers of easy waterside picnics, folks who remember a good chippie when they see one, people after roomy blocks and sunset strolls along the Wynnum‑Manly foreshore — the stuff Concrete Playground and Urban List used to rave about. We’ll keep the esky chilled and the sarcasm ready.
