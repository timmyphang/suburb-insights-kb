# Marsden (QLD)

Slug: `marsden-qld`

We wake up to the same small-town rhythms: coffee can in hand, sunscreen on the back seat, and the same argument about whether the neighbours’ dog actually plays golf better than we do. Back in the day Urban List wrote about Marsden Golf Driving Range as a shiny revamp — “recently renovated,” every bay with its own net, and staff who were “almost all PGA professional golfers.” That’s our Saturday: nets, numbered bays, and the satisfying clack of a driver that, for five minutes, makes us feel like we’ve got a future on tour.

It’s a quiet kind of pride. We don’t show up in a lot of glossy travel lists — mostly we turn up in headlines doing actual work. Time Out once referenced “Marsden” when talking about the Great Barrier Reef, pointing out local threats like poor water quality, crown-of-thorns starfish outbreaks and unsustainable fishing practices. Harsh? Maybe. Accurate? Definitely a conversation starter at the footy club and the school fete.

For years the local press has been more about useful upgrades than influencer-friendly openings. That’s fine by us. We like being the kind of place where a refurbished driving range is newsworthy and where reef health gets mentioned in the same breath as weekend plans. There isn’t much petty local drama in the files — mostly practical stories and quiet improvements — so we keep our dramas small and our barbecues smaller.

Who would hate living here? If you need neon cocktails and a weekly culture column about the latest pop-up, Marsden will feel like a long weekend that never ends. If you like your suburbs loud, fast and constantly reinventing themselves, you’ll be bored by our sensible renovations and reef worry meetings.

Who’ll love it? Golfers who appreciate a proper practice bay (Urban List gave us that shout), folks who want a community that actually talks about local environmental issues, and anyone who prefers real conversation to curated content. We’re not flashy. We aren’t pretending to be. We’re just here — nets, sunscreen and all — keeping our eyes on the reef and our swings steady.
