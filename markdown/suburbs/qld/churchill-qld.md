# Churchill (QLD)

Slug: `churchill-qld`

Back in the day — and by that we mean the internet’s archive pages, because there are no recent articles about Churchill, QLD — we learned something useful: type "Churchill" into a search bar and you’ll mostly pull up other people’s glamour.

We’re proud locals, but we’ll admit the narrative about our postcode has been hijacked. Broadsheet, Time Out and Concrete Playground have been far more interested in Victor Churchill — the fourth‑generation butcher/mini steak temple praised for dark‑green marble, a pink Himalayan salt wall and vintage slicers — than they have in our roundabout. Broadsheet even ran the rumours about Anthony Puharich and Monty Koludrovic opening a Victor Churchill in Crown Melbourne (2027), while Time Out keeps pointing out the tiny 13‑seat wine and cocktail bar attached to that Armadale butcher. Concrete Playground keeps slinging steak lists at Melbourne; we get passed over like a very reasonable rump on its way to a dry‑age display.

Meanwhile, other Churchills gatecrash the results: The Churchill rooftop bar in Auckland gets notes about panoramic views and 160 gins, Churchills Sports Bar in Kingsford gets the “pool and TV” write‑up, and Sitchu’s roundup mentions a Tada Kids Cafe inside a Churchill Centre in Kilburn. Netmums will cheerfully point you to Churchill Gardens Children’s Centre (Pimlico) if you’re hunting parenting tips. All perfectly respectable places — just not ours.

Petty local drama? The algorithm’s our antagonist. We spend weekends making our own case: yes, we have streets and weird council signage, and yes, we’ve seen tourists drive confusedly past a local bakery that, confidentially, no national food site will ever care about. But we don’t mind. If you hate living somewhere that isn’t endlessly flattened into a lifestyle feature — the butcher‑in‑a‑bank or the gin‑soaked rooftop — you’ll hate living here.

Who would love Churchill, QLD? People who prefer being under the radar: the ones who want a suburb that answers to its own rhythm, not a listicle. We’ll keep the local secrets, the quiet corners and the best midweek parking spots — even if Broadsheet never notices.
