# Aspley (QLD)

Slug: `aspley-qld`

We are the Saturday morning tableau: a Toyota Prado inching backwards across two bays at Aspley Village while a Labradoodle on a retractable lead watches judgmentally from the footpath. We like to think it’s “for the kids” and “because the handling’s superb,” but really it’s because the pram, the footy gear and the reusable coffee cup all need their own aerodynamic zones.

Our morning runs like clockwork. 6:30am we’re doing touch footy with the kids at the local oval, Claire already in Lorna Jane leggings and a Highmark hoodie, Prado resplendent with a sticker for the under-9s Hornets. Then an oat flat white, short and extra hot, from Aspley Village Bakery—“and is that organic?”—handed over while someone else inspects a Subaru Outback for mud on the wheel arch. By mid-morning there’s a “quick” trip to Bunnings for a Pinterest-inspired DIY (cabinet handles become garden trellis), then brunch at Grounds & Co. near Gympie Road where the parking war is now a weekly spectator sport.

Drama is our cardio. The Aspley Village group chat is a living thing: last Saturday it hit peak fury when someone parked across two bays and the horn etiquette committee went nuclear. Then there was the PTA meeting about the bake sale roster that detonated into a hedge-trimming debate — who trimmed Mrs O’Leary’s privet without asking? Mrs O’Leary insists the cut was “too modern,” which is code for “someone used hedge shears without a permit from the Nanna Council.” Conversation escalated to passive-aggressive Post-It notes on mailboxes and an emergency strata-style mediation at the playground.

We are ritualists about recycling — unless it’s a greasy pizza box, which mysteriously passes as compostable if you squint. School gate politics happen at 8:30am sharp; loyalty to cafes is sacred, and the suspiciously tiny electric hatchback (Mini Cooper SE, of course) parked beside the Prados is treated with a kind of environmentally fashionable curiosity.

Who would HATE living here? A 25-year-old minimalist cyclist with one carry-on bag, contempt for coffee chains and a wardrobe of vintage denim would find us baffling. They prefer bike lanes, thrift stores and the quiet dignity of not being in a PTA group chat; we prefer weekend logistics, hedge etiquette and very specific oat-milk hierarchies.

Who would LOVE it? Claire, 38, who wears Lorna Jane to pick up bread, waves at the Aspley Hornets while juggling a reusable cup, subscribes to Anytime Fitness and finds joy in a neatly trimmed privet. If you value practical luxe, community small dramas and a place where everyone knows the baker’s oat milk order, welcome — you’ll fit right in.
