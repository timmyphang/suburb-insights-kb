# Currumbin Waters (QLD)

Slug: `currumbin-waters-qld`

Full disclosure: our clippings are older — Broadsheet, Urban List and mates wrote about this place a while back — so we’re reminiscing as much as roasting. Still, Currumbin Waters has always been delightfully indecisive, and we know every corner.

We’ll start at dawn, when Bread Social and Fuel Bakehouse used to bring the morning crowd across the Pacific Motorway, and you could linger at Refinery Coffee or wander into Dust Temple — that greenery-filled space that doubled as a gallery and vintage clothing store. If Guilty Cafe was your sin of choice, you knew where to find Fonzie Abbott coffee and the sort of “crazyshakes” that make dentists wince. The Communal House was where the creative types rented a desk and pretended to edit videos all afternoon.

Come weekend, we oscillated between wet and weird. One minute we’re at Currumbin Creek and the rock pools — rope swings, ledges and all — the next the beach turned into an open-air gallery when SWELL Sculpture Festival rolled in (remember when it returned for 2024 and the sand suddenly had 60-plus giant works?). Families splashed at the Wild Island Adventure at Currumbin Wildlife Sanctuary, then queued for whatever artisan pastry was trending. Merv Craig Park even hosted the community Christmas Carols, because why not sing by a park after wrestling a toddler into swimwear.

The charming contradiction is our petty local drama: we’re simultaneously hinterland and hangover. Eden Health Retreat sits lush and ritualistic in the valley while Balter Brewing tucked into Traders Way like craft beer’s answer to a surfboard. Danni‑Elle Townsend — yes, the pickleball convert from Currumbin Waters — reminded us not to underestimate our sporting pedigree.

Who would hate it here? If you can’t cope with a place that swaps rope swings for sculptures on the weekend, or you loathe breweries, brunches and the communal-clique energy of coworking studios, you’ll be itchy within a week.

Who’d love it? Families who want rock pools and Wildlife Sanctuary splash zones, art lovers who treat SWELL like a pilgrimage, wellness addicts who once escaped to Eden, and anyone who finds joy in a suburb that refuses to pick a single personality. We like it that way.
