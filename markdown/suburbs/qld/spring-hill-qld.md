# Spring Hill (QLD)

Slug: `spring-hill-qld`

We wake up on the wrong side of a very specific hill — Spring Hill’s hill — where the Meriton Suites on Adelaide Street still bookend the skyline and The Alliance Hotel’s black-and-white face has been doing its Victorian-era smirk since 1864. Back in the day the place read like a food critic’s shopping list: Komeyui brought its omakase counter to Wharf Street, La Cache a Vin hid beneath the old St Paul’s Tavern, and Scout occupied that 1920s terrace-front shop with rustic brick and hanging plants. We tell visitors that with a wink and the exact order in which these places once impressed us.

Morning for us meant a slow shuffle down to the heritage-listed Spring Hill Baths — yes, the old in-ground pool that Concrete Playground and Broadsheet have both flagged for its heated waters, grandstand and aqua classes — and past pocket parks like Victoria and Bedford where the suburb reminds you it’s hilly, historic and trying very hard to be genteel. For a decade-plus of lists, Time Out even tipped Spring Hill as one of the most sought-after postcodes for units in 2025, so apparently people like living on inclines with city views.

There isn’t any proper local feud in the clippings we’ve collected — no council kerfuffle to fling — just evidence of a suburb quietly being curated: DAPL in the Amora Hotel championing native ingredients, Kickin’ Inn hauling Cajun seafood boils into Boundary Street (477 Boundary Street, if you like exactitudes), and Arcadium Adventures or the Quest Room promising basement escape-room shenanigans beneath Espie Dods House. The coverage reads like a tasteful brochure with a sense of humour.

Who would hate living here? People who hate stairs, heritage charm, or the idea of bumping into someone who once stayed at The Incholm by Ovolo. Who would love it? Anyone who appreciates being a three-minute scramble from the CBD, heritage baths you can brag about, and a neighbourhood that collected good restaurants and oddball attractions like an earnest magpie collects shiny things.

We’re biased. We live on the slope, we know every step, and yes — we’ll continue to tell the story even if some of the venues mentioned are strictly “in the archives” now.
