# Sunshine Beach (QLD)

Slug: `sunshine-beach-qld`

We start the day with sandy feet and the faint whiff of someone’s holiday laundry — because around here the coastline and the short-term stays wrote the script long before we turned up. Back in the day Broadsheet trailed Eh Frame as that mid-century A-frame on the edge of Noosa National Park and even made a fuss about Domic perched in the dunes overlooking Sunshine Beach. The travel pages loved our views; we learned to shrug and keep the secret spots between the surf and the trees.

Our weekend routine has always been gloriously simple: a walk through Noosa National Park (yes, that 11-kilometre coastal walk that the guides rave about), a loop past Castaways Beach tucked between Sunshine Beach and Peregian, and a slow amble into Sunshine Village — where Urban List once mentioned The Beach Chalet being “just steps away” from the heart of the village. We’ll admit it, we enjoy being on the postcards journalists write.

For a spell Humble on Duke was the buzzy, 14-seat place locals tipped to friends — Middle Eastern flavours, pink-and-green walls and all the intimacy of a neighbourhood secret. Mostly that’s in the timelines now, but like any good local gossip it lives in our memories of nights out when we pretended to know the menu.

The papers sometimes choose to paint the Sunshine Coast as if it’s a whole different country of big headlines. We won’t rehearse those stories as suburb gossip — the truth is quieter: families rally around Sunshine Beach State School (105 Ben Lexcen Drive), which has been celebrated for its unique setting beside the national park and even got a “stunning new playground” the kids still test daily. There are Under Eights Days and Open Days, Raising Mamas meetups, and directories full of the little services that keep a place together when it’s not being Instagrammed.

Who would hate living here? People who loathe coastal walks, small schools, or any town that insists on being scenic and inconveniently lovely. Who’d love it? Families, retirees and anyone who wants a village that still remembers the tide times. We know every dune, and we’ll keep the best routes to ourselves — unless you bring coffee and good manners.
