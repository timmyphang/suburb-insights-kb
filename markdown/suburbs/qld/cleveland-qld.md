# Cleveland (QLD)

Slug: `cleveland-qld`

We stand at the Old Cleveland Point Lighthouse and squint east like it's our civic duty — the same lighthouse that’s been doing the scenic heavy-lifting since 1864. Mornings here are a gentle argument between sea breeze and nostalgia: ferry timetables at Toondah Harbour promising a hop to the Moreton Bay Islands, and the eternal question — do we walk off the ferry with chips folded in paper from that Old Cleveland Road shop that used to be crowned Queensland’s best?

For years the weekend ritual was painfully simple. Someone would suggest “a punt at the Cleveland Sunday Markets”, someone else would drift into Bayside Antiques to argue over lamps and teak, and before you knew it we were politely pushed into The GV at Cleveland Point for rotisserie roasts, pasta, or wood-fired pizza while the bay did its thing. Urban List once wrote up The Grand View Hotel like it was performing a loyalty test for viewers of bay sunsets — and honestly, we failed it gloriously every time.

Back in the day the Raby Bay crowd rebooted the Cleveland Sands Hotel — relaunched in 2022, complete with a beefed-up beer garden, sports bar and trivia — so yes, you could spectate sport, sip, and still claim you were doing culture. There were evenings when 152–166 Shore Street W would steam up with theatrical teppanyaki service and crowds for King Prawns and lobster; the address still rings in our heads like someone calling last orders.

We don’t have a council scandal on the front page or a weekly feud for breakfast TV. Our small-town drama is choosing which sunset to commit to and whether the antiques find counts as a life achievement. Time Out once called our “sea breezy streets” a thing — we liked that.

Who would hate it here? People who need their cities to be loud, neon and permanently urgent. Who would love it? Families catching the ferry to Moreton Bay, antique hunters with pockets of cash, and anyone who believes a lighthouse view solves a week’s worth of problems. We don’t pretend to be a metropolis. We’re the bayside place that knows how to do a sunset, a Sunday market, and a decent pint — often all in the same afternoon.
