# Bardon (QLD)

Slug: `bardon-qld`

We once saw a Subaru Outback double‑parked on Bardon Road, a Cavoodle in the passenger seat, a piccolo oat flat white balanced on the dash and a woman in Lululemon leggings waving like she’d just conquered Mount Coot‑tha — that is Bardon in a single, smug snapshot.  
We walk our Labradoodle up the goat tracks before sunrise, then swap notes about last night’s compost workshop over a second piccolo at Ithaca Coffee; we call it low‑key and organise three fundraisers before lunch.  
Our mornings are ritualised: 6:30am bushwalk to the lookout, 8:00am quick farmer’s‑market run for sourdough and kale, 8:45am the five‑car pick‑up standoff at Bardon State School where tempers flare over a seven‑minute drop‑off that somehow becomes a tribunal on parenting styles.  
We own practical, aspirational SUVs — Subaru Outback, Toyota Prado — and we treat them like accessories to our social calendar.  
We are fluent in local brands: Lululemon for Pilates, a Patagonia vest for layering, and a Cavoodle or Labradoodle who has more Instagram followers than anyone on our street.  
Our weekend looks like a catalogue: dog on leash, kids at sport on the oval, a long brunch ordered exactly the same way — brekky bowl and piccolo oat — at the bakery on the corner, then a long discussion about council footpath repairs while someone defends a compost swap on a muted Facebook thread.  
We are brilliant at performative civics. The weekly meltdown about verge planting is an art form. One neighbour wants native grasses for biodiversity; another wants a pristinely mown lawn because it photographs well for Easter egg hunts and those all‑important “village feel” reels. The debate escalates to a drop‑off war, a passive‑aggressive flyer campaign and, inevitably, a sausage‑sizzle bidding war for the school fete.  
We have clandestine lawn‑mowing competitions and strict rules about bike racks outside cafés. Our local buy/swap group is a social network and a soap opera. Our strata meeting minutes read like casting calls for community theatre.  
Who would hate living here? A nightclub DJ on a Harley in neon trackies who thinks a cul‑de‑sac is a 24/7 festival — they will be petitioned for noise‑reducing curtains within a week and politely volunteered out of the P&C.  
Who would love it? Mid‑30s/40s parents who can recite the best brunch order, value bushwalks, run Pilates and believe in composting; people who want community, decent coffee and a postcode that makes them feel smug about their avocado toast. Welcome to Bardon: bring sensible shoes and an extra picnic blanket for the next polite uprising.
