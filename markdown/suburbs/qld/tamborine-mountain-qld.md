# Tamborine Mountain (QLD)

Slug: `tamborine-mountain-qld`

We have a confession: we still get a little smug when we tell visitors we live on Tamborine Mountain and watch them try to say “Tamborine” properly while peering at the map. We like to point them toward the obvious bits — the Curtis Falls trail through Tamborine Mountain National Park, the Hang Gliders Lookout that makes everyone suddenly interested in breathing, and the Tamborine Mountain Regional Botanical Gardens where you can pretend you’re both cultured and outdoorsy at once.

Our weekend ritual is embarrassingly simple. A rainforest walk (yes, the Tamborine Rainforest Walk and Cedar Creek Falls are the classics), then a meander past the Glow Worm Caves — Broadsheet once reminded everyone they do daily tours — and if funds permit, a nostalgic potter through the antiques strip (Antique Unique Vintage and the like, as listed in local directories). Concrete Playground and Time Out made a fuss about SOL Elements arriving as Australia’s first floating elemental bathhouse; we watched that launch with the polite envy of people who’ve been meditating in the carpark.

There isn’t much real scandal here — the biggest local drama used to be whether Fox and Hounds at 7 Elevation Drive, Wongawallan would serve the best Sunday roast (Broadsheet name-checked their Yorkshires). Mostly the debates are garden-clubby: who grew the best azalea for Springtime on the Mountain (presented by the Tamborine Mountain Garden Club), or which boutique stay at Tamborine Mountain Glades had the dreamiest view. The Tamborine hotel got airtime too — 23 rooms, heated mineral pool — Time Out had feelings about it.

Who would hate living here? People who detest walking, skywalks, wind in the trees, or antiques. Also anyone allergic to that smug hinterland vibe — you’ll be surrounded by distilleries, wineries and people who think a “day trip” requires three different lookouts.

Who would love it? Folks who want a leafy escape an hour from the city, with rainforest walks, the Tamborine Rainforest Skywalk, the odd distillery to console you afterward, and a community that runs a proper SMS service for storms. We’ll keep the mountain for now — but yes, you can visit. Just pronounce it like you mean it.
