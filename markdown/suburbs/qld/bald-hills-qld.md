# Bald Hills (QLD)

Slug: `bald-hills-qld`

We know Bald Hills when we see a Toyota Prado parked askew on Station Street with a half-finished sausage sizzle napkin stuck to the bumper and someone mowing the verge beside the South Pine River at 7am like it’s a religious obligation. We park Holdens and Prados because “we need something big for the kids,” and then load them predominantly with mulch from Bunnings and the weekend haul from the bottle‑o. We order an oat milk flat white at Bald Hills Bakery & Cafe, wear Lululemon as a full‑time uniform and walk an Instagram-famous Cavoodle along the riverfront reserve like it’s our civic duty. We check the train timetable every morning before we check our messages. We wear Hi‑Tec boots to the Sunday footy and call Aldi a cultural institution.

Saturday is choreography here: a “just a couple of screws” run to Bunnings at 8am, one sausage sizzle at the junior footy which could be a sacrament, mow the lawn at 11am, then a riverside stroll along the South Pine with the dog while swapping tips about the best gutter guard. Sunday morning is drop-offs at soccer, an oat flat white from the bakery, and a ceremonial scroll through the Facebook feed and Nextdoor threads for any gardening tips — or the latest kerfuffle.

And there is always a kerfuffle. Our local scandals are deliciously petty. Every weekday morning the commuter parking wars flare at Bald Hills railway station: Brisbane commuters eye off resident kerb space, sticky notes appear on windscreens like grudges on sticky paper, and one week someone filmed the whole thing and uploaded it as if it were prime‑time drama. Then there’s the council collection day change. Suddenly everyone’s a policy expert, a recycling bin gone rogue is national news, and a three‑day petition materialises to demand the council “do better.” We thrive on this micro‑soap opera.

Who would hate it here? The obsessive city‑dweller who cycles everywhere, insists on single‑origin espresso only, refuses to own an SUV and believes backyards should be native food forests would be bewildered. They’d be horrified by our commuter parking etiquette, enraged by our devotional Saturday mower, and scandalised that the local BBQ is judged on sausage‑to‑sauce ratio rather than provenance of the meat.

Who would love it? The mid‑30s tradie‑turned‑weekend‑gardener with Hi‑Tec boots, a Prado, volunteer hours at the junior rugby club, and a soft spot for Bunnings sausage sizzles will feel right at home. We’re friendly, loud, practical and annoyingly proud — and we’ll save you a spot by the river.
