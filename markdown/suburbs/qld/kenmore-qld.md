# Kenmore (QLD)

Slug: `kenmore-qld`

We like to start a Saturday in Kenmore the way our parents taught us: oscillating between purpose and indecision. Back in the day our local ritual was simple — we’d amble down to The Single Guys (yes, after Janey Guo bought it in 2016 it stayed our unofficial community noticeboard, as Broadsheet pointed out), grab coffee, and pretend we’d drop the kids at soccer and actually go to yoga.

If you knew where to look, Kenmore’s dining scene felt like a polite surprise party. Sichuan Bang Bang on Wongabel Street arrived in 2012 (it took over from Eastern Jade, Broadsheet tells us) and became the place we bribed dinner companions with: peking duck, mapo tofu, sizzling Singapore chilli prawns and a BYO bottle to make it all go down smoother, as Concrete Playground helpfully reviewed. Pizzeria Violetta was our supposed Neapolitan fantasy — Urban List says it was popular, but also that it’s now permanently closed, which is how we learn to love and lose pizza.

We’re not fooled by grandiosity. Sitchu has always been blunt: Kenmore is for families who value quiet streets and schooling over flashing nightlife. The Kenmore South Playgroup ran its cheerful little empire on Tuesdays and Thursdays at 9:30am in the lower hall at Kenmore South State School — the kind of detail that proves we’re organised in suburban ways.

Petty local drama? Mostly the kind that involves expanded dining guides. Broadsheet noted neighbourhood spots like Neighbourhood opening up here — a subtle escalator from “local secret” to “everyone’s brunch plan.” Our version of scandal is when the tavern fires up the fireplace sooner than the neighbour expects (Sitchu swears by the Kenmore Tavern’s cosy credentials).

Who would hate living here: night-owl clubbers or anyone convinced their life improves with 24-hour convenience. Kenmore is not for relentless neon or late-night chaos. Who would love it: families, retirees, people who want decent restaurants, a reliable café, and a pub with a fireplace to pretend the winters are dramatic. We’re polite, practical, and full of modest pleasures — and if you’ve got a fondness for BYO dinner and orderly playgroup schedules, you’ll fit right in.
