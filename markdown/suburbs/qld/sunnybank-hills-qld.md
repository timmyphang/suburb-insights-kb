# Sunnybank Hills (QLD)

Slug: `sunnybank-hills-qld`

We start the day like locals do: arguing over whether the library count as a flex. We used to schlep to Sunnybank Hills Library at the corner of Compton and Calam Roads to borrow something that wasn’t already on the kitchen bench. Back in the day our media footprint was mostly food — and honestly, we wear that like a badge.

If you were looking for a weekend itinerary in our neck of the woods (according to the archives), it was a culinary hopscotch. Broadsheet and Benny Lam both name-checked Ru Yi Dim Sum — “a bit of a trek, but worth every kilometre,” they said — and we nodded, dumpling sauce on our sleeves. Urban List dreamt us cyberpunk realness at Daiki Grill And Bar, Concrete Playground counted Ramen Danbo at 4/22 Gowan Road among the city’s ramen go-tos, and Tok Tok’s Korean BBQ at 11/158 Gowan Road got a mention for tableside sizzle. Cream House Cafe at 10/18 Wynne St once had our tiny tyrants entertained with a playground while we negotiated peace treaties over coffee substitutes. Shalom’s ayam goreng made Concrete Playground’s spicy-food list. Milki Desserts in Sunny Park (Shop 23, 342 McCullough Street) got a dessert nod. Sitchu even had the Lunar New Year program mapped across Sunnybank Plaza and Sunny Park with lion dances, fireworks and drone bits we admired from a safe distance.

Petty local drama? The files don’t give us juicy council feuds or soap-opera plotlines — just a thorough, very delicious geography lesson. So our drama is domestic: deciding whether to queue at Ramen Danbo or trek to Ru Yi, and whether the kids’ ride-on cars at Cream House count as cardio.

Who would hate it here: people who think suburbs are bite-sized and bland — they’d be bored by our food density and playground logistics. Who would love it: anyone who judges a neighbourhood by its dumplings, neon Sichuan grills, and the efficiency of a library counter.

We’re proud of our unglamorous rep. We don’t pretend everything’s new and shiny — the stories that put Sunnybank Hills on the map were written a while back — but if flavour is the measure, we’ve been doing the heavy lifting for a long time.
