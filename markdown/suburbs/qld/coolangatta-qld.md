# Coolangatta (QLD)

Slug: `coolangatta-qld`

Back in the day we’d grumble that Coolangatta was “that place on the way to somewhere else” — as Broadsheet cheekily put it — and honestly, we still wear that like a badge. Walk the foreshore at dawn and you’ll catch the same stubborn surf culture: Rainbow Bay’s gentle shoulder to Snapper Rocks, the lifesavers who’ve been doing their rounds since before half of us had front yards (shoutout to Billy Ryan and his decades at the Point Danger Surf Life Saving Branch).

Weekends used to look a bit like a culinary ambush: we’d wander down Marine Parade to the Coolangatta Coastal Market at Queen Elizabeth Park on the second Sunday, queue for pastries after Luna’s Deli swung open its doors, then debate whether Harahetta Ramen’s creamy tonkotsu was sacrilegious on the beach. The Strand at Coolangatta got its big $60 million redevelopment name-checked in council lists, and Kirra Point got very grown-up when Kirra Beach Hotel reopened in November 2023 and Kirra Beach House turned the foreshore into cabana-central (Concrete Playground covered the whole seaside makeover).

Our petty drama? Oh, it’s mostly nostalgic: every June Cooly Rocks On used to roll in with 160,000 people, Elvis tribute concerts and more classic cars than the local mechanic could emotionally handle. We’d hide inside The Pink Hotel’s neon rooms until the pin‑ups and pomade cleared out.

Who would hate living here? If you’re allergic to salt air, surfboards propped in hallways, or the occasional festival traffic that turns Marine Parade into a moving exhibit of sunscreen and vintage convertibles, this isn’t your cup of chamomile. If you can’t stand retirees who actually appreciate a quiet corner (Time Out once put Coolangatta on the retirement shortlist) or food spots that keep evolving, best keep driving north.

Who’ll love it? Surfers, market-hoppers, people who like their dinners with a side of ocean view and a brave pastel hotel aesthetic. We’ll keep our foot in the sand, the town’s mixtape of old lifeguard legend, pop-up markets and pizza-and-cocktail reinventions, and let the rest of the coast call us “the place on the way” — we secretly love being the destination everyone eventually comes back to.
