# Eatons Hill (QLD)

Slug: `eatons-hill-qld`

Back in the day we used to wake up to the distant thrum of someone tuning guitars at the Eatons Hill Hotel and think: yep, suburb’s still breathing. We’re the kind of place where the local pub was less “watering hole” and more launching pad — the Comiskey Group cut its teeth here, and then went off to sprout Sandstone Point, The Doonan, even dream up Coochin Fields and the Aura Hotel on the Sunshine Coast. Small-town ego, national ambitions. We loved it and rolled our eyes at it, sometimes at the same time.

A typical weekend used to look like this: stagger down to the village precinct for milk (or whatever we needed after Beer Fest on the Grass), then wander past the Eatons Hill Hotel to the Comiskey Distillery — which opened next to the hotel and made everything smell suspiciously of ambition. They ran gin-making workshops and cocktail classes; we’d nod like autodidacts and pretend we weren’t just there for the tasting. Beer Fest on the Grass? That’s been a ritual — the 2024 edition reportedly had 250+ beers and ciders and food trucks. If you’ve ever judged someone’s life choices over a pale ale here, you’re not alone.

Petty local drama? We watched our backyard turn into the prototype for every big-venue PR bullet point. CVLTFEST chalked up a second year at the Eatons Hill Hotel in 2024, Aqua and the Vengaboys rolled through during the So Pop tour, and Time Out even had The Fray pencilled in for a 2025 Eatons Hill date. It’s flattering until you realise our quiet acreage on Edenderry Court — the one with the rainforest pocket and views sold on Sitchu — is technically in the concert’s shadow.

Who would hate living here: absolute quiet-lovers, people who think festivals are a personality flaw, or anyone allergic to a chorus of “encore!” at 11pm. Also, anyone who thinks “village precinct” means yoga boutiques instead of two-for-one schnitzels.

Who would love it: anyone who wants acreage with a side of live music, people who enjoy a decent gin workshop and a heavy dose of nostalgia, and anyone who secretly likes that our little local hotel keeps getting recognized on Urban List and Concrete Playground until it becomes the thing everyone else wants to copy. We’re loud, slightly ambitious, and annoyingly good at putting on a show — and we’d have it no other way.
