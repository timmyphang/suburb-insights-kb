# Acacia Ridge (QLD)

Slug: `acacia-ridge-qld`

We start our Saturdays with the sight that tells you everything about Acacia Ridge: a Ford Ranger with a tray full of tools, the tray hosed sparkling next to a suspiciously immaculate backyard BBQ that looks like it could host the Queen—if the Queen liked smokey sausages and a slab of VB. We’ll argue until the servo runs dry that we’re “country people,” even as we drive our Rangers and Hiluxes to the Beaudesert Road servo for a short macchiato (double-shot) and a Bunnings run.

Mornings are choreography: Darren, 42, in his high-vis to the job site and a polo for everything else, drops Milo the Labradoodle at the park after a quick Thrasher Street bakery meat pie. Mums in Lorna Jane leggings and R.M. Williams boots do school drop-offs in a Nissan X-Trail while scrolling the Acacia Ridge Facebook group for who-has-sugar posts and the latest free bedside table. Tradesmen in Toyota Hiluxes with aftermarket bullbars swear by Red Kap shirts and never miss the Saturday Bunnings sausage sizzle; they keep Bunnings vouchers in their wallets like others keep baby photos.

Our weekend routine is sacrosanct: short mac, mulch from Bunnings, junior footy at the local oval, then Sunday roast followed by a ceremonial hose-down of the ute. Nightly duties include a 9pm perimeter check of the quarter-acre and the wheelie bins; it’s the small things that keep the peace.

And oh, the local dramas—bless them. The shopping-centre parking bay turf wars are Olympic-level passive aggression. We had a midweek explosion of outrage when someone left a dual-cab ute overnight in a prized driveway and the Facebook group treated it as national emergency. There’s a months-long feud about a trailer blocking the roundabout, and the council kerfuffle after someone repainted the main roundabout “modern art” style spawned three competing petitions—floral murals, native shrubs, and a furious campaign to “leave it as it bloody was.”

Who would hate living here? The ultra-minimalist cyclist with a Brompton who drinks single-origin oat milk chai and calls 4x4s “aggressive consumption” would bring a tiny backpack to a sausage sizzle and apologise for using a paper plate—then faint at our orange-cone parking solutions and the sacred parishioners of a well-cleaned ute.

But if you love loud tools, loyal cafes, perfectly-timed sausages, backyard BBQs that could double as stage sets, and neighbours who’ll lend you a spade and a laugh, you’ll fit right in. Welcome to Acacia Ridge—come for the pies, stay for the passive-aggressive parking.
