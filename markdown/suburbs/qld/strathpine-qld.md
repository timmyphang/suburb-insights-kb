# Strathpine (QLD)

Slug: `strathpine-qld`

Back in the day the internet remembered us mostly for our pins and our laksa, and honestly, that feels about right.

We start the weekend with a very Strathpine routine: shuffle out, catch one of the buses that — according to Sitchu — “run regularly and on time,” or hop onto Strathpine railway station, which Sitchu kindly reminds us is close by. Then it’s a pilgrimage of small, efficient pleasures. Urban List once hailed Ipoh Laksa Strathpine as “one of the best laksas in Brisbane” — we used to queue for that spicy, soulful bowl like it was a beanie sale at the town hall. Later, if we’re feeling competitive and nostalgic, there’s Strathpine Bowl — Urban List even noted it “knows its way around the pins” and had reopened under new ownership. Yes, we still keep score. Mostly for the laughs.

If you want zen, City Cave used to list Strathpine among its locations; if you want space, Sitchu once had an eyebrow-raising listing on Bells Pocket Road — a renovated high-set home with the option to rent the entire 17 acres for $1,400 per week. For people who think “backyard” should come with grazing rights and a mortgage-sized commitment, that was your ad.

Local scandal? Our archive is suspiciously polite: laksa, bowling, a wellness franchise mention and an acreage listing. No council melodramas, no celebrity feuds, just wholesome suburban self-respect. We’ll take it.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe the idea of actually being able to bowl, enjoy proper laksa, or drive five minutes to Aspley Hypermarket or Westfield Chermside (yes, Sitchu says they’re a short drive away), then Strathpine will grate. Also, if you only collect boutique city problems, you’ll be bored.

Who would love it here? People who like accessible transport, loud lanes at the bowling alley, bowls with reputation (hello, Ipoh Laksa Strathpine), or the notion of owning 17 acres without moving to the end of the map. We’re not flashy; we’re reliably delicious, occasionally athletic and quietly massive when it comes to acreage. Come for the laksa, stay for the strike.
