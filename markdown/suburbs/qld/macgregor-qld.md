# Macgregor (QLD)

Slug: `macgregor-qld`

Back in the day we’d set the GPS for Kessels Road and call it a weekend achievement when everyone made it out of the car without a snack-related mutiny. Macgregor didn’t pretend to be anything chic — it was practical: Bounce at 544 Kessels Road for the small-person Olympics, and Crank Indoor Climbing at 537 Kessels Road when we wanted to remember our shoulders exist. Both places were our excuses to leave the house “for exercise” and come home smelling faintly of foam pit and chalk.

We used to know exactly which lane to queue in for Bounce’s bookings and who’d be the first to conquer Crank’s beginner wall — Crank, by the way, reportedly had 38 top-ropes, 60 metres of bouldering, eight auto-belays and hundreds of routes. It was the sort of place that made us proud and slightly terrified at the same time. Urban List called these spots family-friendly and “rain, hail or shine” salvation; we call them Tuesday.

People keep getting confused when they Google Macgregor and end up reading about Macgregor Terrace in Bardon — Lutece and Smoked Paprika were apparently doing their thing on Macgregor Terrace — or scrolling all the way to Melbourne where some bloke opened Macgregor’s Original smash-burger in Abbotsford. Fair play to them, but that’s not our postcode. We like our indoor trampolines, our climbing gyms and the very specific aroma of post-bounce laundry.

Who would hate living here? If you crave hushed sidewalks and silence on weekends, you’ll be miserable. If you need a suburb that’s all about curated lattes and window-shopping boutiques, you’ll keep turning the GPS back to the city.

Who would love it? Families who know the value of a good indoor play centre, anyone who got hooked on belaying friends up a 60m boulder face, and people who reckon a suburban win is surviving a day out without losing a shoe. We’re not posh. We’re practical. And yes — we will always claim the best parking spot at Bounce like it’s a public service.
