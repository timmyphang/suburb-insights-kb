# Ripley (QLD)

Slug: `ripley-qld`

We live in Ripley, and our favourite weekend pastime is Googling ourselves just to see which Ripley turns up first. Spoiler: it’s usually Andrew Scott or a Sydney Theatre Company review — not our playgroup. We traipse out in the morning, make a cuppa, and watch the internet firmly decide we’re either a moody Netflix miniseries or a stage production at the Roslyn Packer Theatre. Identity crisis? You bet. We own it.

Back in the day our online legacy was humbler and sweeter: Ripley Playgroup’s blog. The old posts about weekly rotas, Baby and Children’s Markets and Make House running free arts-and‑crafts sessions are the real Ripley highlights we brag about at barbecues that probably never happened because, you know, we’re mostly too tired. The playgroup site used to be where neighbours swapped gear, organised markets and reminded each other to bring a hat. Nostalgia suits us.

If you’re after scandal, the archive isn’t much of a goldmine. The only recurring theme in the stories attached to our name is dramatic irony: the world writes glowing things about The Talented Mr. Ripley — Will McDonald, Andrew Scott, Roslyn Packer Theatre — and our suburb gets politely lumped in search results like a reluctant extra. As Concrete Playground and Broadsheet fawn over monochrome trailers and red‑carpet openings, we smirk and update the playgroup rota.

Who would hate living here? People who want their suburb to have only one cultural reference and for that reference to be a local artisan bakery, because every time someone asks where we’re from we have to explain we’re not a Sydney theatre season or a Vanity Film Fest screening at Mr Vain’s. (Yes, Urban List mentioned Mr Vain’s movie nights alongside, you guessed it, The Talented Mr. Ripley.)

Who would love it here? Families who remember the Baby and Children’s Markets, people who don’t mind being part of a minor internet mix‑up, and anyone who thinks it’s funny to say they live in a place that shares a name with a famous con man and a hit Netflix series. We’re small, slightly mischievous, and perfectly fine being the underrated subplot in everyone else’s drama.
