# Ashgrove (QLD)

Slug: `ashgrove-qld`

We start our Saturdays beneath the leopard trees at Dorrington Park — the so‑called "Enchanted Forest" where kids collect seed pods like tiny natural spoils and the musical toadstools make the grown-ups pretend they're not mid‑life crisising over wicker picnic chairs. Back in the day the park has even hosted Ashgrove Rotary's Carols by Candlelight and the much‑loved Halloween Outdoor Cinema, so yes: community, candlelight and questionable thermoses were a package deal.

After corralling the smalls we used to wander past Gerbino’s on Ashgrove Ave — the family‑run Italian bakery the guides couldn't stop raving about for cannoli, danishes and real bread — and grab something that made us feel continentally superior for five minutes. We used to be able to follow that with a proper ritual at Kimino (71 Wardell Street), the Kyoto‑born pour‑over spot Broadsheet wrote up, where ceramics from Japan and rum‑soaked raisin sable cookies made us fancy and slightly anxious about our caffeine provenance.

Saturday afternoons used to be a study in indecision: the Local Larder famously relaunched as Wine & Larder with a French‑style menu and a 400‑bottle shop; Banneton’s Ashgrove was praised for artisan breads; Macho Macho toggled between quaint café by day and swanky bar by night; Milk Cafe lit up Ashgrove Avenue with its bright retro look. Loaded Food Co actually started here in 2016, so we like to claim culinary trendsetting when convenient.

We know what kind of person would hate Ashgrove: anyone allergic to playgrounds, artisanal breads, or the faint suggestion that your suburb might have once inspired a Bluey scene — yes, the Ashgrove Library was the model for the library in the episode "Promises", according to local guides, and you will be judged for not bringing children to verify this.

But who would love it here? Families who live for leafy parks, people who can spend an afternoon arguing over which bakery is the right bakery, and anyone who appreciates a suburb that quietly accumulates culture — from Enchanted Forest slides to tiny Japanese pour‑overs — and then pretends it was always this way. Back in the day, Ashgrove did it with grace; we like to think we still do, even if the seed pods have opinions about us now.
