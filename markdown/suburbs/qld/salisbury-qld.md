# Salisbury (QLD)

Slug: `salisbury-qld`

We pull up outside The Salisbury Hotel on Toohey Road and, for a moment, pretend we're on a proper weekend. The kids sprint to the playground while we debate whether to start at the pub or detour for coffee — the kind of debate that only happens where the pub has a playground and the coffee scene has a history. As Urban List kindly reminded us, The Salisbury Hotel at 668 Toohey Road once made family nights feel slightly more civilised with a kids’ area.

Back in the day we’d wander to Apples on Ainsworth for that homely, Gran’s-kitchen vibe Urban List loved, or argue over which Salisbury favourite actually deserved the title Cups. There was Reload Espresso Bar, improbably housed in a former World War II bullet factory, which always made our brunch selfies look like they’d been shot with a plot twist. And don’t forget that Sprout — Rebecca Foley and Lutz Richter kicked it off in the Food Connect kitchen in Salisbury in 2014 before it got all serious and baker-y, as Broadsheet noted.

Local flavour meant choices: Bare Knuckles BBQ at 617 Toohey Road, with its big smoker and slow-cooked meats, for when we were trying to impress people we barely liked; and Hiker Brewing Co out on Pentex Street, plus Ballistic Beer Co, for when we just wanted a six-pack and a reason to call it culture. Concrete Playground even gave us a spot on the Beermuda crawl — prideful, slightly sticky, and excellent for meeting other people who think “easy-drinking beers” is a personality trait.

Petty local drama? If anything it was being gently refurbished into somewhere slightly shinier — Concrete Playground flagged Australian Venue Co’s makeover tour that touched The Salisbury Hotel — the sort of adulting you grumble about while enjoying their new snacks.

Who would hate living here: anyone allergic to smoky brisket (Bare Knuckles) or beer gardens (Hiker/Ballistic), or fans of sterile suburbia with no personality. Who would love it: families who appreciate a pub with a playground, food-and-beer people who can pair a Sprout-origin story with a plate of shared BBQ, and anyone who thinks a café in a bullet factory is a sellable anecdote. We’ll keep the playground, the smoker and the breweries — someone’s got to maintain local charm.
