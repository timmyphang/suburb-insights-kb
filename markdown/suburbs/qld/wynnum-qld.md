# Wynnum (QLD)

Slug: `wynnum-qld`

We know Wynnum by the sound of the jetty creaking and the particular way the esplanade smells like salt and fried dinner. Back in the day we’d start a weekend at the Wynnum Wading Pool, ferry the kids between the mini waterslide and Wynnum Whale Park, then argue about who gets the last patch of shade on the foreshore.

For years the foreshore was our runway: the Moreton Bay Cycleway—Wynnum to Manly—saw more lycra than a charity ride, and the Wynnum Mangrove Boardwalk was our excuse to look outdoorsy. If we weren’t pretending to exercise we were queuing for local staples: Tasty Pocket down at 150 Bay Terrace, Pepe’s in the old Miss Mexicana spot at 89 Bay Terrace, or grabbing fish and chips from Triton Seafood on Wynnum Road in Wynnum West. There used to be a little cultural odd-couple on Wynnum Road too — Southside Tea Room at 639 Wynnum Road and, next door, Death Valley Bar and Records at 639B Wynnum Road, with the Red Robin Supper Truck habitually parked outside serving whatever the evening required.

Our festival game surprised a few people. As Concrete Playground noted, Wynnum Fringe ballooned into something gloriously chaotic — Shrek-inspired burlesque, ABBA sing-alongs, a ferris wheel and the sort of Daryl Braithwaite booking that makes everyone sing along like it’s 1989. It’s the rare place where salted marshmallows sit beside civic pride; the Fringe drew tens of thousands in recent years and we still brag about it.

Who would hate living here? If you need rooftop bars with invisible cocktails and an ongoing obsession with being "seen", Wynnum will make you restless. If your idea of leisure is an artisanal playlist played at full volume in a CBD laneway, you’ll find our bay breeze suspiciously calm.

Who loves it here? Families who remember the Wading Pool as sacred, weekend cyclists on the Moreton Bay Cycleway, people who like their suburbs with a ferris wheel and a pub that might have a pinball machine. We’re bayside, a bit bonkers, and perfectly content.
