# Ormeau (QLD)

Slug: `ormeau-qld`

Back in the day we were content to be the kind of place that surprises people in very specific ways.

On summer nights we’d tell the kids to stay up late because 13 Katandra Crescent used to be the reason the whole street didn’t sleep — Urban List even flagged it in its Gold Coast Christmas lights roundup. This wasn’t a string-of-lanterns effort: a refreshed seventh edition, over 5,000 energy‑efficient matrix lights and 30‑minute musical shows running nightly from November 30 through December 24. You could hear the neighbours sigh, the GPS recalculating, and at least three different car horns attempting synchronised percussion.

Weekends were less about spectacle and more about survival. For years the local listings had Aqua Kidz Ormeau at 1/52 Blanck Street — the swim school that proudly put the “spa” in splashing with 33°C ozone and UV‑purified mineral water. We used to joke the toddlers got better water treatment than we did. And when someone had a worrying tickle in the chest the Findglocal entry for Radiant Radiology on 270 Lahrs Road was the kind of clinical name that made us feel like our heart had entered a well‑lit boutique: advanced cardiac imaging, Heart Week posts, and neighbouring branches in Pimpama, Yarrabilba and Elanora.

Our culinary culture, such as it is, was immortalised in the form of local promos: Pizza Crew’s Ormeau Road listing (463 Ormeau Road) used to run giveaways tied to match predictions — yes, pizza and sporting optimism in one convenient swipe. Practical, loud, and comforting.

There’s no big scandal to sell you — our press has historically been a mix of festive LEDs, swim school specs and radiology listings — which is a fancy way of saying we’re low‑drama and high‑useful. If you loathe seasonal public spectacles, chlorine that smells like indulgence, or a pizza joint that treats local footy like high art, you’ll hate living here.

But if you love a town that turns into a mini Las Vegas for three weeks, where toddlers graduate from floating noodles and the local businesses actually try to keep the rest of us ticking, Ormeau was and still feels like home. For years the local listings told that story, loud as a 5,000‑light encore.
