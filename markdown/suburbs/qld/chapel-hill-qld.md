# Chapel Hill (QLD)

Slug: `chapel-hill-qld`

Back in the day — and by “back in the day” we mean everything the internet remembers about us — Chapel Hill was the tiny western suburb that stubbornly refused to be boring.

We start our Saturday like proper locals used to: lining up at Gathered Bakers on 2 Kirkdale Road for the sort of slow‑fermented loaves that made queueing feel like a hobby. If you were lucky you’d score one of those pork, apple & sage sausage rolls with tomato chilli jam that Urban List shouted about; if not, well, you learned patience and humility in equal measure.

Afternoons were for dog‑friendly diplomacy at Suburban Social — the place that apparently taught people how to run a sandwich shop, since the Sarni crew in Ascot “started working at Suburban Social in Chapel Hill” (as Broadsheet helpfully traced). Urban List swore it lived on the Indooroopilly border, with a back courtyard that made suburbia feel like a tiny holiday. Sitchu even listed Suburban Social among its 2025 finalists and named it a runner‑up — which is local bragging rights, sort of like getting a ribbon at the school fete.

We also had Flour Leopard at 620 Moggil Road for the gluten‑free crowd — the one place you could bring your coeliac cousin and not worry. And yes, the neighbourhood got proper food‑scene attention: Sitchu noted Chalong opened to Chapel Hill locals in February 2024 with Southeast Asian street‑food vibes, so we weren’t all just bakeries and courtyards.

Who would hate living here? People who need a city skyline and a subway map in their lounge room. If your idea of fun is never having to queue for sourdough or being within shouting distance of five different boutique cocktails, Chapel Hill will make you twitchy.

Who’d love it? Dog walkers, bread snobs, the gluten‑free aunt, and anyone who enjoys a neighbourhood that quietly collects good things — bakeries, a convivial little bar, and the odd excellent new restaurant that pops up and then becomes “ours.” We know every corner, every queue, and we wouldn’t trade our back‑yard suburb for someone else’s skyline — even if we’ll still joke about it.
