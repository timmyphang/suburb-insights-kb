# Boondall (QLD)

Slug: `boondall-qld`

All our sources on Boondall are a bit nostalgic — the press pieces we’ve got are older, so think of this as a cheeky postcard from when the suburbs were still on tour schedules and the boardwalk hadn’t changed a feather.

We start the day on the Boondall boardwalk, pretending we’re on a nature documentary. The Wetlands spread out over something like 1,150 hectares, host roughly 190 bird species and play seasonal host to migratory shorebirds from September to March — Time Out basically wrote our bird census. We swing past the Boondall Wetlands Environment Centre (they used to be open Tue–Sun 9am–4pm in the write-ups) and nod at the Nurri Millen Totem Trail like it’s our public art flex.

Weekends are a split personality. On one hand, Urban List remembered us for pram-friendly walks and even name-checked our stretch when compiling northside breakfasts — flattering, even if the cafés were from someone else’s memory. On the other hand, Concrete Playground and Broadsheet kept listing the Brisbane Entertainment Centre on Melaleuca Drive as the place the whole city would migrate to for big shows — Hans Zimmer, Justice, Lorde, Oprah, Cirque du Soleil, David Byrne — the kind of grand billing that turns our traffic lights into ticket stubs. Broadsheet even pointed out Brisbane locals commuting out to Boondall for the arena — which is adorable and mildly catastrophic for parking.

We admit: you will see the weird combo of peaceful wetlands and stadium fervour. We also have Iceworld Olympic Ice Rinks at 2304 Sandgate Road and V-Golf at the corner of Sandgate Road and Parthenia Street on the lists, so yes — you can accidentally go from birdwatching to mini-golf to ice-skating without changing suburbs.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t handle mosquitoes, intermittent shade on the boardwalk (Time Out gave you the sunscreen warning), or the odd tidal-flat parking scramble when the Entertainment Centre is on, this isn’t your jam.

Who’d love it? Families who like pram-friendly strolls (cheers, Urban List), bird nerds with binoculars, anyone who likes cheap(ish) housing options — Sitchu’s old listings featured everything from a modern 3-bed townhouse on Sean Street to a low-set brick on College Way in College Green Estate — and people who enjoy the absurdity of mangroves and megastars sharing a postcode. We’ll take our wetlands and our weekend crowds, thanks very much.
