# Woodend (QLD)

Slug: `woodend-qld`

We wake up in Woodend to the kind of quiet that makes Melburnians feel guilty for having ever complained about traffic. Back in the day our town got bracketed in travel guides as “about an hour from the city” — Broadsheet and Sitchu both said so — which is either charming or deeply inconvenient depending on how many of your friends still answer texts.

A weekend here used to look the same: a V/Line ride that, as Broadsheet cheerfully pointed out, stops virtually out the front; a mosey down High Street where Holgate Brewhouse (79 High Street) used to ply its award-winning ales — Concrete Playground and Urban List loved telling the origin story of Paul and Natasha Holgate’s backyard brewery — and Bourkie’s Bakehouse would be rehearsing another trophy-winning vanilla slice (Concrete Playground counted decades of medals). Time Out used to remind people we were the kind of place with more artists per capita than you’ll find in a hurry, and that we’d host the Woodend Winter Arts Festival (the 2025 program was splashed about in the press).

We’re proud of our features: Woodend Children’s Park was called “hands-down one of the best parks in the Macedon Ranges,” with places for little climbers and musical play; the Woodend Farmers’ Market used to be the Saturday stop for locally made everything; and Hanging Rock looms like a scenic cousin, where Run the Rock was even listed on the 2026 sporting calendar.

If you hate small towns where everyone knows the baker’s award tally, artisan stores, or the annual festival schedule — or if you demand a late-night nightclub and a postcode that never mentions “Hanging Rock” — you’ll hate living here. We’ll also admit we enjoy being the sort of place that makes weekenders reassess their life choices.

Who would love it? Families who actually use the playgrounds and market, craft-beer pilgrims who appreciate Holgate’s origin myth, and anyone who likes walks that end with a bakery counter and a view of the ranges. Fair warning: most of these write-ups are older, so consider this more of a fond scrapbook than a real-time guide. We wouldn’t have it any other way.
