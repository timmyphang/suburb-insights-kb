# Biggera Waters (QLD)

Slug: `biggera-waters-qld`

We like to think Biggera Waters announces itself with the smell of copper and fermentation, even if that’s technically nostalgia. Back in the day our skyline included the gleam of White Oat Distillery’s impressive copper pipes and the polished-concrete confidence of Black Hops’ big second site at 671 Pine Ridge Rd — places Broadsheet and Urban List remembered for a reason. Black Hops’ main production site was here; it even survived its own little soap opera when a group of investors rescued the brewery from liquidation. We still tell that one at BBQs like it’s local folklore.

Sundays used to roll out with brewery tours and tastings (yes, 14 beers on tap at one point) and White Oat hosting live music and food trucks like it was our unofficial cultural precinct. Harbour Town Shopping Centre would tide the more practical among us over — Yum Cha Cuisine at 147 Brisbane Rd was the kind of hidden gem Urban List sent you to between outlet runs. And for the parents among us, the Movies Under The Stars nights — remember Boss Baby on the big screen? — came with food trucks and enough kids’ fun to convince us we were doing something right.

If you’re allergic to breweries, distilleries or the idea of a Saturday spent sampling things that smell like hops, you’ll hate it here. If you prefer your nightlife to be strictly neon-and-bottle-service, our taste for polished concrete bars and copper plumbing will feel a touch provincial. And if you need a constant stream of new restaurants and tapas bars on every corner, Biggera Waters has always been more "industrial-chic-with-a-water-view" than metropolitan sprinkle.

Who would love it? People who like to be near things that fizz and bubble — craft-beer pilgrims, distillery devotees and families who appreciate outdoor movie nights with a side of food trucks. Also anyone who thinks a shopping day at Harbour Town deserves a proper yum cha detour. We’re not pretentious; we just know our pipes, our pints and our pitch-perfect little community moments — even if most of the shout-outs are from the pre-2024 era. Come for the history, stay for the stories.
