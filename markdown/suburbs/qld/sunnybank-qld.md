# Sunnybank (QLD)

Slug: `sunnybank-qld`

Back in the day we’d stumble into Sunnybank like we owned the place — mission: eat everything that moved for under five bucks. Our weekend starts with a circuit that reads like a very delicious scavenger hunt: Sunnybank Plaza and Sunny Park for the old-school Food Trail, Market Square for its neon-ambition buffet and pastry windows, and Les Atkinson Park when the kids (or inner children) need to run off dumpling energy.

We’ll admit it: we used to map our lives around the Sunnybank Food Trail — the guide used to promise $2, $3 and $5 tasting plates and a day-long, self-guided gorge. Concrete Playground ran with the comeback stories, Urban List cheered on Market Square’s big arrivals, and Broadsheet kept a patient directory of our best bits. Seoul Garden’s massive 800‑sqm, neon-lit takeover at Market Square and Seoul Bistro on Turton Street were the sort of places that made us feel like Brisbane was finally serious about Korean fried chicken. Kingsfood — the iconic Taiwanese spot founded in 1990 — was the reason many of us ventured out on late shifts for proper noodles. Bloom Patisserie and Tetra Fresh Grocer used to sell the kind of sweet and fresh that made us pretend we were doing our food shopping responsibly.

There’s no local soap opera to scandalise — just the gentle civic drama of events coming back after downtime. The Food Trail’s returns (2023, 2024, even chatter for 2026) read like a love letter to cheap taste-testing. Time Out and Sitchu kept reminding everyone that Sunnybank’s a food map people still follow.

Who would hate living here? If you require hermetically sealed suburbia, silence at 6pm and nothing that smells vaguely of chilli, we’d say this place is not for you. If every meal needs to be minimalist and Instagram-filtered, prepare to be baffled.

Who’d love it? Families who want parks like Pengana Playground and Les Atkinson, food obsessives who treat a food trail like a pilgrimage, and anyone who remembers that a great neighbourhood is measured in dumpling lines, pastry cabinets and the sheer glut of choice. We do, and we’re not apologising.
