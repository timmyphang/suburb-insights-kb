# Wavell Heights (QLD)

Slug: `wavell-heights-qld`

Saturday morning in Wavell Heights: we used to emerge blinking into sunlight, wallets heavy with loyalty stamps and opinions. Back in the day Broadsheet announced Alby’s at 199 Bilsen Road — a cheery corner spot that served Single O and beef rendang toasties — and yes, we queued like it was the only thing on the calendar.

Our weekend routine was embarrassingly wholesome. Some of us wandered down Shaw Road to Barrow & Bear (Shop 15, 216 Shaw Road) for a menu that named dishes after cities we’d never visited. Others signed the kids up for whatever sport had a clinic this week — Norths Rugby Club at Hugh Courtney Oval (128–150 Shaw Road) was forever running junior clinics, and Wavell Heights Oztag at 150 Shaw Road kept promising an ever-growing family. The kindergarten at 175 Edinburgh Castle Road quietly ran the local future, one glitter-glue incident at a time.

If you like community noticeboards, congratulations — we do them big. Find It Locally and social groups like the Social Mums Club kept our suburban grapevine well-watered; there are more posts about lost Tupperware and bake-sale volunteers than council minutes. And yes, someone once declared our chips worthy of a list — The Spotted Cod representing Everton Park and Wavell Heights made the crunchy shortlist, which is the suburb’s version of an Oscar.

We’re not claiming we’re a metropolis. Sitchu used to sell the dream that Wavell Heights has easy access to Nundah Village, Westfield Chermside, Brisbane Airport and the M1 — which is shorthand for “conveniently placed for escaping relatives.”

Who would hate living here? If you despise playgroups, sports clinics, and hairdressers being local institutions (Cameo Rose got a shoutout among Brisbane’s favourites), then move along. If your ideal Saturday is a rooftop DJ set and a late-night queue for artisan whatever, Wavell Heights will gently tut at you.

Who would love it? Families, people who favour sensible footpaths over fad, anyone who values a kindergarten on their street and a strong community noticeboard. We’ll keep being quietly suburban, a little bit smug about our chips, and utterly unbothered by the rest of Brisbane’s hurry.
