# Seven Hills (QLD)

Slug: `seven-hills-qld`

Saturday in Seven Hills feels like someone hit snooze on the city and left us with the good bits: a bush track, a fish-and-chipper you brag about, and a cafe that used to serve TimTam pancakes to kids who'd eaten the whole playground first.

Back in the day we’d wander up to The Rabbit Hole — the Urban List used to point out its Seven Hills spot (and Wynnum) for everything from southern fried chicken waffles to breakfast gnocchi. The same write-ups gushed about a playground "practically a whole damn playground out the back" and even a robot that could deliver food, which, yes, made us feel slightly futuristic and mostly smug.

If you like your weekends with eucalyptus perfume and the threat of a koala photo-op, our 50-hectare Seven Hills Bushland Reserve was a favourite pick. Time Out described the two main loop tracks — the short jaunt takes about 25 minutes, both loops roughly 40 — and warned the terrain is dry and rocky and not suitable for prams. Sitchu called the suburb a garden oasis, full of dry rainforest and wildflowers, and the Seven Hills Circuit was shorthand for a 3km loop that local walkers (and their dogs) kept in rotation.

We used to get a little loud about food too. Time Out named The Fish Factory (Morningside / Seven Hills) among the best fish-and-chips in Brisbane — a market, fish-and-chipper and sushi bar all in one, the sort of place you point someone to when you want them to understand why we bristle at people who say suburbs can’t feed well.

For a minute every year we’d clog the footpath for the Seven Hills Festival — Concrete Playground ran it in 2022 — food trucks, pop-up bars, live entertainment; the kind of local chaos we adore.

Who would hate living here? Parents who need pram-friendly hikes, people allergic to hills, and anyone who demands a CBD-level nightlife on a Tuesday. Who would love it? Bushwalkers, families who liked a chaotic playground with their brekkie, seafood devotees, and anyone who enjoys a suburb that remembers how to throw a street party.
