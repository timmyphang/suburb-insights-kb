# Pacific Pines (QLD)

Slug: `pacific-pines-qld`

We know the driveway at 21 Springwood Avenue better than our own mailboxes — every December it’s like we collectively audition for Elf: The Musical. That house has apparently won the Gold Coast Bulletin Christmas Lights Competition more times than we’ve returned library books, and yes, we’ll still circle the street in our pyjamas to judge the tinsel density.

Mornings here are a low-key loop: a school run detour past Brockman Park (the playground Urban List once name‑checked in a round‑up of Gold Coast kid stuff), a wave to whoever’s listing their place on Find It Locally, and a quick scroll through the Social Mums Club posts where mums from Carrara, Nerang and Pacific Pines trade advice, bake sale dates and the odd life hack. For a suburb that pitches itself as “quiet and sought after,” our notice boards (the Find It Locally network even groups us with Gaven, Guanaba, Mount Nathan, Oxenford & Studio Village — group 899) are busier than the roundabout on a school pickup.

Back in the day Urban List pointed out that Western Stomp used to host line dancing at the Pacific Pines Tavern every Tuesday — the kind of place where you can scuff your boots and discover you have two left feet in stereo. And if you ever needed proof that someone out here is trying to monetise suburbian zen, remember the “Peaceful Pacific Pines Airbnb” listing: sleeps eight, four bedrooms, pool and BBQ — basically a luxury version of “please don’t wake the kids.”

Developers used to eye us too: Sitchu had 5 Elkins Street flagged as a prime 1,139sqm elevated block with ocean, skyline and hinterland views. We like to pretend we’ll gatekeep the view, but the sale signs say otherwise.

Who would hate living here? If you think a communal mums group, park playdates and a tavern line‑dance sounds like a horror show, you’ll last a week. Who’d love it? Families who value playgrounds, people who appreciate a proper Christmas spectacle on Springwood Avenue, and anyone after a peaceful suburban Airbnb (at least according to the listings we used to see).

We’re quietly proud: not flashy, a bit festive, and always up for a Tuesday night two‑step — whether you can dance or not.
