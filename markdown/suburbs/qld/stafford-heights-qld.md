# Stafford Heights (QLD)

Slug: `stafford-heights-qld`

We know Stafford Heights like the back of our sunburnt hands — the little hills, the short trips to the shops, and the way a community newsletter can make you feel like a celebrity. Back in the day (and in the local listings that have lived in our inboxes), our weekend started with a wander past places that used to be talked about like they were ours.

We used to pop down Farrant Street where Enoki Coffee & Co held court at 20 Farrant Street — mint green interiors and a strictly vego menu, as The Urban List once told us. Over on Mawson Street The Colour Nine was already being crowned “Stafford’s new cafe hangout” (2/85, 85 Mawson Street — pick your numbering). If we wanted to pretend we were in Naples we’d point our cars at Wilgarning Street and order a wood-fired pizza from Prova Pizzeria at 61 Wilgarning Street, the one Time Out later fussed over for its eye-catching lasagna and adventurous pies. For a Sunday roast that demanded respect, Rode Meats at 269 Appleby Road was the family-owned vote of confidence — family butchers since the ’80s, apparently.

Petty local drama? As Concrete Playground reported, the Stafford Skate Centre on 280 Stafford Road announced it was hanging up its skates after forty years when the building changed hands — a true northside soap opera that involved less shouting and more vinyl wheel recollections. Elsewhere our mums and tiny humans were getting organised: Mamma Collective ran 10-week Mother’s Groups, Stafford Heights State School P&C once hosted a movie night for The Bad Guys 2, and there were even crochet classes for kids at Verrano Cafe — yes, we have a crochet pedigree.

Who would hate living here? Nightlife hunters who want velvet ropes and midnight taxis; people who need a skyline view and a never-ending playlist of city chaos. Who’d love it? Folks who want community that actually shows up — the P&C committee types, the Saturday class regulars, and anyone who appreciates a decent pizza and a butcher that remembers your name.

For years the local listings told our story. We’re small, a touch domestic, and stubbornly charming — and we wouldn’t have it any other way.
