# Elanora (QLD)

Slug: `elanora-qld`

Back in the day we’d wake up, stretch, and face the very serious decision Elanora forces on you: kayak the wetlands or pop into The Pines for dessert before noon. The Elanora Wetlands Reserve — a proper 20‑hectare ribbon of water that brushes the upper reaches of Tallebudgera Creek and links into the Burleigh to Springbrook wildlife corridor — makes us look outdoorsy even if we only paddle for the photos.

Our weekend ritual used to involve a lap of The Pines Shopping Centre: grab something from the Woolworths on the corner at Guineas Creek Road & K P McGrath Drive, say hello to whatever had landed most recently (we watched Melbourne’s YOMG open there like a proud but confused aunt), and maybe pick up flowers from Perrotts Florist — listed at Shop 13a in The Pines and previously known as Skeleton Leaf — because nothing says suburban glamour like a posy next to the frozen peas.

We’ve had nice neighbours in the print world too. Time Out noted the team behind Frenchies Brasserie had a spot in Elanora before they decamped elsewhere; we like to tell visitors it’s proof we can do bistro vibes when required.

Who would hate living here? People who think a ‘day out’ means continental flights and Michelin stars. If you need novelty every weekend, Elanora will bore you with its very steady offerings: the shopping centre, the local library and public transport links, and those property listings that promise an “absolute waterfront position” while nudging you gently toward Palm Beach Currumbin High school catchment. We’re unapologetically suburban like that.

Who will love it? Families who want practical things — an Elanora Primary School stroll, easy access to parks and reserves, and neighbours who know where the good kayak launch is. Localsearch used to remind people there are 34 things to do locally, and yes, that includes the nearby David Fleay Wildlife Park if you’re angling for educational outings that aren’t Zoo‑level drama.

So come for the wetlands, stay for the shopping centre rhythm and the florist that remembers your birthday bouquet. We’ll be here, pretending to be very rugged as we sip something frosty we bought from The Pines and call it culture.
