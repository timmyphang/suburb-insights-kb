# Woombye (QLD)

Slug: `woombye-qld`

We roll out of our drive like it’s a ritual — past paddocks that still remember pineapples, past the Big Pineapple looming on Nambour Connection Road like a fruity lighthouse. For years the Big Pineapple was our go-to bit of kitsch, and when it reopened it came back all shiny: a new cafe, a viewing platform and a playground, and Wildlife HQ moved in so you could cuddle a koala and pretend you’re living your best tourist brochure life.

Mornings used to be simple: a squiz at Frankie's Woombye — Rebecca Galler’s little victory lap for the hinterland — which was voted one of QLD’s most popular cafes in 2024 and turned rural coffee into a local badge of honour. If we weren’t queueing there, we were daydreaming about slipping off to Sixty6 Acres, the luxe farm-stay the listings loved, set on a working farm with freestanding tubs and views over horse paddocks. Or, for the papered romantics among us, Starry Nights in West Woombye was the glamping option roundup writers bookmarked.

The local calendar always had something wholesome on it: Woombye Festival (the old Happy Days) with Whisker Wars and classic cars, or the Woombye Snakes Family Fun Day promising giant games and three-legged races. And yes, someone once proposed a $10 million Sixty6 Acres development on Diddillibah Road — 20 cabins, 19 glamping tents, 49 caravan and RV sites and 44 campsites — which sounded like a plan until the rest of us realised it involved spreadsheets and council approvals.

Who’d hate living here? People who despise a good pineapple-shaped landmark, festival crowds on Pineapple Fields, or the smug calm of a treetop challenge when you want your suburban chaos. Also, anyone allergic to early learning centres: for years the suburb’s childcare listings included Woombye Child Care Centre on Blackall Street and places like Indigo ELC and Little Characters on Schubert Rd — the kind of detail parents use to justify moving out of the city.

Who’d love it? Families, festival-goers, farm-stay romantics and anyone who thinks a giant fruit makes a perfectly reasonable centrepiece for a town square. Back in the day, we thought Woombye was quietly perfect; the reporters just finally started to notice.
