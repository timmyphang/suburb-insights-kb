# Morningside (QLD)

Slug: `morningside-qld`

Back in the day — and by that we mean whatever era Broadsheet, Time Out and Urban List were nosing around — Morningside felt like a surprise packet you didn’t know you’d ordered but secretly loved. We’d start the weekend with a solemn pilgrimage to The Fish Factory on Lytton Road, because Time Out and Broadsheet told us chefs did it for their fish’n’chips and who are we to argue with people who eat for a living.

After that ritual, there’s always the Rivermakers vibe: The Hills of Rivermakers at 82 Colmslie Road used to be an industrial relic turned riverside playground — markets with 150+ stalls (Concrete Playground), Revel Brewing Co in a converted 100‑year‑old building (Urban List), and enough outdoor seating to host your extended family and their opinions. Keeping Mum and Modern Mamas ran mothers groups and first-aid courses at The Hills and 188 Thynne Rd, so by midday the place was equal parts craft beer and car seat instruction.

We’ve also got the niche stuff that makes us proud and slightly smug. Southside Tea Room on Wynnum Rd got the kitschy write‑ups (Broadsheet, Time Out), Flour and Chocolate earned pastry pats on the back (Time Out), and Kasina brought Balkan share plates for those of us who like our dinner with a side of culture.

If you hate toddlers, artisan stalls, and breweries with heritage bones, you’ll loathe Morningside. If you expect a non‑stop nightclub strip, you’ll be disappointed — this is more market‑by‑day, beer‑garden‑by‑evening, and swim class‑by‑weekend. Colmslie Aquatic Centre used to boast a 50m outdoor heated pool, an 18.5m indoor pool and a shallow water play park that made summers bearable for small people (Brisbane Kids). Bring sunscreen and patience.

Who loves it here? Families, food obsessives, and anyone who appreciates a riverside warehouse turned social centre. We’re gloriously suburban but not boring: fishmongers that supply top restaurants, bakeries that earn Time Out nods, and a precinct that hosts chocolate festivals and markets. So come for the fish’n’chips, stay for the beer garden, and remember — we already knew this place was good long before it got its profile pages.
