# Eagleby (QLD)

Slug: `eagleby-qld`

We wake up in Eagleby to the mercifully uncomplicated knowledge that somewhere down at Distillery Road Market someone’s already fired up the DJ and the day is pretending to be a weekend. We are the kind of locals who’ll mosey down for a wander — partly to check the river, partly for the vibe — because Urban List’s 2026 guide still gives Distillery Road Market a wink on its best-markets list and, frankly, we like being right.

Weekends here are a low-key production. The market precinct has that rep: the converted shipping container feel that once housed Sugar Creek Smokehouse doing brisket and ribs, and Perentie Brewing Co. was listed at 24 Distillery Road with a beer garden overlooking the Albert River. The F.E.D was a thing too — launched by Distillery Road Market and Zarraffa’s Coffee founder Kenton Campbell — so if you’re into precinct lore, we’ve got material. The old Beenleigh Rum setup at 142 Distillery Road even used to host a proper restaurant on the riverbank in its heritage-listed days; we remember the photos, if not the table bookings.

Petty local drama? We don’t do suburb-wide soap operas — nature provides. Ex-Cyclone Alfred’s heavy rain and the emergency flood warnings that followed (yes, Pedestrian TV ran it) are a yearly reminder the Albert River has an opinion on our landscaping choices. We grumble, move the bins, then get back to brunching on hypothetical smoked brisket.

Who would hate living here? If you can’t handle the occasional country-music headline — hello, the Lasso Country Music Festival booked into Distillery Road Market with Drake Milligan on the bill (Sitchu, July 11) — or you’re allergic to businesses that started life in shipping containers or heritage distillery bricks, Eagleby will test your patience.

Who loves it? People who want a postcode that will surprise your mates on a Saturday: improv beer gardens, market-driven weekends, a proper slice of rum-distillery history, and events that lean into country charm. We’ll keep showing up, rain-warning or not — mostly because someone has to tell the river it’s overreacting.
