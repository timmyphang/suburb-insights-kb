# Corinda (QLD)

Slug: `corinda-qld`

Back in the day our Saturdays started on Oxley Road like a ritual: we’d stroll past the spot where Same Same But Different first opened — chef Kym Machin’s shiny new Corinda outpost that Broadsheet wrote about — and argue about whether shared lunch dishes were meant to be shared. Nearby, Urban List once flagged Noodle Asia at 595 Oxley Rd as the go-to for gluten-free locals, so we kept one eye on the menu and the other on passing neighbours.

When summer hit, we all knew how to spend $2: Dunlop Park Memorial Swimming Pool made the Concrete Playground lists during Brisbane City Council’s $2 Summer Dips (that run was marked Dec 1, 2024–Feb 28, 2025 on the event pages), and yes, attendance numbers justified the chaos of sunscreen and sunhats. For a while the pool was less a recreational facility and more a community thermostat.

Our local gossip never needed invented drama. Once, a pack of teens hunting for Pokémon wandered into a local church during a funeral and were politely shepherded out by the organist — Pedestrian covered the awkward scene and we all nodded like it was a Tuesday. And if you like your infrastructure with a side of slapstick, our nearby rail bridge has been on a mission to collect overambitious trucks, sparking a strange feud with the Montague St Bridge that the internet enjoyed mocking.

We’ve got a soft spot for community connectors too: the Social Mums Club lists local groups and meet-ups, and anyone who’s ever popped into a mothers’ group knows Corinda does practical neighbourliness better than it does pretense.

Who wouldn’t love living here? People who want a suburb that still remembers its openings, its cheap-summer-swim rituals and the odd headline about teens with a phone app. Who would hate it? If you require nonstop nightlife, boutique-lane self-importance or an avoidance of slightly mortifying local stories, you’ll find Corinda quaintly unforgiving.

We’re proud of that. We like our cafes, our pool lanes and our capacity to turn a teeny local incident into a story we’ll tell for years — politely, from a safe distance.
