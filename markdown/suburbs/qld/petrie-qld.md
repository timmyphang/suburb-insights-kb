# Petrie (QLD)

Slug: `petrie-qld`

We’re Petrie, QLD — yes, the Petrie your GPS forgets when you meant Petrie Terrace, but we get the mix-up. Back in the day the internet liked to gossip about our cousin on Caxton Street — Lefty’s Music Hall, Velveteen Rabbit’s hidden speakeasy and The Barracks precinct with its parade of restaurants — and we don’t mind the attention. We’ve got Old Petrie Town, 48 acres of nostalgia where you can pretend time forgot to install a shopping centre, and a history that makes your weekend walk feel like a low-effort period drama.

Our Saturday routine used to look a bit like this: a nostalgic wander through Old Petrie Town’s gardens, followed (for those who accidentally detour into the city) by being shepherded by mates down Caxton Street past places that used to be a big deal — Lefty’s with its chandeliers and whisky apples, Caxton Street Brewing for trivia nights, and The Barracks, which Broadsheet used to write about when Tellus, Nativo and other eateries set up shop. Concrete Playground once ran excited copy about Krabby’s Crab Boil landing at 61 Petrie Terrace and Fat Belly Jack’s taking over 48 Petrie Terrace — those were the headlines we forwarded to cousins who still think we only have roundabouts.

We’ll be honest: if you despise chandeliers, southern-style bar food or hidden speakeasies, Petrie Terrace-life was never going to be your cup of anything. If you loathe stadium crowds, you’ll sigh every time Suncorp Stadium pushes half the city down Caxton Street for a game. Equally, if nostalgia makes your heart beat faster, and you love a retro bar, live music or a comedy festival that once took over multiple venues in October 2024, you’ll fit right in.

Who do we love here? People who appreciate old timber, honky-tonk vibes and the kind of streets where history is more than a plaque. We’re fond of being the underrated cousin — an easy train ride from the fuss, with Old Petrie Town’s quiet charm and a nightlife legacy that used to keep Caxton Street humming. Come for the history, stay for the stories we’ll reluctantly admit are better than our postcode’s modesty.
