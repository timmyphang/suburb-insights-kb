# Moorooka (QLD)

Slug: `moorooka-qld`

Saturday morning in Moorooka used to be a very particular kind of loaf — the kind that involved a laneway, a paper bag of pastries and the suspicion you’d accidentally wandered into someone’s living room. Back in the day we’d duck down that lane to No. 7 Coffee, the “hidden gem” that loved jazz and used to come alive on Friday nights with music and an ever-changing Indian menu. It felt like our tiny, slightly mysterious cultural embassy.

Weekends meant choices that confused our sensible city friends: Ho Choi — the secret they whisper about when they want cheap, solid Chinese — or a plate at Ethiopian Village on 147 Beaudesert Road (or the Ethio-African Restaurant & Injera Bakery at Shop 1, 131 Beaudesert Road) for those who liked to share a mountain of stews and discover what everyone else had been eating for years. Todd & Pup had just moved in with a shiny fit-out and a breakfast menu that included eggs benedict, vege stack and truffle eggs, and yes, they were dog friendly — the classy kind, not the kind that brings a script.

We also used to have large indoor plant markets: Jungle Collective’s Jungle Boogie at 11A/25 Michlin Street was the thing in 2019 — fiddle leaf figs, birds of paradise, ferns and palms, ticketed entry and a minimum $10 spend, because retail therapy needs a gate fee. Meanwhile, cultural planners listed Moorooka as a spot for Brisbane Serenades block parties — we were on the map for free outdoor concerts featuring acts like Bustamento and Jungaji, which rarely happens to suburbs whose main claim to fame is a good secondhand shop.

Scandal? Barely. The clippings are more into food, plants and public transport plans than soap-opera-level drama. We were even named among the above-ground stations set for upgrades when Cross River Rail was due in 2025 — glamorous stuff for people who get excited about timetables.

Who would hate living here? If you need a daily dose of Very On-Brand city nightlife or a constant stream of trendbait, Moorooka will feel like a weekday. Who would love it? Plant hoarders, people who eat with their elbows, anyone who likes their cultural adventures mixed with sensible train news and a lane-way coffee that remembers your name (or at least your order). We’re not flashy. We’re ours.
