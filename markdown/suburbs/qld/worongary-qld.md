# Worongary (QLD)

Slug: `worongary-qld`

We’ll admit it — we’ve spent many a sleepy Saturday trawling Worongary like it’s our local rom-com set. Back in the day we used to saunter to Le Vintage Boutique Café at 1 Kurilpa St for the kind of timeless charm Time Out rhapsodised about, and Urban List even noted it was pet-friendly (yes, your dog could come and judge your brunch). It felt like a place that quietly collected locals’ best excuses for late starts.

For picnic afternoons we’d head to Lorikeet Park in the Skyridge estate (Skyridge Blvd) because, as Urban List suggested, it’s genuinely pleasant — proper grass, playgrounds and the kind of suburban calm that makes you forget you have emails. Then December rolled around and the neighbourhood would briefly become a festive suburban rave: 405 Hinkler Drive made Urban List’s Christmas lights roundup, and we’d drive past with the windows down, pretending to be moved.

If you wanted curry without a fuss there used to be an Indian restaurant with locations that included Worongary alongside Robina, Varsity Lakes and Southport — handy for when nothing in the pantry looked like effort.

Parents will remember Kids World Gilston at 590 Worongary Road, Gilston (they even ran Open Day and Markets) — their weekday rhythm read like suburban clockwork (the contact page used to list opening hours as Monday–Friday 6:15AM–6:15PM). Practical, comforting, and a reminder that this place is full of routines people actually rely on.

We should point out: all of this is more “for years the locals said” than “right now” — most of our sources are from earlier guides, so think of this as nostalgia, not a postcode Yelp.

Who would hate it here? If you need 24-hour nightlife, a food truck on every corner or a city skyline out the window, Worongary will test your patience. Who would love it? Families, dog people, picnic conspirators, and anyone who appreciates a suburb that quietly collects its own little charms — the café that felt like home, the park that becomes your weekend backyard, and the house on Hinkler Drive that treats December like a performance. We’ll keep the lights on.
