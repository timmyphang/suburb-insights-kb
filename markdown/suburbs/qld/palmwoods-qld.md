# Palmwoods (QLD)

Slug: `palmwoods-qld`

Back in the day we’d wake up in Palmwoods like we owned the slow lane of the Sunshine Coast hinterland — slippers on, sunscreen half-applied, pretending we weren’t about to be late for nothing. Weekend mornings used to be the kind of business the guidebooks liked: Homegrown on Main Street was written up as masters of the ravioli-and-winter-soup vibe (Shop 4, 6 Main Street, dog-friendly with outdoor seating, and Tin Shed Coffee in tow, if you were feeling fancy). Later, a day-trip itinerary from Maleny and Montville would send people down the hill through Hunchy to our little town and point them toward Rick’s Garage — the OTT American diner that UrbanList celebrated for big burgers, shakes, a memorabilia‑filled interior, a large outdoor beer garden and even an upstairs whiskey bar. Big denim jackets not included.

We know Palmwoods has always been a place for people escaping things: Broadsheet and UrbanList featured ‘Into the Woods’, a one‑bedroom cottage on a lush 6.5‑acre former pineapple farm with an outdoor bath, wood‑fired pizza oven and firepit — basically romance with a wifi password. Kookaburra Cabin was touted as having Kondalilla National Park and our train station “on its doorstep” for the tiny‑home crowd who wanted to stare at the sky instead of their phone.

We are proud of our grown‑up country pivot: Alsahwa Estate’s ēthos — planted on 30 acres at 20 Rifle Range Road — was written up as a Mediterranean farm‑to‑plate place (Sophie Phipps’ influence gets a name‑check in the food press), all landscaped gardens and produce you can brag about.

Who would hate it here? People who think “hinterland” means endless nightlife, or those who demand a CBD vibe and a 24/7 shopping mall would have sighed. And anyone allergic to farm‑fresh everything or the idea of a beer garden with elephant statues would file Palmwoods under “nice, but no.”

Who’d love it? Families looking at childcare options historically had choices — Palmwoods Early Learning Centre (1 Harris Dr), the Community Kindergarten on Churchill Street and Kidzco among them — and mums used to gather for Motivate Mums classes at Federation Park. For anyone wanting quiet charm, farm‑to‑table meals and an Airbnb with an outdoor bath, we were, and still secretly are, quietly smug about our slice of hinterland life.
