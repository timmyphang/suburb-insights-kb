# Calamvale (QLD)

Slug: `calamvale-qld`

Back in the day — and by “day” we mean the pile of older write‑ups we could find — Calamvale arrived like the sensible sibling of Brisbane suburbs: reliably useful, a bit earnest, and secretly keen on good food.

Our weekend looks like this: we used to gossip over Urban List write‑ups about Little Dreamer Cafe in Calamvale Shopping Centre (51 Kameruka St) and its cult two‑level breakfasts — savoury board crowned with a pile of waffles, because we do like choices. We’d nod knowingly about Burger Me (15/51 Kameruka Street) and its Black Angus patties, and sigh at the thought of Ru Yi at Calamvale Central for Cantonese barbecue and dumplings if we were feeling fancy. For dessert conspiracy theories there was Famous Mocktails (5/662 Compton Road) with its Dubai chocolate‑style housemade block. None of these mentions are fresh — they were features in older guides — but they stuck in our heads like a good dumpling.

If you have kids (or are secretly eight), Time Out once put Calamvale District Park on a shortlist: a 7.5‑metre Sky Walk with enclosed cabins, net bridges, an 11‑metre slide, a long double zipline, a Möbius climbing strip and a flying fox. It reads like a playground built by someone who loves swings and slightly terrifying engineering. There’s also a Saturday morning parkrun listed on Formby Street that used to gather the keen and the coffee‑deprived at 7:00am.

Our civic life gets a shoutout in directories: Calamvale Central Family Practice was listed inside Priceline Pharmacy at 662 Compton Road; the Calamvale Ward Office for Councillor Emily Kim appears at Shop 10, 168 Algester Rd. The volunteers who run the Calamvale Community site even promoted “Putting the unity back into community,” and mentioned locals like Elaine — small, earnest gestures that say we like to know our neighbours.

Who would hate living here? People who despise playgrounds, directories and practical mid‑suburban charm — the ones who want endless neon nightlife. Who would love it? Families, people who prize good yum cha, burger options and neighbourhood Christmas light pilgrimages (Golden Avenue, 52 Orania Crescent and 1 Pinecroft Close made the festive lists). We’re not flashy. We’re dependable. And honestly, that’s half the appeal.
