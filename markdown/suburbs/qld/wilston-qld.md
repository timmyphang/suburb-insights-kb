# Wilston (QLD)

Slug: `wilston-qld`

We’ll admit it: our idea of a big Saturday is juggling a loaf from Crust & Co while pretending the rest of Wilston doesn’t smell faintly of wood‑fired pizza. But full disclosure — most of what people write about us is a few years old, so consider this a fond look back at the Wilston that the magazines loved.

Back in the day Broadsheet and Time Out trailed us like paparazzi: Antica (Shop 1/70 Kedron Brook Rd) was the pizzeria that graduated from Kombi oven legend to proper trattoria, and Time Out even gave it national pizza plaudits. Crust & Co used to draw lines out the door (we’ll never apologise for queueing for Sebastien Pisasale’s croissants). Willow and Spoon had its retro-kitsch phase, complete with backyard seating and themed dinners, and Freja’s Cafe — before its corner became Clementine — used to tempt you with pear waffles and croissants stuffed with pulled brisket and macaroni ’n’ cheese. Meanwhile, Elska’s tiny 12‑seat degustation made the rounds after a month‑long refit and somehow amassed over 400 bookings — which, yes, we still brag about.

If you liked variety, we had it: Merge in the village pitched smoky yakitori and Mango Lychee Soda; Archer Brewing set its taps inside an old WWII hangar; Fruition Hair offered a slick salon with a solid team; Mumbleberry handled the gift‑shop curation for anyone who wanted to be seen buying local.

Who would hate living here? People who can’t stand queues for good bread, folks who want mega‑marts on every corner, and anyone who thinks 12 seats at a tasting menu is “not worth the drama.” Equally, we can see why some find us a touch smug — we do love to namecheck that Sitchu crowned us one of Brisbane’s most beautiful, family‑friendly suburbs with “open green surroundings” and a strong community heart.

Who would love it? Families, food obsessives, small‑room romantics and anyone who enjoys walking from bakery line to pizzeria without being judged. We’re compact, leafy and quietly proud — and even if the profiles are archival, our neighbourhood still behaves like the kind of place those stories were written about.
