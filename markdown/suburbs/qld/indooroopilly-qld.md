# Indooroopilly (QLD)

Slug: `indooroopilly-qld`

Back in the day we’d saunter out of the Indooroopilly train station like we owned the place — coffee in one hand, school lunch in the other, because Indro was the kind of suburb that folded your entire weekend into a single shopping-centre spreadsheet.

Our Saturday reads like a directory: we used to queue for NKB Express’s milkshakes and hot dogs, nip into Harajuku Gyoza for dumpling nights, and try not to argue over who gets the last Betty’s Burgers concrete — all inside the Indooroopilly Shopping Centre. The mall used to host everything from Brittney Lee Saunders’ free makeup pop-ups to Indro’s Santa World in David Jones on Level 2. Pop Mart opened in July 2023 if you remember your collectible timelines. And if pottery called your name, The Ceramic Studio at 4 & 6/8 Station Road was the local spot to prove you can, in fact, make a bowl.

We’ve watched councils promise shiny infrastructure too: the Riverwalk linking Twigg Street over to the Jack Pesch Bridge and the rail station was the sort of plan we cheered in 2020 and then checked every weekend like it was Christmas. We also survived the less glamorous headlines — in 2021 the shopping centre made the exposure-site list and we all learned the anatomy of contact-tracing alerts.

For the small-people crowd, Indooroopilly does family stuff very well. Kids on Clarence is literally opposite the station at 137–143 Clarence Road, and the area hugs Sir John Chandler Park at the end of Meiers Road — jacarandas, a pirate playground and the Indooroopilly Golf Club as classy background noise. The Indooroopilly State School Indigo Fair and the shopping-centre school-holiday activations used to make this place feel like a calendar you actually wanted to tick.

Who would hate it here? People allergic to convenience, pop-up events, and the intoxicating hum of mall-announcements. Who’d love it? Students, young professionals and families who like having everything within a short stroll — shopping, buses, trains, and a pub in the Station Road dining precinct that’s been doing its thing for years.

We know every corner of Indro — and yes, we’ll still meet you by the mall clock, even if the Riverwalk is still a council promise.
