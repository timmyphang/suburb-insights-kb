# Ascot (QLD)

Slug: `ascot-qld`

We know Ascot by the sight of a Range Rover Velar idling outside St Margaret’s with a Cavoodle wearing a silk bandana in the passenger seat. We wear linen blazers and popped collars the way others wear sunscreen: as theatre. We pull up on Racecourse Road, insist the Velar is “very practical” for school runs to St Margaret’s, and secretly buy our kombucha three-for-two at the Coles pantry sale. 

Our weekend choreography is immaculate. Saturday starts with a soy long black from the corner specialty café while the kids go to tennis. By lunch we’re in a Lululemon get-up, clutching a single-origin soy flat white with extra chocolate dust and smiling as if we’re “working through emails” while the children finish colouring-in at the café. By 2pm the linen blazer comes out for Eagle Farm races. Sunday is boutique grocer run, farmers market, and mowing the tiny but immaculate lawn to the beats of a Spotify playlist called “House on the River.”

Ascot’s local dramas are small and spectacular. Race day at Eagle Farm and Doomben turns our streets into a chess match. Temporary parking laws spawn three passive-aggressive Post-It notes and at least one neighbour-level restraining order between a Lexus driver and a stroller pusher. The Great Hedge Debate never dies: do you sculpt your privet into a Michelangelo topiary or let it live its wild-garden fantasy? Two petitions, one BBQ confrontation, and a very upset tradie later, and the timber Queenslander balustrades are still either safe or doomed, depending on which Facebook thread you started.

We keep a second SUV in the driveway “for guests.” We can explain the difference between Doomben and Eagle Farm in under thirty seconds. We have dogs with their own Instagram pages—well-groomed Labradors that pose on verandahs with artisanal chew toys. We also clutch our spreadsheets like rosaries and patrol the local Buy/Sell groups for garage-sale pearls.

Who would hate living here? A touring musician with a battered Suzuki Swift, tattoos, and a penchant for 3am drum practice would be a disaster. They’d offend unwritten dress-and-decibel codes, confuse race-day etiquette with actual racing knowledge, and treat the polished hedges like a personal drum kit — which will not end well at the next street BBQ.

Who would love it? The 38-year-old corporate project manager in Lululemon with a Tesla Model X, a personalised number plate, and a Labrador that has a better publicist than most politicians. They’ll adore the rhythm, the rituals, and the way every weekend feels like a slightly sunburnt version of Royal Ascot. Welcome to Ascot.
