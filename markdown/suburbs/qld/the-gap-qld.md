# The Gap (QLD)

Slug: `the-gap-qld`

Back in the day our weekend began like clockwork: we’d pile the smalls into the car and point the ute-ish thing towards Enoggera Reservoir at Walkabout Creek, because Walkabout Creek has been the thing we brag about since 1866 (Brisbane Kids reminded us it’s been part of the landscape a very long time). 

By mid-morning, Time Out once had us ticked as a farmers’ market suburb — The Gap Farmers’ Market was a listing that made us feel smug and mildly cultured. If the kids weren’t dragging sand into the car from Paten Park’s enormous sandpit (Urban List tells us Paten Park has a split-log activity beam and upgrades we secretly love), we’d be dodging tennis balls at The Gap Tennis Club precinct on 200 Settlement Road — or at least pretending we’d join, then heading to the picnic under the gums instead.

Urban List also whispered about Little Hoi An, “hidden like the precious gem it is,” supplying banh mi that tasted like civilisation had arrived on our doorstep — a fact we trot out when visitors ask why we don’t live closer to town. Sitchu estate listings humbly reminded everyone The Gap is under 13 km to the Brisbane CBD and that some Parkside Crescent houses back onto council green space, which is real estate copy for “we like trees more than traffic.”

For the small-people logistics, CareforKids once catalogued Curiosity Tree at 83-85 Payne Road (7am–6.30pm), The Gap Cubbyhouse Montessori on 177–181 Payne Road, and C&K The Gap Community Kindergarten at 1174–1180 Waterworks Road — which is to say we are very good at lining up childcare and pretending to be relaxed about it.

Who would hate living here? If you’re a nightclub migrator who needs trams at midnight, or someone allergic to sandpits and sensible school catchments (Hilder Road State School, The Gap State High School get name-checked in listings), this leafy cul-de-sac paradise will feel like being marooned in suburbia.

Who would love it here? Families who want easy bushwalks at Walkabout Creek, dog people who actually use Paten Park’s upgrades, and anyone who appreciates being “hidden like a precious gem” yet still mysteriously under 13 km from the CBD. We’ll see you at the reservoir — bring sunscreen and a story about the time Little Hoi An saved your lunch.
