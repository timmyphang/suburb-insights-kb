# Karrabin (QLD)

Slug: `karrabin-qld`

We live in Karrabin — an 11 km² shrug of Ipswich that somehow manages to feel both nowhere and right where you left your keys. Back in the day (and by that we mean the older community listings we’ve been clinging to), this place was a map of useful stuff and gentle ambitions: C&K - West Moreton Community Kindergarten was there for the tiny revolutionaries, Camp Australia used to run OSHC at West Moreton Anglican College on Keswick Road, and the Ipswich Pistol Club sat at 47 Keswick Road with its very public phone number for anyone oddly punctual about target practice.

Our mornings used to be logistics rather than aesthetics: drop the kids where the childcare listings said they could be squeezed in (57 services in Karrabin, apparently, with 39 of those listing vacancies and an average cost of $132.31/day — yes, the data is older, but the wallet math still stings). If something went skewiff, we knew the brass-plate route: Brassall Clinic, Walloon Medical Centre, Ipswich Hospital — all listed as the local medical lifelines when needed.

We like to imagine Karrabin as the place that politely refuses to be anything flashy. We’re bordered by Amberley, Blacksoil, Brassall, Ironbark, Walloon and Wulkuraka, which sounds like a bingo card of perfectly sensible neighbourhood names. We live in the City of Ipswich, vote in Ipswich West and send our federal hopes to Blair — the trifecta of civic pride and rate notices.

Who would hate living here? If you need rooftop bars, late-night kebab hunts or an entire personality built around being “seen” in the city, Karrabin will look like a page you accidentally scrolled past. We have room, routines and childcare listings that read like practical poetry.

Who would love it? Families who want space and sensible options, people who value the ordinary — the kindergarten that was, the OSHC that used to run the school gates, the community directory that lists every sensible service. We’re not trying to be Melbourne; we’re just being Karrabin, quietly doing the things older directories say we used to do — and doing them with local straight-faced pride.
