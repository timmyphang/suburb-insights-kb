# Fortitude Valley (QLD)

Slug: `fortitude-valley-qld`

Back in the day we could time a whole weekend by the Valley’s pulses: sunrise wandering James Street, pretending the Calile Hotel’s rooftop pool was a civic monument, then drifting down to Ada Lane for whatever the day demanded — shopping, murals, or a last-minute dinner that felt like civilisation.

We remember when the Brunswick Street Mall was our nervous system: live‑music flyers on every lamppost, Nine Lives Gallery Upstairs at 330 Brunswick Street Mall showing the kind of art that made us feel cultured between pints, and the Fortitude Music Hall looming over it all — that huge, 3,000‑plus room that made our town sound like a capital. Bigsound used to spill into venues like 256 Wickham and Ric’s Big Backyard, turning the whole precinct into a sweaty industry of hope.

The petty stuff? Classic Valley. Gerard’s Bistro — the one Johnny Moubarak ran for 14 years — announced it would close with a final service on July 4 after Moubarak sold the place; apparently an interstate hospitality team was coming in to remake the floorboards. The Crowbar crew, meanwhile, were lining up Never Enough at 690 Ann Street: a late‑night restaurant and record bar, all about seasonality and after‑hours records. Change has always been our constant.

Who would hate living here: anyone who needs their evenings silent and their Saturdays quiet. If you loathe rooftop bars, nightclub-sized music halls, neon mini‑golf in a converted 1885 church, or spontaneous street parties, the Valley will not be your friend.

Who would love it: people who want to be in the middle of an all‑ages festival one week and an art opening the next — Valley Fiesta and the Moon Festival have long turned Brunswick Street into a carnival, and the Brisbane Powerhouse/Fortitude Music Hall circuit still brings kids’ shows and chaos. Food and drink obsessives and live‑music devotees will recognise this place as a messy, thrilling patchwork of institutions, experiments and inevitable turnovers.

Yes, we know what they say about us. They also missed the bit where the Valley keeps reinventing itself and somehow still feels like home.
