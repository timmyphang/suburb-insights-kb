# Geebung (QLD)

Slug: `geebung-qld`

Back in the day we used to tell people Geebung was the north-side’s secret weapon — low-key, practical and oddly proud of it. For years the local press agreed: Fox Coffee was the little roastery-café that grew out of a “rough‑around‑the‑edges warehouse in Geebung” (Concrete Playground), Roasting Warehouse Specialty Coffee on Newman Road was sneaking Mont Blancs into the suburbs, and Nutri Hitt on Newman Road was winning praise for the healthy cheesecake options Urban List couldn't resist.

Our weekend looks the same in every write‑up: a spin through 7th Brigade Park for the kids or the dog — Time Out even trumpeted it as one of the north side’s big green lungs, five playgrounds and acres of picnic shade — then a detour to whichever coffee roastery the articles were fawning over. Broadsheet once pointed out Little Nyonya had a spot at the Geebung Bowls Club, so Saturday lunch felt like winning: curry laksa or char kway teow after chasing a ball on the green. Concrete Playground remembered Fox Coffee’s decade of roasting and the way locals — families, cyclists, dog‑walkers — used to flock to that corner of Ellison Road.

Our peak local spectacle, as the features kept proving, was the small, stubborn collection of proper businesses: a drive‑thru called Taste My Bean on Robinson Road East that opened shockingly early, Miss Savage’s formal boutique on Morrisby Street for the year‑12 crowd, and the odd brewery pop‑up — remember Green Beacon’s one‑day Geebung event with tours and pints? It was the sort of place where the most heated argument was over which roastery did the better seasonal special.

Who would hate Geebung? If you need a skyline terrace every weekend, Michelin pretension in walking distance or a non‑stop nightlife calendar, we’ll politely wave you across the motorway. We’re not pretending to be a glossy inner‑city playground.

Who would love it here? Families who want decent schools (Geebung State School, small and solid, was a local staple), people who rate big parks over bottle service, and anyone who appreciates a bowls club that doubles as a source of excellent Malaysian classics. We wear our modesty like a good sunscreen — unflashy, reliable and, somehow, exactly right.
