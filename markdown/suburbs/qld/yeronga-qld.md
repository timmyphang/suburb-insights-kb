# Yeronga (QLD)

Slug: `yeronga-qld`

We always say Yeronga is the kind of suburb that smells faintly of river mud and yesterday’s pastry — and we mean that as a compliment. Back in the day we’d start a weekend drift under the jacarandas at Jacaranda Park, which Urban List cheerfully promises will “brighten your day” with purple plumes. Coffee came from the old haunts: Steve Drew opened Evo Espresso in 2007 and moved it to a bigger courtyard in 2012, and Nessun Dorma used to roast beans in‑store — they even had a rotating single‑origin called ‘Eduardo’, if you can believe it. Those were our small pleasures.

We’d stroll the Brisbane Corso toward John Walker Place — Brisbane Kids praised its river views and the small conveniences (toilets, some fitness gear, “coffee nearby”) — and occasionally pretend we were training for a scenic run along the river and over the Eleanor Schonell Bridge, because Concrete Playground once told us that was a thing people do.

Yeronga has always been quietly aspirational. Time Out tucked Osteria Epoca into the bend of the river at 17 Orontes Rd, and Seven South famously opened at 92 Hyde Road in 2016 to replace The Hyde Out — Urban List went ga-ga for its hotcakes and smoky ribs. Golly Gosh, per Urban List, used to be the go‑to for anyone with a sweet tooth who needed their waffles to be medically decadent. Meanwhile Yeronga Bakery & Cafe earned points for asking if we wanted mushy peas on our pies — someone’s got their priorities straight.

Our community centre grew out of flood recovery work — Community Plus lists the Yeronga Community Centre at 62 Park Rd (entrance via Villa St) and a multipurpose room with a 60" TV and wheelchair access; practical, unflashy, useful. The Yeronga Craft Beer and Food Festival used to bring Sate House, Food Mood and Shanks‑a‑Lot to the park, plus live music and a jumping castle for the littluns, according to Concrete Playground.

Who would hate it here? If you need a skyline view from your espresso machine, you’ll grumble. Who would love it? Families who like river walks, four outdoor pools at Yeronga Park Memorial Swimming Pool (Broadsheet mentioned it in the summer dips round‑up), local festivals and cafes with history. We know every corner — even the spots that only the locals miss when they’re gone.
