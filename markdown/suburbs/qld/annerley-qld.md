# Annerley (QLD)

Slug: `annerley-qld`

Back in the day — and by “back in the day” we mean the stack of older write‑ups that is basically our internet memoir — Annerley was the kind of suburb reviewers liked to trot out when they wanted “authentic” without the elbowing. We know the scene: we’d amble down Ipswich Road past Vintage Revival at 468 Ipswich Rd and the Annerley Arcade hoard of retro toys, then duck into a cafe for something that sounded exotic in a directory listing.

Broadsheet once praised rich Ethiopian beef and fluffy injera, and flagged charcoal‑grilled chicken banh mi and golden tostadas as the sort of bargain treasures you could find around here. Time Out and Concrete Playground wrote fondly about Cafe O‑Mai (15 Cracknell Road) — yes, the pho brewed for 48 hours and the Vietnamese affogato that made reviewers nod — and Ben O’Donoghue’s Billykart Kitchen cropped up everywhere as that chef‑owned spot that put Annerley on the foodie map.

We used to brag that you could spend an afternoon in the Arcade (Decades Past, Shop 17 at 478 Ipswich Road gets a mention) rummaging for comics and Pokemon tins, then pop into VEND Marketplace at 289 Ipswich Road for a pet‑friendly wander. For those who liked structured hobbies, Dance Culture at 474 Ipswich Road was the place for Latin lessons; families filed into Junction Park Primary playgroups and the Annerley Library at 450 Ipswich Road for storytime. Local weird‑and‑wonderful businesses like Annerley Aquarium on Old Cleveland Road showed up in the listings, too.

If you hate second‑hand shops, busy markets, or the idea of a neighbourhood that proudly keeps a midnight chemist (Annerley Midnight Chempro, 548 Ipswich Road) on its strip, then honestly, you’d hate Annerley. If you like cheap, surprising food finds, community fetes (we’re looking at you, Annerley Junction Fest 2024: FIESTA), and a main drag with more character than a glossy mall, you’ll feel oddly at home.

We don’t have a stack of fresh reviews to point to — these are older snapshots — but the vibe they captured still holds: small‑scale, slightly eccentric, and stubbornly local. Come for the thrifted treasures, stay because you accidentally signed up for a mums’ group at VEND and never quite left.
