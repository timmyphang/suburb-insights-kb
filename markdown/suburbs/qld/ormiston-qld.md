# Ormiston (QLD)

Slug: `ormiston-qld`

Back in the day, we’d while away a Sunday on the verandah of Ormiston House — the old Captain Louis Hope place from 1862 at 277 Wellington Street — pretending we were on a genteel movie set while the bay did all the heavy lifting. As Concrete Playground noted, the house used to serve Devonshire tea on the verandah each Sunday (noon–4pm), surrounded by 14 acres of gardens and those very particular Moreton Bay views that make you speak softer for no good reason.

Our weekend ritual was deceptively simple: stagger out to the gardens, sniff the roses like we were doing horticulture, then accept jam and scones like a solemn civic duty. The place has that weirdly civilised energy — you half-expect someone to produce a silver tea strainer and a long-winded letter to the council. We loved it. We still talk about it. The articles are older, sure, so consider this a fond postcard rather than an up-to-the-minute events guide.

Local drama? The paperwork on that’s slim — which is fortunate, because if our biggest scandal is whether to sit north or south of the verandah, we’re living very well. The records we have prefer gardens and history to he-said-she-said. That’s fine by us; history beats hysteria any day.

Who would hate living here? If you despise verandahs, heritage homes, and gardens big enough to get lost in while you’re pretending you’re on a picnic, then no, Ormiston is not for you. If you require neon signs, thumping nightlife, or a caffeine-fueled corner cafe every 200 metres — fair go, you’ll be restless.

Who would love it? People who like a Sunday that resembles a sepia photograph, who cherish trees, sea air and a sense that someone once took landscape seriously. If a house with 14 acres and a verandah overlooking Moreton Bay sounds like a good idea, well, that’s us — and we’ll happily sip whatever the past offered, even if the listings that told us about it are from yesteryear.
