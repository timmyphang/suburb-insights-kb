# Dutton Park (QLD)

Slug: `dutton-park-qld`

Back in the day we’d stagger out under a jacaranda canopy to find the kind of breakfast the internet would write sonnets about. Dutton Park’s mornings used to look like a travel guide: the purple confetti at Dutton Park Cemetery, the small army of parents shepherding kids to the Butterfly Magic Playspace in Harmony Gardens, and the aroma of coffee drifting from Annerley Road.

We’d start at Penny Coffee Co — yeah, the neat corner spot at 109 Annerley Road with subway tiles and that Shani Finch mural — and then wander across to the old photo‑shop that became Nos Bakehouse, now famous (in print, anyway) for its shokupan sandos and souffle‑style French toast. Little G used to be a local pizza whisperer on Gladstone Road before it moved on to Woolloongabba; we still talk about those tiny pies like it was a family heirloom.

Weekends were for Boggo Road Markets at the Old Boggo Road Jail on Annerley Road, because nothing says suburbia like picking herbs beside a heritage prison. The papers preferred to catalogue our cafes and market stalls over scandal; Concrete Playground even mentioned Cross River Rail plans that would one day tinker with Dutton Park station — exciting for people who enjoy infrastructure as theatre.

We’re quietly civic-minded too: the Romero Centre at 8 Dutton Street and the Leukaemia Foundation’s ESA Village at 41 Peter Doherty Street show the suburb’s softer side. There’s also the Eleanor Schonell Bridge — the Green Bridge — that links us to UQ’s St Lucia, so yes, academia and bicycle traffic are part of our charm.

Who would hate living here? If you need a sprawling postcode, hate jacarandas, or can’t bear the idea of a bakery that used to be a photo shop and a market next to a heritage site, Dutton Park will test your patience. Also: if you genuinely despise pockets of small‑town pride disguised as inner‑city convenience, move along.

Who would love it? Families who adore the Butterfly Magic Playspace, food nerds nostalgic for Penny Coffee Co and Nos Bakehouse, market devotees who queue at Boggo Road Markets on Sundays, and anyone who likes living where community services, university culture and surprising cafes quietly rub shoulders. Postcode 4102 — population about 1,471 — modest, purple‑bloomed and unpretentiously ours.
