# Clayfield (QLD)

Slug: `clayfield-qld`

We know the moment: it’s a Saturday and we’re ambling down Sandgate Road arguing about whether a coffee should ever be frozen. Back in the day Broadsheet made us say, “I’m going to My Mistress,” and sure enough Natalie Vostok and Rhiannon Redmond’s cosy, joking little cafe used to feel like a grown‑up hiding spot from the traffic. Nearby, Kim Peabody and Shona Brice’s Greens & Grains opened a sister shop on the same strip (Broadsheet again) and we quietly judged each other’s pastry choices while pretending salads were dinner.

If you liked novelty, Peter and Penny Wolff’s Queen of Pops delivered coffee as an actual popsicle — an idea so Queensland it begged to be both admired and photographed. For those who preferred something heartier, Urban List and Sitchu were insistent about Clayfield Seafood Market (Shop 5, 823 Sandgate Road) doing proper fish-and-chips that weren’t trying too hard. Harris Farm’s Clayfield arrival got a shout for the cheese selection. Sakana, Mamaku and Wooden Horse all cropped up in the guides too — Time Out even listed Wooden Horse at 278 Junction Rd as a local bistro that once felt like ours.

We’ve got culture tucked between the CBD and the airport, and Time Out’s museum crawl remembered the oddest local gem: the Queensland Telecommunications (Telstra) Museum, full of telegrams and rotary phones for the nostalgics among us. Sitchu liked to remind newcomers that Kalinga Parklands and Kedron Brook make hiding from Sandgate Road’s rush embarrassingly easy.

Petty local scandal? None of the write-ups gave us juicy feuds — just a comfortable sameness of cafes, a BYO Indonesian dinner or two, and mums’ groups. Mummas Circle and MumCo meetups at Clay Church were doing their thing, which means our Saturdays often tilt toward playmats and polite coffee chit‑chat.

Who would hate it here: anyone who wants nonstop nightlife, edgy street art or a place that never has a school run in sight. Who would love it: families, nostalgia buffs, people who enjoy arguing about the right way to eat a popsicle coffee, and anyone who thinks a decent fish-and-chip shop is peak suburban romance. Back in the day, Clayfield wore all that with a grin — and we still pretend it does.
