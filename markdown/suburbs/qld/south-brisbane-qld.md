# South Brisbane (QLD)

Slug: `south-brisbane-qld`

We start our Saturday where everyone else pretends they stumbled upon the city’s best idea: Streets Beach. Sun on Clem Jones Promenade, kids charging the Aquativity, and us pretending the Riverside Green playground’s Sky Walk wasn’t making us feel ancient. Time Out used to call it a riverside paradise; we’ll take that, sunscreen and all.

Back in the day we’d stroll Fish Lane like it was a pilgrimage — a laneway full of elbow-to-elbow charm, Marlowe’s art-deco glamour rubbing shoulders with Bar Rosa and the three-storey antics of Southside (salt-and-pepper cuttlefish sangas and Moreton Bay bug char kway teow, Broadsheet helpfully reminds us). Brooki Bakehouse even went big on the Southside; Fish Lane’s never been short of a pastry to explain away as “breakfast.”

Local drama? Oh, we’ve had a carnival of it. The Fox Hotel went full soap opera after the 2022 floods and was earmarked by the Ghanem Group to reopen — rumours promised a multi-level rebirth (late 2025 was the date people bandied about). And who can forget the Night Noodle Markets’ most memorable Michelin-style critique: Pedestrian’s cockroach-in-buns exposé — the kind of culinary plot twist that keeps the precinct’s PR folks gainfully employed.

Who would hate living here? People who crave silence at 6pm, for one. Southbank Beer Garden’s river views and family-friendly vibe (Time Out notes it’s a favourite for families and dog owners) mean there’s always someone with sticky fingers or a folding chair setting their life priorities to “picnic.” Also, if small cocktail bars don’t do it for you, try squeezing into Maker on Fish Lane — Time Out’s tiny 20-seat bar — and re-evaluating your life choices.

Who would love it here? Culture nerds and food nerds, obviously. GOMA and QPAC sit like cultural bouncers at the precinct’s doorstep (Sitchu and Broadsheet nod to the cultural precinct), while OTTO, River Quay and the roster of Ma Pa Me, Popolo and Calida Argentinian give us reasons to book before a show (Urban List’s practical advice, thank you). Basically: if you like art, theatre, long meals and the occasional cockroach mystery, South Brisbane has been doing its thing for years — gloriously loud, hugely hospitable and always ready for another lane-way renaissance.
