# Coolum Beach (QLD)

Slug: `coolum-beach-qld`

Back in the day — or at least according to every travel roundup that stopped by long enough to Instagram a volcanic rock — we wake up in Coolum Beach the same way: the carpark at Mount Coolum is half-full, someone’s already lacing up for the 1.6km grunt to the summit, and the rest of us are pretending our morning is a “gentle coastal reset.” Time Out told the world to stay here for “laid-back coastal vibes,” so naturally we’ve kept the chill quota full.

Mornings mean a dip off the patrolled surf (the caravan park crowd have always appreciated the direct access), then propping ourselves up for a feed — Urban List once ran through the local restaurants and even previewed Rosa Italian as our Amalfi coast cameo — and Sitchu named Canteen Kitchen + Bar, Coolum Surf Club and Coolum Beach Hotel among the post-swim options. We’ll admit: we like a place that lets sand dry in the seats while you choose between boardwalk views and volcanic cliff selfies.

Local drama? Oh, we’ve had our mini soap. Junkee once rolled into Palmersaurus — Clive Palmer’s palace of golfing nostalgia — and declared it “might just be the saddest place on earth,” complete with Jeff the T‑Rex on the ninth and a Courier‑Mail‑credited rumour that the resort was bleeding half a million a month. Meanwhile Concrete Playground wrote about a future Kelly Slater surf ranch coming to Coolum, because why not add a bit of eco‑resort grandiosity to our sleepy surfer’s paradise?

Who would hate living here: people who need a tram every five minutes, shoppers who prefer a mall to a boardwalk, or anyone allergic to actual nature — the dune and heathland walks and the Coolum Boardwalk are not decorative. Also, if you can’t handle community movie nights in Tickle Park (yes, there was a Flicks in the Park screening of IF), this place will make you twitchy.

Who would love it: campers who know Coolum Beach Caravan Park is basically beachfront glamping, families who turn up for the new pump track at Lions and Norrie Job Park on David Low Way, and anyone who thinks climbing a 208m volcanic dome for sunrise is a sensible way to start the day. We’ll keep the sand, the surf, and the slightly dramatic resort history — you bring the sunscreen.
