# Enoggera (QLD)

Slug: `enoggera-qld`

We like to pretend our Saturdays begin with quiet dignity; in reality we’re the ones who get to the reservoir carpark three minutes late and declare it “full” like it’s a personality trait. Back in the day we'd wander from the Walkabout Creek Information Centre down to the heritage‑listed Enoggera Reservoir where the green, gently sloping banks meet cool water and someone inevitably says “should we kayak?” — because yes, Walkabout Creek Adventures used to hire canoes and paddleboats, and the carpark filled up fast.

Our morning ritual is equal parts trail and brag: a spin of the Enoggera Reservoir Circuit — Time Out named it one of Australia’s top hikes — or a lazy paddle on the SUPs that Urban List swears are perfect for wildlife spotting. If climbing is more your brand of petty competitiveness, remember 9 Degrees at 72 Pickering Street? The solar‑powered bouldering gym with about 120 routes and 430 sqm of climbing terrain. We used to measure friendships in who could top out without swearing.

For years the local retail therapy happened over on Enoggera Terrace in nearby Red Hill: Plum Tucker, Couverture & Co and Botanica Real Food — all those cosy directory entries that made us think we were very, very cultivated. Taya Kitchen on Shop 18, 254 South Pine Road even ran Thai cooking classes for people who liked to pretend they’d mastered a curry by Sunday.

Our biggest civic dispute? Not council rates — it’s who gets the last picnic spot at Bellbird Grove in D’Aguilar National Park, or whether the temporary closure of the hire hut (Concrete Playground, we saw you) was a sign from the universe. We’re small‑town petty in the best way: our stadium is a dam, our gala is the Reclink Community Cup at Enoggera Memorial Park (remember July 28, 2024?), and our locals will tell you the Enoggera Bikeway and Enoggera Creek make up for any pretension deficit.

Who would hate it here: anyone allergic to mud, hills or sharing a parking bay with actual humans. Who would love it: hikers, kayakers, climbers, commuters who count Enoggera Station as a blessing, and anyone who thinks a lakeside picnic beats a rooftop cocktail. We’re unpolished, outdoorsy and annoyingly proud of our reservoir. Come for the views, stay because you can’t bear to leave the loop.
