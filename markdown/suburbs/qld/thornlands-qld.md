# Thornlands (QLD)

Slug: `thornlands-qld`

We know exactly how a Saturday starts in Thornlands: we’re still half-asleep and the kids have already declared war on the backyard. For years the local coverage described Thornlands Community Park as our big-arse consolation prize — “a mini fenced-in adventure-land” with a massive netted climbing structure, giant slides, water play, a dinosaur dig and a very cute cubby. As Brisbane Kids put it, the place was built to exhaust children into submission (and it worked).

If you want coordinates for our chaos, King Country Bayside sits at 53 Dinwoodie Road — that’s the kind of address we use to direct grandparents and the dodgy GPS. The park’s fully fenced layout and those gigantic concrete gears mean we can sit on a bench pretending to sip something sophisticated while our offspring perform gravity experiments.

For the mums among us, the digital village is real: Mums4Mums turned up in the archives as a proper support network — registration forms, groups that focus on Matrescence, and professionally run sessions. In other words, if you need an ally who understands tantrums that last longer than the school bell, that’s our people. Sitchu even mentioned Thornlands in a roundup of Brisbane nail salons, which is peak suburban flex: we’ll school you in playground politics and manicure recs.

Petty drama? The press around here is modest, so we don’t have a running tabloid feud — mostly park reviews, mums’ meetups and the occasional national festival line-up that sounds fun until you remember it’s happening in Brisbane proper. Broadsheet once ran the Wildlands Festival 2026 lineup for Brisbane, which is aspirational for us (we’ll cheer it on from the cul-de-sac).

Who would hate living here: anyone who needs a 24/7 neon nightclub, bespoke CBD brunches or an artisanal water-bottle obsession — this isn’t your scene. Who would love it: families, people who count fenced playgrounds as a public good, and anyone who enjoys community groups that actually do something useful.

We may not be glamorous. But our kids can conquer a netted climbing structure, and our mums have a manual. That’s Thornlands — reliable, a little sandy, and absolutely ours.
