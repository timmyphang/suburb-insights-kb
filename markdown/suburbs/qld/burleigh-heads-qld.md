# Burleigh Heads (QLD)

Slug: `burleigh-heads-qld`

Back in the day we were the sleepy Gold Coast suburb you drove through on the way to Surfers Paradise. Then Broadsheet wrote a few love letters, Time Out started crowning our point breaks, and suddenly everyone wanted a piece of Burleigh Heads. We noticed.

Mornings used to begin with a loop around Burleigh Head National Park — the Oceanview Track and the Rainforest Circuit taking us up to Tumgun Lookout, keeping an eye out for whales out past Snapper Rocks. After that, we'd wander into town, past the foreshore plans people were talking about (they were proposing to tinker with John Laws Park, rework the Burleigh Pavilion Plaza and realign the Oceanway) and queue up at whatever bakery got the nod in the guide. The Paddock Bakery was name-checked as a staple; the Village Markets and Burleigh Farmers Markets at Burleigh Heads State School on the Gold Coast Highway were where we picked up local produce and gossip. On the last Sunday of the month the Art and Craft Markets would do their thing and the Rotary’s Great Burleigh Duck Race would float by as proof we still liked rubber ducks more than high-rises.

There was always theatre. Concrete Playground wrote that the Burleigh Pavilion — “The Pav” to some — had taken over 43 Goodwin Terrace as a 640‑person beachfront behemoth, and that the Mondrian was due to bring a 24‑storey, 208‑room splash to the skyline in 2025. Urban List and Time Out used to tell visitors to book ahead for Rick Shores (that fried Moreton Bay bug roll got a mention), Light Years and other seafront favourites. Once a tabloid sniffed out a celebrity lunch at “a swanky Asian fusion” spot here and the place for a hot minute felt like a runway.

Who would hate it here? If you loathe surfers, queues for patios, and people who have opinions about right-hand barrels, this is not your paradise. If you can’t cope with the idea of a 640‑seat beachfront bar or a hotel that was slated to change the skyline, maybe stay inland.

Who would love it? Surfers (we were even singled out in a surf-spot study), walkers who know the headland tracks, market lovers, and anyone who likes their beachside life to come with excellent restaurants and just enough local colour to keep things interesting. We may have changed, but we’re still Burleigh.
