# Eight Mile Plains (QLD)

Slug: `eight-mile-plains-qld`

We know the traffic lights at Warrigal Road like the back of our hand — it’s where we debate whether the morning’s worth the almond to sugar ratio. Back in the day Urban List put Reno Fine Patisserie at 4/261 Warrigal Road on a pedestal for flaky croissants and almond masterpieces, and we took that as gospel; Rice Paper Vietnamese was also on Urban List’s radar as the go‑to for quick pho and rice vermicelli takeaway. Those were the bragging rights we waved at visitors.

These days the headline is less about who makes the butteriest pastry and more about moving people: the Brisbane Metro now actually links Eight Mile Plains to Roma Street and, via the 169 corridor, UQ Lakes. As Concrete Playground previewed, services on the 169 during peak can run every five minutes, with low‑floor design, mobility aid bays, onboard wifi and USB charging points — yes, the chargers you secretly need on a Monday.

Housing here is the kind of practical charm we like. We’ve watched listings like 15/41 Bleasby Road — light‑filled townhouses in friendly complexes, pool and BBQ facilities and even an onsite unit manager — pop up on Sitchu when people decide they want low‑maintenance and a yard that won’t keep you up at night.

Our local infrastructure reads like a community handbook: an Eight Mile Plains State School for the littlies, a community health centre that was listed at 51 McKechnie Drive, and services like Multicap at 269 Padstow Rd that were on the directory for local support. And if you need to know who’s putting on a sausage sizzle or a swap meet, the Find It Locally noticeboards have you covered.

Who would hate it here? If you’re after non‑stop nightlife, boutique pretension or living somewhere that’s never been name‑checked for a croissant, you’ll find us a bit too suburban‑sensible. Who would love it? Commuters who actually use the Metro, families who want local schools and health links, and anyone who appreciates a top‑tier almond croissant — even if that one was a former local celebrity, we still talk about it like it’s a religious experience.
