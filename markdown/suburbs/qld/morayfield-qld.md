# Morayfield (QLD)

Slug: `morayfield-qld`

Back in the day — or at least in the dusty archive we dug through — Morayfield felt like a weekend plan you could set your watch by. We’d roll out from Leda Boulevard, drop the bub at the Mum and Bub Hub on Dickson Road (they were open 8–8, seven days — lifesavers, according to the website), and pretend we were running errands rather than staging a tiny suburban victory parade.

By nightfall we’d end up at BITE Markets at North Harbour on Nolan Drive — Concrete Playground used to list the place as the shipping‑container precinct that ran Friday and Saturday nights, 4–10pm, with a $3 adult entry, room for more than 600 cars and, crucially, a dog-friendly policy. There was even a Doggo Festival: two evenings of pupper stalls, prizes and a dog fashion show, because where else will your corgi get couture?

If it was a health-first kind of Saturday, there was a mention of pop‑up vax clinics at Bunnings Morayfield — the story framed it as the kind of weekend initiative that paired the jab with a snag. Bunnings itself sits on the corner of Morayfield & Walker Roads, according to the listings, and Harvey Norman used to dominate at 245 Morayfield Rd (Localsearch gave them a solid 4.3 stars — not bad for a warehouse of impulse purchases).

We know the sensible ones among us shopped at Morayfield Road Super Cycles on 135 Morayfield Road when the kids demanded bikes, and the locals swore by the long list of takeaway options the directory showed across town. And yes, Junkee once nicknamed Sidney Phillips “the mayor of Morayfield” — our small‑town music cred, if you will.

Who would hate living here? People who can’t handle a $3 market entry, a dog in a bow tie, or a sausage sizzle at a pop‑up clinic. Also, anyone allergic to practical retail therapy.

Who would love it? Families who need the Mum and Bub Hub, bargain hunters navigating Harvey Norman and Bunnings, cyclists from Morayfield Road Super Cycles, market lovers who live for Friday and Saturday nights at BITE Markets — and anyone who enjoys small suburban joys that make a postcode feel stubbornly like home.
