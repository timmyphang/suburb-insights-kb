# Tallebudgera (QLD)

Slug: `tallebudgera-qld`

Back in the day — and by that we mean every weekend we could wrestle the kids out of our hammocks — we’d pile into the car and head for Tallebudgera like it was a secret handshake. We know the slow rhythm here: a paddle out on Tallebudgera Creek, a cautious nod to the folks who launch kayaks toward the river mouth or upstream among the mangroves toward Schuster Park (Urban List told us that’s a thing), then a quiet retreat to a shady patch of grass.

If you want the postcard moment, Araluen Swimming Hole is our favourite humble brag. As Broadsheet noted, the Araluen spot — corner of Araluen and Tallebudgera Creek Roads, rope swing and all — is where you pretend you’re a mountain nymph and actually just squeal when the water’s cold. Time Out’s whisper about Echo Beach being the “less crowded” cove? We’ll confirm: the walk from Burleigh Head National Park is worth the smug look on your mate’s face.

We aren’t immune to a little luxe, either. For years wellness roundups pointed to Gwinganna Lifestyle Retreat — Time Out even mentioned its Southern Hemisphere-sized spa — and to bathhouse options like The Bath House and Greenhouse Bathhouse in the valley. And yes, the Custard Canteen at Tallebudgera Creek once earned its directory fame for Portuguese custard tarts; we won’t pretend we didn’t queue.

Petty local drama? Not really our style — unless you count when Bleach* turned up with 30-minute mini concerts in the valley (Concrete Playground — remember that?). Suddenly our secret nooks had programs and timetables. It was like seeing your quiet uncle with a microphone.

Who would hate living here: anyone who thinks the only valid weekend involves flashing lights and crowded laneways. If you need a constant hit of frenetic city “energy,” Tallebudgera will make you twitch.

Who would love it: families who want a safe, netted swim spot on clear waters (yes, even the family blogs raved), anyone after paddling and quiet beaches, and the wellness types who used to book long weekends at Gwinganna. We’re low-key, a bit smug, and perfectly happy to keep it that way — as long as you bring the custard tarts.
