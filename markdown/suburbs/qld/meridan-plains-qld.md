# Meridan Plains (QLD)

Slug: `meridan-plains-qld`

We live on the north‑west edge of Caloundra where the paddocks leaned into suburbia and someone decided it was time to build a life on the fringe. As Wikipedia notes, Meridan Plains used to be properly rural; now we're the proud result of residential sprawl edging toward the Bruce Highway and the beaches people insist are "close by."

Mornings here are a tidy family shuffle: drop-offs (or used to be — see below) at Pacific Lutheran College on Woodlands Blvd, a wave at the Meridan Community Church on 70 Springs Drive, and the occasional playgroup that was listed at Meridan State College on Friday mornings in 2026. For years the local calendar lived and breathed around community centres: Raising Mamas used to hold sessions at the Meridan Community Centre (also 70 Springs Dr), and the kid-centric pages we find are full of term schedules and pram-friendly notes.

We should say up front: there aren't any recent hard-news features about Meridan Plains in our files — we're leaning on older listings, council pages and the parenting blogs, so think of this as affectionate local folklore rather than a current events bulletin.

Our more unlikely local claim to fame is municipal: the Meridan Plains extractive resource area sits in the Mooloolah River floodplain — a proper 1,095 hectares (714ha potential extraction, 381ha separation). It sounds epic on paper and gives us something to point at when we want to feel important.

Petty local drama? The archives are disappointingly wholesome. Most rows here are over playgroup bookings, kids-eat-free nights at Parklands Tavern (238 Parklands Blvd — it was listed as offering kids eat free on Tuesdays at 5.30pm), and boutique services like Brooklyn And Billie at Shop 1, 5 Forest Park Street. Hardly prime tabloid material, which we secretly prefer.

Who would hate it here: anyone after a 24/7 nightlife, an artisan-deli boulevard or daily breaking headlines. Who would love it: families who want green spaces, short drives to Caloundra, easy Bruce Highway access and the comforting hum of community groups, schools and a tavern that used to do cheap Tuesday dinners. We’ll take that quiet pride, thanks very much.
