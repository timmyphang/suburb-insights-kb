# Wacol (QLD)

Slug: `wacol-qld`

Back in the day (and yes, the internet still has receipts), our weekend began at the RSPCA campus in Wacol — not because we’re saints, but because where else can you sip mediocre coffee watching rescue cats plot world domination? Urban List once put the Black Cat Cafe on the map; it was run by the RSPCA and, shock horror, the cats were available for adoption. We loved that about Wacol: practical, slightly chaotic, full of animals with better manners than half our neighbours.

Mornings look like this: a wander through Pooh Corner Bushland Reserve on Wolston Rd at dawn — the kangaroo-spotting guide on Brisbane Kids swore it’s prime time — then a lazy splash at Carole Park Swim Centre. The old listings remind us the pool had a 25m heated lane, a beach pool and even a wading pool, which made it the perfect place to both teach kids to swim and gossip about whose esky went missing at the last community barbecue.

Petty local drama? We’ll keep it factual: once upon a pandemic, Concrete Playground flagged a public-health alert linked to a Caltex in Wacol. We dutifully monitored symptoms and felt very grown-up about it for approximately three days. More recently-ish (well, proposals are still on their way to becoming realities) planners talked about a green bridge from Bellbowrie to Wacol — pedestrians, cyclists and maybe public transport. We attended the community consultation in spirit if not in person, mainly so we could tell guests we’re on the route to the future.

Who would hate living here: anyone who needs boutique laneway cafés and rooftop aperitivos on demand. Also, if you’re allergic to industrial hum, say hi to Metal‑Tech Industries — MTI’s been doing precision sheet metal in Wacol for decades, and that’s part of our honest-to-goodness backbone.

Who’d love it: families and animal lovers. The RSPCA Family Fun Day (yes, Brisbane Kids listed that winter school-holiday event) means free face painting, flyball and adoptable fluff. Pooh Corner’s wildlife, the community pools and a genuine sense that the suburb is more about real life than Instagram filters — that’s Wacol, and we own it.
