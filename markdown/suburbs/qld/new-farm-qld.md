# New Farm (QLD)

Slug: `new-farm-qld`

Back in the day (and by “back in the day” we mean all the lovingly archived guides and reviews we could find), our Saturday in New Farm always started like this: we’d wander New Farm Park under those enormous fig trees, dodge a Jugger match, and pretend we’re casual about city views while the Jan Powers Farmers’ Markets tempt us with picnic goods.

From there it was a gentle drift through Merthyr Village — where New Farm Seafood was writing the sustainable-market-and-diner column in our stomachs (think bug rolls and grilled barramundi, if the reviews are to be believed). We used to queue for a buzzy weekend brekky at New Farm Deli, or pop into Idle, which evolved from Agnes Bakery and apparently served Baghdad eggs and tonka‑bean kouign‑amann for the more adventurous among us. Double Shot had that riverside espresso-bar vibe, and yes, Time Out once made a case for Death Before Decaf being gloriously hardcore coffee-meets-skate-culture. Urban List even kept a shortlist for the caffeine-obsessed — Blue Bear Coffeehouse and Mylk + Ko made appearances in those lists.

Local drama? The internet had its moments: Pedestrian TV ran a bizarre little tale about a trespasser who apparently broke into a Brisbane home to use the pool — which made us triple‑check our backyard gates and question our British seaside levels of neighbourly trust. Otherwise, the scuffle here is more about who snagged the last sourdough at the market than anything mayoral.

Who would hate living here? If you loathe fig trees, baby swings, artisan picnic boxes (Grape & Grazing, we see you), riverside strolls and people who know the Brisbane Powerhouse’s Moonlight Cinema schedule, this isn’t your postcode. Sitchu’s profile doesn’t help if you’re allergic to affluent leafiness.

Who would love it? Young professionals, families addicted to playground reputations, people who rate a weekend by how many eateries they can name — 075 Pinsa Romana, À la Bonne Franquette — and anyone who’ll get excited about a neighbourhood Italian, like Pici, which was slated to take over Gertie’s site. We’re biased, obviously: for a compact patch of riverside charm, New Farm’s nostalgia-laden playbook still reads pretty well.
