# Yandina (QLD)

Slug: `yandina-qld`

Back in the day our Saturday started before the sun got uppity — we’d head down to the Yandina Country Markets on North Street (6am–12pm, if you remember) and pretend we were doing local-business civic duty while furtively buying too many plants. The Ginger Factory on 50 Pioneer Road used to be our excuse for doing family things — new arcade, free playground — so parents could pretend it was educational while kids practised sugar-fuelled chaos.

We were the town that didn’t try to be Mooloolaba. Spirit House on 20 Ninderry Road was the restaurant that quietly transformed us: bamboo-heavy grounds, pagodas, whole fish and coconut soup that made inland Yandina look like a proper destination rather than a convenient turn-off. Bar Deco was our little European affectation — a Belgian beer and wine garden tucked into town, proving we could be cosy and continental when we wanted to be. Collective Haus at 19 Farrell Street was where you went if you liked your homewares with a side of provenance and your secondhand HEM line to carry home.

Local trivia: the Yandina Hotel used to be the pub everyone could agree on, Yandina Historic House sat at 3 Pioneer Road to remind us we had history, and Yandina Station (684 North Arm/Yandina Creek Road) was the wedding-ready cattle station you’d mention when putting on airs. Terella Brewing even started sprouting in the rural patch between Yandina and Eumundi, because of course it did — craft beer needs pastoral Instagram shots.

Our petty municipal subplot was low-key: the Yandina Feed Barn on 13 Farrell Street sent community posts about day-old chicks and recently scoring grants for improved security, which is the small-town equivalent of council drama. And for the toddler set, the Little Farmers Playgroup — Ride Pete the Pony and all — was the sort of wholesome chaos only we could coordinate.

Who would hate living here? The beachfront juice-and-surf-boutique crowd twenty minutes away — we’re too leafy, too slow, and don’t care about your curated tan. Who’d love it? Families, farmers-market romantics, food pilgrims still whispering Spirit House’s name, vintage hunters from Collective Haus, and anyone who appreciates a pub that remembers your face. We’re a town that wears its modest charms like an old hat — a bit frayed, thoroughly loved, and ready to make you a cup of ginger tea.
